# Effect of Low-Temperature Fluctuations on Martian Steel Structures: Numerical and Analytical Studies

**Authors:** Yongtao Bai, Jing Li, and Xuhong Zhou

**Abstract:** This paper employs numerical simulation methodologies to scrutinize the displacement variations and stress distribution within a full-scale circular arch steel structure subjected to diverse thermal cycles, thereby assessing the influence of severe cryogenic temperature fluctuations on Martian steel infrastructure. The findings contribute to understanding the resilience of such structures within fluctuating low-temperature conditions, offering an empirical foundation for architectural design and material selection for Martian bases. DOI: [10.1061/JCRGEI.CRENG-875](https://doi.org/10.1061/JCRGEI.CRENG-875).

## Introduction
Mars presents environmental extremities such as reduced gravity and extreme temperature fluctuations. The thin atmosphere results in significant day-night temperature differences. This study prioritizes gravity, internal air pressure, and extreme temperature fluctuations as main factors. Numerical simulations (ABAQUS) investigate the fatigue performance of steel structures under alternating low temperatures on Mars.

### Martian Environment Considerations
- **Diurnal Cycle:** ~24h 40min.
- **Thermal Cycle:** -110°C to +20°C simulated as a triangular wave (16h cooling, 8h warming).
- **Atmosphere:** Tenuous, density ~1% of Earth's.

### Structural Design
- **Form:** Semicircular arch (5m radius, 10m span, 50mm thickness).
- **Loads:** Internal pressure (101.325 kPa), External regolith pressure (18.687 kPa for 3m shield), Gravity (3.73 m/s²).
- **Material:** Steel (selected due to available iron/nickel on Mars surface).

## Results and Discussion

### Structural Integrity
- No cracking or failure was observed after 1-10 Martian years of simulated cycles.
- The 50mm-thick steel structure meets service life criteria (minimum 5-10 years).

### Stress Distribution
- Without temperature cycles: Stress localized on the domed arch.
- With temperature cycles: Stress concentration shifts to the floor member and the junction between the arch and floor.

### Displacement Variations
- Vault displacement under no-temperature cycles: ~2.70 mm.
- Vault displacement under thermal cycling: Ranged from 12.1651 mm to 13.1124 mm.
- Displacement does not increase monotonically; it reached a peak at 1,374 cycles before stabilizing/fluctuating due to stress redistribution.

## Conclusion
1. The steel semicircular arch structure is capable of surviving the Martian thermal environment for at least 10 Martian years.
2. Structural design for Mars should feature thickened floor members to manage stress concentrations induced by thermal cycles.
3. The interface between the dome and floor must be optimized to mitigate fatigue and improve durability.
