# Materials and design concepts for space-resilient structures

**Authors:** Mohannad Z. Naser, Alaa I. Chehab
**Publication:** Progress in Aerospace Sciences 98 (2018) 74–90

## Abstract
This paper presents a state-of-the-art literature review on recent developments of “space-native” construction materials and highlights evolutionary design concepts for “space-resilient” structures. It explores ISRU, harvesting of elements, and enabling technologies like lab-grown materials and 3D printing.

## 1. Introduction
Space colonization requires habitats that withstand extreme environments (radiation, vacuum, thermal cycles, microgravity). Utilizing in-situ resource utilization (ISRU) is critical due to the high cost of transporting materials from Earth ($20,000/kg to the Moon).

## 2. Space Environment
- **Atmosphere/Vacuum:** Lack of atmosphere leads to high radiation, micrometeoroid impacts, and material outgassing.
- **Temperature:** Fluctuations on the Moon range from -173°C to 127°C.
- **Gravity:** Moon (1/6g) and Mars (1/3g) affect structural loading and mechanics.

## 3. Space Resources
- **ISRU:** Lunar soil contains silica, alumina, calcium oxide, and iron oxides.
- **Harvesting:** Mining minerals from asteroids (e.g., Phobos, Deimos) or comets using robotic tools.
- **Bio-materials:** Spider silk and silkworm proteins for high strength-to-weight reinforcement.

## 4. Properties of Space Construction Materials
### 4.1. Concrete Derivates
- **Hydraulic Concrete:** Limited by the need for water.
- **Sulfur Concrete:** Non-hydraulic, created by melting sulfur (120–150°C) and mixing with regolith. Compressive strengths: 39–75.7 MPa (Lunar) and 20–63 MPa (Martian).
- **Geopolymers:** Produced by reaction between aluminosilicate (regolith) and alkaline solutions. Strengths: 16.6–33.1 MPa.

### 4.2. Metals and Alloys
- **Aluminum (Al):** Low density, low melting point, used for rigid modules and frames.
- **Magnesium (Mg):** Excellent vibration damping and radiation shielding.
- **Titanium (Ti):** High strength and corrosion resistance but harder to extract.

### 4.3. Advanced Materials
- **Composites:** Organic matrix (Gr/E), metal matrix (SiC/Al), and ceramic matrix (C/C).
- **Shape Memory Alloys (SMA):** Used for self-deployable mechanisms and joining structural members.

## 5. Processing Methods
- **Solar/Laser Sintering:** Focused energy melts regolith grains to bond them (e.g., D-Shape technology).
- **Microwave Sintering:** Volumetric heating; can penetrate up to 13.4 mm in lunar simulant.
- **Dry-Mix/Steam-Injection (DMSI):** Producing 50 mm thick concrete cubes with 69 MPa strength.

## 6. Structural Concepts
- **Class I (Pre-fabricated):** Standardized rigid modules (e.g., Apollo/ISS style outposts).
- **Class II (Deployable/Inflatable):** High packing coefficient; uses high-strength fabrics (Kevlar) and rigidization via chemical reaction.
- **Class III (ISRU-based):** Utilizing regolith shielding or 3D-printed structures.
- **Underground Bases:** Utilizing natural lava tubes (30m diameter) to provide 10-20m of natural shielding.
- **Mobile Bases:** Roving habitats for reconnaissance and exploration range expansion.

## 9. Conclusions
Realizing space exploration requires overcoming limitations in material processing and automated robotics. Concrete and its derivatives (sulfur/geopolymer) remain the highest potential candidates for large-scale Class III construction.
