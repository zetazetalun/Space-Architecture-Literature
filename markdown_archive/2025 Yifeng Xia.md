# Lunar base infrastructure construction: Challenges and future directions\n\n## Metadata\n- **Journal:** Automation in Construction 176 (2025) 106251\n- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.autcon.2025.106251\n- **Authors:** Yifeng Xia, Yuyue Gao, Wenbin Han, Xinyi Li, Cheng Zhou, Yan Zhou, Lieyun Ding\n- **Keywords:** Extraterrestrial construction, Extreme environment, Resource utilization, Structure design\n\n## Abstract\nThis article reviews existing lunar construction concepts and designs, analyzing their importance for sustained human presence on the moon. It summarizes research progress in transportation, navigation and communication, energy, and habitation infrastructure, addressing construction processes, site selection, building materials, and structural design. Additionally, the article highlights the challenges and future directions in lunar infrastructure construction.\n\n## 1. Introduction\nGlobal space initiatives like the Artemis Program (NASA), ILRS (China/Global), and Terrae Novae (ESA) are focused on lunar bases as testbeds for Mars exploration. Construction requires overcoming bottlenecks in materials science, manufacturing, and intelligent equipment due to the extreme environment (vacuum, microgravity, radiation, and abrasive dust).\n\n## 2. Transportation Infrastructure\n- **Landing/Launching Pads:** Essential for dust mitigation. Methods include robotic teleoperation and in-situ manufacturing (vacuum sintering of interlocking bricks).\n- **Roads:** Utilizing microwave, solar, or laser sintering to solidify regolith for industrial load-bearing pavements.\n- **Elevators:** Long-term concepts for cislunar transport using high-strength materials (carbon nanotubes/graphene).\n\n## 3. Navigation and Communication\n- **Systems:** NASA’s LunaNet framework and terrestrial GNSS extension. Use of 5G cellular technology for local surface communication.\n\n## 4. Energy Infrastructure\n- **Solar:** Focus on the lunar South Pole (craters like Shackleton) for >80% sunlight exposure.\n- **Nuclear:** NASA’s 40 kW fission surface power concepts for constant supply during lunar nights.\n- **Storage:** Using processed regolith as thermal storage materials and regenerative fuel cells.\n\n## 5. Habitation Infrastructure\n- **Launched Habitats (Class I/II):** Rigid modules (Class I), expandable/inflatable structures (Class II) like TransHab.\n- **In-Situ Habitats (Class III):** 3D printing (Lunar Ampullae, Sinterhab) and masonry assembly (Xuan Wu plan, Voussoir dome).\n- **Subterranean:** Utilizing natural lava tubes for radiation and micrometeorite protection.\n\n## 6. Challenges and Future Directions\n1. **Material Fabrication:** Scalability from laboratory to mass production of large structural elements under 1/6g.\n2. **Transportation:** Lack of regular Earth-Moon logistics and limited surface mobility.\n3. **Energy:** Scaling power to industrial levels beyond 40 kW conceptual designs.\n4. **CNPT:** Interoperability and signal stability in complex lunar topography.\n5. **Life Support:** Long-term durability and closed-loop recycling efficiency.
