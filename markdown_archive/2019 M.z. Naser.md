# Space-native construction materials for earth-independent and sustainable infrastructure

**Author:** M.Z. Naser  
**Journal:** Acta Astronautica  
**Volume:** 155 (2019) 264–273

## Abstract
A successful space exploration requires establishing permanent and earth-independent infrastructure that are not only resilient to the extreme environment of space but also preferably made of sustainable and indigenous “space-native” construction materials. This review covers feasibility of exploiting in-situ lunar and Martian resources as well as harvesting of elements and compounds, from near Earth objects (NEOs), to produce extraterrestrial materials suitable for construction of space-based infrastructure. This review also details material features and characteristics required to withstand the unique, and harsh, effects of space environment. In essence, this paper reviews past and recent advancements in construction-based materials that could be used in the design and development of space human bases and highlights design consideration for sustainable human settlements.

## 1. Introduction
Transportation of construction materials from Earth costs between $5000 to $20,000 per kg to the Moon. Utilizing in-situ resources (ISRU) to build infrastructure on the Moon and Mars is essential for economical and sustainable exploration. Studies have verified that both the Moon and Mars hold materials that can be processed into concrete and metals.

## 2. Space Environment and Resources
### 2.1. Space Environment
Main differences between Earth, Moon, and Mars include radiation, vacuum, and low gravitational forces. 
- **Lunar Temperature:** −173 to 127 °C.
- **Martian Temperature:** −60 to 125 °C.
- **Impact:** Micrometeorites reach speeds of 20–70 km/s.

### 2.2. Space Resources
- **Regolith:** Surficial layer of fine particles. Lunar regolith contains SiO2 (37-45%), Al2O3 (19-21%), and FeO (8-16%).
- **Basalt:** Mafic rock with compressive strength of 140–290 MPa (4-6 times stronger than terrestrial concrete).
- **NEOs:** Asteroids can be mined for minerals like nickel, iron, cobalt, and water.

## 3. Properties of Space-Native Materials
### 3.1. Concrete-like Hybrids
- **Lunar Concrete:** Can achieve strengths up to 76 MPa. Requires water, which is a major constraint.
- **Polymer Concrete:** Uses resins/epoxies as binders. Compressive strengths vary from 17 to 60 MPa.
- **Sulfur Concrete:** Water-free; made by heating sulfur (120–150 °C) and mixing with regolith. Achieves strengths of 5.5–17 MPa (can be improved with fiber reinforcement).
- **Geopolymers:** Formed by interaction between aluminosilicates and alkaline solutions. Strengths between 16.6 and 33.1 MPa.

### 3.2. Metals and Alloys
- **Aluminum & Magnesium:** Suitable due to low density. Aluminum found in lunar Highlands (~15% soil weight).
- **Iron & Titanium:** Abundant on Moon and Mars. Titanium (Ti6Al4V) offers high strength and low thermal expansion.

## 4. Design Considerations
Space structures can carry 3 to 6 times more mass than on Earth due to low gravity, allowing for larger spans or slender members. Shielding (1-6m of regolith) is proposed to protect against radiation and thermal fluctuations.
