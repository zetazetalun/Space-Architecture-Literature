# Regolith Sintering and 3D Printing for Lunar Construction: An Extensive Review on Recent Progress

**Authors:** Muhammad Shazwan Suhaizan, Phuong Tran, Ash Exner, Brian G. Falzon
**Published in:** Progress in Additive Manufacturing (2024)
**DOI:** 10.1007/s40964-023-00537-1

## Abstract
In-Situ Resource Utilisation (ISRU) is seen as essential for constructing lunar infrastructure. This review evaluates various sintering techniques using lunar regolith simulants to demonstrate feasibility for lunar construction. Processing parameters are assessed for their influence on mechanical properties such as density, hardness, and porosity.

## 1. Background of Lunar Construction
Constructing a safe human habitat on the Moon requires shielding from extreme temperatures (-248 °C to 77 °C), radiation, micrometeoroids, and lunar dust. Transporting materials from Earth costs approximately $3k/kg to $62k/kg, making ISRU the only sustainable solution for large-scale development.

## 2. Sintering Techniques for ISRU

### 2.1 Bulk Sintering
*   **Radiant Heat Sintering:** Straightforward method using infrared radiant heat. Sintering temperatures typically range between 1100 and 1380 °C. Compressive strengths of 30–200 MPa were achieved, with higher results linked to pre-pressing forces.
*   **Microwave Sintering:** Uses electromagnetic radiation (2.45 GHz). Lunar soil couples well with microwaves. Compressive strengths reached up to 70 MPa. Requires careful control to avoid 'runaway' heating.
*   **Spark Plasma Sintering (SPS):** Combines pulsed electric current and external pressure. Produces the highest densities (up to 2.9 g/cm³) and allows lower sintering temperatures compared to radiant methods.

### 2.2 Focused Sintering (Additive Manufacturing)
*   **Laser Sintering (SLS/SLM):** Uses a high-power laser to fuse small volumes of powder. Capable of high precision for mechanical parts but energy-intensive. Compressive strengths recorded up to 30 MPa with high porosity.
*   **Solar Sintering:** Utilizes concentrated sunlight via heliostats. Ideal for taking advantage of local energy. The RegoLight project demonstrated building elements with 2.31 MPa compressive strength.
*   **Direct Ink Writing (DIW):** Uses a mixture of regolith simulant and organic binders to extrude pastes. Requires post-processing (sintering) and the transport of binders from Earth.

## 4. Conclusion
Exploiting local materials is the most pragmatic approach for lunar infrastructure. Radiant sintering is suitable for bulk shielding, while laser and solar sintering are better for precision components. Major challenges include verifying high-temperature behavior in vacuum/microgravity and developing non-destructive testing methods.
