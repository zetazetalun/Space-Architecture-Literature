# Review of research into bioregenerative life support system(s) which can support humans living in space

**Authors:** Hong Liu, Zhikai Yao, Yuming Fu, Jiajie Feng
**Journal:** Life Sciences in Space Research
**Year:** 2021
**DOI:** 10.1016/j.lssr.2021.09.003

## Abstract
To travel beyond Earth and realize long-term survival in deep space, humans need to construct Bioregenerative Life Support Systems (BLSS), which reduce the requirement for supplies from Earth by in situ regenerating oxygen, water, and food, and prevent pollution of extraterrestrial bodies by recycling waste. This paper reviews the development of BLSS from the 1960s to the present, focusing on Earth-based experimental facilities and the transition toward extraterrestrial application.

## 1. Introduction
Crewed deep-space exploration to the Moon and Mars requires BLSS to minimize supplies from Earth. BLSS is an artificial closed ecosystem composed of humans, plants, animals, and microorganisms, combining biotechnology and engineering control.

## 2. BLSS System Framework
Theoretical and technological systems of BLSS are multidisciplinary, including biology, environmental engineering, ecology, materials science, and systems engineering. The development path involves studying individual units, followed by artificial ecosystem interactions and finally Earth-based simulation experiments.

## 3. Research Progress of Earth-based BLSS
### 3.1 Soviet Union/Russia (BIOS-3)
- **BIOS-3 (1972):** A 315 m³ facility that achieved >90% closure in gas and water circulation and met 40–45% of food needs for three people. It achieved 95% closure in specific experiments.

### 3.2 United States (NASA & Biosphere 2)
- **LMLSTP:** Achieved one-person closed survival for 15 days, and four-person survival for up to 91 days. Wheat provided 25% of oxygen and 5% of food.
- **HDU-DSH:** Habitat Demonstration Unit established in the Arizona desert.
- **Biosphere 2:** The largest closed ecological system facility (1.3 hectares, 1.8 × 10⁵ m³). The second experiment produced 100% of the food needed.

### 3.3 Japan (CEEF)
- Introduced the first animal unit (goats). It used physical and chemical equipment for rapid material circulation but was large and required significant external control.

### 3.4 Europe (MELiSSA)
- Focused on a modular, five-compartment design (waste decomposition, photosynthetic bacteria, nitrifying bacteria, microalgae/higher plants, and crew).
- **DLR Lunar Greenhouse:** Study for a semi-cylindrical hybrid structure (20 m length, 3 m radius) with rigid end-caps for lunar deployment.

### 3.5 China (Lunar Palace 365)
- **Lunar Palace 1 (2013):** A 300 m³ system. In 2014, a 105-day experiment achieved 100% O2 and H2O regeneration and 55% food regeneration.
- **Lunar Palace 365 (2017-2018):** A 370-day experiment with four crew members achieved 98.2% overall material closure and 83% food regeneration.

## 4. Prospect of Spatial BLSS
Extraterrestrial BLSS requires adaptation to gravity, radiation, and magnetic field differences. The lunar polar regions are proposed for initial experiments due to stable light energy and possible water ice.

## 5. Development Strategy (Three-Stage)
1. **First Stage:** Hydroponics-based with some in situ soil-like substrates; 100% oxygen/water regeneration.
2. **Second Stage:** Expansion using large amounts of in-situ soil-like substrates; animal protein production (e.g., mealworms).
3. **Third Stage:** Large-scale bases with cycled inorganic salts and fish cultivation.

## 6. Evaluation Indices
1. Regeneration rate (%) of O2, H2O, and food.
2. Stability and reliability.
3. Energy/Space consumption per person (kWh•m³/person).
4. Equipment weight (kg/person).
5. Labor intensity (hour/person).
