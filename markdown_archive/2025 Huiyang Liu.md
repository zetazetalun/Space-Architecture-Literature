# Failure modes and energy evolution of basalt in uniaxial compression tests after cryogenic freeze-thaw cycles

**Authors:** Huiyang Liu, Changyu Jin, Bo Sun, Xin Zhao, Jun Bai, Xin Chai, Lingyue Hou  
**Journal:** Environmental Earth Sciences (2025) 84:515  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1007/s12665-025-12529-1

## Abstract
This study examines the effects of cryogenic freeze-thaw (F-T) cycles (-80 °C to 100 °C) on the mechanical properties of basalt specimens, simulating the extreme temperature fluctuations found on celestial bodies like the Moon and Mars. Using uniaxial compression tests, acoustic emission (AE) monitoring, and scanning electron microscopy (SEM), the research found that P-wave velocity decreases by 12% and uniaxial compressive strength (UCS) declines by 26–33% after multiple cycles. The damage is primarily concentrated in the superficial layer (7.9–11.7 mm depth), leading to a significant increase in tensile failure.

## Introduction
Extraterrestrial exploration requires understanding the behavior of local materials under extreme conditions. For example, Mars has an annual mean temperature of -63 °C, and the Moon experiences even more extreme fluctuations. Frequent F-T cycles induce engineering geological disasters, such as rock slope instability. Basalt, widespread on the Moon and Mars, is considered a viable natural building material for constructing extraterrestrial bases.

## Materials and Methods
- **Specimen Preparation**: Intact basalt from a dormant volcano in China (main minerals: plagioclase and pyroxene). Density: 2.9 g/cm³, P-wave velocity: 5800 m/s. Standard cylindrical specimens (Ø 50 mm × 100 mm) were used.
- **Test Procedure**: 
  - Vacuum saturation for 24 hours.
  - Cryogenic F-T cycles: -80 °C (12 h) and 100 °C (12 h) per cycle.
  - Cycle counts: 0, 15, and 25.
  - Monitoring: Uniaxial compression using a TAW-2000 machine; AE monitoring using SH-II acquisition system; Microscopic analysis via SEM.

## Experimental Results

### P-wave Velocity
- P-wave velocity reduces as cycle counts increase.
- After 25 cycles, velocity decreased by 12% (from 5834 m/s to 5113 m/s).

### Mechanical Behaviors and Strength
- **0 Cycles**: Peak stress 249 MPa.
- **15 Cycles**: Peak stress 183 MPa (26% reduction).
- **25 Cycles**: Peak stress 166 MPa (33% reduction).
- **Strain**: F-T cycles increase the strain rate in the compaction stage to approximately 0.7%.

### Acoustic Emission (AE) Monitoring
- **F-T Treated Specimens**: Exhibit bimodal energy release patterns.
- **Damage Depth**: After 15 cycles, damage area radial distance was 7.9 mm; after 25 cycles, it reached 11.7 mm.
- **Failure Type**: Proportion of tensile failure increased from 10.3% (0 cycles) to 28.8% (25 cycles).

### Microscopic Analysis (SEM)
- **0 Cycles**: Predominantly transgranular (TG) failure; particles tightly contacted.
- **25 Cycles**: Obvious intergranular (IG) failure characteristics; sharper corners in new fractures and increased porosity due to frost heave and thermal stress.

## Conclusion
1. Cryogenic F-T cycles cause significant mechanical degradation in basalt.
2. Damage evolves from the surface inwards, with the affected volume reaching up to 46.7% after 25 cycles.
3. Failure modes transition from brittle transgranular fractures to more complex tensile and intergranular failures.
4. These findings provide theoretical guidance for extraterrestrial building construction and geotechnical design in extreme environments.
