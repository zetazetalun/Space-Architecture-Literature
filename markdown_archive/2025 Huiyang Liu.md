# Failure modes and energy evolution of basalt in uniaxial compression tests after cryogenic freeze-thaw cycles ## Abstract This study explores the mechanical impact of extreme temperature fluctuations on basalt, a common material found on the Moon and Mars. Researchers conducted uniaxial compression tests and monitored acoustic emissions on basalt specimens subjected to cryogenic freeze-thaw (F-T) cycles ranging from -80 to 100 degrees Celsius. Key results indicate significant reductions in P-wave velocity and uniaxial compressive strength, with failure modes shifting toward increased tensile failure as thermal damage accumulates. ## Introduction The research notes that harvesting extraterrestrial resources from the Moon, Mars, and asteroids is becoming necessary due to Earth's resource depletion. Basalt is highlighted as a primary candidate for building material on these bodies. However, extreme temperature changes on celestial surfaces cause thermal stress and erosion. The paper aims to provide a theoretical basis for using basalt in extraterrestrial geotechnical engineering by studying its failure mechanisms under space-simulated thermal cycling. ## Materials and Methods Basalt specimens were obtained from a dormant volcano and analyzed using X-ray diffraction, identifying plagioclase and pyroxene as main minerals. Cylindrical specimens (50mm x 100mm) were saturated and subjected to 0, 15, and 25 freeze-thaw cycles. Each cycle lasted 24 hours, involving 12 hours of cooling at -80 degrees Celsius followed by 12 hours of heating at 100 degrees Celsius. Uniaxial compression was performed using a microcomputer-controlled servo machine, with acoustic emission (AE) sensors monitoring crack development and scanning electron microscopy (SEM) used for post-test microstructural analysis. ## Experimental Results and Discussion ### P-wave Velocity P-wave velocity decreased by approximately 12 percent over 25 cycles, indicating the development of micro-fractures within the rock structure. ### Mechanical Behavior The peak uniaxial compressive strength of the basalt declined by 26 percent after 15 cycles and 33 percent after 25 cycles. Stress-strain curves showed that F-T treated specimens exhibited a more pronounced micro-fracture compaction stage, making them more sensitive to initial stress. ### Failure Modes Specimens not subjected to thermal cycling showed brittle, transgranular fracture. In contrast, thermally cycled specimens exhibited surface spalling and a transition toward intergranular failure. Acoustic emission data revealed that thermal damage primarily occurs in the superficial layers of the rock, with damage depth increasing from 7.9mm to 11.7mm as the number of cycles increased. ## Conclusion The study concludes that cryogenic cycling deteriorates the structural integrity of basalt by promoting intergranular cracks and reducing the energy storage capacity of the material. The findings suggest that when using basalt for construction on celestial bodies, engineers must account for a significant reduction in bearing capacity and a shift in failure mechanisms due to the local thermal environment.
