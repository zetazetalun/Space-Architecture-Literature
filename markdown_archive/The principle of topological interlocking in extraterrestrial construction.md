# The principle of topological interlocking in extraterrestrial construction\n\n**Authors:** A.V. Dyskin, Y. Estrin, E. Pasternak, H.C. Khor, A.J. Kanel-Belov\n**Journal:** Acta Astronautica 57 (2005) 10-21\n**DOI:** 10.1016/j.actaastro.2004.12.005\n\n## Abstract\nApplications of a newly established principle of topological interlocking to different types of extraterrestrial construction are considered. Topological interlocking arises when elements of special shapes (osteomorphic bricks) are arranged such that neither can be removed without disturbing neighbors. This method allows for mortar-free structures that tolerate low precision in production and assembly, ideal for in-situ resource utilization (ISRU) and robotic construction. Modular extraterrestrial bases and flexible spacecraft can also be organized using this principle.\n\n## 1. Introduction\nExtraterrestrial structures are categorized into: (1) structures built from basic elements like bricks manufactured in-situ, and (2) modular structures based on Earth-manufactured units. In-situ production avoids the high cost and logistical difficulty of transporting cement and water from Earth, especially since mortar curing in vacuum produces poor results.\n\n## 2. Principle of Topological Interlocking\nInterlocking is achieved by geometry and arrangement where building elements are locked when the assembly is constrained at the periphery. Historical examples include tetrahedral assemblies. Osteomorphic bricks use curved surfaces to allow both planar and corner structures, leading to self-positioning during assembly.\n\n## 3. Extraterrestrial Construction based on Osteomorphic Bricks\n### 3.1 Mortar-free structures\nOsteomorphic bricks allow for structures without binder phase. They are self-adjusting, relaxing the need for high-precision robotic positioning or manufacturing. Constraints can be provided by natural features (craters, pits) or artificial means (external frames, internal post-tensioning cables).\n### 3.1.4 In situ production\nProduction is likely based on sintering regolith dust. Methods include sintering in moulds (microwave or external heating) or Selective Laser Sintering (SLS). SLS allows simultaneous manufacturing of entire layers already in place.\n\n## 4. Protective Cladding\nOsteomorphic tiles can be used as cladding for pre-manufactured modules to provide radiation and thermal protection. Double-skin walls can eliminate radiation leakage through joints.\n\n## 5. Interlocking Modular Structures\nModular bases can be organized as clusters of interlocked modules (tetrahedra, cubes, octahedra). This permits assembly on uneven ground through lateral forces.\n\n## 6. Conclusion\n1. Osteomorphic bricks permit mortar-free, self-adjusting, and fracture-resistant structures.\n2. Topological interlocking is applicable for protective cladding and modular base assembly.\n3. Spaceships can be built as interlocking assemblies of independent modules for mission flexibility.
