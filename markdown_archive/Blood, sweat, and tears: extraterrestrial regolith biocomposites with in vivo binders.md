# Blood, sweat, and tears: extraterrestrial regolith biocomposites with in vivo binders  ## Abstract The exploitation of resources in situ — including regolith, water, and atmospheric gases — will be important for crewed missions to Mars. This work explores using human serum albumin (HSA) as a binder for simulated Lunar and Martian regolith to produce 'extraterrestrial regolith biocomposites (ERBs).' HSA-based ERBs displayed compressive strengths up to 25.0 MPa. Incorporation of urea further increased strength to 39.7 MPa. The study demonstrates potential for 3D-printing and explores the bonding mechanism involving supramolecular beta-sheet networks.  ## 1. Introduction Early extraterrestrial colonies will likely exploit regolith as bulk material for radiation and meteor shielding. Strongly bonded extraterrestrial regolith could serve as cost-effective construction material. Several companies are developing additive manufacturing (3D-printing) for habitat construction based on in situ regolith with binders. This study focuses on in vivo resources (crew-produced).  ## 2. Materials and Methods HSA was dissolved in water (30 wt%) and infused into syringes packed with Lunar (LHS-1) or Martian (MGS-1) simulant. Samples were dehydrated at 65°C. Uniaxial compression tests followed ASTM C39/C39M-20. Recombinant spider silk was also tested.  ## 3. Results and Discussion ### 3.1 Compressive Strength ERBs with 35 wt% HSA reached 25.0 MPa (LHS-1). The addition of 3M urea to 30 wt% HSA increased the UCS of LHS-1 based ERBs to 39.7 MPa. ### 3.2 3D-Printing Potential A mixture of MGS-1 and 15 wt% HSA was manually extruded in layers to a height of 2 cm, demonstrating structural integrity after dehydration. ### 3.3 Adhesion HSA showed an average ultimate shear strength of 5.2 MPa on glass substrates.  ## 4. Conclusions Crew-produced HSA can serve as a binder for regolith, producing materials with strengths exceeding standard concrete.
