# Designing Biopolymer-Bound Regolith Composites for Maximum Compressive Strength

**Authors:** I. Rosa, A. Coto, M. I. Allende, M. D. Lepech, and D. J. Loftus  
**Affiliations:** Dept. of Civil and Environmental Engineering, Stanford Univ.; Space Bioscience Research Division, NASA Ames Research Center.

## ABSTRACT
Extraterrestrial construction presents many interesting and new challenges. Transporting large amounts of construction materials from Earth is cost prohibitive. Thus, to take advantage of limited in situ extraterrestrial resources, this work focuses on a novel class of biopolymer-bound soil composite (BSC) materials. These quasi-brittle composites are produced by desiccating a mixture of soil, water, and a biopolymer binder to create a versatile material with uniaxial compressive strength comparable to concrete. This paper expands upon a BSC design methodology for regolith soils. Previous work has been successful in reliably designing BSC materials with graded sands that exhibit compressive strengths above 20 MPa. Lunar regolith, JSC-1A simulant presents an additional challenge due to the broad ranges of the soil particle sizes. Nineteen BSC mixes using JSC-1A regolith were tested with varying biopolymer solution concentrations and biopolymer to soil ratios. A maximum compressive strength of 25 MPa was obtained at a biopolymer concentration of 48% and a biopolymer to soil ratio of 12.4%. A brief discussion on the effect of manufacturing variables on compressive strength and bulk density is presented. Finally, a design tool is introduced to design regolith BSC for desired compressive strength for future use in the structural design of structures on the Moon, Mars, or other extraterrestrial bodies.

## 1. INTRODUCTION
The manufacture of Ordinary Portland Cement Concrete (OPCC) accounts for 5% of global anthropogenic CO2 emissions. There is a need for new construction materials that consume less energy to produce, can be recycled, and utilize local resources. NASA’s current efforts to develop closed-loop building material systems for long-duration missions in resource-constrained environments serve as a platform for these developments. Biopolymer-bound Soil Composites (BSC) leverage natural protein-based binders and locally sourced aggregates to provide mechanical performance similar to OPCC while satisfying sustainability requirements for both space and terrestrial applications.

## 2. EXPERIMENTAL DESIGN
The compressive strength of BSC materials is a function of the soil maximum bulk density (ρ_bulk_soil), the maximum concentration in solution of the biopolymer (%w/w_BP), and the biopolymer solution density (ρ_BP_sol). For JSC-1A, the maximum bulk density from literature is 2.01 g/cm³ (vibration method) and 1.75 g/cm³ (wet compaction). Mixes with biopolymer solution concentrations of 30%, 38%, 43%, 48%, and 53% were investigated using bovine blood plasma proteins (BSA and immunoglobins).

## 3. MATERIALS AND METHODS
*   **Aggregate:** JSC-1A lunar soil simulant.
*   **Binder:** AP920 Bovine Plasma (78% protein).
*   **Manufacturing:** Dry-mixing of soil and protein, followed by water addition. Compaction was performed in a cylindrical mold (25.4 mm ID) at 5 MPa in two lifts. Specimens were desiccated at 60°C for 24 hours.
*   **Testing:** Unconfined uniaxial compression testing using an MTS Criterion Model 43 machine.

## 5. CONCLUSIONS
JSC-1A/AP920 BSC materials exhibit a linear relationship between compressive strength, dry bulk density, and biopolymer-to-soil ratio when the saturation ratio is less than or equal to unity. Strengths up to 25 MPa make this material suitable for micrometeoroid shielding or airstrips. Future work will investigate 3D printing manufacturing methods.
