# A review on design and construction of the lunar launch/landing infrastructure

**Authors:** Xinyi Li, Yuyue Gao, Yan Zhou, Wenbin Han, Cheng Zhou
**Journal:** Advances in Space Research (2024)
**DOI:** 10.1016/j.asr.2024.07.006

## Abstract
With advances in automated construction and deeper Moon exploration missions, the concept of "building a house on the moon" is becoming increasingly feasible. The launch/landing (LL) infrastructure plays a vital role in ensuring the safety of landers and astronauts. This review explores designs, construction methods (material production to processes), experiments, and future directions, highlighting 3D-printed biomimetic structures.

## 1. Introduction
The Moon serves as humanity's primary gateway. Recent successful missions (Artemis Program, etc.) have spurred renewed interest. The safety of launch and landing is key to accomplishing exploration missions. Unlike Earth, the Moon lacks established facilities, and plumes from landers cause surface damage and dust dispersion.

## 2. Design of the Lunar Landing/Launching Infrastructure
### 2.1. Launch/landing area
Plumes expand rapidly in a vacuum. A zoned design is necessary to accommodate thermal and flow impingement. Size recommendations for LL pads range from 18.8m (Susante and Metzger) to 100m (Olympus project/Harada).

### 2.2. Launch/landing pad
Approaches include:
- **Paving:** Cost-effective; uses rocks or 3D-printed interlocking tiles.
- **3D Extrusion Printing:** Complex geometric structures (e.g., Olympus project).
- **Integrated Design:** Projects like "The Brute" (perimeter berms) and "Lunar Pad" (double-layer hollow structures).

### 2.3. Blast walls and trenches
Used to intercept high-speed regolith ejecta. Designs include inward-deflecting (Sunflower Vault) and outward-deflecting berms.

## 3. In Situ Construction
### 3.1. 3D printing based on concrete extrusion
Involves mixing lunar soil with polymers, sulfur, or geopolymers. Technology includes Contour Crafting (CCC) and Additive Construction with Mobile Emplacement (ACME).

### 3.2. Sintering, melting and assembly
- **LASSS (Large Area Surface Sintering System):** Rover-mounted resistive heater.
- **Solar Sintering:** Using Fresnel lenses to melt top surface.
- **Laser Powder Bed Fusion:** Selective Separation Sintering (SSS) for interlocking tiles.

### 3.3. Building with moon rocks
Rock pavements with armor stones (15cm diameter) are used for peripheral zones to resist shear forces.

## 4. Terrestrial Testing
### 4.1. Building material testing
Evaluation of simulants (JSC-1A, FJS-1) under different temperatures. Sintering peaks around 1100-1200°C.

## 5. Challenges and Future Directions
1. **Multiphysics modeling:** Difficulty in simulating vacuum/low-gravity plume-surface interactions.
2. **Large-scale construction:** Cracking caused by stress concentration in 3D printing/casting.
3. **Terrestrial scaling:** Difficulty in replicating combined vacuum, low gravity, and high thermal shock.

**Future Directions:** Hybrid-driven simulation models, bio-inspired anti-erosion structures (e.g., Sunflower Vault), and autonomous maintenance systems.
