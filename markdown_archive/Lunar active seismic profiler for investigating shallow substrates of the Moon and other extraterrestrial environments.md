# Lunar active seismic profiler for investigating shallow substrates of the Moon and other extraterrestrial environments

**Authors:** Takeshi Tsuji, Taizo Kobayashi, Junji Kinoshita, Tatsunori Ikeda, Tomoki Uchigaki, Yuichiro Nagata, Taichi Kawamura, Kazunori Ogawa, Satoshi Tanaka, Akito Araya
**Journal:** Icarus 404 (2023) 115666
**Published:** 8 June 2023

## Abstract
To investigate the structures and properties of shallow material in extraterrestrial environments, we have developed a lunar active seismic profiler (LASP), which integrates active seismic sources and receivers in a short (~1 m) array that can be deployed on a rover. The small active source generates a chirp waveform that sweeps over a wide frequency range (20–250 Hz). The receivers are coupled to the ground by their own weight, but nevertheless deliver acceptable performance. We improve the signal-to-noise ratio of the seismic signal by stacking waveforms, enabling a minimal source to support the exploration of geological substrates. By processing shot gather of the LASP data, we calculated a dispersion curve of surface waves and obtained an accurate S-wave velocity profile from surface to 1 m depth. The results suggest that regions with even small amounts of subsurface ice (~0.5 wt%) can be identified in LASP results by their anomalously high S-wave velocity. Field experiments demonstrate that the LASP can support estimates of 3D S-wave velocity distributions and the identification of a buried geological boundary. The LASP also detected different degrees of compaction at the experimental site. If two rovers carry these profilers, or if a single rover combines one with a separately deployed receiver or active seismic source, seismic refraction and seismic reflection analyses can be conducted simultaneously in addition to surface wave analyses. These seismic surveys for longer-offset data achieve the exploration of deeper substrates in extraterrestrial settings such as the Moon, Mars, and other solid bodies.

## 1. Introduction
Seismic studies yield information about planetary interiors. While Apollo and InSight used passive data, active-source seismic surveys obtain subsurface information in a short time. Future autonomous surveys by rovers could explore extensive areas. Subsurface targets (<1 m) are important because they may contain ice deposits for resources. Furthermore, the distribution of regolith and depth to bedrock are critical for constructing base camps. Measurements of regolith properties (compaction) are crucial for developing human-occupied rovers and installations.

## 2. Lunar Active Seismic Profiler (LASP)
The LASP is a rover-transportable system with active sources and multiple receivers on a minimal array <1 m long. It is designed to obtain S-wave velocity profiles between the surface and 1 m depth.

### 2.1. Seismic Sources
Uses a Portable Active Seismic Source (PASS) with a motor generating a chirp waveform (20-250 Hz). Continuous waveforms improve signal-to-noise through stacking.

### 2.2. Receivers
Typically uses an array of eight geophones (~100 g each) coupled to the ground by their own weight. Geophones are suspended from the frame on thin strings to decouple from array vibrations.

## 3. Surface Wave Analysis
Uses Multi-channel Analysis of Surface Waves (MASW) and Continuous Wavelet Transform (CWT-MASW) to estimate phase velocity dispersion curves, which are then inverted to obtain S-wave velocity profiles.

## 4. Results and Interpretation
- **Depth Profile:** S-wave velocity was estimated with accuracy of ±5 m/s.
- **Geophone Placement:** Surface-coupled geophones produced stable dispersion curves similar to buried geophones.
- **3D Structure:** 3D S-wave models detected a buried concavity and gradients in compaction (S-wave velocities 65 m/s to 100 m/s).

## 5. Discussion and Summary
LASP can image the shallow subsurface with high accuracy. It is sensitive enough to identify regions with 0.5 wt% ice saturation (detected by a ~40 m/s velocity anomaly). This information is useful for constructing base camps and telescope deployments.
