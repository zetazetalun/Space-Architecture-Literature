# Lunar active seismic profiler for investigating shallow substrates of the Moon and other extraterrestrial environments

**Authors:** Takeshi Tsuji, Taizo Kobayashi, Junji Kinoshita, Tatsunori Ikeda, Tomoki Uchigaki, Yuichiro Nagata, Taichi Kawamura, Kazunori Ogawa, Satoshi Tanaka, Akito Araya  
**Journal:** Icarus 404 (2023) 115666  
**Publisher:** Elsevier Inc.

## Abstract
To investigate the structures and properties of shallow material in extraterrestrial environments, we have developed a lunar active seismic profiler (LASP), which integrates active seismic sources and receivers in a short (~1 m) array that can be deployed on a rover. The small active source generates a chirp waveform that sweeps over a wide frequency range (20–250 Hz). The receivers are coupled to the ground by their own weight, but nevertheless deliver acceptable performance. We improve the signal-to-noise ratio of the seismic signal by stacking waveforms, enabling a minimal source to support the exploration of geological substrates. By processing shot gather of the LASP data, we calculated a dispersion curve of surface waves and obtained an accurate S-wave velocity profile from surface to 1 m depth. The results suggest that regions with even small amounts of subsurface ice (~0.5 wt%) can be identified in LASP results by their anomalously high S-wave velocity. Field experiments demonstrate that the LASP can support estimates of 3D S-wave velocity distributions and the identification of a buried geological boundary.

## 1. Introduction
Seismic studies have yielded information about the interiors of planetary bodies that cannot be obtained in any other way. Active-source seismic surveys can obtain subsurface information in a short time and a well-controlled manner. To support seismic investigations of the lunar subsurface with portable equipment, the authors developed a Lunar Active Seismic Profiler (LASP). The shallow substrate is an important target because it may contain ice deposits that could be used as a resource. Furthermore, the distribution of regolith and the depth to bedrock are critical information for constructing base camps.

## 2. Lunar Active Seismic Profiler (LASP)
The LASP is a rover-transportable system with active sources and multiple receivers on a minimal array <1 m long. It is designed to obtain depth profiles of S-wave velocity between the ground surface and 1 m depth.

### 2.1. Seismic sources
The source system uses a small motor (<2 cm in diameter) rotating an eccentric mass upon an axis to generate a chirp waveform that ascends or descends in frequency between 20 and 250 Hz.

### 2.2. Receivers
The design typically uses an array of eight geophones. They are coupled to the ground by their own weight (~100 g for each) and suspended from the frame on thin strings to decouple the geophones from array frame vibrations.

## 3. Surface wave analysis
The study improved the stability of phase velocity estimation by introducing the continuous wavelet transform (CWT) approach into multi-channel analysis of surface waves (MASW) (CWT-MASW).

## 4. Results and interpretation
### 4.1. Depth profile of S-wave velocity
The LASP was deployed on a sand beach site. A S-wave velocity profile was obtained via inversion. Accuracy of the estimated S-wave velocity was calculated using the bootstrap method, showing accurate estimations (±5 m/s).

### 4.2. Equipment and analyses to improve dispersion curves
Laboratory experiments confirmed that geophones coupled to the surface by their own weight produce acceptable dispersion curves comparable to buried geophones. CWT-MASW yielded cleaner and more stable dispersion curves than conventional MASW.

### 4.3. Three-dimensional S-wave velocity structure
Several intersecting seismic profiles were acquired in a prepared experimental plot (6.5 m x 9 m) containing a 1 m deep conical excavation. The S-wave velocity ranged from 60 m/s near the surface to 260 m/s at depth, successfully identifying differing degrees of roller compaction and identifying the buried concavity.

## 5. Discussion and summary
The results demonstrate that LASP enables high-accuracy shallow subsurface imaging, sensitive enough to investigate the distribution of ice (detecting as low as 0.5 wt% ice saturation). The ability to detect 3D subsurface structures and map the degree of consolidation at shallow levels provides information critical for the construction of base camps, deployment of telescopes, and rover design for future missions.

## Appendix A. Relationship between S-wave velocity and ice saturation
Laboratory experiments measured S-wave propagation in a simulated lunar regolith cooled from room temperature to -18 °C. Results showed that S-wave velocity increased as a nearly linear function of ice saturation, with an S-wave velocity anomaly of ~40 m/s detecting 0.5 wt% ice saturation.
