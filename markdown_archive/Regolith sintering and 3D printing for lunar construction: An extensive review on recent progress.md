# Regolith sintering and 3D printing for lunar construction: An extensive review on recent progress\n\n## Abstract\nIn-Situ Resource Utilisation (ISRU) is increasingly being seen as a viable and essential approach to constructing infrastructure for human habitation on the moon. This review presents a comprehensive study on the development of several sintering techniques of lunar regolith simulant to demonstrate its feasibility for ISRU on the moon. Various critical processing parameters are evaluated in pursuit of creating a structural material that can withstand the extreme lunar environment.\n\n## 1 Background of lunar construction\nA lunar base must shield humans from extreme temperature fluctuations (-248 °C to 77 °C), solar/cosmic radiation, micrometeoroid impacts, and lunar dust. Transporting materials from Earth is prohibitively expensive (e.g., ~$3k/kg via SpaceX Falcon 9). ISRU utilizing regolith is necessary for sustainable development.\n\n## 2 Regolith simulants and soils\nLunar regolith contains >40% silicon, with portions of Ti, Al, and Fe. Sintering behavior is influenced by mineral phases like agglutinates, volcanic glasses, and rocky minerals (mafics and feldspaths).\n\n## 3 Bulk sintering\n### 3.1 Radiant heat sintering\n- Sintering temperature impact: Ideal sintering occurs ~25 °C below the melting point (~1100 °C for mare simulants).\n- Mechanical properties: Compressive strengths of 30–200 MPa were achieved. Finer particles and high pre-pressing forces (100–200 MPa) improve density.\n### 3.2 Microwave sintering\n- Uses 2.45 GHz radiation. Heating rate increases significantly around 400 °C. Challenges include 'runaway' heating and non-uniformity.\n- Mechanical properties: Strengths generally 10–50 MPa, with one trial reaching 70 MPa with ilmenite additives.\n### 3.3 Spark plasma sintering (SPS)\n- Combines pulsed current, external pressure, and rapid heating. Produces higher densities (up to 2.9 g/cm³) than radiant sintering at lower temperatures.\n\n## 4 Focused sintering\n### 4.1 Laser sintering\n- Rapid heating with high-power lasers. Lunar regolith is an excellent absorber in the 1000 nm range. Can achieve low porosity but risks high residual stress.\n### 4.2 Solar sintering\n- Uses concentrated sunlight. The RegoLight project demonstrated 3D printing with solar power. Compressive strengths reached ~2.31 MPa. Energy efficient but dependent on solar cycle.\n\n## 5 Additive manufacturing applications\n- **Direct Ink Writing (DIW):** Uses regolith mixed with liquid binders (e.g., PLGA) to create micro-trusses. Strengths range from 1 to 19 MPa.\n\n## 6 Conclusion\nRegolith is the primary material for lunar habitats. Sintering methods vary in complexity, energy consumption, and resulting material properties. SPS and laser sintering offer high precision/strength, while solar sintering offers superior ISRU sustainability.
