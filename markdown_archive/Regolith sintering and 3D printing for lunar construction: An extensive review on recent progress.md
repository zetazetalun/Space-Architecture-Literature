# Regolith sintering and 3D printing for lunar construction: An extensive review on recent progress

**Authors:** Muhammad Shazwan Suhaizan, Phuong Tran, Ash Exner, Brian G. Falzon  
**Journal:** Progress in Additive Manufacturing (2024) 9:1715–1736  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1007/s40964-023-00537-1

## Abstract
In-Situ Resource Utilisation (ISRU) is increasingly being seen as a viable and essential approach to constructing infrastructure for human habitation on the moon. This review presents a comprehensive study on the development of several sintering techniques of lunar regolith simulant to demonstrate its feasibility for ISRU on the moon. Various critical processing parameters are evaluated in pursuit of creating a structural material that can withstand the extreme lunar environment.

## 1. Background of Lunar Construction
A lunar base must shield humans from extreme temperature fluctuations (-248 °C to 77 °C), solar/cosmic radiation, micrometeoroid impacts, and lunar dust. Transporting materials from Earth is prohibitively expensive ($3k/kg with Falcon 9). ISRU using local lunar soil (regolith) is the only sustainable solution.

## 2. Regolith Characteristics
- **Bulk density:** 1.5–1.66 g/cm³.
- **Thermal conductivity:** 0.001 W/mK in vacuum.
- **Composition:** High silicon (>40%), variable FeO (15-20% in mare, ~5% in highland), and Al2O3 (marker for origin).
- **Melting Point:** 1100–1380 °C.

## 3. Sintering Techniques

### 3.1 Bulk Sintering
- **Radiant Heat Sintering:** Straightforward; uses heating elements. Compressive strengths of 30–200 MPa achieved. Vacuum environments can reduce sintering temperatures by 25–100 °C.
- **Microwave Sintering:** Uses dielectric absorption (typically 2.45 GHz). Strengths range from 10–50 MPa. Susceptors help prevent 'runaway' heating.
- **Spark Plasma Sintering (SPS):** Combines pulsed current and pressure. Produces high density (up to 2.9 g/cm³) and hardness (up to 8.51 GPa) at lower temperatures.

### 3.2 Focused Sintering
- **Laser Sintering:** High precision for small components. Absorbs laser energy well in the 1000 nm range. Can achieve ~30 MPa strength but often suffers from high porosity (35-65%).
- **Solar Sintering:** Uses concentrated sunlight. The **RegoLight** project demonstrated the fabrication of interlocking bricks. Strengths are lower (approx. 2.31 MPa), suited for bulk shielding.

### 3.3 Direct Ink Writing (DIW)
Uses a mixture of regolith and a binder (e.g., PLGA) to create complex structures. Micro-trusses achieved strengths of 1–19 MPa. Requires thermal post-processing to remove binders.

| Technique | Advantages | Disadvantages |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Radiant Heat** | Simple; processes large volumes | Low ramp rates; long sintering times |
| **Microwave** | Rapid heating; deep penetration | Thermal cracking; difficult control |
| **Spark Plasma** | High density; enhanced properties | Requires pressure; expensive equipment |
| **Laser** | Complex geometries; less waste | High energy requirement; shrinkage |
| **Solar** | Uses renewable energy; low power | Sunlight dependent; high maintenance |
| **DIW** | Complex structures; low cost | Requires binders; post-processing needed |

## 4. Conclusion and Future Work
- Verification of high-temperature behavior in true lunar soil (vs simulants) is needed.
- Development of low-complexity, non-destructive testing for lunar-manufactured materials.
- Optimization of focused sintering in vacuum and microgravity conditions.
