# Concept for the Future Utilization of Lunar Underground Space and Adaptive Design Strategies

**Authors:** Xing Xu, Minghui Xue  
**Journal:** Buildings 2025, 15, 4057  
**Published:** 11 November 2025  
**DOI:** [https://doi.org/10.3390/buildings15224057](https://doi.org/10.3390/buildings15224057)

## Abstract
This paper reviews the historical development and current research on lunar underground space utilization, proposes five guiding principles for its progressive exploration and construction, and presents a phased “1.0–4.0 era” framework for systematic development. Additionally, based on an adaptive design theoretical framework, spatial, environmental, and climatic strategies are proposed to guide future lunar habitation and ensure sustainable extraterrestrial development, providing a comprehensive reference for long-term planning and construction of lunar underground habitats.

## 1. Introduction
The Moon presents an extremely hostile surface environment characterized by microgravity, near-vacuum conditions, extreme diurnal temperature variations, and high radiation. Consequently, contemporary research has gradually shifted focus from lunar surface facilities toward the development and utilization of lunar underground spaces, such as lava tubes, which provide enhanced environmental stability.

## 3. Conceptual Vision and Phases
The study proposes a phased development logic for lunar underground utilization:

*   **Stage 1.0: Point-Based Underground Facilities:** Initial small-scale facilities focused on communication hubs and short-term habitation modules using Earth-based prefabrication.
*   **Stage 2.0: Linear Underground Facilities:** Utilizing lava tubes to create interconnected networks of transportation corridors and strip-shaped residential areas.
*   **Stage 3.0: Areal Underground Facilities:** Excavation within the lunar thermal stable layer (approx. 1m depth) to support large-scale multifunctional base complexes.
*   **Stage 4.0: Three-Dimensional Underground Ecological City:** Integrated subterranean urban networks featuring simulated sunlight, intelligent air regeneration, and closed-loop ecological systems (BLSS).

### Material Utilization (ISRU)
*   **Lunar Basalt:** Preferred construction material with compressive/tensile strengths 10x greater than conventional concrete.
*   **Lunar Regolith:** Can be sintered via microwave heating into ceramic materials; used for 3D printing with cementitious composites.
*   **Metals:** Utilization of titanium, iron, chromium, and nickel found in lunar soil for structural components.

## 5. Conclusions
Underground spaces hold comprehensive potential for protection, safety, and climate regulation. Future research should focus on developing environmental simulation platforms and digital adaptive models to optimize lunar underground habitat construction, ensuring an intrinsic continuity between terrestrial architectural experience and extraterrestrial development.
