# Concept and preliminary structural analysis of a crater-covering dome for future lunar habitats

**Authors:** Magdalena Mrozek, Dawid Mrozek, Mateusz Smolana, Lorna Anguilano
**Published in:** Scientific Reports (2025) 15:24744
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-07901-x

## Abstract
The prospect of establishing a human presence on the Moon has transitioned from science fiction to an achievable goal. The Artemis program aims to establish a habitat enabling extended crew stays. This paper presents a conceptual lunar habitat created by covering a 17 m diameter crater in the Mare Tranquillitatis with a structure made from lunar regolith-based geopolymer. Five shapes were analyzed: two concave domes, a flat circular slab, and two convex domes. Structural analysis was performed using Finite Element Method (FEM), employing material data from literature and original strength tests of alkali-activated material based on lunar regolith simulants. The analysis revealed the advantages of concave-shaped structures, where internal pressure induced compressive stress, taking advantage of the material’s superior compressive strength over tensile strength.

## Categorization of Lunar Habitats
- **Class I:** Pre-integrated structures manufactured on Earth.
- **Class II:** Pre-fabricated structures assembled on-site (e.g., inflatables).
- **Class III:** ISRU-based structures primarily composed of lunar materials. This paper focuses on Class III.

## Building Materials and Technology
- **Material:** Lunar regolith-based geopolymer (GP). The samples consisted of: 
  - 10 molar sodium hydroxide (NaOH) solution.
  - Sodium silicate water glass (activator).
  - LHS-1 lunar highland regolith simulant (filler).
- **Curing Conditions:** Simulated lunar conditions in a thermal vacuum chamber at 60°C and 50 hPa pressure.
- **Proposed Technology:** 3D printing (Extrusion-based or Powder Bed Fusion) is discussed as a primary candidate for erecting Class III structures.

## Structural Analysis (FEM)
Five configurations for a 17 m crater cover (thickness 0.5 m) were tested:
- **Model A:** Concave dome, radius 48 m, rise -1.0 m.
- **Model B:** Concave dome, radius 94 m, rise -0.5 m.
- **Model C:** Flat circular slab.
- **Model D:** Convex dome, radius 94 m, rise +0.5 m.
- **Model E:** Convex dome, radius 48 m, rise +1.0 m.

### Material Properties Tested
| Property | Value |
| :--- | :--- |
| Average Volumetric Density | 1750 kg/m³ |
| Tensile Strength ($f_t$) | 5.5 MPa |
| Compressive Strength ($f_c$) | 9.0 MPa |
| Young’s Modulus ($E$) | 8.13 GPa |

### Vertical Displacements (cm) at 1000 hPa
| Model | HR (Pinned) | FR (Fixed) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Model A (Concave) | 2.25 | 2.53 |
| Model C (Flat) | 191.98 | 20.65 |
| Model E (Convex) | 2.25 | 2.53 |

## Conclusions
The numerical analysis demonstrates that concave domes are superior for lunar habitats designed to hold internal atmospheric pressure. In Model A (concave), internal pressure induces a compression stress state across both surfaces, effectively sealing the habitat and staying within the material's strength limits. Conversely, flat and convex designs are prone to high tensile stresses and cracking, which would compromise airtightness.
