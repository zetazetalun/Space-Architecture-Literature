## Abstract
A successful space exploration requires establishing permanent and earth-independent infrastructure that are not only resilient to the extreme environment of space but also preferably made of sustainable and indigenous “space-native” construction materials. This review covers feasibility of exploiting in-situ lunar and Martian resources as well as harvesting of elements and compounds, from near Earth objects (NEOs), to produce extraterrestrial materials suitable for construction of space-based infrastructure.

## 1. Introduction
Migration to the Moon or Mars is highly dependent upon development of resilient, economical, and sustainable infrastructure. Transportation costs range between $5000 to $20,000 per kg to the Moon. Utilizing in-situ resources (ISRU) could reduce the overall cost of a human settlement from $100 billion to an average of $40 billion.

## 2. Space environment and resources
Space environments differ from Earth due to radiation (solar/galactic), vacuum, and low gravity. The lack of atmosphere on the Moon/Mars increases the impact effect of space debris (meteorites).

### 2.2.1 In-situ resource utilization (ISRU)
Regolith (fine particles covering the surface) can be processed into construction materials. Lunar and Martian regolith contain silicon dioxide ($SiO_2$), alumina ($Al_2O_3$), sulfur (S), and iron (FeO).

## 3. Properties of space-native construction materials

### 3.1 Concrete-like hybrids
*   **Sulfur Concrete:** Water-free, created by heating sulfur (120–150 °C) and mixing with regolith. Compressive strengths reach 39–75.7 MPa.
*   **Polymer Concrete:** Uses polymeric resin binders. Strengths vary from 17 to 60 MPa.
*   **Geopolymers:** Formed by the interaction between aluminosilicate and an alkaline solution. Compressive strengths of 16.6–33.1 MPa.

Lunar highlands contain ~15% aluminum oxide. Magnesium is found in olivine. These can be used to produce aluminum, magnesium, iron, and titanium structural components.

## 4. Design considerations
Structures on the Moon and Mars can carry 3 to 6 times the mass on Earth due to low gravity, allowing for larger spans and thinner members. Foundations must be placed below the depth of thermal cycling to avoid settlement issues. Shielding (1–6 m of regolith) is proposed to protect against radiation and micrometeorites.

## 6. Summary and conclusions
*   ISRU eludes to the possibility of producing cementitious materials.
*   Water-free construction (sulfur, polymer) is essential.
*   Sustainable infrastructure can be fabricated with minimal Earth-based materials.
