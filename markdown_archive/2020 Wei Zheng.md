# Mechanical behavior of the metal parts welded with extraterrestrial regolith simulant by the solar concentrator in ISRU & ISRF application

## 1. Introduction
The study focuses on in-situ resource utilization (ISRU) and in-situ fabrication and repair (ISFR) for sustainable Moon and Mars exploration. It evaluates using direct solar energy as a heat source to weld metal parts together using regolith as the soldering medium, aiming to reduce the need for binders transported from Earth.

## 2. Experimental Methodology
- **Materials:** Q235B ferroalloy and TA2 titanium alloy were used for the metal parts. Extraterrestrial regolith simulants HIT-L-1 (Lunar) and HIT-M-1 (Martian) were used as solder. 
- **Solar Apparatus:** A Fresnel lens with a 450 mm diameter was used to concentrate solar radiation, achieving a focal heat flux density of approximately 56.5 MW/m2.
- **Welding Process:** A 'sandwich' method was employed where a 50 mg layer of regolith was placed between two metal ends. The focal spot was moved along the interface to induce melting and fusion.

## 3. Results and Discussion
- **Mechanical Performance:** 20 welded specimens were tested for tensile strength. The failure mode for all specimens was brittle failure. 
- **Tensile Strength Data:** 
  - Ferroalloy + Lunar Simulant (Fe-L): 2.94 MPa
  - Ferroalloy + Martian Simulant (Fe-M): 1.66 MPa
  - Titanium Alloy + Lunar Simulant (Ti-L): 4.95 MPa
  - Titanium Alloy + Martian Simulant (Ti-M): 2.59 MPa
- **Microstructure:** SEM and XRD analysis revealed that the welding strength is derived from wustite and silicate melts fusing into the metal substrates. Ferroalloy joints showed homogeneous fusion, while titanium alloy joints were more inhomogeneous.

## 4. Conclusions
The research demonstrates the feasibility of welding metal with concentrated solar energy and regolith simulants. While the current tensile strength is low (less than 2% of the base metal), the method offers a potential solution for astronaut-assisted repairs and fabrication in extraterrestrial environments.
