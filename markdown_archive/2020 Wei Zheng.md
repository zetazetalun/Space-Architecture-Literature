# Mechanical behavior of the metal parts welded with extraterrestrial regolith simulant by the solar concentrator in ISRU & ISRF application

**Authors:** Wei Zheng, Guofu Qiao
**Published in:** Advances in Space Research 65 (2020) 2303–2314
**Received:** 29 October 2019; **Accepted:** 9 February 2020

## Abstract
The use of in-situ resources plays an important role on future extraterrestrial human activities for the facility repair and habitat construction, especially in sustainable space exploration of Moon and Mars. A method of the metal welded with extraterrestrial regolith simulant using solar processing under ambient conditions is presented. Metal parts are made of Q235B ferroalloy and TA2 titanium alloy into standard tensile members according to the ASTM code. They are disconnected from the middle in advance, and then welded together with lunar and Martian regolith simulant under ambient conditions, respectively. The entire welding process and precautions are detailed. The results show that it is possible to weld metal parts together with extraterrestrial regolith simulant by the solar concentrator. 

## 1. Introduction
The long-term explorations of the Moon and Mars require mission sustainment and a steady supply of resources. In-situ resource utilization (ISRU) and in-situ fabrication and repair (ISFR) technologies can significantly reduce the required launch mass and cost. This research focuses on using solar energy as a heat resource to fabricate building elements and repair components using abundant basaltic-formed regolith.

### 2.2 Welding Process
A Fresnel lens (450 mm diameter) was used to concentrate solar energy. The average heat flux density at the focal spot was approximately 56.5 MW/m².
1. A layer of regolith (~50 mg) was placed between two metal halves.
2. The Fresnel lens concentrated light to form a weld pool.
3. The focal spot moved along the interface to fuse the parts.
4. Weldments were buried in a powder bed to ensure slow cooling and minimize brittle failure.

**Failure Mode:** All specimens exhibited brittle failure. Titanium alloys showed higher joint strength than ferroalloys when using the same regolith simulant. 

## 4. Conclusions
The study demonstrates the feasibility of welding metal parts with extraterrestrial regolith simulant using a solar concentrator. While current joint strengths are low (less than 2% of the base metal), the method provides a binderless path for ISRU-based fabrication and repair. The strength derives from oxygenated compounds fusing into the metal base at high temperatures.
