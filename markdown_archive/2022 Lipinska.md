# Biological growth as an alternative approach to on and off-Earth construction

**Authors:** Monika Brandić Lipińska, Chris Maurer, Dave Cadogan, James Head, Martyn Dade-Robertson, Ivan Glaucio Paulino-Lima, Chen Liu, Ruth Morrow, Debbie G. Senesky, Magdalini Theodoridou, Maikel C. Rheinstädter, Meng Zhang and Lynn J. Rothschild

**Published:** 19 September 2022  
**Journal:** Frontiers in Built Environment  
**DOI:** 10.3389/fbuil.2022.965145

## Abstract
A critical aspect of human space exploration is the ability to construct habitats while minimizing payload mass. This research proposes the use of fungal biocomposites for growing extra-terrestrial structures directly at the destination. These mycelium-based materials are fire-resistant, insulating, and can be used independently or in conjunction with regolith. The study outlines the potential for material fabrication, assembly, maintenance, and repair in extreme environments.

## 1. Introduction
Transporting materials to Low Earth Orbit costs between $2,720 and $10,000 per kg. Space architecture must prioritize locally available building materials (ISRU) and energy-efficient technologies. This paper introduces "growing architecture" using Engineered Living Materials (ELMs) as an alternative to rigid modules or inflatable structures sent from Earth.

## 3. Mycelium as a Medium
Fungal mycelium consists of branching, thread-like hyphae. Biocomposites are created by sending fungal spores, nutrients, and a growth framework to the destination. 

### 3.1 Material Properties
Measured mechanical properties of mycelium biocomposites:
- **Ganoderma Lucidum:** Elastic modulus ~275 MPa, Strength ~26 MPa.
- **Phellinus Linteus:** Elastic modulus ~21 MPa, Strength ~1.57 MPa.

### 3.2 Comparison with ISRU Materials
- **Mycoterial:** Compression strength 26 MPa, Modulus 275 MPa, U-value 0.33.
- **Lunar Regolith:** Compression strength 17.24 MPa.
- **Ice:** Compression strength 3 MPa, U-value 2.22.
- **Sintered Basalt:** Compression strength 206 MPa, U-value 20.

## 4. Off-Earth Myco-architecture Approaches

### 4.1 Standalone Approach (Moon)
Uses inflatable structures as a foundational form and nutrient delivery system. Once inflated, mycelium grows within the cavities of the multi-hulled structure. The grown "mycoshell" provides insulation and structural integrity.

### 4.2 Reinforcement Approach (Mars)
Mycelium acts as a binder for Martian regolith. This reduces the biomass needed from Earth and utilizes loose rock/dust to provide radiation protection and atmospheric pressure containment.

## 5. Conclusion
Mycelium biocomposites offer a path to regenerative and adaptive habitats with low upmass requirements. While in the early stages, the integration of synthetic biology with architecture allows for self-healing structures and in-situ resource utilization.
