# Properties and Characteristics of Regolith-Based Materials for Extraterrestrial Construction

**Authors:** Cheng Zhou, Yuyue Gao, Yan Zhou, Wei She, Yusheng Shi, Lieyun Ding, Changwen Miao
**Journal:** Engineering 37 (2024) 159–181
**DOI:** 10.1016/j.eng.2023.11.019

## Abstract
The construction of extraterrestrial bases has become a new goal in deep space exploration. In situ resource-based construction is promising for its sustainability and economic benefits. This review surveys 13 types of typical construction materials, comparing their mechanical, thermal, optical, and radiation-shielding properties. Future directions include high-throughput characterization and extraterrestrial experiments.

## 1. Introduction
Establishment of infrastructure on extraterrestrial bodies is a future goal (e.g., Artemis, ILRS). In situ construction methods using regolith are preferred due to transportation costs. Materials can be fabricated via concrete curing or sintering/melting.

## 2. Construction Materials and Processes
### 2.2 Extraterrestrial construction materials
Materials include:
- **Concrete:** Sulfur, biopolymer, geopolymer, silicate, magnesium, and poly-based concrete.
- **Sintered/Molten:** Spark plasma sintering (SPS), microwave sintering, laser sintering/melting, solar sintering, and hot pressed sintering.

## 3. Mechanical Properties
- **Compressive Strength:** Sintered samples (2.31 to 428.10 MPa) generally outperform concrete samples (0.90 to 76.80 MPa).
- **Flexural Strength:** Sintered samples range from 0.23 to 129.50 MPa; concretes range from 2.30 to 47.80 MPa.
- **Hardness:** Sintered samples exhibit significantly higher microhardness compared to concrete-like materials.
- **Fatigue:** Critical for permanent structures subject to thermal cycles and external loads.

## 4. Thermal and Optical Properties
- **Thermal Conductivity:** Lower conductivity is preferred for insulation. Sintered samples vary significantly based on porosity (higher porosity leads to lower conductivity).
- **CTE:** Reasonable coefficient of thermal expansion (CTE) is necessary to withstand temperature fluctuations (-196 to 150 °C).

## 5. Radiation-Shielding Properties
- **Proton Shielding:** Geopolymer concrete (50–100 cm) can provide sufficient protection.
- **Neutron Shielding:** Sintered regolith (80–150 cm) provides protection comparable to Aluminum.
