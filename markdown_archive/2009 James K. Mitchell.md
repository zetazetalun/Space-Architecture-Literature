## Geotechnical Engineering Considerations for Extraterrestrial Infrastructure Construction

James K. Mitchell<sup>1</sup>, Sc.D., P.E., Dist. M. ASCE and W. David Carrier III<sup>2</sup>, Sc.D., P.E., Fellow, Life M. ASCE

<sup>1</sup>University Distinguished Professor Emeritus, Virginia Tech, Blacksburg, VA 24061-0105; jkm@vt.edu
<sup>2</sup>Director, Lunar Geotechnical Institute, Lakeland, FL 33807-5056; wdcarrier@verizon.net

**ABSTRACT:** It is only a matter of time until space exploration enters a new phase that will include additional manned and unmanned landings and explorations on the Moon, Mars, and perhaps on asteroids. Detailed knowledge of the soils and rocks will be needed for the design of foundations for structures, for excavation and underground space utilization, for design of surface vehicles and construction of transportation systems, for evaluation of their suitability as construction materials and development of construction methods. In-situ resource utilization can be expected to play a major role in these activities owing to the high cost and difficulty in transporting needed materials from Earth. The extreme environments in which the work is to be carried out will complicate virtually every activity to be undertaken.

### THE NATURE AND GEOTECHNICAL PROPERTIES OF THE SOILS ON THE MOON

Lunar soil, or regolith, consists of rock fragments, mineral grains, glass, and agglutinates. It is characterized as broadly-graded silty fine sand (SM) or sandy silt (ML). 

**Lunar Soil Mechanical Properties:**
- **Relative Density:** 0-30% at surface, increasing to >90% below 30 cm.
- **Bulk Density:** 1.3 g/cm³ to 1.8 g/cm³.
- **Shear Strength (0-60 cm):** Friction Angle 48-51°, Cohesion 1.3-1.9 kPa.

### THE NATURE AND GEOTECHNICAL PROPERTIES OF THE SOILS ON MARS

Martian soil is more complex due to volcanic, hydro, and aeolian processes. It contains silicon, iron, aluminum, magnesium, and sulfur, with iron oxidation causing the red color. 

**Martian Soil Mechanical Properties:**
- **Bulk Density:** 1.0 to 1.8 g/cm³.
- **Internal Friction Angle:** 20-45° depending on the site.
- **Cohesion:** 0.1 to 1.0 kPa.

### GEOTECHNICAL ENGINEERING CONSIDERATIONS

1. **Mobility:** Generally feasible, but rocky/soft zones (especially on Mars) pose risks.
2. **Dust:** Electrostatic adhesion and health/equipment risks are significant.
3. **Foundations:** High density at shallow depths on the Moon supports high loads; Mars is more variable.
4. **Excavation:** Lunar holes remain open; vertical walls stable to ~2m.
5. **ISRU:** Essential for sustainability; lunar regolith can be used for oxygen extraction and building materials.
