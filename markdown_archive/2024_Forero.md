# Seismic Vulnerability Assessment of Non-Structural Elements Inside an Inflatable Lunar Habitat

**Authors:** Oscar D. Forero, Julio A. Ramirez, Ph.D., and Shirley J. Dyke, Ph.D.
**Publication:** Earth and Space 2024, ASCE

## Abstract
The existence of moonquakes has often been overlooked in lunar habitation design. This study develops a vulnerability assessment procedure for essential non-structural elements (NSE), specifically the environmental control and life support system (ECLSS), inside an inflatable habitat. The findings suggest that significant disturbances from moonquakes may result in long-lasting vibrations requiring effective mitigation measures beyond standard launch-load designs.

## Introduction
Interest in Lunar exploration via NASA's Artemis program highlights the need for surface habitat concepts, particularly inflatable habitats (Class II). While designed to survive launch vibrations, their vulnerability to shallow moonquakes remains under-researched. This paper compares performance under launch vibrations versus shallow moonquakes for a torus-shaped inflatable habitat.

## Case Study
- **Structure:** Torus-shaped inflatable structure mounted on a lander.
- **Height:** 6 meters, two floors.
- **Support:** Cantilever hydraulic jack system and aluminum pedestal.
- **NSE Analyzed:** ECLSS universal pallet (0.60m x 0.60m x 1.80m).

## Launch Dynamic Environment
Payloads face dynamic loads from engine ignition, liftoff, and aerodynamic pressures. Tests include:
- **Sine Sweep Test:** Lateral loads at 4 octaves per minute.
- **Random Vibration Test:** Based on NASA-STD-7001B workmanship criteria.

## Lunar Seismic Motion Environment
- **Data Source:** Apollo Passive Seismic Experiment (APSE).
- **Key Event:** 28 shallow moonquakes (High-Frequency Teleseismic events).
- **Observations:** Ground acceleration near sources can reach 1.6g to 4.0g. Beyond 50 km, acceleration flattens near zero.

## Numerical Simulation and Results
- **Simulation Method:** Newmark's method for dynamic equations of motion with 5% critical damping.
- **Primary Structure Response (1.0gL):** Deployed structure maximum displacement of 2.4cm. Duration of vibration exceeds one hour (compared to minutes for launch).
- **Undeployed Structure Response:** Maximum displacement of 0.6cm.
- **Scaling:** A scale factor of 3.0gL was identified as the ground acceleration required to approximate launch-environment deformation responses.

## Discussion
Lunar seismic environments operate at low frequencies (<10Hz), which can cause resonance in surface habitats. The disparity in response between launch loads and seismic loads indicates that designing solely for launch is insufficient for long-term lunar surface habitation integrity. Material integrity, fracture, and fatigue must be considered for the prolonged duration of seismic events.
