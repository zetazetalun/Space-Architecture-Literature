# Design of an Autonomously Deployable Mars Habitat

**Authors:** Rudolf Walther Erdem Neumerkel, Miruna Vecerdi, Sandra Häuplik-Meusburger  
**Publication:** 50th International Conference on Environmental Systems (ICES-2021-398)  
**Date:** 12-15 July 2021

## Abstract
This paper presents a proposal for the design of an autonomously deployable habitat for a long-duration manned mission on Mars. Key drivers in the design were the payload and volume constraints of Starship and addressing habitability challenges implied by Martian conditions. The habitat is conceived to be transported in compacted form and to expand to a habitable volume by autonomously deploying after being placed on the Martian surface. The integration of several different kinetic structures is proposed: a protective casing, a vertically sliding core, radially expanding girders, and an inflatable membrane.

## Design Method
The design focuses on an integrated deployment mechanism fulfilling transportation constraints. The system consists of:
1.  **Protective Casing:** A rigid shell divided into 3 identical parts that interlock during transport.
2.  **Telescopic Core:** The primary rigid structure containing water/waste management and technical units. It features a water-filled cupola for natural lighting and radiation protection.
3.  **Expanding Girders:** Radially oriented foldable girders that push the casing elements outward using torsional and compressive springs.
4.  **Inflatable Membrane:** A multi-layered system adapted from TransHab concepts.

### Membrane Configuration
- **Interior Layer:** Nomex finish, redundant air bladders (CepacHD200), and Kevlar felt layers.
- **Middle Part:** Restraint layer of woven Kevlar belts.
- **Outer Layer:** Dust protection and a fillable chamber layer for loose regolith (up to 100cm thick).

## Arrival and Deployment
The Starship vehicle can transport two stacked units. Deployment steps include:
1. Casing elements unlock.
2. Compressive springs release girders to push casing outwards.
3. Membrane inflates, building pressure to 1 bar.
4. Interior core frame slides vertically, doubling in height.
5. Robotic filling of the outer membrane chambers with ground-up regolith for radiation/MMOD protection.

## Conclusion
The design addresses transportation constraints and Martian environmental challenges (no atmosphere, radiation, MMOD). It proposes a modular, reconfigurable habitat that can be deployed quickly and protected using ISRU regolith filling rather than energy-intensive sintering.
