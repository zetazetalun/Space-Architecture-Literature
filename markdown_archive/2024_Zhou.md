# Properties and Characteristics of Regolith-Based Materials for Extraterrestrial Construction

**Authors:** Cheng Zhou, Yuyue Gao, Yan Zhou, Wei She, Yusheng Shi, Lieyun Ding, Changwen Miao
**Journal:** Engineering (2024)
**DOI:** 10.1016/j.eng.2023.11.019

## Abstract
The construction of extraterrestrial bases has become a new goal in the active exploration of deep space. Among the construction techniques, in situ resource-based construction is one of the most promising because of its good sustainability and acceptable economic cost, triggering the development of various types of extraterrestrial construction materials. This paper surveys 13 types of construction materials regarding their reliability and applicability in extreme environments, focusing on mechanical, thermal, optical, and radiation-shielding properties.

## 1. Introduction
Future exploration plans, including the Artemis plan (NASA) and the International Lunar Research Station (CNSA/Roscosmos), necessitate infrastructure on extraterrestrial bodies. ISRU (In situ resource utilization) methods are the most promising due to the high cost of transportation from Earth.

## 2. Extraterrestrial Construction Materials

### 2.1 Materials Classification
Extraterrestrial construction materials can be fabricated in two main ways:
1. **Concrete Curing:** Includes sulfur concrete, biopolymer concrete, geopolymer concrete, polymer concrete, silicate concrete, and magnesium concrete.
2. **Sintering/Melting:** Includes processes like hot-pressed sintering, microwave sintering, spark plasma sintering (SPS), laser sintering/melting, solar sintering, and digital light processing (DLP).

### 2.2 Typical Simulants
Various simulants such as JSC-1, MLS-1, FJS-1, CAS-1, DNA-1, OB-1, TJ-1, NU-LHT, BP-1, and KLS-1 for lunar soil, and JSC Mars-1, MMS, NEU Mars-1, and MGS-1 for Martian soil are summarized with their intended simulation aims.

## 3. Mechanical Properties

### 3.1 Strength Comparison
- **Concrete Samples:** Compressive strength ranges from 0.90 to 76.80 MPa. Geopolymer and magnesium concrete tend to show better performance in this category.
- **Sintered/Molten Samples:** Compressive strength ranges from 2.31 to 428.10 MPa. DLP and SPS techniques achieve significantly higher strengths.
- **Flexural Strength:** Concrete specimens range from 2.30 to 47.80 MPa; sintered specimens range from 0.23 to 129.50 MPa.

### 3.2 Hardness and Fatigue
- **Hardness:** Molten glassy lunar regolith exhibits microhardness of approx. 6.09 GPa and nano-hardness of 9.76 GPa. DLP samples vary significantly based on sintering conditions.
- **Fatigue:** Geopolymer concrete strength reduces by 45%–70% after temperature shock, while magnesium oxychloride composites show better stability under temperature cycles.

## 4. Thermal and Optical Properties
- **Thermal Expansion:** CTE (Coefficient of Thermal Expansion) of microwave-sintered lunar regolith is approx. 5 × 10⁻⁶ °C⁻¹, similar to lunar basaltic regolith breccias.
- **Thermal Conductivity:** Solar-sintered samples show the lowest thermal conductivity and density, while laser-molten samples show the highest.
- **Optical:** Solar energy absorption of regolith simulant powder is calculated at 0.77, increasing to 0.92 after sintering at 900 °C.

## 5. Radiation-Shielding Property
- **Proton Shielding:** 50–100 cm of geopolymer concrete provides sufficient shielding for long-term residency.
- **Neutron Shielding:** Sintered regolith (approx. 80 cm) provides GCR neutron shielding comparable to Al.
- **Heavy Ion Shielding:** Less than 50 cm of compacted regolith (density 1.4 g/cm³) is sufficient for stopping primary GCR ions.

## 6. Challenges and Optimization
- **Microstructure:** Porosity and cracks are identified as the most critical factors affecting both mechanical and thermal properties.
- **Optimizations:** Adjusting reactant ratios, adding fibers (e.g., nanofibers in geopolymer), and optimizing sintering parameters (heating rate, pressure) are effective strategies.

## 7. Conclusions
The review highlights that while laboratory results are promising, long-term durability in real extraterrestrial environments remains the greatest unknown. Future research should prioritize high-throughput characterization and extraterrestrial experiments.
