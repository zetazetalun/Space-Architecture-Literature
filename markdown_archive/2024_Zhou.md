# Properties and Characteristics of Regolith-Based Materials for Extraterrestrial Construction

**Authors:** Cheng Zhou, Yuyue Gao, Yan Zhou, Wei She, Yusheng Shi, Lieyun Ding, Changwen Miao
**Journal:** Engineering 37 (2024) 159–181
**Published:** 2024

## Abstract
The construction of extraterrestrial bases has become a new goal in the active exploration of deep space. Among the construction techniques, in situ resource-based construction is one of the most promising because of its good sustainability and acceptable economic cost, triggering the development of various types of extraterrestrial construction materials. A comprehensive survey and comparison of thirteen types of typical construction materials is discussed in terms of their reliability and applicability in extreme extraterrestrial environments.

## 1. Introduction
Establishment of infrastructure on an extraterrestrial body is a future goal (e.g., Artemis, International Lunar Research Station). In situ construction methods using extraterrestrial regolith as the main raw material are prioritized due to transportation costs. Materials can be fabricated via concrete curing or sintering/melting.

## 2. Extraterrestrial Construction Materials

### 2.1 Construction Feasibility and Materials
- **Regolith Simulants:** Developed to simulate actual samples (Apollo, Luna, Chang’E, etc.). Examples include JSC-1, MLS-1, FJS-1, CAS-1, OB-1, and MMS.
- **Forming Principles:**
  - **Concrete Curing:** Sulfur, Biopolymer, Geopolymer, Silicate, and Magnesium.
  - **Sintering/Melting:** Hot pressing sintering, Microwave sintering, Spark Plasma Sintering (SPS), Solar sintering, and Melting.
  - **Additive Manufacturing:** Laser sintering/melting and Digital Light Processing (DLP).

## 3. Mechanical Properties
- **Compressive Strength:**
  - Concrete specimens: 0.90 to 76.80 MPa.
  - Sintered/Molten specimens: 2.31 to 428.10 MPa.
- **Findings:** Magnesium concrete and DLP-processed materials provide superior mechanical properties. DLP-sintered regolith reached 428.10 MPa, while solar sintering showed lower performance (2.31-2.49 MPa).
- **Hardness:** Sintered samples exhibit microhardness and nano-hardness significantly higher than concrete-like materials.
- **Fatigue:** Critical for permanent structures under thermal cycles (96 K to 397 K). Geopolymer concrete strength reduced by 45%-70% after temperature shock.

## 4. Thermal and Optical Properties
- **Thermal Conductivity (K):** Porous samples have lower K, better for insulation. Solar-sintered samples showed the lowest K and density.
- **Thermal Expansion:** CTE values for microwave-sintered lunar regolith (~5 x 10^-6 °C^-1) are similar to actual lunar basaltic regolith breccias.
- **Optical:** Sintered samples demonstrate high solar absorption ability (~0.77 to 0.92), potentially serving as energy-storage receivers.

## 5. Radiation-Shielding Properties
- **Proton/Neutron Shielding:** Geopolymer concrete with depth 50–100 cm provides significant protection. Sintered regolith (80 cm layer) can significantly improve GCR and SPE neutron shielding.
- **Heavy Ion Shielding:** compacted powder (46 cm) or loose regolith (150 cm) is effective against GCR nuclei.

## 6. Challenges and Future Directions
- **Characterization:** Need for high-throughput and multiphysics characterization (coupling loads, radiation, thermal).
- **Durability:** Long-term performance evaluation (>1 year) is currently lacking in laboratory studies.
- **Testing:** Transition from ground simulations to real extraterrestrial experiments (e.g., Space Station tests).
