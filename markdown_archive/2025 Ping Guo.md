# A Highly Sensitive and Reliable Humidity Sensor for Low-Water-Pressure Detection in a Vacuum Environment via PEDOT:PSS Films

## Authors
Ping Guo, Xuyang An, Xinxin He, Yinhua Hu, Jiatai Chen, Guoyun Zhou, Jinbiao Fan, Weiwei Zhang, Binbin Zhang, Shengyuan Jiang, and Jia Zhang

## Abstract
Exploring extraterrestrial water resources has far-reaching significance for future human space resource utilization and base construction. Consequently, the development of effective sensing technologies for in situ water detection is a critical endeavor. This work presents a highly sensitive and reliable water molecules sensor based on poly(3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene)/poly(styrenesulfonate) (PEDOT:PSS) films. The device demonstrates exceptional performance under high-vacuum conditions (1.0 x 10^-3 Pa), achieving an extremely low detection limit of 0.02 Pa (148 μg/mL) and exhibiting outstanding reversibility, repeatability, and long-term stability. Furthermore, the sensor successfully underwent rigorous survivability tests designed to simulate the harsh conditions of space, showing no significant performance degradation. Meanwhile, through in situ film surface/thickness and spectroscopic analyses, a deeper understanding of the hydrogen-bond-based interactions between water molecules and PEDOT:PSS has been elucidated. This research provides valuable new insights and a promising platform for the development of advanced sensors aimed at detecting water on other planetary bodies.

## Key Findings
- **Detection Capability:** The PEDOT:PSS sensor can detect water molecules under high vacuum (1.0 x 10^-3 Pa) with a lower detection limit of 0.02 Pa (148 μg/mL).
- **Stability and Survivability:** The device shows excellent reversibility and long-term stability over 28 days. It passed rigorous space-simulated survivability tests including impact testing, thermal cycling (-80 to 250 °C), and humidity resistance without degradation.
- **Sensing Mechanism:** Water absorption causes the PSS shells to swell, increasing the distance between conductive PEDOT cores and thus increasing electrical resistance.

## Technical Specifications
- **Material:** PEDOT:PSS films deposited via electrohydrodynamic jet printing.
- **Sensitivity:** 0.290%/Pa for pressures < 20 Pa; 0.146%/Pa for 20-950 Pa.
- **Response Time:** 58 seconds to saturation at 20 Pa.
- **Recovery:** Baseline resistance recovery ≥99.7% for low-pressure ranges.

## Implications for Space Architecture
This sensor technology is critical for identifying and mapping lunar water ice distributions. Such data is foundational for ISRU (In-Situ Resource Utilization) strategies, enabling the selection of optimal sites for lunar habitats (Class III) and ensuring the availability of resources for life support and fuel.
