# A Highly Sensitive and Reliable Humidity Sensor for Low-Water-Pressure Detection in a Vacuum Environment via PEDOT:PSS Films

**Authors:** Ping Guo, Xuyang An, Xinxin He, Yinhua Hu, Jiatai Chen, Guoyun Zhou, Jinbiao Fan, Weiwei Zhang, Binbin Zhang*, Shengyuan Jiang*, and Jia Zhang*

**Journal:** Nano Letters, 2025, 25, 14850−14858
**Publisher:** American Chemical Society (ACS)

## Abstract
Exploring extraterrestrial water resources has far-reaching significance for future human space resource utilization and base construction. Consequently, the development of effective sensing technologies for in situ water detection is a critical endeavor. This work presents a highly sensitive and reliable water molecules sensor based on poly(3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene)/poly(styrenesulfonate) (PEDOT:PSS) films. The device demonstrates exceptional performance under high-vacuum conditions (1.0 × 10−3 Pa), achieving an extremely low detection limit of 0.02 Pa (148 μg/mL) and exhibiting outstanding reversibility, repeatability, and long-term stability. Furthermore, the sensor successfully underwent rigorous survivability tests designed to simulate the harsh conditions of space, showing no significant performance degradation.

## Introduction
Water is a basic need for life and life-sustaining activities. In aerospace, exploration of lunar water resources yields insights into the geological environment and has significance for future lunar resource utilization and base construction. Currently, detection relies on remote sensing and high-energy physics, but these lack spatial resolution and high signal-to-noise ratios. Therefore, miniaturized sensors adapted for lunar rovers or fixed payloads are required to monitor water released from regolith in real-time.

## Experimental and Methods
- **Sensitive Material:** PEDOT:PSS, a conductive polymer.
- **Fabrication:** Electrohydrodynamic jet printing technique using a 60 μm dispensing needle.
- **Simulated Environment:** A detection chamber comprising a vacuum source (1.0 × 10−3 Pa), water supply pipelines controlled via ALD valves, and temperature control (−80 to −250 °C).
- **Characterization:** Transmission electron microscopy (TEM), Fourier transform infrared (FTIR) spectroscopy, and Raman spectroscopy.

## Performance and Results
- **Vacuum Limit:** Tested under 1.0 × 10−3 Pa.
- **Detection Range:** 0.2–950 Pa.
- **Detection Limit:** 0.02 Pa (148 μg/mL), which is approximately one-fifth of the lowest previously reported levels.
- **Sensitivity:** 0.290%/Pa for pressures < 20 Pa; 0.146%/Pa for 20–950 Pa.
- **Response Time:** 58 seconds to reach saturation at 20 Pa.
- **Recovery:** Baseline resistance recovery of ≥99.7%.
- **Survivability:** Tested against impact shocks, thermal cycling, and high humidity resistance with no performance degradation.

## Sensing Mechanism
The sensing mechanism is attributed to the hydrophilicity of PSS. Water absorption causes the PSS shell to swell, increasing the distance between conductive PEDOT cores and thus increasing electrical resistance. This process is reversible upon water detachment.

## Conclusion
The developed sensor provides a promising platform for detecting water on other planetary bodies, supporting future space exploration and the utilization of extraterrestrial water resources for base construction.
