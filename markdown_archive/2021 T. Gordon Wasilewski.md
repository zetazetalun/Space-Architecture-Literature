# Experimental Investigations of Water Extraction Process within Permanently Shadowed Regions of the Moon

**Author:** T. Gordon Wasilewski

## Abstract
Heat and mass transfers occurring on the surface and within the subsurface of extraterrestrial bodies during natural and future industrial heating processes require knowledge of thermal properties of porous dry and icy materials. A series of experiments have been employed to simulate borehole heating of icy regolith samples under low vacuum (0.1 to 1.5 Torr) and very low temperatures (>93 K), using JSC-1A analog prepared as 'mud-pies' with water contents from 0 to 20% wt. Real-time measurements of temperatures and vacuum chamber pressures were recorded. To decipher thermal properties data, 3D finite element method (FEM) modelling has been used to simulate the behavior of both the samples and the vacuum chamber. The results show that thermal conductivities vary with temperature, pressure, ice content, and porosity. These findings improve our understanding of processes applied in extraterrestrial water mining and visualize the engineering challenges involved.

## Introduction
The current paradigm in rocky planet research is to 'follow the water' for scientific and commercial reasons. Water is considered the most promising material resource in space with the fastest commercial utilization potential. On airless or thin-atmosphere bodies, water exists as ice on the surface and within the subsurface. Thermal properties of these deposits are not well-constrained due to the dynamic nature of phase changes within porous space.

## Experimental Method and Setup
- **Facility:** Center for Space Resources at the Colorado School of Mines.
- **Vacuum System:** Medium vacuum chamber (0.3375 m³), scroll pump providing 0.1 Torr.
- **Regolith Analog:** JSC-1A with a Particle Size Distribution (PSD) D50 of ~400 microns.
- **Sample Preparation:** Cylindrical samples (~500 g, 2.5" height, 3.28" diameter) prepared as 'mud-pies', frozen to -30 °C, and cryo-cooled in a liquid nitrogen bath.
- **Heating Process:** OMEGA CSH-1011001 cartridge heater (7 W) placed in the middle of the sample; temperature and pressure recorded over 6-hour test runs.

## Data Processing and Modeling
The research recreated the experimental environment using COMSOL Multiphysics 5.2. The model used a 3D heat equation accounting for phase change:

`rho * Cp * dT/dt + rho * Cp * u · ∇T + ∇ · q = Q` 

Where effective thermal conductivity (keff) is expressed as the sum of conduction, radiation, and gas conduction components. The model also incorporated Darcy's law for the pressure domain to simulate gas flow from sublimation.

## Conclusions
- Heat and mass transfer within icy deposits is computationally intensive but can be effectively modeled via FEM.
- The dynamism of ice phase changes and the pressure domain are critical for understanding thermal properties of volumetric mixtures with ice.
- Co-dependent thermal, pressure, and mass evolution render water extraction in planetary environments counter-intuitive and non-linear.
