# Experimental Investigations of Water Extraction Process within Permanently Shadowed Regions of the Moon\n\n## Abstract\nHeat and mass transfers occurring on the surface and within the subsurface of extraterrestrial bodies during natural and future industrial heating processes require knowledge of thermal properties of porous dry and icy materials. A series of experiments have been employed to simulate borehole heating of icy regolith samples under low vacuum (0.1 to 1.5 Torr) and very low temperatures (>93 K), using JSC-1A analog prepared as \"mud-pies\" with water contents from 0 to 20% wt. Real-time measurements of temperatures in an array of points and vacuum chamber pressures were recorded. To decipher thermal properties data in this inverse heat equation problem, a 3D finite element method modelling has been used to simulate the behaviour of both the samples and the vacuum chamber. The determined thermal conductivities vary with temperature, pressure, ice content, and porosity, while heat capacities vary with temperature, ice content, and porosity.\n\n## Introduction\nThe current paradigm in research of rocky planets is to \"follow the water\". Of all material resources in Space, water has the most promising and possibly fastest commercial utilization potential. On airless bodies, water exists as ice on the surface or within the subsurface. Regardless of origin, thermal properties of water ice deposits in planetary environments are not well-constrained.\n\n## Experimental Method and Setup\nExperiments were conducted at the Center for Space Resources at the Colorado School of Mines using a medium vacuum chamber (0.3375 m³). The JSC-1A regolith analog was used. \n\n### Experimental Steps:\n- Mud-pie batch preparation with specific regolith-water proportions.\n- Cylindrical samples preparation (~500 g, 2.5 in height, 3.28 in diameter).\n- Samples freezing to -30 °C.\n- Cryo-cooling in liquid nitrogen bath.\n- Heater and thermocouples insertion.\n- Sample heating in simulated PSR conditions (0.1 to 0.2 Torr).\n\n## Data Processing\n### Heat Transfer Model\nThe process is governed by a 3D heat equation accounting for phase change:\n\n`ρC_p (∂T/∂t) + ρC_p u ⋅ ∇T + ∇ ⋅ q = Q`\n\nWhere properties of phase change material include:\n- Density (ρ): `θρ_{icy} + (1 - θ)ρ_{desiccated}`\n- Specific Heat (C_p): Mixed based on phase fraction θ.\n- Thermal Conductivity (k): `θk_{icy} + (1 - θ)k_{desiccated}`\n\n### Table 1: Effective thermal conductivity parameters for dry Lunar regolith in vacuum\n| Lunar regolith type | Parameter A [Wm⁻¹K⁻⁴] | Parameter B_c [-] | Amorphous conductivity k_{am} | Reference |\n|---|---|---|---|---|\n| Apollo soil | 1.3 ⋅ 10⁻¹¹ | (9.9 ⋅ 10⁻⁴ + 9.2 ⋅ 10⁻¹⁰ ⋅ φ²)(1 - φ) | Complex polynomial | Woods-Robinson et al. 2019 |\n| JSC 1-A (1690 kgm⁻³) | 3.31 ⋅ 10⁻¹¹ | 6.35 ⋅ 10⁻⁴ | -9.53 ⋅ 10⁻⁴ ⋅ T + 2.4 | Sakatani et al. 2018 |\n\n## Results and Discussion\n- **Thermal Dynamics**: Discrepancies between model and experiment suggest that transition intervals for phase change are smoother and larger than previously estimated, occurring between -50 °C and 50 °C.\n- **Ice Content Effects**: Low ice content surfaces are easier to heat to a target temperature; high ice content (e.g., 14-16%) surfaces are significantly harder to heat up due to increased thermal conductivity and heat capacity.\n- **Permeability**: Higher permeability in regolith allows for successful outgassing; unsuccessful pressure decrease could switch phase change from sublimation to fusion.\n\n## Conclusions\nHeat and mass transfer within icy deposits is computationally intensive and non-linear. The study proved that understanding volumetric mixtures with ice requires accounting for the dynamism of phase change and pressure domains. Thermal evolution trends can be recreated using FEM models if physics are thoroughly considered.
