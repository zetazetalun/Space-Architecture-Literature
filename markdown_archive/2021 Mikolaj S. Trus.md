# The Research Facility on the Moon  ## Abstract  The research facility on the Moon is an experimental, structural design for space exploration. It was developed as a Student Master Thesis at Cracow University of Technology. Modularity, multifunctionality, and simplicity were key objectives. The facility consists of seven modules formed from repetitive, easy-to-transport components. The project is designed to host twelve astronauts and is expandable.  ## Introduction  ### Concept  The idea was born in February 2019. The Moon environment presents challenges like 1/6th gravity, lack of atmosphere, meteors, solar radiation, and extreme temperature amplitudes. The design aims to find innovative structural solutions, protect from external damage, and provide a functional proposal.  ## Methods  *   **Hand drawing:** Used for initial visualization, leading to a spherical shape as the most efficient form. *   **Virtual model:** Created in Blender 3D to establish geometry. Truncated icosahedrons were identified as the most useful modular form. *   **3d printing:** Used to verify results of virtual design through physical scale models. *   **Technical drawings:** CAD-based sections and floorplans were created to determine functional interiors.  ## Results  ### Construction  The design utilizes a rigid assembly system rather than inflatables to ensure predictability in lunar gravity. The structure is based on a truncated icosahedron (12 pentagons, 20 hexagons) which is triangulated for stability. The exoskeleton is made of a light rod construction system. Spherical modules can be divided into 20 repetitive elements for transport.  ### Protection  A multilayer shell is used for protection. It features an internal thermal insulation layer (20 cm), an external layer (5 cm), and a vacuum-filled narrow space (5 cm) between them. The final step is covering the structure with radiation-absorbing regolith.  ### Function  The facility includes seven spherical modules. Three are living areas (housing 4 astronauts each), and others are dedicated to dining, laboratories, and physical activity.  ## Discussion  Compared to the Foster + Partners lunar habitation concept, which uses inflatable structures and robotically printed regolith shells, this concept uses repetitive rigid components and regular polyhedrons, facilitating additive growth of the facility.  ## Conclusion  The research proves the potential of regular polyhedrons in extraterrestrial architecture. Transport issues are resolved through modular components, and the design meets the physical requirements of the lunar environment.
