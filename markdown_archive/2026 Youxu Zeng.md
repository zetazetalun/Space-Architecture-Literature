# Mechanical and thermal properties of pressureless sintered lunar regolith simulant: Analysis of sintering parameters and pore structure

## Abstract
Sintering has emerged as one of the most promising techniques for in-situ resource utilization in lunar construction. Pressureless sintering, owing to its process simplicity and low equipment requirements, offers a feasible route for fabricating large and complex structural components. This study systematically investigates the effects of sintering temperature, holding time, and atmosphere on the mechanical properties, pore structure, thermal behavior, phase evolution, and microstructure of pressureless-sintered compacts using CUG-1B lunar regolith simulant. Results indicate that compacts sintered in air exhibit superior mechanical strength and reduced open porosity. Sintering temperature exerted a more significant influence on compact properties than holding time. Furthermore, sintering kinetics and pore-property relationships were explored, revealing that open pores (rather than isolated pores) predominantly degrade mechanical performance. In contrast, isolated pores significantly enhance thermal insulation. These findings provide critical insights and theoretical support for the application of pressureless sintering of lunar regolith in future large-scale extraterrestrial construction.

## 1. Introduction
Constructing durable infrastructure on the Moon presents substantial challenges due to extreme environment conditions. In-situ resource utilization (ISRU) is a critical strategy enabling the use of abundant local materials, particularly lunar regolith (LR). This study focuses on pressureless sintering of CUG-1B simulant under varying conditions (temperature, time, atmosphere).

## 2. Materials and Methods
### 2.1. Raw Materials
- **Simulant:** CUG-1B (produced by China University of Geosciences).
- **Composition:** Primarily SiO2 (45.64%), Al2O3 (13.40%), FeO (9.22%), CaO (7.80%), MgO (9.16%).
- **Density:** 3.077 g/cm³.

### 2.2. Sample Preparation
- **Compaction:** 100 MPa for 30 seconds.
- **Specimen Size:** Φ 20 × 20 mm.
- **Sintering Temperatures:** 1125 °C, 1150 °C, and 1175 °C.
- **Holding Times:** 60 min, 180 min, and 300 min.
- **Atmospheres:** Air and Argon.

## 3. Results and Discussion
- **Phase Evolution:** Sintered compacts are dominated by plagioclase (anorthite and albite), augite, and olivine. Specimens in air contain hematite (Fe2O3) and magnetite (Fe3O4).
- **Macroscopic Morphology:** Air-sintered samples are reddish-brown (oxidation of ilmenite); argon-sintered are gray-brown.
- **Microstructure:** Air-sintered compacts exhibited greater density and smaller pore sizes compared to argon-sintered versions.
- **Mechanical Properties:** Compressive strength increases with temperature and holding time. Air-sintered samples show much higher strength than argon-sintered ones.
- **Thermal Properties:** Conductivity increases with temperature (-100 to 80 °C) then stabilizes. Isolated porosity effectively curbs the rise in thermal conductivity caused by reduced total porosity.

## 4. Conclusions
1. Oxygen-containing environments (air) significantly enhance mechanical strength compared to inert atmospheres.
2. Sintering temperature is the dominant factor for strength; holding time has a weaker effect.
3. Open porosity predominantly degrades mechanical performance, while isolated porosity enhances thermal insulation. This provides a guideline for balancing load-bearing capacity and insulation in lunar structures.
