# Robotic 3D printed lunar bionic architecture based on lunar regolith selective laser sintering technology

**Authors:** Philip F. Yuan, Xinjie Zhou, Hao Wu, Liming Zhang, Lijie Guo, Yun Shi, Zhe Lin, Jinyu Bai, Youhai Yu, and Shanglu Yang
**Journal:** Architectural Intelligence (2022) 1:14
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1007/s44223-022-00014-9

## Abstract
The lunar base is not only an experimental station for extraterrestrial space exploration but also a dwelling for humans performing this exploration. Building a lunar base presents numerous obstacles and requires environmental perception, feedback design, and construction methods. An integrated fabrication process that incorporates design, 3D printing workflow, and construction details to build a bionic, reconfigurable and high-performance lunar base prototype is presented in this paper. The feasibility of the technical strategy proposed in this paper is verified by the presented empirical samples.

## 1. Introduction
Construction of the lunar base encompasses four stages: preliminary preparation, infrastructure construction, base construction, and post-operation. Crucial research roles include site selection, base planning, space design, and base construction using in situ resources.

## 3. Architectural conceptual design for lunar habitats
The research proposes a concept based on the mollusk shell structure, featuring:
1. Environmental adaptation (vacuum, radiation, extreme temperatures).
2. Bionic geometric prototype (shell-nacre structural layers).
3. Integrated 3D printing control system.

## 4. Lunar base 3D laser-printing construction material and technology
Polyaryletherketone (PAEK) is used as a binder to lower sintering temperature and improve efficiency. The research utilizes a dual-light field coupling laser-printing technology (low-power large light spot for warming + high-power focused spot for sintering).

## 5. Experiments
- **Material:** JSC-1A synthetic lunar regolith + PAEK.
- **Sintering Window:** 360°C to 450°C (Melting at 310°C, decomposition at 510°C).
- **Optimal Mix:** 20% resin content was preliminarily selected based on cost-effectiveness and structural performance.
- **Performance:** Compressive strength increased from 5 MPa to 20 MPa as processing pressure increased from 5 MPa to 20 MPa.

## 6. Conclusion
In situ construction is the most cost-effective phase for lunar base development. 3D printing technology offers the flexibility needed for the challenging lunar environment. This study's bionic shell approach and regolith-PAEK hybrid powder provide a viable pathway for high-performance extraterrestrial habitation.
