# Mesoscale Mechanisms Governing the Shear Strength of Lunar Regolith: Effects of Low Confining Stress and Irregular Particle Morphology

**Journal:** Materials  
**Volume:** 19, 1439  
**Date:** April 2026  
**Authors:** Jun Chen, Ruilin Li, Yukun Ji, Pinqiang Mo

## Abstract
Understanding the mechanical behavior of lunar regolith is critical for future lunar excavation and construction. This study uses triaxial compression tests on CUMT-1 lunar regolith simulant and Fujian standard sand, alongside Discrete Element Method (DEM) simulations, to establish a macro-mesoscopic strength correlation model. Findings show that irregular particle morphology and low geostatic stress significantly enhance the internal friction angle of the regolith.

## 1. Introduction
The lunar regolith layer is the primary load-bearing foundation for future lunar bases. Its shear strength is determined by relative density, particle size, morphology, and in situ stress. This study addresses the gap in understanding the mesoscopic mechanisms under low confining stress.

## 2. Material and Methods
### 2.1. CUMT-1 Lunar Regolith Simulant
- **Components:** Volcanic ash and Fe3O4.
- **Production:** High-temperature sintering and impact crushing to simulate meteorite impacts.
- **Morphology:** Exhibits abundant sharp edges (mean texture, angularity, and aspect ratio are higher than standard terrestrial sand).

### 2.2. Experimental Scheme
- **Apparatus:** GDS triaxial apparatus.
- **Confining Pressures:** 10, 20, 30, 50, 100, and 150 kPa.
- **Relative Density:** 92% (highly compacted).

## 3. Results and Discussion
### 3.1. Triaxial Test Results
CUMT-1 exhibits higher peak stress and more pronounced strain-softening compared to standard sand. The peak friction angle increases as confining pressure decreases.

### 3.2. Macro-Mesoscopic Correlation
A new model relates normalized deviatoric stress (σ̂d) to mesoscopic strength (f̂mes):
**σ̂d = 14.74 f̂mes − 1.5** (R² = 0.97)

## 4. Conclusions
1. Low geostatic stress and high surface roughness contribute to high internal friction angles.
2. Irregular morphology increases peak shear strength by enhancing interparticle contact forces.
3. Strength varies with depth; depth-dependent variations must be considered in lunar foundation design.
