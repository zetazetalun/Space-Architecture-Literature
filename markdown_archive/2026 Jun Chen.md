# Mesoscale Mechanisms Governing the Shear Strength of Lunar Regolith: Effects of Low Confining Stress and Irregular Particle Morphology

**Authors:** Jun Chen, Ruilin Li, Yukun Ji, and Pinqiang Mo  
**Journal:** Materials (MDPI)  
**Date:** Received: 27 February 2026; Published: 3 April 2026  
**DOI:** 10.3390/ma19071439

## Abstract
Understanding the mechanical behavior of lunar regolith is critical for the success of future lunar excavation and construction missions. Irregular particle morphology and low geostatic stress are recognized as key factors contributing to the high internal friction angle of this unique extraterrestrial geomaterial. This study uses consolidated drained triaxial compression tests on CUMT-1 lunar regolith simulant and Fujian standard sand, supplemented by discrete element simulations (DEM), to analyze mechanical responses. A robust macroscopic–mesoscopic strength correlation model was established to predict the normalized deviatoric stress of granular assemblies.

## 1. Introduction
The lunar regolith layer forms the primary load-bearing foundation for future lunar bases. Investigating its mechanical strength is essential for upcoming lunar surface activities. Shear strength is determined by relative density, particle size distribution, morphology, in situ stress, and single-particle strength.

## 2. Material and Methods
### 2.1. CUMT-1 Lunar Regolith Simulant
CUMT-1 consists of volcanic ash and Fe3O4, produced via high-temperature sintering and impact crushing to simulate meteorite impacts. It exhibits abundant sharp edges and textures, resembling Chang’e-5 samples.

## 3. Results and Discussion

### 3.1. Triaxial Test Results
CUMT-1 exhibits significantly higher peak stress and more pronounced strain-softening compared to standard sand. As confining pressure decreases, both the peak friction angle and residual friction angle increase.

### 3.2. Biaxial Test Results (DEM)
Increases in friction coefficient and angularity enhance shear strength. The peak stress occurs earlier with higher angularity.

## 4. Conclusions
1.  A novel macro–mesoscopic strength correlation model was proposed (R² > 0.97).
2.  Friction coefficient and angularity lead to a rise in interparticle contact force, enhancing peak friction angle.
3.  Decreased confining pressure reduces coordination number but increases normalized interparticle contact force, causing peak friction angle to increase.
4.  Low confining stress and high surface roughness on the lunar surface contribute to higher internal friction angles, impacting drilling resistance and rover mobility.
