# DEM Investigation on the Collecting Characteristics and Performance Evaluation of Lunar Soil Within a Hollow Drilling Tool\n\n**Authors:** Ge Yu, Meng Li, Hongyi Ge, Jiangyuan Zhang, Ju Wang, Kefan Cao, Xizhong An\n**Journal:** International Journal for Numerical and Analytical Methods in Geomechanics\n**Publisher:** Wiley\n**Date:** 2026\n**DOI:** 10.1002/nag.70389\n\n## Abstract\nLunar soil collection is pivotal for advancing extraterrestrial exploration, enabling in-situ mineral resource utilization, sustainable lunar base construction, and scientific investigations into lunar geological history. In this study, the collection behavior of lunar soil within a hollow drilling tool was investigated using the Discrete Element Method (DEM) based on the Hertz-Mindlin contact model. The effects of key parameters including inner diameter of the drill pipe (D), friction coefficient (μ), and height of the complement interior the drill bit (H) were analyzed. Findings provide insights for optimizing sampling tools to balance efficiency and sample integrity in extraterrestrial missions.\n\n## 1. Introduction\nThe collection and analysis of lunar soil is essential for the utilization of lunar in-situ resources. Hollow drilling tools are capable of multiple removable sampling missions. Understanding the kinetic and mechanical characteristics of soil-tool interaction is crucial for improving sampling efficiency.\n\n## 2. Numerical Method and Simulation Conditions\n### 2.1 Governing Equations\nThe Hertz-Mindlin no-slip model was employed for DEM simulations. Particles follow Newton's Second Law, with contact forces based on Hertz theory and Mindlin simplifications.\n\n### 2.2 Simulation Conditions\n- **Particles:** 390,000 spherical particles (0.4–1.6 mm size distribution).\n- **Gravity:** 1.6 m/s².\n- **Tools:** Drill pipe with external helix and drill bit with interior complement.\n\n### Table 1: Particle and tool parameters utilized in simulations\n| Parameters | Values |\n| :--- | :--- |\n| Particle Diameter, Dp (mm) | 0.4–1.6 |\n| Particle Density, ρp (kg/m³) | 2900 |\n| Initial Porosity | 0.465 |\n| Young's Modulus (Particles), Ep (MPa) | 50 |\n| Feeding Speed, n (mm/min) | 120 |\n| Rotational Speed, vp (rpm) | 120 |\n\n## 3. Results and Discussion\n### 3.1 Effects of Drill Pipe Inner Diameter (D)\nIncreasing D enhances sample mass but also increases the disturbance rate. As D increases, particles have more space to move, leading to lower porosity and higher homogeneity in the sampled core.\n\n### 3.2 Effects of the Friction Coefficient (μ)\nDecreasing the friction coefficient increases the sample mass. High friction leads to higher energy dissipation and the formation of force arches that hinder particle entry.\n\n### 3.3 Effects of the Complement Height (H)\nReducing H increases sample mass and reduces the disturbance rate. A larger complement height exacerbates radial movement and turbulence within the drill bit.\n\n## 4. Conclusions\n1. Larger D, smaller μ, and smaller H significantly increase sample mass (ms).\n2. Sample movement is divided into three regions: new sample region, radial movement region, and flow region.\n3. Quadratic regression models (RSM) predict sample mass and disturbance rate with high accuracy (R² > 0.95).
