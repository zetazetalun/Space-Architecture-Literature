# DEM Investigation on the Collecting Characteristics and Performance Evaluation of Lunar Soil Within a Hollow Drilling Tool

**Journal:** International Journal for Numerical and Analytical Methods in Geomechanics  
**Publication Year:** 2026  
**Authors:** Ge Yu, Meng Li, Hongyi Ge, Jiangyuan Zhang, Ju Wang, Kefan Cao, Xizhong An  

## Abstract
Lunar soil collection is pivotal for advancing extraterrestrial exploration, enabling in-situ mineral resource utilization, sustainable lunar base construction, and scientific investigations. This study investigates the collection behavior of lunar soil within a hollow drilling tool using the Discrete Element Method (DEM) based on the Hertz-Mindlin contact model. Key parameters such as inner diameter (D), friction coefficient (μ), and height of the complement (H) were analyzed for their effects on macroscopic (mass, coring ratio) and microscopic (pore size, disturbance) properties. Results provide guidance for optimizing lunar soil sampling tools to balance efficiency and sample integrity.

## 1. Introduction
Recent years have seen increased interest in lunar exploration for scientific exploration and the establishment of lunar bases utilizing additive manufacturing and ISRU. Hollow drilling tools, capable of being transported by rovers for multiple missions, are vital for collecting high-quality samples. This paper aims to fill the gap in microscopic analysis of drilling parameters and particle-level mechanisms.

## 2. Numerical Method and Simulation Conditions
### 2.1 Governing Equations
The Hertz-Mindlin no-slip model was employed to calculate normal and tangential contact forces using Newton's Second Law. 

### 2.2 Simulation Conditions
Approximately 390,000 spherical particles with a Gaussian distribution (0.4–1.6 mm) were simulated. Parameters were calibrated against angle of repose (AOR) experiments and triaxial compression tests.

## 3. Results and Discussion
### 3.1 Effects of Inner Diameter (D)
*   **Sample Mass (ms):** Increasing D from 14 to 22 mm increases the space for particles, enhancing sample mass to a maximum of 11.24g.
*   **Disturbance Rate (I):** Larger D leads to higher disturbance rates as more motion space allows for greater relative displacement of particles.
*   **Collection Law:** The sample is divided into three regions: New sample region, Radial movement region, and Flow region.

### 3.2 Effects of Friction Coefficient (μ)
*   Lower friction coefficients (μ) reduce energy dissipation, allowing greater particle mass to enter the drill pipe but increasing the disturbance rate.

### 3.3 Effects of Complement Height (H)
*   Reducing H increases sample mass and reduces the disturbance rate. Higher H values exacerbate radial movement and turbulence near the drill bit.

### 3.4 RSM Prediction Models
Quadratic regression models were developed to predict mass and disturbance:
- **Sample Mass (ms):** R² = 0.9896
- **Disturbance Rate (I):** R² = 0.9643

## 4. Conclusions
1. Larger D, smaller μ, and smaller H increase sample mass.
2. Smaller D, larger μ, and smaller H significantly reduce particle disturbance.
3. Particle movement patterns (Wave-like rotation) explain the efficiency trade-offs.
4. Prediction models provide reliable solutions for forecasting sampling outcomes under varying hardware parameters.
