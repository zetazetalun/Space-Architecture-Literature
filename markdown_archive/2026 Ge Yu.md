# DEM Investigation on the Collecting Characteristics and Performance Evaluation of Lunar Soil Within a Hollow Drilling Tool

## Abstract
Lunar soil collection is pivotal for advancing extraterrestrial exploration, enabling in-situ mineral resource utilization, sustainable lunar base construction, and scientific investigations into lunar geological history. In this study, the collection behavior of lunar soil within a hollow drilling tool was investigated using the Discrete Element Method (DEM) based on the Hertz-Mindlin contact model. The effects of key parameters of drilling tool in direct contact with the sample, including inner diameter of the drill pipe ($D$), friction coefficient ($\mu$), and height of the complement interior the drill bit ($H$) on macroscopic properties (sample mass, coring ratio, uniformity) and microscopic properties (pore size, disturbance) were systematically analyzed.

## 1 Introduction
A number of pioneering technologies have been proposed for establishment of lunar bases by utilizing lunar soil, including additive manufacturing and extraterrestrial artificial light synthesis. The collection and analysis of lunar soil is particularly important for the utilization of lunar in situ resources and the development of related instrumentation. The hollow drilling tool is capable of being transported by the lunar rover for multiple removable sampling missions.

## 2 Numerical Method and Simulation Conditions

### 2.1 Governing Equations
The Hertz-Mindlin no-slip model represents a classical discrete element contact model employed for the simulation of lunar soil. The motion of particles is driven by the normal and tangential contact forces concurrently based on Newton's Second Law.

### 2.2 Simulation Conditions
About 390,000 spherical particles following a Gaussian size distribution in the range of 0.4 ~ 1.6 mm were simulated. Parameters such as Young's modulus, Poisson's ratio, and friction coefficients were calibrated against lunar soil simulants.

## 3 Results and Discussion

### 3.1 Effects of the Inner Diameter of the Drill Pipe
Increasing $D$ enhances sample mass but increases the disturbance rate. The sample can be divided into three regions: the new sample region, the radial movement region, and the flow region. The disturbance rate $I$ increases monotonically as $D$ increases from 14 to 22 mm.

### 3.2 Effects of the Friction Coefficient
Decreasing $\mu$ increases sample mass. Lower friction allows a greater mass of particles to enter the drill pipe per unit time. A larger $\mu$ reduces particle mobility, resulting in a lower $I$.

### 3.3 Effects of the Height of the Complement
Both sample mass ($m_s$) and collection rate ($\tau_m$) gradually increase as the height of the complement $H$ decreases. A higher $H$ exacerbates radial movement of surface particles inside the drill bit, leading to higher disturbance.

## 4 Conclusions
1. Larger $D$, smaller $\mu$, and smaller $H$ significantly increase sample mass ($m_s$).
2. Smaller $D$, larger $\mu$, and smaller $H$ can significantly reduce disturbance ($I$).
3. The quadratic regression models for sample mass and disturbance rate exhibited high predictive accuracy ($R^2 > 0.95$).
