# DESIGN OF COMPOSITE SAMPLING SYSTEM FOR SURFACE SOIL OF EXTRATERRESTRIAL BODIES

**Authors:** Qi-Chen Sun, Zeng Zhao, Jun-Shan Gong, Zhong-Wang Yin, Xiao-Ming Lai, Hai-Bin Yu, Zhong-Xian Zhao, Chao Wang, and Hui Lv
**Journal:** THERMAL SCIENCE: Year 2025, Vol. 29, No. 2B, pp. 1267-1272
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.2298/TSCI2502267S

## Abstract
Space resource development has become a frontier hot topic in international space exploration. Amidst the growing scarcity of Earth’s resources and rare metals, the harnessing of asteroid resources has risen to prominence as a principal avenue of exploration. The utilization of asteroid resources necessitates extensive regolith mining operations. Catering to the demands of extensive space activities, extraterrestrial construction, and resource development, this paper presents the design and simulation analysis of an extraterrestrial surface regolith composite sampling system. The findings demonstrate that the composite sampling approach, which combines spiral drilling and transport with kinetic energy directional excitation, is highly effective. It is capable of collecting no less than 500 g within a mere 5 seconds at a rotation speed of 3000 rpm, while the reaction force exerted by the brush remains below 5 N, thus satisfying the requirement for a low reaction force.

## 3. Scheme Design of Sampling System
The proposed system addresses challenges such as weak surface gravity, irregular shapes, and varied soil compositions (gravel, ice-soil mixtures, metallic rocks).

### 3.1 Design of Kinetic Energy Directional Sampling
Uses a motor-driven brush on a rotating shaft to impart velocity to regolith particles, directing them into a sample channel. This is effective for small particles in microgravity.

### 3.2 Design of Auger Drilling Transport Sampling
Employs a spiral flange to cut and transport regolith from deeper levels. Features a small spiral angle and deep groove structure to retain samples within the storage cavity.

### 3.3 Design of Compound Sampling Principle
Combines the strengths of both methods: subsurface drilling crushes rocks and excavates regolith, while high-speed brushes direct the liberated particles into the collection container.

## 4. Simulation Analysis and Experiment
### 4.1 Collection Efficiency
Simulations were conducted at a constant feed speed of 0.01 m/s. Results show high efficiency at 3000 rpm.

| Feed speed [ms⁻¹] | Speed [rpm] | Time [s] | Equivalent mass of particles collected [g] |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 0.01 | 1000 | 5 | 579.6 |
| 0.01 | 2000 | 5 | 702.8 |
| 0.01 | 3000 | 5 | 729.4 |

### 4.2 Reaction Force
The stability of the system depends on minimizing reaction forces. The maximum reaction force excited by the brush was found to be:
- Z-axis: 6.6 N
- X-axis: 0.78 N
- Y-axis: < 1.3 N

## 5. Conclusion
The composite sampling system integrating spiral drilling and kinetic energy directional acquisition demonstrates superior adaptability to asteroid surface conditions. It achieves peak efficiency at 3000 rpm, collecting ≥500 g in 5 seconds with a peak reaction force below 7 N, making it ideal for microgravity operations.
