# Earth-based unmanned solar sintering system for lunar bricks with regolith simulant

## Metadata
- **Journal:** Automation in Construction 179 (2025) 106486
- **Authors:** Cheng Zhou, Yanan Lu, Yuyue Gao, Yan Zhou, Shifeng Wen, Lieyun Ding
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.autcon.2025.106486

## Abstract
The concentrated sintering in-situ construction method, using solar energy as the energy source and lunar regolith as the raw material, offers a promising approach for lunar brick production. This paper introduces an unmanned solar-powered sintering system (USSS) to fabricate lunar bricks using a regolith simulant. The system autonomously tracks the sun's movement and employs Fresnel lenses to concentrate solar energy for sintering. Experimental results demonstrated the production of simulated lunar regolith bricks with adequate strength under vacuum conditions.

## 1. Introduction
Lunar regolith is an abundant material and solar energy is a uniquely convenient energy source. Direct sintering of lunar regolith is a feasible in-situ construction strategy. Existing experimental machines lack advanced sensing and motion systems. This study proposes an unmanned solar sintering system (USSS) designed for autonomous operation and improved interparticle bonding through molding and pressurization.

## 2. System Design (USSS)
The USSS consists of three primary subsystems:
- **Motion Subsystem:** Governs dual-axis motion (pitch and rotation) for real-time solar tracking.
- **Lens Subsystem:** Employs a Fresnel lens to create high-heat spots for sintering.
- **Forming Subsystem:** A vacuum tank with a graphite mold designed for sintering under controlled environments.

## 3. Simulation and Performance Analysis
Numerical models using TracePro analyzed the impact of design parameters:
- **Ring Spacing:** Minor effect on spot irradiance; 0.3 mm spacing achieved optimal energy utilization.
- **Focal Length:** Increasing focal length enhances maximum light irradiance but expands spot size.
- **Lens Radius:** Larger radii capture more flux, but increase energy loss due to larger propagation volumes.
- **Double-Lens Configuration:** Outperforms single-lens setups in gathering energy from both short and long-wave bands, with a total energy utilization efficiency of 38.211%.

## 4. Experimental Results
Experiments were conducted using HUST-1 simulated lunar regolith:
- **Tracking Accuracy:** The system maintained a frequency of 1-2 min for refresh, achieving precision within 1 degree.
- **Heating Curve:** Temperatures reached 700-800°C within several hours.
- **Vacuum vs. Atmosphere:** Vacuum-sintered bricks showed superior density and uniformity compared to atmospheric-sintered ones.
- **Compressive Strength:** Vacuum-sintered components achieved 0.4–0.8 MPa; atmospheric-sintered components achieved 0.2–0.5 MPa.

## 6. Conclusions
The USSS established preliminary capabilities for automated solar sintering. Future work will focus on lightweighting, diversified mold designs, and integration into complete production lines (e.g., lunar soil delivery and brick picking systems).
