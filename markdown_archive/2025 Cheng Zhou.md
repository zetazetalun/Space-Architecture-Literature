# Earth-based unmanned solar sintering system for lunar bricks with regolith simulant

**Authors:** Cheng Zhou, Yanan Lu, Yuyue Gao, Yan Zhou, Shifeng Wen, Lieyun Ding
**Journal:** Automation in Construction
**Volume:** 179 (2025) 106486
**Date:** Available online 21 August 2024

## Abstract
The concentrated sintering in-situ construction method, using solar energy as the energy source and lunar regolith as the raw material, offers a promising approach for lunar brick production. This paper introduces an unmanned solar-powered sintering system (USSS) to fabricate lunar bricks using a regolith simulant. The performance of the concentrating module was analyzed through numerical simulations. An integrated experimental prototype was deployed, and experiments were conducted to form simulated lunar regolith bricks with adequate strength.

## 1. Introduction
Construction of bases on the lunar surface is a strategic priority. Utilizing solar energy for direct sintering of lunar regolith presents a highly feasible in-situ construction strategy. Existing machines often lack advanced sensing/motion or rely on molten powder bed forming, which has weak bonding. This study develops an automated system employing molding and pressurization to enhance particle bonding.

## 3. Design of USSS
The Unmanned Solar Sintering System (USSS) consists of three subsystems:
1.  **Motion Subsystem:** Dual-axis motion (pitch and rotation) for solar tracking.
2.  **Lens Subsystem:** Uses a Fresnel lens to concentrate light into a high-heat spot.
3.  **Forming Subsystem:** A vacuum tank with an internal chamber for mold placement (graphite mold), including thermal and vacuum monitoring.

## 6. Experimental Tests and Results
### 6.1. Materials
The research used **HUST-1** simulated lunar regolith, derived from volcanic ash from Jilin Province, China, which closely resembles Apollo 14 samples.

### 6.3. Analysis of Experimental Results
*   **Machine Performance:** The auto-tracking system achieved precision within 1 degree adjustments every 1–2 minutes.
*   **Thermal Performance:** The mold temperature rose nearly 100°C every 10 minutes initially, reaching a limit between 700°C and 800°C.
*   **Brick Properties:** Formed bricks were cylindrical (approx. 20 mm diameter, 30 mm height).
*   **Compressive Strength:**
    *   Vacuum-sintered: 0.4–0.8 MPa
    *   Atmospherically-sintered: 0.2–0.5 MPa

### 6.4. Effects of Environment
*   **Vacuum vs. Air:** Vacuum sintering significantly enhanced density and uniformity by eliminating air interference which causes regional variability and large pores.
*   **Temperature:** Densification improved notably as temperatures increased from 500-600°C to 700-800°C.

## 7. Conclusions
The USSS successfully demonstrated automated orientation and sintering of regolith simulant. Vacuum conditions were shown to be critical for the density and strength of the formed lunar bricks. Future iterations will focus on enhanced thermal insulation to achieve higher strengths.
