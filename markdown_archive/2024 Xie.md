# The first biological experiment on lunar surface for Humankind: Device and results

**Authors:** GengXin Xie, JingHang Ding, YuanXun Zhang, MaoZhi Ren, Dan Qiu, Xi Wang
**Journal:** Acta Astronautica 214 (2024) 216–223
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.actaastro.2023.10.023

## Abstract
The Bioregenerative Life Support System (BLSS) is indispensable for extraterrestrial bases. The Chinese Chang’e 4 lunar lander carried a micro-ecosystem called Biological Experiment Payload (BEP) to the back of the Moon. The payload (2.608 kg) contained cotton, potato, Arabidopsis, and rape seeds, fly eggs, and yeast. Cotton seeds germinated in 22 h on the Moon, faster than the 53 h recorded on Earth. Seedlings on the Moon survived the lunar night temperatures of −52 °C, remaining green while terrestrial controls died. This experiment proved plants could grow on the lunar surface using natural sunlight and microgravity.

## 1. Introduction
Building a lunar base requires self-sufficiency due to high transportation costs. BLSS, an artificial ecosystem with producers, consumers, and decomposers, is a critical technology for supplying food, oxygen, and water.

## 2. The Biological Experiment Payload (BEP)
- **Structure:** Cylindrical aluminum shell (173 mm diameter, 198 mm height).
- **Biological Chamber:** 128 mm diameter, 72 mm height.
- **Components:**
  - **Seedbed:** Circular (110.1 x 81.3 mm) using vermiculite as substrate.
  - **Watering:** 18 ml water storage; uses heated petroleum jelly as a valve.
  - **Lighting:** Light-guiding fibers (10 mm diameter) to channel natural lunar sunlight.
  - **Thermal Control:** Multilayer insulation and thermoelectric coolers/heaters.
  - **Sensors:** CCD cameras, temperature, and pressure sensors.

## 3. Results
### 3.1. Germination and Growth
- **Cotton:** First germination captured at 22 h post-watering. Leaves reached 8 x 12 mm by Jan 12. 
- **Morphology:** Lunar seedlings showed curved stems, fat nodes near the substrate, and curled leaves.
- **Survival:** Moon seedlings remained green after the lunar night (-52 °C), whereas Earth-based synchronization experiments died (blackened/collapsed).

### 3.2. Payload Performance
- **Weight:** < 3 kg.
- **Power:** < 15 W.
- **Air Leakage:** < 1.9 × 10⁻⁵ Pa·cm²/s.
- **Internal Pressure:** 98 to 104 kPa during the first lunar day.

## 4. Discussion & ISRU Implications
Beyond biology, the paper highlights that future lunar bases must address ISRU (In Situ Resource Utilization), specifically:
- Improving lunar soil for cultivation.
- Utilizing lunar rocks as construction materials.
- Using lunar lava tube caves for base construction.
- Producing oxygen and water from lunar minerals.

## 5. Conclusion
The BEP demonstrated the viability of plant cultivation on the Moon using natural sunlight, providing a foundational step for future bioregenerative life support in space habitats.
