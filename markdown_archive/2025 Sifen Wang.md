# Control Network Construction for LRO NAC Images Based on Refining Tie Points by Matching With Shaded LOLA DEM\n\n**Authors:** Sifen Wang, Xun Geng, Jiansheng Li, Tao Li, Junming Yu, Ancheng Wang, Jin Wang, Pengying Liu, Zhen Peng, Xin Ma, Yinhui Wang, Yuying Wang, and Guohua Chang\n**Journal:** IEEE Journal of Selected Topics in Applied Earth Observations and Remote Sensing, Vol. 18, 2025\n\n## Abstract\nThe upcoming exploration activities in the lunar south pole require high-resolution mapping products to support landing site selection and navigation. The special terrain and illumination conditions in the lunar south pole pose significant challenges to photogrammetric processing. Existing lunar mapping products exhibit limited coverage and accuracy in the south pole region and cannot meet the exploration missions’ requirements. In this study, a new control network construction method is proposed based on refining tie points by image matching between the approximate orthophotos of lunar reconnaissance orbiter narrow-angle camera (NAC) images and shaded digital elevation model (DEM) maps. The shaded DEM maps are generated using lunar orbiter laser altimeter terrain models with the same acquisition time of on-orbit NAC images, making them have very similar illumination conditions to the on-orbit images. Using the shaded DEM maps as a transition, we establish the coordinate transformation relationship between the stereopairs of NAC images by computing the homograph transformation matrix in orthophoto space. Then, the candidate control measures in the control network are refined in orthophoto space and then converted to the original image space. We conducted experiments using two sets of NAC images near the lunar south pole. The experimental results demonstrate that the proposed method can effectively solve the problems of image matching and control network generation for lunar south pole images.\n\n## I. Introduction\nThe lunar south pole (LSP) is the focus of current deep space exploration activities. Major space agencies have implemented or plan to conduct various exploration activities in the LSP, including the Artemis-3 lunar landing and astronaut exploration mission planned by NASA and China’s Chang’e-7 and Chang’e-8 missions. There are numerous permanently shadowed regions (PSRs) in the LSP, where it is believed that there may be a large amount of water ice. Water ice can serve as a vital resource for sustaining human life and in fuel production for future deep space exploration activities.\n\n## Methodology\nThe study proposes a control network construction method based on image matching between shaded DEM maps and the approximate orthophotos of the LRO NAC images. The shaded DEM maps are generated using the same photograph time as the on-orbit LRO NAC images to ensure similar illumination conditions. The methodology integrates USGS ISIS software modules with a new refinement method for candidate control measures.\n\n### Key Steps:\n1. **Data Preparation:** LOLA DEM, LRO NAC images, and SPICE kernels.\n2. **Preprocessing:** Format conversion, radiometric calibration, and SPICE initialization.\n3. **Orthophoto Generation:** Create approximate orthophotos and downsample to DEM resolution.\n4. **Shaded DEM Generation:** Use photograph time to simulate Sun position and generate shaded maps.\n5. **Image Matching:** SIFT-based matching between NAC orthophotos and shaded DEMs.\n6. **Refinement:** Predict and refine pixel coordinates of candidate control measures using homograph matrices.\n\n## Results and Discussion\nExperimental results using two datasets near the LSP show that for the 10 m/pixel LOLA DEM, the pixel coordinate difference is less than two pixels. The reprojection error of the bundle adjustment was 0.48, and the root mean square error of images was 0.43–0.72 pixels. The method significantly improves the success rate of image matching in the complex lighting environment of the lunar south pole.\n\n## Conclusion\nThe proposed method provides high prediction accuracy for determining candidate control measures, laying a foundation for high-quality mapping products essential for lunar south pole landing site selection and engineering operations.
