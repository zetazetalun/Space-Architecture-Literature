# Two-stage DRL with hybrid perception of vision and force feedback for lunar construction robotic assembly control\n\n**Authors:** Li Boxin, Wang Zhaokui\n**Journal:** Acta Astronautica 229 (2025) 357–373\n**DOI:** [10.1016/j.actaastro.2025.01.017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.actaastro.2025.01.017)\n\n## Abstract\nLunar construction is necessary for long-term deep space exploration and resource utilization. Robotic assembly is an indispensable technology. This paper proposes a two-stage Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) control method with hybrid vision and force feedback to perform assembly operations end-to-end. A 1:1 simulation scenario and a ground robot prototype were used to verify the method, achieving a sim-to-real pipeline using multi-Aruco visual calibration.\n\n## 1. Mission Overview\n- **Robot (LCSR):** Features an omnidirectional mobile chassis, lifting mechanism, 6-DOF end effector, 6D force sensor, and RGB-D cameras.\n- **Task:** Two-point docking with locking mechanisms, relevant for future lunar infrastructure assembly.\n\n## 2. Control Method\n- **Stage 1: Free Spatial Posture Exploration.** Uses Vision-TD3 network for pose alignment based on RGB-D imagery.\n- **Stage 2: Constrained Contact Interaction.** Uses Force-TD3 network for precise fine-tuning and locking based on force sensors.\n- **Reward Function:** Staged reward based on visual pose norm (Stage 1) and first-order derivative of piecewise force (Stage 2).\n\n## 3. Simulation Results\n- **Setup:** CoppeliaSim with Bullet Physics 2.83.\n- **Performance:** Vision-TD3 converged after ~250k steps; Force-TD3 converged after ~350k steps. Relative errors reduced to 5mm (position) and 2 degrees (orientation).\n\n## 4. Ground Experiments\n- **Success Rate:** 80% (24 out of 30 experiments).\n- **Sim-to-Real Pipeline:** Multi-Aruco visual calibration effectively eliminated reality gaps and enabled autonomous pre-training.\n\n## 5. Technical Parameters\n| Parameter | Value |\n| :--- | :--- |\n| Lifting-Z Range | 0 to 2.5 m |\n| Assembly Part Weight | 200 kg |\n| Force Sensor Range | ±30 kN |\n| Action Space | 6-dimensional pose displacement |\n\n## Conclusion\nThe proposed two-stage DRL method facilitates autonomous, intelligent assembly in unstructured lunar environments, bridging the gap between simulation and real-world application.
