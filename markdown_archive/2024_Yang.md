# Cross-section extraction and model reconstruction of lava tube based on L1-medial skeleton

## Abstract
Lunar lava tube caves are prime candidates for lunar base site selection due to their unique geographical structure and potential as shelters. However, obtaining detailed data directly from their interiors is challenging, making the study of terrestrial analogues critical. This paper explores a method for extracting lava tube parameters and constructing 3D models using LiDAR point cloud data. The approach involves data preprocessing, axis construction via an L1-medial skeleton algorithm, cross-sectional feature extraction, and 3D reconstruction. The method achieved high accuracy, with 99% of model positional errors falling within 0.15 m.

## 1. Introduction
Lava tubes are roofed passages formed by flowing lava. On the Moon and Mars, deep skylights similar to terrestrial lava tubes have been observed. These natural shelters offer protection from extreme temperature fluctuations, cosmic rays, lunar dust, and meteorites, making them suitable for lunar base construction.

## 2. Related Works
### 2.1 State-of-the-art of lava tube detection research
Since the 1990s, research has focused on the formation mechanisms and utilization value of lunar lava tubes. Terrestrial studies show that lunar lava tubes are likely much larger than those on Earth but remain structurally stable, offering environments for potential settlement.

### 2.2 Construction and application of 3D models
Current modeling methods are categorized into cross-section-based and geometry-based modeling. This research improves upon these by using an L1-medial skeleton for more accurate axis extraction in irregular natural tunnels.

## 3. Research Area
The primary experimental site is the Shenyang Cave lava tube in Jingpo Lake Geopark, China. This region's Cenozoic volcanic rocks provide a representative environment similar to lunar lava tubes.

## 4. Methodology
1. **Data Preprocessing**: Voxelization to reduce noise and point cloud volume.
2. **Lava Tube Axis Extraction**: Using a novel algorithm based on the L1-Medial Skeleton to simplify 3D points into a 1D central axis.
3. **Extraction of Cross-sections**: Sampling at 0.5 m intervals along the axis to calculate geometric parameters (area, length, width).
4. **Mesh Model Construction**: Creating a 3D mesh by establishing topological relationships between cross-sectional points.

## 5. Results
### 5.1 Accuracy Evaluation
Using Hausdorff distance, the 3D model accuracy was validated against measured data. 99% of points fell within a 0.15 m error margin. Errors were highest at sharp bends or areas with significant diameter variation.

### 5.2 Level of Detail (LoD)
The method allows for flexible adjustment of cross-section density, enabling different LoD levels for tasks ranging from equipment installation to high-precision crack detection.

## 6. Summary and Discussion
The L1-medial-based approach accurately extracts the central axis of complex lava tubes. This facilitates precise calculations of internal volume, which is critical for pressurized habitat planning. Sealing and filling lava tubes with gas could create protective shells for lunar bases, significantly reducing rebuilding costs.
