# Cross-section extraction and model reconstruction of lava tube based on L1-medial skeleton

**Authors:** Qiao Yang, Zhizhong Kang, Teng Hu, Zhen Cao, Chenming Ye, Dongming Liu, Haoxiang Hu, Shuai Shao
**Journal:** International Journal of Applied Earth Observation and Geoinformation 132 (2024) 104062
**DOI:** 10.1016/j.jag.2024.104062

## Abstract
Lunar lava tube caves, due to their unique geographical structure and potential as shelters, have repeatedly emerged as prime candidates or priority areas during discussions on lunar base site selection. This paper explores a method for extracting lava tube parameters and constructing 3D models using LiDAR point cloud data. The approach utilizes an L1-medial skeleton algorithm for precise axis capture, enabling the extraction of geometric parameters such as cross-sectional area, length, and width. The method achieves a high degree of accuracy (99% within 0.15m positional error), providing technical support for extraterrestrial volcanic studies and lunar base planning.

## 1. Introduction
Lava tubes are roofed passages formed by flowing lava. On the Moon and Mars, deep skylights and gravity detections confirm the presence of underground caves. These natural shelters offer protection from extreme temperature fluctuations, cosmic rays, lunar dust, and meteorites, making them suitable for lunar base construction.

## 2. Methodology
The study proposes a four-step methodology based on the L1-medial skeleton:
1. **Data Preprocessing:** Voxelization (0.05m size) to remove noise and reduce data volume.
2. **Axis Construction:** An iterative algorithm based on the L1-Medial Skeleton to simplify the 3D point set into a 1D central axis.
3. **Cross-sectional Feature Extraction:** Sampling every 0.5m along the axis to derive cross-sectional points and geometric parameters.
4. **3D Model Reconstruction:** Integrating cross-sectional data into a comprehensive 3D mesh model.

## 3. Results and Findings
- **Accuracy:** 99% of extracted cross-sectional points had an error of less than 0.12m. 99% of the model's positional errors were within 0.15m.
- **Lava Tube Volume:** The method enabled the calculation of internal volumes for terrestrial segments, providing a blueprint for lunar applications.

## 4. Discussion and Application to Space Architecture
The research outcomes provide a foundation for:
- **Site Selection:** Quantifying the size and stability of lava tubes for habitation.
- **Internal Planning:** Providing accurate 3D models for the layout of equipment and inflatable modules (Class II/III habitats) inside the tubes.
- **Stability Assessment:** Accurate volume and structural data allow for better pressure-bearing analysis for future pressurized habitats.
