# Applying Terrestrial Geo-Material Science Methodology to Lunar ISRU Construction

## Metadata
- **Authors:** H. Patel, P. Suermann, N. Shumaker, S. Deitrick, J. W. Bullard, and R. C. Ewing
- **Year:** 2021
- **Publisher:** American Society of Civil Engineers (ASCE)
- **Conference:** Earth and Space 2021

## Abstract
Building on research for military expeditionary construction, this research aims to establish methods for the design and testing of material mixtures derived from the lunar surface with minimal additives. The paper reviews opportunities and challenges of concreting and sintering, emphasizing the need for regolith simulants representative of the lunar highlands.

## Introduction
Infrastructure such as landing pads, roads, and radiation shielding is critical for lunar return. On Earth, geotechnical assessments are standard; on the Moon, we lack equivalent predictive capabilities for geotechnical properties necessary for engineering permanent occupation using indigenously developed materials (ISRU).

## Lunar Surface Materials
### Terrain Classes
- **Mare:** Basaltic lava flows, younger, thinner regolith (~15% of surface).
- **Highlands:** Anorthosite, older, thicker regolith, highly cratered.

### Regolith Properties
- **Density:** 1.30 g/cm³ to 1.92 g/cm³.
- **Size:** Average particle size of 72 μm. Soil (<1 mm) is ~70% by mass; dust (<20 μm) is ~20%.
- **Composition:** Mineral fragments, glasses, and agglutinates (fused impact glass particles).

## Construction Methods and Challenges
### Concreting Methods
- **Challenges:** Scarcity of calcium and liquid water (requires ~20% mass for hydration).
- **Binders:** Geopolymers (aluminosilicates) or sulfur binders (using troilite/FeS found in regolith).
- **Sulfur Binder Issues:** High volatility, toxic fumes, and poor durability under thermal cycling.

### Sintering Methods
- **Mechanism:** Fusing grains at 60-80% of melting temperature (800°C–1000°C).
- **Challenges:** Lunar regolith (70 μm) is coarser than standard sinterable powders (7 μm).
- **Promising Tech:** Spark Plasma Sintering (SPS) for rapid densification.

## Methodology: THAMES
The **Thermodynamic Hydration and Microstructure Evolution Simulator (THAMES)** is proposed to predict stable mineralogical components and simulate 3D virtual microstructures for lunar concrete or sintered parts to calculate engineering properties like compressive strength.

## Conclusion
The study aims to adapt terrestrial geomaterial models to lunar conditions to establish ASCE/ASTM standards for ISRU, supporting NASA's goal to stay on the Moon by 2024.
