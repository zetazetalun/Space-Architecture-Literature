# The principle of topological interlocking in extraterrestrial construction\n\n**Authors:** A.V. Dyskin, Y. Estrin, E. Pasternak, H.C. Khor, A.J. Kanel-Belov\n**Journal:** Acta Astronautica 57 (2005) 10 – 21\n\n## Abstract\nApplications of a newly established principle of topological interlocking to different types of extraterrestrial construction are considered. Topological interlocking arises when elements of special shapes (usually convex or nearly convex, such that no stress concentration develops) are arranged in such a way that neither of them can be removed from the assembly without disturbing the neighbouring elements. Two types of extraterrestrial structures are considered: mortar-free structures built from engineered interlocking bricks (osteomorphic bricks) and modular extraterrestrial bases or spaceships organized in topologically interlocking assemblies. The self-adjusting property of these bricks permits erecting structures which tolerate low precision of production and assembly, making the proposed method suitable for in situ produced bricks and low-cost assembling machinery.\n\n## 1. Introduction\nExtraterrestrial structures can be categorized into: (1) structures built from basic elements like bricks manufactured in situ and (2) modular structures based on Earth-manufactured units. Mortar-free construction is ideal for vacuum environments where curing traditional binders is problematic.\n\n## 2. Principle of Topological Interlocking\nTopological interlocking is achieved by geometry and arrangement where no single element can be removed from an assembly constrained at the periphery. Historically, assemblies of tetrahedra were used, but modern designs use osteomorphic bricks with curved surfaces that prevent displacement and promote self-alignment.\n\n## 3. Extraterrestrial Construction based on Osteomorphic Bricks\n### 3.1 Mortar-free Structures\nOsteomorphic bricks allow structures to be built without mortar. Key benefits include:\n- **Self-adjustment:** High precision positioning is not required, aiding robotization.\n- **Tolerance:** Tolerance to variations in block dimensions.\n- **Constraint requirements:** Requires unidirectional peripheral constraint (provided by natural craters, external frames, or post-tensioning cables).\n\n### 3.2 In Situ Production\nProduction technologies include:\n- Sintering in a mould (microwave or external heating).\n- Selective Laser Sintering (layer-by-layer manufacturing of entire brick layers).\n\n## 4. Results and Findings\nExperiments demonstrate that interlocking assemblies have high fracture resistance. A reported test showed an assembly failing at an average indentation load of 11.93 kN, while a solid plate of the same material failed at 9.9 kN (approx. 20% higher performance for interlocking).\n\n## 5. Interlocking Modular Structures\nModular bases can be designed using interlocking polyhedra (tetrahedra, cubes, octahedra). These configurations allow for self-adjustment upon assembly and high tolerance to missing blocks (specifically square-tiling and osteomorphic types). Spaceships can be assembled in-orbit from independent modules held together by slight horizontal thrust components.\n\n## 6. Conclusion\n1. Osteomorphic bricks enable self-adjusting, mortar-free structures ideal for in-situ production and robotic assembly.\n2. The structures possess high fracture propagation resistance.\n3. Modular structures benefit from lateral-force-driven assembly even on uneven ground.\n4. Interlocking modules offer flexible configurations for spacecraft.
