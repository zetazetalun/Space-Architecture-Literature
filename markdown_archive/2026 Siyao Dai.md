# Primary Stress Factors and Adaptive Mechanisms of Microalgae in Space Environments and Their Applications in Space Life Support Systems

**Authors:** Siyao Dai, Weiying Feng, Jeffrey Lae, Xuezheng Yu, Chia Min Ho, Fang Yang, Qingfeng Miao, and Pengcheng Duan  
**Journal:** Plants (MDPI)  
**Date:** February 2026  
**DOI:** 10.3390/plants15050697

## Abstract
Cyanobacteria and eukaryotic microalgae are considered ideal candidates for bioregenerative life support systems (BLSS) due to high metabolic efficiency and ecological robustness. This review summarizes microalgal responses to space stressors (radiation, vacuum, temperature, microgravity) and their practical applications in atmospheric regulation, food supplementation, and wastewater recycling for long-duration space missions and planetary base construction.

## 1. Introduction
Sustainable life support systems are urgent for space exploration. Microalgae position themselves as leading biological candidates for BLSS, informing the optimization of life support and resource utilization technologies for off-world colonization.

## 2. Primary Stress Factors of Space Environments
- **Space Radiation:** UV radiation (UVB, UVC), solar energetic particles (SEP), and galactic cosmic rays (GCR) cause DNA damage and oxidative stress.
- **Extreme Vacuum:** Induces cellular dehydration and membrane rupture; however, species like *Chroococcidiopsis sp.* show high tolerance.
- **Extreme Temperatures:** Large-amplitude fluctuations (e.g., -19 °C to 71 °C) cause denaturation and suppress metabolism.
- **Microgravity:** Influences nutrient diffusion and physiological responses, though effects are species-specific.

## 4. Applications in Space Life Support Systems
- **Atmospheric Regulation:** *Chlorella sp.* can achieve oxygen production rates of 15 mmol/(L·h); ~73 L of culture can meet the daily demand of one astronaut.
- **Food Supplementation:** Algae provide 50–70% protein by dry weight and essential fatty acids (EPA, DHA).
- **Water Purification:** *Chlorella* and *Scenedesmus* show high nitrogen and phosphorus removal rates (87–93%) from human waste.

## 5. Research Prospects and Challenges
Future research focuses on: 
1. Photobioreactor (PBR) optimization for spectral control.
2. Utilizing In-Situ Resources (ISRU) such as regolith and lithic matrices for localized cultivation.
3. Large-scale automated harvesting and processing technologies.

## 6. Conclusions
Microalgae, particularly cyanobacteria capable of forming biofilms (*Chroococcidiopsis sp.*), are ideal candidates for long-term exposure and ISRU on planetary surfaces.
