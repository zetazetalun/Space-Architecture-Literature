# Bearing capacity of lunar soil ground under extraterrestrial environmental effects

**Authors:** Banglu Xi, Mingjing Jiang, Pinqiang Mo, Jiaqiang Yang, Zhenhua Zhang  
**Journal:** Computers and Geotechnics 165 (2024) 105923  
**DOI:** 10.1016/j.compgeo.2023.105923

## Abstract
Lunar environment features a low gravity field and ultra-high vacuum which shows significant effects on the bearing behavior of lunar soil ground for future lunar habitation. Therefore, the 2D Discrete Element Method (DEM) was employed to simulate plate load tests under different gravity fields in non-vacuum and high-vacuum environments. The results show that a bilinear line bounded by 1g can describe the gravity effect on ultimate bearing capacity and subgrade reaction. The affected area becomes deeper and wider with decreasing gravity in a high-vacuum environment, providing critical data for analytical models of lunar habitat foundations.

## 1. Introduction
Lunar habitation is central to future space exploration, serving as an ISRU supply center and scientific platform. Geotechnical engineering is required for accurate understanding of lunar soil-tool interaction and foundation design. The Moon's low gravity and high vacuum (leading to van der Waals forces) increase internal friction and cohesion, necessitating investigation into how these factors affect bearing capacity to ensure habitat safety and economic design.

## 2. DEM Model of Lunar Regolith
The Distinct Element Method (DEM) captures the rolling resistance $M$ due to irregular shapes and van der Waals force $F_v$ in ultra-high vacuum:

$$M = \begin{cases} \frac{K_n \beta^2 r^2}{12}\theta, & M < \frac{K_n u_n \beta r}{6} \\ \frac{K_n u_n \beta r}{6}, & M \geq \frac{K_n u_n \beta r}{6} \end{cases}, \quad F_v = \frac{A \beta r}{6\pi D^3}$$

## 3. Results and Discussion

### 3.1. Ultimate Bearing Capacity
The ultimate bearing capacity ($q_u$) increases non-linearly with increasing gravity. High-vacuum conditions exhibit higher bearing capacity than non-vacuum due to soil shear strength enhancements. A gravity factor $s_{ng}^{qu}$ is proposed to describe this effect:

$$s_{ng}^{qu} = q_u^{ng} / q_u^{1g}$$

Fitting parameters for $s_{ng}^{qu}$ suggest a bilinear relationship bounded by 1g. The slopes in low-gravity fields ($n_g < 1$) are significantly higher than in high-gravity fields ($n_g > 1$), meaning centrifuge tests (high-g) cannot be directly extrapolated to lunar conditions without correction.

### 3.2. Soil Response and Failure Modes
- **Non-vacuum:** As gravity decreases, the wedge-shaped flow beneath the plate becomes larger and deeper, resulting in a deeper but narrower affected area.
- **High-vacuum:** The affected area evolves both deeper and wider with decreasing gravity. This difference is driven by the relative dominance of van der Waals forces over self-weight at low gravity levels.

## 4. Concluding Remarks
1. A bilinear model bounded by 1g describes gravity factors for bearing capacity. Low-gravity fields allow the ground to bear a larger mass because gravity affects the load more significantly than it reduces soil capacity.
2. Particle velocity fields reveal a dynamic balance in failure modes (Hill mode vs. Prandtl analysis).
3. Affected zones are wider and deeper in high-vacuum environments under low gravity compared to Earth-like conditions.
