# Bearing capacity of lunar soil ground under extraterrestrial environmental effects

**Authors:** Banglu Xi, Mingjing Jiang, Pinqiang Mo, Jiaqiang Yang, Zhenhua Zhang  
**Journal:** Computers and Geotechnics 165 (2024) 105923  
**Published:** 12 November 2023 (Online)

## Abstract
Lunar environment features a low gravity field and ultra-high vacuum which shows significant effects on the bearing behavior of lunar soil ground for future lunar habitation. Therefore, the 2D Discrete Element Method was employed to simulate the plate load tests under different gravity fields in non-vacuum (i.e., excluding the van der Waals force) and high-vacuum (i.e., including the van der Waals force) environments, analyzing the bearing behavior and soil response under extraterrestrial environmental effects.

## 1. Introduction
Lunar habitation plays an important role in the future of human society. From a perspective of geotechnical engineering, it requires an accurate understanding of the lunar soil-tool interaction on the Moon. The lower gravity field affects soil-plate interaction due to low initial stress, and the ultra-high vacuum leads to an increase in internal friction and cohesion due to van der Waals forces.

## 6. Concluding Remarks
1. A bilinear line bounded by 1g can describe the gravity effect on gravity factors for ultimate bearing capacity. The slopes are different in low- and high-gravity fields, implying centrifuge tests (high-gravity) cannot be directly applied to predict lunar (low-gravity) behavior.
2. The particle velocity field provides a realistic affected zone showing dynamic balance between flowing out and moving downward.
3. High vacuum increases bearing capacity; the affected areas become deeper and wider with decreasing gravity in vacuum, whereas they become deeper and narrower in non-vacuum.
