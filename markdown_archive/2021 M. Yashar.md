# Building Information Modeling (BIM) Workflows for Construction Sequencing & 4D-Planning of 3D-Printed ISRU Surface Habitats

**M. Yashar, P. Michealsen, B. Hammond, J. Alvizar, C. Ciardullo, M. Morris, R. Pailes-Friedman, G. Bell, R. Tucker, T. Shen, S. Austin, L. LeBlanc**

## ABSTRACT
Future autonomously constructed surface habitats on the Moon and Mars will leverage additive manufacturing technologies to create airtight and durable structures. BIM workflows present significant value in the design and engineering of space systems, particularly habitat facilities including pre-integrated hardware (ECLS, airlocks, suitports). In 2019, team SEArch+/Apis Cor won first place in Virtual Construction (BIM) Level 2 within NASA’s Phase 3 Challenge for a 3D-Printed Habitat on Mars. This paper defines the landscape of BIM use and simulation as it applies to robotic deployment of space habitats.

## INTRODUCTION
Future long-duration missions will benefit from robotic precursor missions that autonomously construct infrastructure prior to crew arrival. Construction sequences must consider site conditions, ISRU feedstock quantities, power access, and environmental resources. SEArch+/Apis Cor merged BIM workflows between Rhino/Grasshopper, Autodesk Revit, Navisworks, and proprietary slicer software to estimate completion time and material quantities.

### BIM for Manufacturing and 3D-Printing
BIM enables the conversion of 3D geometry into CNC codes (G-code) for additive deposition mechanisms. While BIM is widely used in terrestrial AEC for planning, its application for real-time automated data collection and construction progress detection is still maturing. For space habitats, BIM must represent simplified geometries for additive manufacturing and integrate material extrusion through machine- and tool-paths.

## NASA’s Centennial Challenge: Mars X-House
The SEArch+ / Apis Cor submission (Mars X-House) prioritized radiation shielding and human-centered design. The competition required:
- Pressure-retaining living space of at least 93 m².
- Supporting four astronauts for one year.
- 4D construction sequence simulating machinery, AM, and component emplacement (ECLS, airlocks).
- BIM model Level of Development (LOD) 200 for MEP/ECLSS and LOD 400 for structural components.

### Software Workflow
- **Rhino/Grasshopper:** Parametric modeling of the regolith structural shell.
- **Autodesk Revit:** Interior design and assignment of material-based families.
- **Navisworks:** 4D construction sequencing, assigning model elements to schedule activities and calculating durations based on printer trajectory speed.
- **BIM 360:** Cloud-based collaboration and clash detection.

## CONCLUSION
Successful autonomous construction requires robotically placing wall apertures, interior partitions, and structural reinforcement. 3D-printed structural prototypes from the NASA challenge demonstrate that BIM can coordinate 3D-printing systems, ISRU regolith machinery, and space robotics within a unified construction sequence. Future research must address real-time "Dynamic BIM" or Digital Twins to handle communication delays and site deviations on Mars.
