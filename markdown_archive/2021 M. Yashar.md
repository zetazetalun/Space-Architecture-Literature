# Building Information Modeling (BIM) Workflows for Construction Sequencing & 4D-Planning of 3D-Printed ISRU Surface Habitats

**Authors:** M. Yashar, P. Michealsen, B. Hammond, J. Alvizar, C. Ciardullo, M. Morris, R. Pailes-Friedman, G. Bell, R. Tucker, T. Shen, S. Austin, L. LeBlanc  
**Publication:** Earth and Space 2021, ASCE, pp. 1394-1408.

## Abstract
Future autonomously constructed surface habitats on the Moon and Mars will leverage additive manufacturing technologies to create airtight structures. BIM workflows present significant value in the design and engineering of habitat facilities, including pre-integrated hardware such as ECLSS, airlocks, and suitports. 4D-simulation in BIM software facilitates mission planning for robotic habitat construction. This paper defines the landscape of BIM use for space habitats and describes the SEArch+/Apis Cor project's contribution to BIM workflows for an ISRU surface habitat.

## Introduction
Future long-duration exploration missions require robotic precursor missions to autonomously construct infrastructure prior to crew arrival. Construction sequences must consider site conditions, ISRU material quantities, feedstock storage, and power access. The SEArch+/Apis Cor team merged BIM workflows (Rhino/Grasshopper, Autodesk Revit, Navisworks) with proprietary slicer software to estimate completion time and material quantities.

### Uses of BIM in AEC
BIM enables holistic collection of linked building information and integration of models from shared databases. It shifts design from representative lines to building object elements embedded with metadata (material properties, quantity takeoff, assembly info).

### Challenges for Automated Construction
BIM alone is insufficient for directed planning of automated construction. There is a lack of specific software to represent the complexity of automated processes and streamline data acquisition for robotic control systems.

## NASA’s Centennial Challenge for a 3D-Printed Habitat on Mars
The challenge focused on habitat design and technology for large-scale additive construction using in-situ materials (regolith) and recyclables. 

### Virtual Construction Levels
Teams were asked to create BIM models with detailed information on materials and construction sequencing. The Mars X-House by SEArch+ / Apis Cor won first place in the Final Virtual Design level. It prioritizes radiation shielding while allowing natural light and supporting crew well-being.

### Requirements
*   Pressure-retaining living space: ≥ 93 m².
*   Support for 4 astronauts for one year.
*   BIM model and 4D construction sequence simulation.
*   Integration of ECLS, airlocks, suit hatches, view ports, and rover hatches.

### Software Workflow
*   **Autodesk BIM 360:** Cloud-based platform for collaboration.
*   **Rhino/Grasshopper:** Parametric modeling of the regolith structural shell.
*   **Autodesk Revit:** Interior design and family assignment for material usage.
*   **Autodesk Navisworks:** Creation of the 4D construction sequence by assigning elements to schedule activities.

## Findings & Results
*   **LOD Standards:** The submission required Level of Development (LOD) 200 for MEP/ECLSS and LOD 400 for structural/pressure-retaining walls.
*   **4D Sequencing:** Simulation demonstrated two mobile 3D-printers and mobile platforms coordinated to print the shell (regolith concrete) and interior structure (HDPE).
*   **Integration:** Successful representation of pre-integrated hardware (windows, mechanical core) within the automated construction workflow.

## Conclusion
Autonomous construction requires precise timing between 3D-printing and the robotic placement of pre-integrated modules (windows, airlocks, ECLS). Dynamic BIM frameworks updated in real-time with worksite events are essential for human-machine collaboration and error correction in Mars precursor missions.
