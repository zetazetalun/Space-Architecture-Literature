# Karst Cave as Terrestrial Simulation Platform to Test and Design Human Base in Lunar Lava Tube

## Abstract
Developing efficient approaches to building a suitable environment for humans on the moon plays a key role in future long-term sustainable lunar exploration activities. The lava tubes discovered by the Kaguya mission offer huge potential sites to host such bases. Through computation and analysis, we show that lunar lava tubes offer stable structures, suitable temperatures, low radiation doses, and low meteorite impact rates. We proposed using Earth’s karst caves to simulate extraterrestrial lava tubes, selected caves in Chongqing as the simulation site, and demonstrated feasibility from structural and environmental aspects. Research directions include cave sealing technology, efficient daylight systems, and internal circulation of artificial ecosystems.

## 1. Introduction
Extraterrestrial environments are extremely hostile. To survive long-term, humans need stable shelters. Lunar lava tubes, formed by volcanic activity, offer ideal habitats with hard basalt roofs and stable internal environments. Widths are expected to reach 1-2 km and lengths tens to hundreds of kilometers.

### 2.2. Safety Assessment
- **Radiation:** Depths >6m protect against cosmic rays/solar wind. Even <1m protects against solar flares.
- **Meteorites:** Tubes avoid surface impacts. The safety ratio of target thickness-to-crater diameter is <0.87.
- **Moon Dust:** Interior zones are protected from charged solar wind, reducing dust production.
- **Moon Quakes:** Lava tubes have survived millions of years of seismic activity.

## 3. Using a Karst Cave to Simulate Lunar Lava Tubes
Karst caves (e.g., in Chongqing) serve as analogs due to similar structural (semicircular) and environmental properties (isolated, stable temperature). 
- **Structural:** Chongqing caves (e.g., Wulong) can reach 77 km in length.
- **Environmental:** Temperatures range from 14 to 25°C. 
- **Insulation:** Caves require sealing. Preliminary work suggests extruded lunar soil has better sealing properties than sintered material.

## 4. Conclusion
Lunar lava tubes are superior habitat candidates due to low energy consumption and radiation protection. Terrestrial karst caves provide a platform to study sealing, daylighting, and autonomous construction using ISRU materials.
