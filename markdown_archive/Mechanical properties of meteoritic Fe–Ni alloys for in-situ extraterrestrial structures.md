# Mechanical properties of meteoritic Fe–Ni alloys for in-situ extraterrestrial structures\n\n**Authors:** Aaron A. Ahles, Jonathan D. Emery, David C. Dunand\n**Journal:** Acta Astronautica 189 (2021) 465–475\n\n## Abstract\nA critical requirement for outposts and settlements on the Moon and Mars is the use of in-situ resources to create pressurized, human-rated habitats with low mass, high reliability, and easy processability. This study assesses the capability of binary Fe–Ni alloys (5–11 wt% Ni), typical of M-type asteroids and iron meteorites. Strength increases linearly with Ni concentration while ductility decreases. A pressurized habitat (Quonset hut, 5 m radius) modeled via finite element analysis indicates that 10 mm thick Fe–7Ni plates can safely contain 1 bar of pressure on the Moon or Mars, requiring approximately 12–31 metric tons of material.\n\n## 1. Introduction\nExtraterrestrial settlements require materials with high tensile strength and toughness. Sintered regolith and concrete are weak in tension, making them unsuitable for thin pressurized shells. Iron-based alloys (steels) are excellent candidates due to their performance across cryogenic temperatures and availability via meteorites found on the Martian and Lunar surfaces. NASA rovers have identified multiple metallic meteorites on Mars, providing a ready-made source of pre-alloyed metal.\n\n## 2. Experimental Study\nBinary Fe-xNi alloys (x = 5, 7, 9, 11, 13 wt%) were arc-melted and studied in an as-cast condition to represent melted and cast meteorite material. Results and Discussion\n- **Microstructure:** Alloys showed a fine martensitic structure with grain sizes of ~90 μm.\n- **Temperature Dependence:** Yield stress increases steeply with decreasing temperature, fitted to an Arrhenius-like model. At Lunar night temperatures (102 K), yield stress for Fe-7Ni is predicted to exceed 1100 MPa.\n- **Structural Modeling:** A 'Quonset hut' habitat with 5 m radius and 10 m length was modeled. For a 1 bar internal atmosphere, 10 mm thick plates provide safety against plastic deformation.\n\n## 4. Conclusion\nFe–Ni meteorites can be collected, melted, and cast into metallic sheets for welding into pressurized structures. This method significantly reduces the mass and energy costs compared to shipping steel from Earth or complex oxide reduction processes in-situ.
