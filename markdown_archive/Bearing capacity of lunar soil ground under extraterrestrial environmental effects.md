# Bearing capacity of lunar soil ground under extraterrestrial environmental effects

**Authors:** Banglu Xi, Mingjing Jiang, Pinqiang Mo, Jiaqiang Yang, Zhenhua Zhang
**Journal:** Computers and Geotechnics 165 (2024) 105923
**Available online:** 12 November 2023

## Abstract
Lunar environment features a low gravity field and ultra-high vacuum which shows significant effects on the bearing behavior of lunar soil ground for future lunar habitation. Therefore, the 2D Discrete Element Method was employed to simulate the plate load tests under different gravity fields in non-vacuum and high-vacuum environments, analyzing the bearing behavior and soil response. The results show that a bilinear line bounded by 1g can be employed to describe the gravity effect on the gravity factors for ultimate bearing capacity and coefficient of subgrade reaction. The slopes are different in low and high-gravity fields, indicating that high-gravity effects in a centrifuge cannot be directly applied to predict behavior in a low-gravity field. 

## 1. Introduction
Lunar habitation plays an important role in the future of human society, serving as a supply center for in-situ resources (ISRU). From a geotechnical perspective, accurate understanding of lunar soil-tool interaction is vital for construction. The lunar environment differs from Earth due to lower gravity (1/6g) and ultra-high vacuum, which increases internal friction and cohesion through van der Waals forces.

## 4. Bearing Behavior

### 4.2 Ultimate Bearing Capacity
The ultimate bearing capacity ($q_u$) increases non-linearly with the gravity field. The increase is faster in low-gravity fields than in high-gravity fields. High vacuum conditions significantly increase $q_u$ compared to non-vacuum environments.

### 4.3 Coefficient of Subgrade Reaction
The coefficient $K$ (slope of the load-settlement curve) increases with gravity level. In high-vacuum environments, $K$ is larger than in non-vacuum environments due to higher deformation modulus.

## 6. Concluding Remarks
1. A bilinear line bounded by 1g describes gravity effects on factors for ultimate bearing capacity and subgrade reaction. Slopes differ in low and high-gravity fields.
2. Particle velocity fields show a dynamic balance between flowing out to the surface and moving downward (Hill mode).
3. In non-vacuum, the affected area becomes shallower and wider with decreasing gravity. In high-vacuum, it becomes deeper and wider with decreasing gravity level.
4. Lunar soil on the Moon can bear a larger mass than on Earth because the gravity level affects the lunar base weight more significantly than the bearing capacity reduction.
