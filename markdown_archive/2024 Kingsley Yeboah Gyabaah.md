# 3D printable regolith filled shape memory vitrimer composite for extraterrestrial application

## Abstract
This study investigates a neoteric approach in manufacturing lunar regolith-filled shape memory vitrimer (SMV) composites for extraterrestrial applications. A SMV with robust mechanical properties was combined with locally available lunar regolith to form a composite material. We explored conventional synthesis as well as 3D printing methods for manufacturing the composite. Compressive strength, flexural strength, and impact resistance of the composite were tested. A compressive strength of 96.0 MPa was recorded for composite with 40 wt% regolith ratio at room temperature. The 3D printed SMV-regolith composite using a liquid crystal display (LCD)-based printer exhibited a compressive and tensile strength of 139.16 MPa and 13.99 MPa, respectively, and a good shape memory effect. SMV regolith composite is a promising material for lunar base applications due to its simple manufacturing process, excellent mechanical properties, and low energy consumption.

## Introduction
Developing an independent extraterrestrial settlement has for decades been a staple of science fiction literature. Space agencies like NASA, ESA, and CNSA have all demonstrated a long-term commitment to lunar base construction. One of the biggest obstacles is the exorbitant cost of shipping raw materials from Earth. For this reason, in-situ resource utilization (ISRU) technology offers a potential remedy. Lunar regolith has the potential as a building material or as raw material for industrial processes.

## Materials and Methods
- **Binder:** Bisphenol A glycerolate dimethacrylate (BPAGMA).
- **Filler:** Lunar Regolith Simulant (LRS) from The Exolith Lab.
- **Manufacturing:** Conventional UV-curing and 3D printing (LCD-based vat photopolymerization using an Anycubic Photon Mono 3D Printer).

### Healing Efficiency
Healing efficiency reached up to 97.4% for 20% regolith composites based on crack initiation energy after the first healing cycle.

## Conclusions
A shape memory vitrimer regolith composite was successfully synthesized and tested. The material exhibits high mechanical strength, self-healing capabilities, and recyclability. LCD-based 3D printing was demonstrated as a feasible method for complex geometries, supporting the potential for autonomous lunar construction.
