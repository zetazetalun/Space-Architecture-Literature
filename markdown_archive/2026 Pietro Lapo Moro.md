# Mars habitat: A holistic approach to design extraterrestrial structures under extreme environments

**Authors:** Pietro Lapo Moro, Klajdi Toska, Mariano Angelo Zanini, Maurizio Pajola, Flora Faleschini
**Journal:** Acta Astronautica 248 (2026) 370–390
**DOI:** 10.1016/j.actaastro.2026.06.008

## Abstract
Martian exploration represents one of the most ambitious goals of the 21st century. This paper proposes a new Martian habitat designed through a rational and scientific approach. A new framework for extraterrestrial habitats design is conceived and applied to Mars. Environmental aspects such as reduced gravity, seismic activity, radiation, wind, and asteroid impacts are identified. A review of locally-available construction materials and additive manufacturing methods is provided. Finally, a new habitat module called 'Erethea' is proposed, consisting of an external 3D printed shell verified via finite element (FE) analysis and inner inflatable modules.

## 1. Introduction
The use of in-situ materials, such as Martian regolith-based composites, is attractive for reducing mass and cost. Initiatives like the NASA 3D-Printed Habitat Challenge have promoted additive manufacturing but often lacked a comprehensive integrated structural design process.

### 1.1 Methodological Framework
The proposed framework follows a sequential yet iterative bottom-up approach: planetary characterization -> mission/site definition -> load identification -> architectural configuration -> material selection -> structural verification.

## 3. Mars Habitat Design
### 3.2 Loads Definition
*   **Wind Load:** Negligible for structural systems due to low atmospheric density, though dust storms affect solar panels.
*   **Asteroid Impacts:** Probability of impact on a 100 m² habitat is extremely low (~0.01% in 50 years).
*   **Radiation:** Shielding must reduce cumulative dose below 600 mSv (NASA limit). A minimum thickness of 1.5 m of regolith/marscrete is identified as sufficient.
*   **Seismic Load:** A ground motion attenuation law was calibrated using InSight data for 21 marsquakes.
*   **Thermal Load:** Diurnal fluctuations (up to 150K range) induce thermal stresses.

### 3.3 Material and Construction Technique Selection
Sulfur concrete (sulfurcrete) is proposed. 
*   **Composition:** Optimal mix is 35-50% sulfur with Martian regolith.
*   **Properties:** Compressive strength of 48-63 MPa; rapid setting within 24h.
*   **Construction:** High-temperature extrusion/3D printing.

## 4. Case Study: ERETHEA Mars Habitat
### 4.4 Architecture
*   **External Shell:** 1.5 m thick sulfurcrete shell, 25.8 m total diameter.
*   **Internal Modules:** Deployable inflatable structures with a 5-layer stratigraphy (AO cover, MLI, MMOD shielding, bladders, restraint layer).
*   **Layout:** Central dome with a surrounding toroidal ring. Modules include EM1 (3m), EM2 (5m), and EM3 (7m).

### 4.5 Structural Analysis
*   **Software:** Strand7 (FE analysis).
*   **Findings:** The structure works predominantly in compression. Max compressive stress (0.696 MPa) is significantly lower than material strength (19.5 MPa design value). Thermal wave penetration depth is only 4.78 cm, protecting the interior.

## 5. Conclusion
The paper concludes that life on Mars is technically conceivable using ISRU materials and established engineering frameworks. The 'Erethea' design demonstrates that a multidisciplinary approach can overcome extreme environmental constraints.
