# Mars habitat: A holistic approach to design extraterrestrial structures under extreme environments

**Authors:** Pietro Lapo Moro, Klajdi Toska, Mariano Angelo Zanini, Maurizio Pajola, Flora Faleschini
**Journal:** Acta Astronautica 248 (2026) 370–390
**DOI:** 10.1016/j.actaastro.2026.06.008

## Abstract
Martian exploration represents one of the most ambitious goals of the 21st century. This paper proposes a new Martian habitat designed through a rational and scientific approach, integrating engineering, material science, and geology. A framework for extraterrestrial habitat design is applied to the case of Mars, identifying environmental aspects like reduced gravity, seismic activity, radiation, wind, and asteroid impacts. The study explores locally available construction materials (ISRU) and additive manufacturing. The final proposal consists of an external 3D-printed shell (sulfurcrete) and inner inflatable-balloon modules.

## 1. Introduction
Key focuses include In-Situ Resource Utilization (ISRU) and Martian regolith-based composites to reduce the mass and cost of transported materials. The NASA 3D-Printed Habitat Challenge (NMHC) is noted as an important milestone, though this paper seeks to provide a more comprehensive structural design process integrating environmental loads and material behavior.

## 2. Methodological Framework
The design follows a sequential, iterative workflow:
1. Scientific background development (Mars environment).
2. Mission type and landing site definition.
3. Habitat concept exploration (architectural forms/volumes).
4. Material selection (mechanical performance, ISRU availability).
5. Structural verification (FE analysis under Martian loads).

## 3. Case Study: ERETHEA Mars Habitat

### 3.1 Material: Sulfurcrete (Marscrete)
- **Composition:** 50% sulfur (binder) and 50% Martian regolith simulant.
- **Properties:** High compressive strength (48–63 MPa), rapid setting (24h), and chemical/freeze-thaw resistance.
- **Production:** Involves heating sulfur to 120°C and mixing with preheated regolith (~170°C). Requires pressurized/insulated robotic systems for 3D printing.

### 3.2 Design Configuration
- **External Shell:** 3D-printed sulfurcrete, 1.5m thick for radiation shielding.
- **Internal Structure:** Modular inflatable units (Class II) made of multilayer softgoods (MLI, MMOD shielding, redundant bladders).
- **Geometry:** Central dome connected to a surrounding toroidal ring. Total external diameter: ~25.8 m.

### 3.3 Environmental Actions and Loads
| Load Type | Design Consideration | Result/Finding |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Gravity** | Design-driving | 3.73 m/s² used for dead load calculation. |
| **Wind** | Not design-driving | Negligible dynamic pressure due to thin atmosphere (~1% of Earth's). |
| **Seismic** | Design-driving | PGA of 1.14 m/s² (0.31 Martian g) assumed for a 6.0 Mw event. |
| **Radiation** | Design-driving | 1.5m thick shell reduces annual dose to <50 mSv. |
| **Asteroids** | Not design-driving | Probability of significant impact over 50 years is ~6.4e-12. |
| **Thermal** | Design-driving | Large diurnal fluctuations (150K to 305K) induce critical thermal stresses. |

## 4. Conclusion
The study confirms the feasibility of establishing a human outpost on Mars using ISRU materials. The Erethea habitat design demonstrates structural integrity under Martian seismic and thermal loads, provided that a holistic design approach considering planetary characterization is followed.
