# Lunar active seismic profiler for investigating shallow substrates of the Moon and other extraterrestrial environments

## Authors
Takeshi Tsuji, Taizo Kobayashi, Junji Kinoshita, Tatsunori Ikeda, Tomoki Uchigaki, Yuichiro Nagata, Taichi Kawamura, Kazunori Ogawa, Satoshi Tanaka, Akito Araya

## Abstract
To investigate the structures and properties of shallow material in extraterrestrial environments, we have developed a lunar active seismic profiler (LASP), which integrates active seismic sources and receivers in a short (~1 m) array that can be deployed on a rover. The small active source generates a chirp waveform (20–250 Hz). By processing data, the authors obtained an accurate S-wave velocity profile from surface to 1 m depth. Results suggest subsurface ice (~0.5 wt%) can be identified by anomalously high S-wave velocity. The LASP also detected different degrees of compaction, providing critical information for constructing base camps and large installations on the Moon.

## 1. Introduction
Seismic studies are vital for understanding planetary interiors. While Apollo and InSight relied mostly on passive data, active-source surveys can obtain subsurface information quickly. Information about regolith distribution and bedrock depth is critical for constructing base camps and developing large human-occupied rovers or telescopes.

## 2. Lunar Active Seismic Profiler (LASP)
- **Design**: Rover-transportable system with active sources and multiple receivers on a <1 m array.
- **Depth Range**: Designed for shallow (<1 m) surface material.
- **Seismic Sources**: Motor-based portable active seismic source (PASS) generating chirp waveforms (20–250 Hz).
- **Receivers**: Array of eight geophones coupled to the ground by their own weight (~100 g each), suspended by strings to prevent frame vibration interference.

## 3. Surface Wave Analysis
Uses Multi-channel Analysis of Surface Wave (MASW) and improved CWT-MASW (Continuous Wavelet Transform) to calculate dispersion curves and estimate S-wave velocity structure.

## 4. Results and Interpretation
### 4.1 Depth Profile of S-wave Velocity
Field tests at a sand beach (lunar proxy) showed clear surface waves. S-wave velocity profiles were inverted with an accuracy of ±5 m/s.
### 4.2 Lab Comparison
Demonstrated that geophones coupled by weight on the surface (lunar style) perform comparably to buried geophones.
### 4.3 3D S-wave Velocity Structure
Experiments detected spatial variations in compaction. Lane A (least compacted) showed ~65 m/s at 0.1m depth, while Lane B/D/E (compacted) showed ~85–100 m/s.

## 5. Discussion and Summary
- **Ice Identification**: Sensitivity is sufficient to detect 0.5 wt% ice saturation (~40 m/s velocity anomaly).
- **Construction Application**: LASP can map lithological boundaries and degree of consolidation, essential for rover design and base camp planning.
- **Scaling**: For deeper exploration (>1 m), multiple rovers can be used to create long-offset seismic arrays.
