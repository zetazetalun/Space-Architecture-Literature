# Lunar active seismic profiler for investigating shallow substrates of the Moon and other extraterrestrial environments\n\n**Authors:** Takeshi Tsuji, Taizo Kobayashi, Junji Kinoshita, Tatsunori Ikeda, Tomoki Uchigaki, Yuichiro Nagata, Taichi Kawamura, Kazunori Ogawa, Satoshi Tanaka, Akito Araya\n\n**Published in:** Icarus 404 (2023) 115666\n\n## Abstract\nTo investigate the structures and properties of shallow material in extraterrestrial environments, we have developed a lunar active seismic profiler (LASP), which integrates active seismic sources and receivers in a short (~1 m) array that can be deployed on a rover. The small active source generates a chirp waveform (20–250 Hz). By processing shot gather of the LASP data, we calculated a dispersion curve of surface waves and obtained an accurate S-wave velocity profile from surface to 1 m depth. Results suggest subsurface ice (~0.5 wt%) can be identified by anomalously high S-wave velocity. Field experiments demonstrate that the LASP can support estimates of 3D S-wave velocity distributions and identification of buried geological boundaries and degrees of compaction.\n\n## 1. Introduction\nActive-source seismic surveys can obtain subsurface information in a short time. Shallow substrates are important targets because they may contain ice deposits used as a resource. Distribution of regolith and depth to bedrock are critical information for constructing base camps. Measurements of regolith properties (compaction) in situ are crucial for large rovers and telescopes.\n\n## 2. Lunar Active Seismic Profiler (LASP)\n- **Array Length:** <1 m.\n- **Depth Range:** Surface to 1 m.\n- **Seismic Source:** Portable active seismic source (PASS) using a motor to rotate an eccentric mass to generate a chirp waveform (20-250 Hz).\n- **Receivers:** Array of eight geophones (~100 g each) suspended by thin strings to decouple from the frame.\n\n## 3. Surface Wave Analysis\nMulti-channel analysis of surface waves (MASW) and continuous wavelet transform (CWT-MASW) are used to estimate S-wave velocity structure. The S-wave velocity of shallow cover sediment was typically <120 m/s for unsaturated material.\n\n## 4. Results\n- **Accuracy:** S-wave velocity estimated with accuracy of ±5 m/s.\n- **Ice Detection:** S-wave velocity increases by ~40 m/s with 0.5 wt% ice presence.\n- **Compaction:** Field tests detected gradients in S-wave velocities (65 m/s to 100 m/s at 0.1 m depth) corresponding to different degrees of soil compaction from a motorized roller.\n\n## 5. Summary\nLASP images shallow subsurface with high accuracy. It can detect 3D subsurface structures, lithological boundaries, and compaction levels, which are vital for designing rovers and base camp infrastructure.
