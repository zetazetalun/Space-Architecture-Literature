# 3D-printed Lunar regolith simulant-based geopolymer composites with bio-inspired sandwich architectures

**Journal:** Journal of Advanced Ceramics  
**Date:** 2023, 12(3): 510–525  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.26599/JAC.2023.9220700

## Abstract
Over time, natural materials have evolved to be lightweight, high-strength, tough, and damage-tolerant due to their unique biological structures. This study reports the application of an ink-based 3D printing strategy to the structural design of a Lunar regolith simulant-based geopolymer (HIT-LRS-1 GP). High-precision carbon fiber/quartz sand-reinforced biomimetic patterns inspired by the cellular sandwich structure of plant stems were fabricated. The results showed that these architectures exhibited stable non-catastrophic fracture characteristics and significant anisotropy of mechanical properties. Compressive strengths of the honeycomb, triangular, wave, and rectangular sandwich patterns reached up to 46.7 MPa in the Z-axis direction.

## 1. Introduction
Lunar exploration has increased the demand for in situ resource utilization (ISRU) to minimize transportation costs. Geopolymers are potential alternatives to cement as they can be synthesized from Lunar regolith with minimal Earth-delivered materials. This study aims to overcome the inherent brittleness of geopolymers by combining structural design (biomimetic sandwich architectures) and fiber reinforcement.

## 2. Materials and Methods

### 2.1 Preparation of Lunar regolith simulant
The simulant (HIT-LRS-1) was designed to match the mineral composition of the Maria terrain of the Moon, similar to JSC-1A. It consists of basalt powder (40 wt%), plagioclase (20 wt%), forsterite (10 wt%), and other oxides.

### 2.2 3D printing of Geopolymer Inks
Inks were prepared using chopped carbon fiber (600 μm length) and quartz sand as reinforcements in the geopolymer matrix. Direct Ink Writing (DIW) was used with a 0.82 mm nozzle diameter.

### 2.3 Design of biomimetic structures
Four regular cellular sandwich architectures were constructed, inspired by plant rhizomes (e.g., *Elytrigia repens*):
1. Honeycomb
2. Triangular
3. Wave
4. Rectangular

## 3. Results and Discussion

### 3.1 Rheological properties
The addition of quartz sand and carbon fiber significantly increased the initial viscosity and yield stress (τ₀) of the ink, improving printability and shape retention. The 1Csf/20QS@HIT-LRS-1 GP ink showed a yield stress of ~8628 Pa.

### 3.2 Mechanical Properties
- **Flexural Strength:** Reached 41.6 MPa with 1.00 vol% carbon fiber reinforcement (a 50.7% increase over the non-reinforced composite).
- **Compressive Strength (Y-axis vs Z-axis):** All structures showed higher strength in the Z-axis (parallel to the printing direction).

| Structure | Y-axis Strength (MPa) | Z-axis Strength (MPa) | Max Fracture Strain (Y/Z) |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| Honeycomb | 15.6 | 46.7 | 10.2% / 12.1% |
| Triangular | 17.9 | 26.5 | 6.7% / 13.7% |
| Wave | 11.3 | 23.8 | 5.8% / 13.6% |
| Rectangular | 20.1 | 34.4 | 5.9% / 13.9% |

## 4. Conclusions
The study successfully demonstrated the DIW of Lunar regolith-based geopolymer composites. The biomimetic architectures provided a pseudo-plastic fracture characteristic, preventing catastrophic failure. The honeycomb sandwich pattern exhibited the best overall performance in the Z-axis, making these composites robust candidates for Lunar base construction.
