# Mechanical properties of meteoritic Fe–Ni alloys for in-situ extraterrestrial structures\n\n## ABSTRACT\nA critical requirement for outposts and settlements on the Moon and Mars is the use of in-situ resources to create pressurized, human-rated habitats with low mass (i.e., high tensile strength), high reliability (i.e., high toughness) and easy processability. This study assesses the capability of binary Fe–Ni alloys - with Ni content of 5–11 wt%, typical of M-type asteroids and iron meteorites - to meet these requirements. We measure the hardness and tensile properties of these Fe–Ni alloys at 21 °C in the as-cast state, with grain sizes of ~90 μm. As the Ni concentration increases from 5 to 11 wt%, strength increases near linearly: from 260 to 440 MPa for the yield stress, and from 340 to 560 MPa for the ultimate tensile strength. Ductility however decreases, also near-linearly, from 20 to 3%. The yield stress of Fe-(5,7,9)Ni alloys increases steeply with decreasing temperature. A pressurized habitat consisting of flat plates of Fe–Ni alloy welded into a half cylinder is modeled via finite element analysis. For a ~400 m3 habitat with a 1 bar atm on the Moon or Mars, the thickness of the Fe–7Ni plates can be as low as 10 mm to prevent plastic deformation.\n\n## 1. Finite element modeling of half-cylindrical pressurized habitats\nFeasibility studies via finite element analysis (using COMSOL) simulated a habitat suitable for human occupancy on the Moon or Mars, subjected to high tensile stresses due to internal pressurization.\n\n## 4. Results and discussion / 5. Conclusions\nFe–Ni alloys with 5–11 wt% Ni show mechanical properties well suited for tensile load-bearing applications. A plate thickness of 10 mm is required to remain safely below the yield stress of cast Fe–7Ni despite stress concentrations at welds. The 12 MT mass of the metallic shell requires 20–30 iron-nickel meteorites (~500 kg mass).
