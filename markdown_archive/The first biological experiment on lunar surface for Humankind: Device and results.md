# The first biological experiment on lunar surface for Humankind: Device and results

## Abstract
The Bioregenerative Life Support System (BLSS) is an indispensable part of extraterrestrial bases. The Chinese Chang’e 4 lunar lander carried a micro-ecosystem called Biological Experiment Payload (BEP) to assess the effects of low gravity, intense radiation, and light on the lunar surface. The payload contained cotton, potato, Arabidopsis, and rape seeds, fly eggs, and yeast. Cotton seed germination occurred in 22 h on the Moon compared to 53 h on Earth. The experiment demonstrated that plants could grow on the lunar surface even under intense radiation and 1/6 g gravity.

## 1. Introduction
Building a lunar base requires self-sufficiency due to high transportation costs. BLSS is a critical technology for supplying food, oxygen, and water. While terrestrial facilities like BIOS-3 and Biosphere-2 have demonstrated material cycle closure, the effect of the real lunar environment (1/6 gravity, radiation) on these systems remained uncertain until the BEP experiment.

## 2. The Biological Experiment Payload (BEP)
### 2.1. Specifications
- **Weight:** 2.608 kg
- **Dimensions:** 173 mm (diameter) × 198 mm (height)
- **Internal Volume:** 0.82 L (0.42 L bio-activity space)
- **Shell Material:** Aluminum
- **Seal Leakage Rate:** < 1.9 × 10⁻⁵ Pa·cm²/s

### 2.2. Components
- **Biological Seedbed:** Contained Brassica napus, Potato, Cotton, and Arabidopsis seeds; Drosophila (fly eggs); and Saccharomyces (yeast).
- **Substrate:** Vermiculite.
- **Watering System:** 18 ml water storage; used heated petroleum jelly as a valve to release water.
- **Lighting:** Natural sunlight guided via light-guiding fibers and a 10 mm diameter hole.
- **Thermal Control:** Multilayer insulation and thermoelectric coolers (TEC) to manage temperatures between -52 °C and 38 °C.

## 3. Results of biological experiments on the lunar surface
- **Landing Site:** Von Kármán crater (back of the Moon).
- **Timeline:** Experiment began January 3, 2019. 
- **Germination:** Cotton seed germinated 22 h after watering (vs 53 h on Earth).
- **Morphology:** Lunar seedlings had curved stems, fat streamlined nodes close to the substrate, and curled leaves.
- **Survival:** Seedlings survived a lunar night (temperatures reaching -52 °C) and remained green, whereas ground-sync controls died/blackened under simulated conditions.
- **Total Operation:** 1300 h over five lunar days.

## 4. Discussion and ISRU Perspectives
The experiment proved cultivation on the Moon is possible. Future research must address:
- Improving lunar soil for cultivation.
- Utilizing lunar rocks as construction materials.
- Using lunar lava tube caves to build bases.
- Producing oxygen and water from lunar minerals.

## 5. Conclusion
The BEP was the first human-led biological experiment on the lunar surface. Despite limitations in weight and power, the successful germination of cotton proves that a micro-controlled ecosystem can survive the lunar environment.
