# Building a lunar base with 3D printing

**Published:** January 31, 2013  
**Source:** Phys.org (via ESA)

Setting up a lunar base could be made much simpler by using a 3D printer to build it from local materials. Industrial partners including renowned architects Foster + Partners have joined with ESA to test the feasibility of 3D printing using lunar soil.

## Project Overview
Terrestrial 3D printing technology has produced entire structures. This industrial team investigated if it could similarly be employed to build a lunar habitat. 

### Design Concept
Foster + Partners devised a weight-bearing 'catenary' dome design featuring:
- **Structure:** A cellular structured wall designed to shield against micrometeoroids and space radiation.
- **Weight-to-Strength Ratio:** A hollow closed-cell structure reminiscent of bird bones.
- **Internal Environment:** Incorporates a pressurized inflatable to shelter astronauts.

### Construction Process
1. **Internal Setup:** A pressurized inflatable dome is assembled.
2. **External Protection:** Robots cover the inflated dome with a layer of 3D-printed lunar regolith.
3. **Printing Technology:** The UK’s Monolite supplied the **D-Shape printer**, featuring a mobile 6m frame with a printing array of nozzles.
4. **Material Process:** 
   - Simulated lunar material is mixed with magnesium oxide to create a 'paper-like' substrate.
   - A binding salt (structural 'ink') is applied, converting the material into a stone-like solid.

## Key Technical Findings
- **Demonstration:** A 1.5 tonne building block was produced as a proof-of-concept.
- **Speed:** Current printers build at approximately 2m per hour; next-generation designs aim for 3.5m per hour (enabling a full building in a week).
- **Vacuum Performance:** Testing by Alta SpA and Scuola Superiore Sant'Anna confirmed that by inserting the printer nozzle beneath the regolith layer, 2mm-scale liquid droplets stay trapped by capillary forces, preventing the liquid from boiling away in a vacuum.
- **Material Simulant:** Basaltic rock from a volcano in central Italy was identified as a 99.8% match for lunar soil.

## Challenges and Future Work
- **Temperature Control:** 3D printing works best at room temperature. Lunar temperatures fluctuate wildly, suggesting the lunar poles may be the most suitable locations due to moderate temperature ranges.
- **Lunar Dust:** Further study is required regarding the management of hazardous lunar dust and thermal factors.
