# The Multiplanetary Future of Plant Synthetic Biology

**Authors:** Briardo Llorente, Thomas C. Williams, and Hugh D. Goold
**Journal:** Genes (MDPI)
**Published:** 10 July 2018
**DOI:** 10.3390/genes9070348

## Abstract
The interest in human space journeys to distant planets and moons has been re-ignited, with plans for manned missions to Mars in the near future. Plants could play a critical role in generating oxygen, fixing carbon, and recycling waste and water, as well as providing food and biomass feedstock for microbial manufacture. However, plants evolved for Earth's biosphere and will not perform optimally in extraterrestrial habitats. The construction or transportation of plant growth facilities and the availability of resources like sunlight and water are limiting factors. This paper discusses bioengineering endeavors to enable plants to thrive in a Martian greenhouse and proposes a roadmap for adapting life to Mars.

## 1. Taking Full Advantage of Plants on Extraterrestrial Human Outposts
Developing robust technologies to enable sustainable long-duration human operations in space is paramount. Plants contribute to bioregenerative life support systems (BLSS) by supplying $O_2$, fixing $CO_2$, and recycling waste/water ($H_2O$), while providing food and precursors for medicines and materials. On-site utilization increases self-sufficiency and reduces logistical costs of resupply from Earth.

### Challenges for Extraterrestrial Farming:
- **Limited Inputs:** Sunlight intensity on Mars is ~43% of Earth's.
- **Environmental Hazards:** UV and cosmic radiation.
- **Controlled Conditions:** Need for temperature, humidity, and pressure maintenance in greenhouses.
- **Resource Extraction:** Water is primarily available as ice, requiring energy for extraction.

## 2. Refactoring Plants for Enhanced Performance on Mars
Synthetic biology provides the means to outpace evolution for Martian conditions. Areas of focus include:

### 2.1. Enhancing Photosynthesis and Photoprotection
- **Spectral Coverage:** Engineering plants to access UV or infrared regions for energy.
- **UV Tolerance:** Developing superior protection mechanisms for UV-transparent greenhouses.
- **Carbon Fixation:** Exploiting the high $CO_2/O_2$ ratio on Mars for more effective Rubisco activity.

### 2.2. Improving Drought and Cold Tolerance
- **Water Efficiency:** Manipulating stomatal pores or engineering crassulacean acid metabolism (CAM).
- **Cryoprotection:** Expression of ice-binding proteins and synthetic circadian regulation to anticipate Martian night temperature drops.

### 2.3. High Yield and Functional Food
- **Architecture:** Redesigning root systems for optimal nutrient acquisition and altering plant architecture for high-density cultivation in limited greenhouse space.
- **Biofortification:** Improving nutritional properties and quality traits.

## 3. Tailoring Microorganisms to Complement Plant Life
Microbes are essential for removing toxic compounds from Martian soil (regolith) and converting biomass into materials, chemicals, and medicines.

### 3.1. Conditioning Martian Soil
- **Perchlorate Reduction:** Martian soil contains high levels of perchlorates, which are toxic. Engineered bacteria can reduce perchlorates, detoxifying soil and potentially releasing water.
- **Desiccation Mitigation:** Bacteria engineered to produce extracellular polysaccharides to bind soil particles.

## 4. A Roadmap: The Mars Biofoundry
A 'Mars Biofoundry' is proposed as an automated and versatile platform for high-throughput phenotyping and engineering of biological systems under simulated Martian conditions. This includes a design-build-test cycle to accelerate the development of high-performing plants and microbes.

## 5. Conclusion
Efforts to adapt life to Mars will lead to advancements in food security, environmental protection, and agricultural productivity on Earth.
