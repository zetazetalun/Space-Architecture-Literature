# Lunar regolith simulant-based triboelectric nanogenerators: Toward sustainable energy harvesting from resources on the moon

## Abstract
The exploration of extraterrestrial materials for energy harvesting, generation and storage is important for futuristic material evolution and use. In this work, we introduce a lightweight, flexible triboelectric nanogenerator (TENG) that uses lunar regolith simulant particles (LRPs) embedded in polydimethoxysilane (PDMS) to harvest mechanical energy. The optimized device containing 30 wt % of ≤ 45 µm LRPs yields an open-circuit voltage Voc of ~10.5 V, a short-circuit current Isc of ~2.2 µA, and a peak power density of 3.0 µW cm⁻² under a force of 2.5 N at 10 Hz. The device shows 95% performance retention after 36,000 cycles, offering insight for future space materials employed in composite design.

## 1. Introduction
Establishing sustainable power sources on the lunar surface is crucial for long-term missions like lunar bases and resource utilization. TENGs rely on the triboelectric effect and offer a scalable, low-cost solution for converting mechanical energy into electricity, which is particularly useful during long lunar nights when solar power is limited.

## 2. Results and Discussions
- **Material Composition**: The LRPs used are 75% Lunar Highlands (LHS-1) simulant and 25% anorthosite agglutinates.
- **Optimization**: Voltage output improves with finer grain sizes. The PDMS/LRPs-45 (≤ 45 µm) showed the highest output due to increased surface area and interfacial contact.
- **Performance**: Optimal loading was found at 30 wt %. Higher concentrations (e.g., 70%) led to overcrowding and reduced effective surface contact.
- **Robustness**: The device was tested against 'stone showering' (simulating mechanical impacts like meteor showers), maintaining stable output.
- **Applications**: Successfully powered a light-emitting diode (LED) after rectification.

## 3. Conclusion
This work demonstrates the feasibility of using LRPs as a dopant in PDMS films to improve TENG performance for mechanical energy harvesting on the lunar surface. The study underscores the potential for resource-driven energy strategies to support sustainable space exploration.
