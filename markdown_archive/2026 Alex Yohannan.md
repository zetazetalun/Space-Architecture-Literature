# Lunar regolith simulant-based triboelectric nanogenerators: Toward sustainable energy harvesting from resources on the moon

**Journal:** Applied Materials Today (2026)
**Authors:** Alex Yohannan, Jayraj V. Vaghasiya, Keval K. Sonigara, Martin Pumera
**DOI:** 10.1016/j.apmt.2025.103071

## Abstract
The exploration of extraterrestrial materials for energy harvesting and storage is vital for future material evolution. This work introduces a lightweight, flexible triboelectric nanogenerator (TENG) using lunar regolith simulant particles (LRPs) embedded in polydimethoxysilane (PDMS) to harvest mechanical energy. Optimized devices containing 30 wt% of ≤ 45 µm LRPs yielded an open-circuit voltage (Voc) of ~10.5 V, a short-circuit current (Isc) of ~2.2 µA, and a peak power density of 3.0 µW cm⁻². The device showed 95% performance retention after 36,000 operation cycles, demonstrating potential for sustainable energy harvesting in lunar environments.

## 1. Introduction
Establishing sustainable power sources on the lunar surface is crucial for long-term missions like lunar bases. While solar panels are effective, they are limited during the 14-day lunar nights. TENGs offer a promising alternative by converting mechanical energy (vibrations, human motion, or even meteor showers) into electricity using on-site resources.

## 2. Results and Discussions
### 2.1 Device Design
- **Active Layer:** LRPs (Lunar Highlands LHS-1 simulant with anorthosite agglutinates) embedded in a PDMS matrix.
- **Electrodes:** Copper (Cu) and Aluminum (Al) foil current collectors.
- **Substrate:** 3D-printed polylactic acid (PLA) support.
- **Operation Mode:** Contact-separation mode facilitated by a polyurethane foam spacer.

### 2.2 Optimization of Performance
- **Grain Size Effect:** Finer grain sizes (≤ 45 µm and ≤ 63 µm) resulted in higher voltage outputs due to increased surface area and better interfacial contact.
- **Concentration Effect:** The 30 wt% LRP composition was found to be optimal. Higher concentrations (e.g., 70 wt%) led to overcrowding and reduced effective contact area, decreasing output to ~5 V.
- **Force and Frequency:** Voltage output increases with external force (up to 12.5 V at 5 N) and higher frequencies (tested up to 10 Hz).

### 2.3 Durability and Practical Application
- **Stability:** The device maintained performance over 36,000 cycles with minor fluctuations.
- **Mechanical Robustness:** Successfully underwent bending, twisting, and stretching tests.
- **Simulation:** Demonstrated energy harvesting from simulated 'stone showers' (dropping stones onto the device), yielding up to 27 V with varying weights.

## 3. Conclusion
Lunar regolith particles serve as effective triboelectric fillers. This proof-of-concept demonstrates that lunar soil can be utilized for on-site energy generation, supporting future long-duration missions and resource-constrained environments.

## 4. Experimental Section
- **Materials:** SYLGARD 184 silicone elastomer; LHS-1 Lunar Highlands Simulant.
- **Fabrication:** PDMS and LRPs were mixed (10:1 curing ratio), degassed, and cast into 3D-printed molds using a doctor blade technique, then cured at 80°C.
