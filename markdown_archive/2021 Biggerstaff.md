# Determining the yield stress of a Biopolymer-bound Soil Composite for extrusion-based 3D printing applications

**Authors:** Adrian Biggerstaff, Gerald Fuller, Michael Lepech, David Loftus  
**Published in:** Construction and Building Materials 305 (2021) 124730  
**Published date:** 8 September 2021

## Abstract
Biopolymer-bound Soil Composite (BSC) is a novel class of construction materials with potential use in extraterrestrial infrastructure development and more sustainable construction on Earth. Little is known about the rheological properties of this new material and its suitability for extrusion-based 3D printing (E3DP). This paper presents methods for testing BSC yield stress and proposes hydrodynamic and frictional yield stress models for predicting the static and dynamic yield stress of BSC mixtures. Using experimental results, a soil volume fraction range for BSC is identified that may be suitable for E3DP based on extrudability and shape stability requirements.

## 1. Introduction
Extrusion-based 3D printing (E3DP) has emerged as a viable alternative to form-based construction. For E3DP, material rheology is critical; materials must have a low enough yield stress to be pumped/extruded but high enough yield stress to maintain shape stability after deposition. 

BSC mixtures are being explored as sustainable alternatives to cement. Specifically, BSC has potential for extraterrestrial construction (Moon/Mars) through In Situ Resource Utilization (ISRU). This paper explores BSC mixtures consisting of lunar regolith simulant (JSC-1A), bovine blood proteins (binder), and deionized water.

## 2. BSC-E3DP mixture design considerations
BSC strength originates from biopolymer cross-links binding soil particles. Designing for E3DP requires balancing dried state compressive strength with wet state pumpability. This paper limits the BSC soil volume fraction (\phi_{BSC}) to <80% of the soil's maximum packing fraction (\phi_{mp}) to ensure pumpability.

## 5. Conclusion
This paper found that BSC using low-cohesion soil is suitable for E3DP within a soil volume fraction range of 0.435 ≤ ϕ_{BSC} ≤ 0.548. The study presented hydrodynamic and frictional models as a method for determining static (τ_{sys}) and dynamic (τ_{dys}) yield stress, which can aid designers in assessing the printability and shape stability of BSC for extraterrestrial habitats.

***

## Key Findings
1. Biopolymer-bound Soil Composites (BSC) using lunar regolith simulant and blood protein are viable for 3D printing within a specific soil volume fraction range (0.435–0.548).
2. The research establishes that thixotropy in these materials is primarily caused by particle reconsolidation rather than chemical bonding, with a regime transition from hydrodynamic to frictional behavior occurring at ϕ_{BSC} = 0.52.
3. The study provides predictive mathematical models for static and dynamic yield stress to ensure material pumpability and structural shape stability in lunar construction scenarios.

## Findings and Results
- **Findings**: The researchers developed and validated two rheological models: a hydrodynamic model (Chateau-Ovarlez-Trung) and a frictional model (De Larrard). They identified that the transition from a hydrodynamic-dominated regime to a frictional-dominated regime occurs at a soil volume fraction of 0.52.
- **Result**: A printable soil volume fraction range for lunar regolith BSC was determined to be 0.435 to 0.548. Static yield stress values for the tested mixtures ranged from 46 Pa to 3644 Pa. Extrusion was limited to mixtures where the soil volume fraction was less than 80% of the maximum packing fraction (ϕ_{mp} = 0.689 for JSC-1A).
