# 3D-printed Lunar regolith simulant-based geopolymer composites with bio-inspired sandwich architectures

**Journal:** Journal of Advanced Ceramics  
**Year:** 2023, 12(3): 510–525  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.26599/JAC.2023.9220700

## Abstract
This study reports the application of an ink-based 3D printing strategy to create Lunar regolith simulant-based geopolymer (HIT-LRS-1 GP) composites. Reinforced with carbon fiber and quartz sand, biomimetic patterns inspired by plant stems (honeycomb, triangular, wave, and rectangular sandwich structures) were fabricated to address the inherent brittleness of geopolymers. The results demonstrate that these architectures provide non-catastrophic fracture characteristics, effective damage tolerance, and high compressive strength, making them suitable candidates for lunar habitat construction.

## 1. Introduction
Lunar base construction necessitates In Situ Resource Utilization (ISRU) to minimize transportation costs. Geopolymers are identified as potential alternatives to cement due to their low density and thermal conductivity. This research focuses on overcoming the brittleness of geopolymer materials through extrinsic structural design using Direct Ink Writing (DIW).

## 2. Materials and Methods
### 2.1 Preparation of Lunar Regolith Simulant (HIT-LRS-1)
- **Composition:** Based on the JSC-1A simulant, including basalt powder, plagioclase, forsterite, albite, ilmenite, hematite, silica, alumina, and cristobalite.
- **Synthesis:** Ball milling of powders followed by sieving (80-mesh) and drying.

### 2.2 3D Printing of Geopolymer Inks
- **Ink Formula:** LRS powder + alkaline silicate solution (NaOH + sol-silica) + chopped carbon fiber (600 μm length) + quartz sand.
- **Printing:** Direct Ink Writing (DIW) using a 0.82 mm nozzle, 40 mm/s speed, and 70–80 psi pressure.

### 2.3 Design of Biomimetic Structures
Four regular cellular sandwich architectures were constructed:
1. Honeycomb
2. Triangular
3. Wave
4. Rectangular

## 3. Results and Discussion
### 3.1 Rheological Properties
- The ink (1Csf/20QS@HIT-LRS-1 GP) exhibited shear-thinning behavior and high yield stress (~8628 Pa), essential for maintaining shape after extrusion.

### 3.2 Mechanical Properties
- **Flexural Strength:** 1.00 vol% carbon fiber increased strength by 50.7% to 41.6 MPa.
- **Anisotropy:** Compressive strength in the Z-axis was significantly higher than in the Y-axis.

## 4. Conclusions
The study successfully demonstrated 3D printing of LRS-based geopolymer composites. The bio-inspired sandwich patterns achieved stable, non-catastrophic failure characteristics and high compressive strength (up to 46.7 MPa), providing a robust structural solution for lunar base development.
