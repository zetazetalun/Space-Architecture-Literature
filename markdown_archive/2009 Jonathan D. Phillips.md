# Soils as extended composite phenotypes

**Jonathan D. Phillips**

*Tobacco Road Research Team, Department of Geography, University of Kentucky, Lexington, KY 40506-0027, United States*

## Abstract
Several recent theories and conceptual frameworks in pedology, ecology, geomorphology, and evolutionary biology, taken together, suggest the notion that earth’s soils are not just strongly influenced by biota, but represent selective pressures. These ideas point in the direction of many aspects of soils as expressions of the effects of genes through the effects of organisms (i.e., extended phenotypes). The cumulative, interacting, overlapping effects of these extended phenotypes as manifested in the soil represent an extended composite phenotype. If this is the case, then we should expect major changes in biological evolution to be reflected in major changes in the types of soils. The notion of genetic signatures in soil morphology also has implications for the search for extraterrestrial life, and extends the notion of Earth as a set of tightly-coupled, densely interwoven systems.

## 1. Introduction
This paper argues that Earth’s soils are an expression of the cumulative impacts of the biosphere on surface processes. Soils may represent the single best indication of the nature, state, and condition of earth surface systems. If soils are expressions of biological evolution, implications are profound for understanding the evolution of earth systems.

## 2. Conceptual Framework
### 2.1. Soils as biotic constructs
Van Breemen (1993) argued that soils are influenced by biota to increase net primary productivity. This includes weathering, increasing nutrient supply, and water holding capacity.
### 2.2. Biomantles
Soils are increasingly viewed as continuously-mixed biomantles (Johnson, 1990). Biomechanical activities can result in differentiation rather than homogenization of soil layers.
### 2.3. Extended phenotypes and niche construction
Dawkins (1982) introduced the extended phenotype, including all effects of a gene on the environment. Ecosystem engineers (Jones et al., 1994) alter the environment to influence selective pressures.

## 3. Paleosols and coevolution
If soils are extended composite phenotypes, evidence should exist in the stratigraphic record. Paleosol evidence shows emergence of soils associated with bunch grassland and sod grassland as those vegetation types emerged between 39 and 6 Ma.

## 4. Tree–soil interactions
Interactions in the Ouachita Mountains demonstrate that biotic changes due to selective pressures (e.g., fire frequency) are associated with changes in soil type and morphology. Pines vs. hardwoods show distinct differences in root architecture and uprooting frequency, impacting soil thickness and rock fragment distribution.

## 5. Discussion and conclusions
Soils as extended composite phenotypes imply a relationship between the bio- and pedospheres similar to that between the biosphere and atmosphere. This framework suggests possibilities for searching for life on other planets, as an explicit view of soils may yield clues about potential extraterrestrial pedologic signatures of life.
