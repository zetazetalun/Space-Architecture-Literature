# Blood, sweat, and tears: extraterrestrial regolith biocomposites with in vivo binders

**Authors:** A.D. Roberts, D.R. Whittall, R. Breitling, E. Takano, J.J. Blaker, S. Hay, N.S. Scrutton  
**Journal:** Materials Today Bio 12 (2021) 100136  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.mtbio.2021.100136

## Abstract
In this work, we explore the use of human serum albumin (HSA) — a common protein obtained from blood plasma — as a binder for simulated Lunar and Martian regolith to produce so-called ‘extraterrestrial regolith biocomposites (ERBs).’ Employing a simple fabrication strategy, HSA-based ERBs were produced and displayed compressive strengths as high as 25.0 MPa. The incorporation of urea — which could be extracted from the urine, sweat, or tears of astronauts — could further increase the compressive strength by over 300% in some instances, with the best-performing formulation having an average compressive strength of 39.7 MPa. Furthermore, we demonstrate that HSA-ERBs have the potential to be 3D-printed.

## 1. Introduction
Early extraterrestrial colonies will likely exploit loose regolith as bulk material for radiation and meteor shielding. Mechanical stabilization is necessary to prevent erosion. HSA produced by astronauts in vivo could be extracted and used as a binder, circumventing the need for heavy equipment required for other ISRU binder methods.

## 2. Materials and Methods
- **Simulants:** Lunar Highlands Simulant 1 (LHS-1) and Martian Global Simulant 1 (MGS-1).
- **Binder:** Human Serum Albumin (HSA) in 30 wt% aqueous solution.
- **Additive:** Urea (simulating extraction from urine).
- **Fabrication:** Infusion of protein solution into packed regolith, followed by dehydration at 65°C for 20 hours.
- **Testing:** Uniaxial compression tests (ASTM C39/C39M-20), CD spectroscopy for structural analysis, and FE-SEM.

### 3.2 3D-Printing Potential
A scoping experiment demonstrated the feasibility of 3D-printing HSA-ERBs using manual extrusion of a 3:1 regolith-to-HSA (15 wt% solution) mixture. The resulting structure sustained a force of 600 N.

### 3.3 Bonding Mechanism
The mechanism was attributed to the dehydration-induced reorganization of HSA into a densely hydrogen-bonded β-sheet network, similar to spider silk cohesion.

## 4. Discussion
HSA-ERBs can be produced with minimal ex situ components. A 72-week Mars mission crew could potentially produce enough HSA to expand habitat space for one additional astronaut. The material is fully recyclable via dissolution.

## 5. Conclusions
Human biological resources (HSA and urea) are viable binders for regolith biocomposites, exceeding the compressive strength of standard concrete in optimized formulations. This approach reduces payload mass and mission complexity.
