# Biopolymers can be used for consolidation of lunar and Martian regoliths

**Authors:** Nitin Gupta, Aloke Kumar, Koushik Viswanathan  
**Journal:** Acta Astronautica 245 (2026) 897–907  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.actaastro.2026.03.042

## Abstract
Creating long-term extraterrestrial habitats to support continuous human occupation requires structures that can bear load, while remaining resilient to large temperature fluctuations. Exploiting locally available regolith is part of a paradigm termed in situ resource utilization (ISRU) that advocates minimal dependence on terrestrial supplies via local resource exploitation for construction. In this work, we report on a consolidation method for making load-bearing structures out of lunar and Martian regolith simulants using biopolymer binders. We quantify the compressive and tensile strength of the final brick-like structures, termed biopolymer regolith composites, and investigate their dependence on biopolymer concentration and chemistry. A pressure-assisted die casting (PADC) process is developed that can produce bricks with compressive strengths of ~ 15 MPa and tensile strengths of around 2 MPa, from regolith simulants. We finally report on the performance of these bricks under large temperature and pressure variations and find that their strengths remain unchanged. We believe that this makes biopolymer regolith composite bricks suitable candidates for use in harsh extraterrestrial environments, while adhering to the guidelines of the ISRU paradigm.

## 1. Introduction
A key challenge in creating long-term extraterrestrial habitats lies in building structures that can bear load while offering protection from radiation and temperature fluctuations. Locally available regolith is the most abundant construction material. In situ resource utilization (ISRU) seeks to minimize terrestrial dependence. Various consolidation methods have been explored, including thermal techniques (sintering) and biological approaches (MICP). However, thermal methods are energy-intensive, and MICP involves complex biological controls. This study explores biopolymer binders combined with a novel Pressure-Assisted Die Casting (PADC) process.

**Biopolymers:** Guar gum (GG), Xanthan gum (XG), and Acacia gum (AG).

### 2.2 Fabrication Protocols
1.  **Simple Casting (SC):** Hand-rammed mixture in a mold, oven-dried at 60°C for 12 h.
2.  **Pressure-Assisted Die Casting (PADC):** Pre-fill mixture compacted under 5 tons in a hydraulic press, followed by oven-drying at 60°C for 12 h.

### 3.1 Mechanical Performance
*   **SC Method:** Low strength (0-5 MPa); samples often incapable of bearing load without binder.
*   **PADC Method:** Achieved compressive strengths (σc) of ~15 MPa with 1% GG.
*   **Binder Concentration:** Optimal GG concentration was found to be ~2% for lunar and ~0.5% for Martian regolith.
*   **Environmental Stability:** Bricks maintained strength after exposure to temperatures ranging from -80°C to 175°C and low pressure (~20 mbar).

## 4. Discussion and Summary
Polysaccharide biopolymers effectively consolidate regolith into load-bearing structures. PADC bricks surpass minimum requirements (3-7 MPa) for lunar and Martian habitats. The process requires minimal energy compared to sintering and can utilize biologically produced binders, making it a frontrunner for scalable extraterrestrial construction.
