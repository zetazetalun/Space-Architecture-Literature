# Biopolymers can be used for consolidation of lunar and Martian regoliths

## Abstract
Creating long-term extraterrestrial habitats to support continuous human occupation requires structures that can bear load, while remaining resilient to large temperature fluctuations. Exploiting locally available regolith is part of a paradigm termed in situ resource utilization (ISRU). In this work, we report on a consolidation method for making load-bearing structures out of lunar and Martian regolith simulants using biopolymer binders. A pressure-assisted die casting (PADC) process is developed that can produce bricks with compressive strengths of ~ 15 MPa and tensile strengths of around 2 MPa. These strengths remain unchanged under large temperature and pressure variations.

## 1. Introduction
A key challenge in creating long-term extraterrestrial habitats lies in building structures that can bear load while offering protection from radiation and temperature fluctuations. ISRU seeks to minimize dependence on terrestrial supplies. Various consolidation methods have been explored, including sintering and microbial induced calcite precipitation (MICP). Sintering requires high energy, while MICP involves complex biological controls. Biopolymers offer a sustainable, low-energy alternative that could be produced from biomass in life-support systems.

## 2. Materials and Methods
### 2.1. Regolith simulants and biopolymers
Four simulants were used: LHS-1 and LMS-1 (lunar), and MGS-1 and MGS-1S (Martian). Three polysaccharide biopolymers were tested: guar gum (GG), xanthan gum (XG), and acacia gum (AG).

### 2.2. Simple casting (SC) and pressure-assisted die casting (PADC)
Two protocols were used: SC (hand-rammed in molds) and PADC (compacted using 5-ton hydraulic press). Mixtures contained 100g regolith, 0-2% biopolymer, and 15ml water, followed by oven drying at 60 °C for 12 h.

## 3. Results
### 3.1. Strength of SC and PADC bricks
PADC bricks produced strengths of ~15 MPa. Martian simulants (MGS-1/MGS-1S) showed some inherent binding due to hydrated silica, while lunar simulants required biopolymers for consolidation. 1% GG significantly enhanced strength and water resistance.

## 4. Discussion & Summary
Biopolymer regolith composite (BRC) bricks exceed the minimum structural requirements (3-7 MPa) for lunar and Martian habitats. PADC requires minimal energy compared to sintering and is scalable for extraterrestrial construction.
