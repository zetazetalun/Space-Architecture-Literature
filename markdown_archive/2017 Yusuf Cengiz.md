# SPACE RESEARCH AND EXTRATERRESTRIAL CONSTRUCTION INDUSTRY

**Authors:** Yusuf Cengiz TOKLU (Okan University), Ali Erdem ÇERÇEVIK (Bilecik Seyh Edebali University)
**Conference:** 2017 IEEE 8th International Conference on Recent Advances in Space Technologies (RAST)

## Abstract
Advance of civilization has been realized through the ages by exploration of unknown parts of the Earth and by exploiting new resources thus acquired. As a natural continuation of this process, humans will now explore and exploit new areas beyond Earth, i.e. in space. Habitation on these new space bodies will firstly necessitate construction of outposts. This article presents an overview of space research together with innovations in the space construction industry and the feedback of these studies to the construction industry in general.

## I. INTRODUCTION
Humanity's expansion follows a continuous loop of resource exploitation leading to economic and technological growth, which in turn enables further expansion. This process, which led to globalization on Earth, is now leading to "spatialization."

## II. SPATIALIZATION - WORLD 2050
The "world" of humans in 2000 was Earth and its immediate orbit. By 2100, it is predicted to include the Moon, Mars, and the space between them. Manned missions to Mars are expected around 2050.

## V. HABITATION / CONSTRUCTION ON SPACE BODIES
Construction on space bodies will be carried out by civil engineers in collaboration with scientists. The primary goal is directing natural resources found on these bodies for the use of man. Carrying 1 kg of material from Earth to the Moon costs approximately $20,000; for Mars, it is significantly higher. Thus, maximizing the usage of in-situ materials is essential.

Initial uses of ISRU include:
- Coverage of structures by regolith for radiation protection.
- Construction of walls using reinforced regolith.
- Forming structures by gabions filled with in-situ dust.

### B. A better understanding of structures
Structural design must account for internal pressure as a primary load, as atmosphere is lacking. Cracks are not permissible. Innovation areas include:
- Constructions in lava tubes.
- Habitats embedded in regolith.
- Tensegric structures.
- Inflatable structures.
- Extensive use of prestressing.

### C. Automated construction
Due to the dangers of manual labor, mining, earthwork, and construction will be advanced using robotics. The construction industry will be fully automated.

### D. Special construction machines
Lunar and Martian conditions require special machines: electrically operated, fully automatic/tele-commanded, lightweight, and dust-resistant.

### F. Building Information Systems
Structures will be smart/mechatronic systems, controlled by central management systems using sensors for pressure, temperature, CO2, and structural stress.

## CONCLUSIONS
The passage from globalization to spatialization marks the next step in human history. Success will depend on the feedback loop of innovations realized for these hostile environments.
