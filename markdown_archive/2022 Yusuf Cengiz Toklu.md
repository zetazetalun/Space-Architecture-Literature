# Lunar soils, simulants and lunar construction materials: An overview\n\n**Authors:** Yusuf Cengiz Toklu, Pinar Akpinar\n**Journal:** Advances in Space Research 70 (2022) 762–779\n**DOI:** 10.1016/j.asr.2022.05.017\n\n## Abstract\nOne of the hot subjects in space civil engineering is the research for establishing outposts on space bodies, starting with the Moon. This paper presents an overview on three aspects: the nature of in-situ soils from lunar missions, the variety of lunar soil simulants produced on Earth, and their use in producing lunar construction materials.\n\n## 1. Introduction\nConstruction materials to be used in lunar structures will be mostly raw materials found on the Moon (regolith) due to the high cost of transportation. Total original lunar samples are currently less than 400 kg (US Apollo: 382 kg; Soviet Luna: 300 g; China Chang’e 5: 2 kg).\n\n## 2. Lunar soil simulants\nSimulants are terrestrial materials mimicking lunar soil. The paper lists 54 simulants developed until August 2021, including MLS-1, ALS, JSC-1/JSC-1A, FJS-1, OB-1, CAS-1, GCA-1, NU-LHT, and others from diverse countries (USA, Japan, China, Korea, Canada, India, Australia, Germany, UK, Turkey, Italy).\n\n## 4. Regolith-based ordinary cements and concretes\nRequires water, which is scarce on the Moon. Sublimation in vacuum and extreme temperatures (+120 C) make standard hydration difficult.\n\n### 4.2. Sulfur-based lunar concretes\nWater-less. Sulfur melts at 119 C and hardens at 148 C. Reaches 70-80% strength in 24 hours. Limitations include sublimation and melting at lunar daytime temperatures.\n\n### 4.5. Lunar construction materials with 3D printing\nAdditive Manufacturing (AM) is key for complex geometries. Technologies include granular material with liquid binder (D-Shape) and sintering-based processes (laser, solar, microwave). Sitta and Lavagna (2018) reported 22.7 MPa for 3D printed components using NU-LHT-2 M simulant.\n\n## 5. Conclusive remarks\nLunar construction material research is in its infancy. There is a need for standardized testing and high-fidelity simulants that match original samples' properties.
