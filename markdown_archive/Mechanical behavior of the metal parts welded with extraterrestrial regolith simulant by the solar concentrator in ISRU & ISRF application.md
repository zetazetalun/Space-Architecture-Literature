# Mechanical behavior of the metal parts welded with extraterrestrial regolith simulant by the solar concentrator in ISRU & ISRF application

**Authors:** Wei Zheng, Guofu Qiao  
**Journal:** Advances in Space Research 65 (2020) 2303–2314  
**DOI:** 10.1016/j.asr.2020.02.008

## Abstract
The use of in-situ resources plays an important role on future extraterrestrial human activities for the facility repair and habitat construction, especially in sustainable space exploration of Moon and Mars. A method of the metal welded with extraterrestrial regolith simulant using solar processing under ambient conditions is presented. Metal parts are made of Q235B ferroalloy and TA2 titanium alloy into standard tensile members according to the ASTM code. They are disconnected from the middle in advance, and then welded together with lunar and Martian regolith simulant under ambient conditions, respectively. The results show that it is possible to weld metal parts together with extraterrestrial regolith simulant by the solar concentrator. The average ultimate tensile strength of ferroalloy specimens welded with lunar and Martian regolith simulant is 2.94 MPa and 1.66 MPa; The average ultimate tensile strength of titanium alloy specimens welded with lunar and Martian regolith simulant is 4.95 MPa and 2.59 MPa. Moreover, the failure mode of all weldments was brittle failure.

## 1. Introduction
The long-term explorations of the Moon and Mars require mission sustainment through in-situ resource utilization (ISRU) and in-situ fabrication and repair (ISFR). This paper proposes a method for welding metal together with extraterrestrial regolith simulant by solar concentrator at ambient conditions.

- **Q235B carbon steel (Ferroalloy):** Yield strength 235 MPa, UTS 453 MPa.
- **TA2 titanium alloy:** Yield strength 373 MPa, UTS 516 MPa.

### 2.2. Welding Process
A Fresnel lens (450mm diameter) was used to concentrate solar energy. The heat flux density at the focal spot was approximately 56.5 MW/m². Metal parts were arranged in a sandwich configuration with a layer of regolith (50 mg) between them. Solar heat was applied to melt the metal surface and regolith, creating a fused joint.

### Characterization
- **Failure Mode:** Brittle failure was observed in all specimens.
- **Microstructure:** SEM/EDS showed that regolith fused into the base metal. For ferroalloys, the fusion was homogeneous, while for titanium alloys, it was inhomogeneous.
- **Phase Composition:** XRD analysis indicated the presence of wustite and silicate melts in the fusion zone, contributing to the joint strength.

## 4. Conclusions
The study demonstrates the feasibility of welding metal parts using extraterrestrial regolith as a solder via solar concentration. While the tensile strengths are currently low (<2% of base metal strength), the method provides a path for in-situ fabrication and repair on the Moon and Mars.
