# Preparation and characterization of elevated and cryogenic temperature-resistant regolith-based epoxy resin composites

**Journal:** Construction and Building Materials 387 (2023) 131560  
**Authors:** Chengwei Bao, Yanen Wang, Ray Tahir Mushtaq, Xiaohu Chen, Zhisheng Liu, Xinpei Li, Minyan Liu  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.conbuildmat.2023.131560

## Abstract
The construction of lunar bases requires in-situ resource utilization (ISRU). This study investigates regolith-based polymer composites as a solution due to simple manufacturing and excellent mechanical properties. Specimens were prepared using the Taguchi method to optimize proportions. Results show high resistance to extreme temperatures (180°C and -196°C) and stable performance through freeze-thaw cycles, providing potential for rapid repair of launch pads and runways.

## 1. Introduction
Lunar construction faces high transportation costs and extreme environment challenges. ISRU strategies, specifically polymer forming, offer a low-energy consumption pathway for establishing habitats and shielding against radiation and meteorite impacts. Polymerization allows for the preparation of mortar-type structures using silicate-based materials (regolith) and additives like epoxy resin.

## 2. Materials and Methods
### 2.1 Materials
- **Lunar Regolith Simulants (LRS):** LR150 (D50 ≈ 150μm) and SC80 (D50 ≈ 80μm).
- **Resin Matrix:** Hybrid system of AG-80 (tetrafunctional epoxy) and E42 (bisphenol A type).
- **Curing Agents:** Hybrid of Polyetheramine (D400) and m-Xylylenediamine (MXDA).
- **Additives:** Silane coupling agent (KH-560).

### 2.2 Optimization (Taguchi Method)
An L16 (4^5) orthogonal array was used with the following factors:
1. m_ca/m_ep (Curing agent to resin ratio): 30%, 40%, 50%, 60%.
2. m_lrs/m_ep (Regolith to resin ratio): 100%, 200%, 300%, 400%.
3. Curing Temperature: 20°C, 40°C, 60°C, 80°C.

## 3. Results and Discussion
### 3.1 Mechanical Strength
The maximum 24h compressive and flexural strengths were achieved by SC80-based samples:
- **Compressive Strength:** 156.22 MPa
- **Flexural Strength:** 103.40 MPa

**Key Finding:** Curing temperature significantly affects compressive strength, while the curing agent ratio has the most impact on flexural strength. The optimal formulation was found at a curing temperature of 60°C.

### 3.2 Freeze-Thaw Performance
After 10 freeze-thaw cycles (180°C to -196°C), the composites maintained:
- Compressive strength > 160 MPa.
- Flexural strength > 70 MPa.
The samples exhibited a "tempering" effect where high temperatures facilitated further curing, offsetting thermal stress degradation.

## 4. Conclusion
1. LRS (LR150 and SC80) are viable feedstocks for polymer concrete.
2. The optimal formulation ratio (curing agent:matrix:regolith) was determined as 0.4:1:4 at 60°C.
3. The material is suitable for lunar construction applications like landing pads and habitats due to its resistance to extreme thermal fluctuations.
