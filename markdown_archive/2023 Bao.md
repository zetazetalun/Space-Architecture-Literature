# Preparation and characterization of elevated and cryogenic temperature-resistant regolith-based epoxy resin composites

**Journal:** Construction and Building Materials 387 (2023) 131560  
**Authors:** Chengwei Bao, Yanen Wang, Ray Tahir Mushtaq, Xiaohu Chen, Zhisheng Liu, Xinpei Li, Minyan Liu

## Abstract
The construction of lunar bases requires the integration of multiple manufacturing techniques. In-situ utilization of lunar regolith for fabrication has been proven feasible, among which polymer forming possesses promising prospects due to its sample manufacturing process, excellent mechanical properties, and low energy consumption. This study investigates the feasibility of regolith-based polymer composites using the Taguchi method to optimize proportions. Specimens resistant to elevated (180°C) and cryogenic (-196°C) temperatures were prepared. Results revealed maximum compressive and flexural strengths of 156.22 MPa and 103.40 MPa, respectively, with stability maintained over ten freeze-thaw cycles. This provides potential for rapid repair and reinforcement of lunar launch pads and runways.

## 1. Introduction
The Moon is rich in various resources, making it a perfect place for deep space exploration. However, the high cost of transporting materials from Earth necessitates In-Situ Resource Utilization (ISRU). Current ISRU research concerns include harvesting oxygen and water and preparing construction materials. Establishing extraterrestrial bases requires shielding against solar radiation, meteorite impacts, and large temperature fluctuations. 

## 2. Material and Methods
### 2.1. Materials
*   **Lunar Regolith Simulants (LRS):** LR150 (provided by Third Research Institute of China Aerospace Science and Industry Corporation) and SC80 (purchased from SimuTek Co. Ltd).
*   **Matrix:** Epoxy resins (E42 and AG-80).
*   **Curing Agents:** Polyetheramines (D400) and m-xylylenediamine (MXDA).
*   **Coupling Agent:** Silane (KH-560).

### 2.2. Mortar Preparation
LRS was ground in a ball mill. Two types of epoxy resins were mixed with LRS and curing agents. The system was vacuum defoamed and molded. Specimens were tested for compressive and flexural strength according to GB 2567–2021.

## 3. Results and Discussion
*   **LRS Characterization:** The LRS consists primarily of SiO2 and Al2O3 (over 60 wt%). XRD analysis confirmed Pyroxene, Plagioclase, Ilmenite, and Olivine.
*   **Mechanical Properties:** Compressive strength increases with curing temperature up to 60°C. Maximum strength was observed at an LRS-to-resin ratio of 4:1.
*   **Freeze-Thaw Performance:** After 10 cycles (-196°C to 180°C), compressive strength remained stable around 165 MPa. Thermal shrinkage reduction in the AG-80 resin system helped facilitate thermal stress reduction.

## 4. Conclusion
1. Regolith-based polymer composites showed maximum compressive strength of 156.22 MPa.
2. Curing temperature is the most significant factor for compressive strength, while the curing agent mass ratio most affects flexural strength.
3. The composites exhibit excellent low-temperature resistance and can accommodate extreme lunar temperature fluctuations.
