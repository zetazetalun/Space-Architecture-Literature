# Fiber-reinforced lunar geopolymers synthesized using lunar regolith simulants

**Authors:** Solomon Debbarma, Xijun Shi, Anthony Torres, Mehrab Nodehi
**Journal:** Acta Astronautica 214 (2024) 593–608
**Published:** 2024 (Available online 11 November 2023)
**DOI:** 10.1016/j.actaastro.2023.11.013

## Abstract
In-situ resource utilization (ISRU) is essential for supporting lunar additive construction and minimizing transportation mass. This study presents the formulation of fiber-reinforced geopolymers using lunar regolith simulants (Lunar Mare Simulant LMS-1 and Lunar Highlands Simulant LHS-1). Investigations covered simulant type, flowability, curing temperature effects, fiber reinforcement (basalt and human hair), and mechanical properties. Results showed that LHS-1 geopolymers exhibited higher compressive strength than LMS-1. High-temperature curing (93 °C) significantly improved strength for LHS-1. A 50/50 mix of LMS-1 and LHS-1 yielded a compressive strength of 29.75 MPa and a flexural strength of 2.32 MPa. Fiber additions improved flexural strength by up to 32% (basalt) and 15% (human hair) but reduced compressive strength due to increased porosity.

## 1. Introduction
Conventional concrete production on the Moon is impractical due to the absence of limestone and the high energy requirements for cement production. Geopolymers, synthesized by alkali activation of silica (Si) and aluminum (Al) rich materials, offer an alternative. Lunar regolith is a suitable precursor due to its Si/Al ratio (between 2.5 and 3). This research explores using basalt fibers (BF) and human hair fibers (HHF) as reinforcement to mitigate the inherent brittleness of geopolymers in the harsh lunar environment.

## 2. Materials and Methods

### 2.1. Regolith Simulants
- **LMS-1 (Lunar Mare Simulant):** Simulates Apollo-era mare samples; high pyroxene and glass-rich basalt content.
- **LHS-1 (Lunar Highlands Simulant):** High anorthosite content; simulates highlands terrain.

### 2.2. Reinforcements
- **Basalt Fiber (BF):** 12 mm length, 16 μm diameter.
- **Human Hair Fiber (HHF):** Length up to 15 mm.

### 2.3. Mix Proportions and Curing
Geopolymers used a liquid-to-binder (l/b) ratio of 0.45. Sodium silicate was used as the activator.

## 3. Results and Discussion

### 3.1. Flowability
LHS-1 (Mix B) showed higher flow (85 mm) than LMS-1 (Mix A, 74 mm). The 50/50 blend (Mix D) achieved the highest flow (94 mm). Fiber addition reduced flowability by 9% to 26%.

### 3.2. Compressive Strength
- **Mix B (LHS-1):** 31.26 MPa (93 °C curing). Ambient curing resulted in failure to harden.
- **Mix A (LMS-1):** ~8.45 MPa regardless of curing condition.
- **Mix D (50/50 Control):** 29.75 MPa.
- **Fiber Effect:** Adding BF reduced compressive strength by 28–63% due to higher porosity.

### 3.3. Flexural Strength
- **Mix D (Control):** 2.32 MPa.
- **Mix G (1.2% BF):** 3.07 MPa (+32% improvement).
- **Mix I (1.5% HHF):** 2.67 MPa (+15% improvement).

### 3.4. Microstructural Analysis
SEM analysis showed that fibers act as nucleation sites for geopolymerization products. LHS-1's higher calcium content and lower Si/Al ratio (1.92) compared to LMS-1 (3.78) resulted in a more integrated Si-Al tetrahedral structure and higher strength.

## 4. Conclusions
1. LHS-1 is a more effective precursor for geopolymers than LMS-1 due to its chemical composition.
2. Curing at high temperatures (approx. 93 °C) is critical for strength development in high-anorthosite regolith geopolymers.
3. Basalt and human hair fibers significantly enhance flexural strength and ductility, which is vital for brittle lunar concrete.
4. Compressive strength of 31 MPa exceeds the lunar requirement (estimated at 5.8 MPa for simple structures).
