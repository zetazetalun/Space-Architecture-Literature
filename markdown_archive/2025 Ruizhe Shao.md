# Evaluation of high-strength concrete for lunar applications: Mechanical behaviour under static and dynamic loads at cryogenic temperatures

**Authors:** Ruizhe Shao, Chengqing Wu, Jun Li, Kaiyi Chi, Zizheng Yu  
**Journal:** Construction and Building Materials  
**Date:** 2025

## Abstract
Substantial advancements in human sciences have greatly accelerated the exploration of celestial bodies. The construction of lunar bases now faces unique and severe environmental challenges, requiring materials that ensure structural integrity and stability. This study examines the static and dynamic mechanical properties of an innovative cement-free lunar high-strength concrete (LHSC). The multi-physics coupling effects of the extreme temperatures (20 °C, −70 °C, and −170 °C) and varying strain rates (approximately 30–200 s⁻¹) were considered to evaluate the dynamic responses of LHSC using a 100 mm-diameter impact apparatus. The findings indicated that the compressive strength of LHSC increased from 129.5 to 153.6 MPa as the temperature decreased, with dynamic strength rising up to 70.8% above static levels at −170 °C. Computed tomography (CT) scanning revealed shrinking pores and expanding ice crystals inside LHSC, which strengthened its matrix but also intensified microcrack propagation, reducing the material deformability.

## 1. Introduction
The Moon represents the principal frontier for human extraterrestrial exploration. Alkali-activated concrete (AAC), or geopolymer concrete, offers a significant advancement for lunar construction due to its ability to utilize mineral components in lunar regolith, aligning with In-Situ Resource Utilisation (ISRU) principles. AAC uses water as a reaction medium rather than a reactant, allowing for potential recovery rates of up to 98% in vacuum-curing systems.

### 2.2. Testing Methods
- **Static Tests:** Uniaxial compressive and split-tensile tests at 20 °C, −70 °C, and −170 °C.
- **Dynamic Tests:** Split Hopkinson pressure bar (SHPB) for high strain rates (30–200 s⁻¹).
- **Imaging:** High-speed camera (5 million fps) and X-ray CT scanning (Siemens Artis Pheno).

## 3. Results and Discussions
- **Static Strength:** Compressive strength increased from 129.5 MPa (20 °C) to 153.6 MPa (−170 °C). Split-tensile strength increased from 8.3 MPa (20 °C) to 9.2 MPa (−170 °C).
- **Dynamic Response:** Dynamic strength increased with strain rate across all temperatures. At −170 °C, dynamic strength exceeded static values by up to 70.8%.
- **Brittleness:** Cryogenic conditions significantly increased material brittleness, leading to abrupt fracture and reduced energy absorption (W).
- **CT Analysis:** Revealed that ice crystals fill micro-pores, strengthening the matrix under static load but initiating microcracks that propagate rapidly under dynamic stress wave propagation.

## 4. Conclusions
1. LHSC shows notable increases in static strength at cryogenic temperatures due to ice-induced matrix densification.
2. Dynamic compressive strength significantly surpasses static levels, peaking at −170 °C.
3. Material brittleness increases under cryogenic conditions, resulting in severe fragmentation under high impact.
4. DIF for both compression and split-tension increases at lower temperatures, indicating higher rate sensitivity.
5. CT scanning confirms that ice formation reduces pore size but facilitates microcrack expansion under dynamic loads.
