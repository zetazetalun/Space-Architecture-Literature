# Evaluation of high-strength concrete for lunar applications: Mechanical behaviour under static and dynamic loads at cryogenic temperatures

**Authors:** Ruizhe Shao, Chengqing Wu, Jun Li, Kaiyi Chi, Zizheng Yu  
**Journal:** Construction and Building Materials, 492 (2025) 142966  
**DOI:** 10.1016/j.conbuildmat.2025.142966

## Abstract
Substantial advancements in human sciences have greatly accelerated the exploration of celestial bodies. The construction of lunar bases now faces unique and severe environmental challenges, requiring materials that ensure structural integrity and stability. This study examines the static and dynamic mechanical properties of an innovative cement-free lunar high-strength concrete (LHSC). The multi-physics coupling effects of the extreme temperatures (20 °C, − 70 °C, and − 170 °C) and varying strain rates (approximately 30–200 s⁻¹) were considered to evaluate the dynamic responses of LHSC using a 100 mm-diameter impact apparatus.

## 1. Introduction
Concrete, extensively employed as a building material on Earth, emerges as an ideal candidate for lunar construction projects. Alkali-activated concrete (AAC) or geopolymer concrete allows for the utilization of lunar regolith, aligning with In-Situ Resource Utilisation (ISRU) principles and reducing water consumption compared to Ordinary Portland Cement (OPC).

## 2. Experimental Setup and Methodology

- **Additives:** Class F fly ash, silica fume, and GGBFS to enhance reactivity.

### 2.2. Testing Conditions
- **Static Tests:** Uniaxial compression and split-tensile tests.
- **Dynamic Tests:** Split Hopkinson pressure bar (SHPB) for high strain rates (30–200 s⁻¹).
- **Temperatures:** 20 °C (ambient), −70 °C, and −170 °C (cryogenic).

## 3. Results and Discussions

### 3.1. Mechanical Strength
- **Static Compressive Strength:** Increased from 129.5 MPa (20 °C) to 153.6 MPa (−170 °C).
- **Static Split-Tensile Strength:** Increased from 8.3 MPa (20 °C) to 9.2 MPa (−170 °C).
- **Dynamic Increase Factor (DIF):** Sensitivity to strain rate increased as temperature decreased.

### 3.2. Microstructural Analysis (CT Scanning)
Computed tomography revealed that at cryogenic temperatures, pores shrink as ice crystals form. While ice strengthens the matrix (ice-induced stiffening), it also promotes microcrack propagation, increasing brittleness and reducing deformability.

## 4. Conclusions
1. LHSC shows notable increases in static strength at cryogenic temperatures (up to 18.6% increase at −170 °C).
2. Dynamic compressive strength surpasses static levels by up to 70.8% at −170 °C.
3. Ice formation within the pore network enhances stiffness but accelerates microcrack initiation under dynamic loads.
4. Split-tensile DIF values exceed compression DIF values across all temperatures, indicating higher rate sensitivity in tension.
