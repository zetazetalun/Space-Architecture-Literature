# Effects of in-situ admixtures and simulated Martian regolith on the mechanical properties and microstructure of basic magnesium sulfate cement for potential martian construction

**Authors:** L.I. Lingyu, Y.U. Hongfa, M.A. Haiyan, Zhao Yanbing, Zhang Honglei, L.U. Mingyang, Liu Weifeng, Kang Yanqi, Zhao Zonghao

**Journal:** Construction and Building Materials 528 (2026) 146495

## Abstract
With the continuous advancement of Mars exploration, in-situ resource utilization (ISRU) for Martian infrastructure has become a research hotspot. Basic magnesium sulfate cement (BMSC) has the potential to be fully prepared from Martian resources. In this study, a conceptual ISRU pathway based on Martian magnesium sulfate minerals and phosphate-bearing regolith is proposed. Reactive MgO, MgSO₄, and simulated regolith powder were used to prepare a BMSC-Martian regolith composite system (BMSC-MS). Two potentially in-situ obtainable additives, phosphoric acid and citric acid-based beverage powder, were introduced. The results show that phosphoric acid exhibits significantly better regulation performance. At an optimal dosage of 1 wt%, the BMSC-MS system achieved a 28-day compressive strength of 39.09 MPa. Findings provide preliminary material-level assessment of BMSC-based composites for future extraterrestrial construction.

## 1. Introduction
Establishment of Martian bases will provide platforms for scientific exploration and long-term human settlement. Cementitious materials are recognized as indispensable for infrastructural development. To mitigate transportation costs, utilizing Mars' natural resources via ISRU is the optimal solution.

## 2. Conceptual ISRU pathway for Martian BMSC
Reactive MgO, water, and sulfuric acid can be obtained via thermal decomposition of hydrated magnesium sulfate minerals. Martian regolith (rich in forsterite) can be leached by sulfuric acid to obtain supplementary MgO. Phosphoric acid can be extracted from Martian phosphate minerals, and citric acid can be recycled from astronaut beverages.

## 3. Materials and experimental methods
- **Simulated Martian Regolith (NUAA-1M):** Sourced from volcanic scoria; chemical composition similar to Gale Crater regolith (73.41% SiO₂+Al₂O₃+Fe₂O₃).
- **Binder:** Light-burned MgO (80% active) and MgSO₄·7H₂O.
- **Admixtures:** Phosphoric acid (H₃PO₄), Lemon powder (citric acid source).
- **Mix Proportion:** Regolith content fixed at 60 wt%; M/S molar ratios of 4, 5, and 6 investigated.

## 4. Results and discussion
- **Fluidity & Setting:** Mixes showed 180–210 mm spread. Setting times (20–70 min) are suitable for 3D printing buildability.
- **Mechanical Properties:** Samples with 1 wt% H₃PO₄ showed the best performance (39.09 MPa compressive strength at 28 days).
- **Microstructure:** H₃PO₄ promotes 5·1·7 phase formation and M-(A-)S-H gel development while suppressing Mg(OH)₂ precipitation.
- **Porosity:** Optimized system reduced total porosity to 20.89%.

## 5. Conclusions
1. A conceptual ISRU pathway for Martian BMSC was proposed.
2. Phosphoric acid outperformed citric acid as a performance regulator.
3. Optimal performance (39.09 MPa) achieved at M/S ratio of 4 with 1 wt% H₃PO₄.
4. Simulated regolith exhibits pozzolanic reactivity in the BMSC environment.
