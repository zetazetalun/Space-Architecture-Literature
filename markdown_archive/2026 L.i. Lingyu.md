# Effects of in-situ admixtures and simulated Martian regolith on the mechanical properties and microstructure of basic magnesium sulfate cement for potential martian construction

**Journal:** Construction and Building Materials 528 (2026) 146495
**Authors:** L.I. Lingyu, Y.U. Hongfa, M.A. Haiyan, Zhao Yanbing, Zhang Honglei, L.U. Mingyang, Liu Weifeng, Kang Yanqi, Zhao Zonghao
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.conbuildmat.2026.146495

## Abstract
With the continuous advancement of Mars exploration, in-situ resource utilization (ISRU) for Martian infrastructure has become a research hotspot. Basic magnesium sulfate cement (BMSC) has the potential to be fully prepared from Martian resources. This study proposes a conceptual ISRU pathway based on Martian magnesium sulfate minerals and phosphate-bearing regolith. Material feasibility was evaluated under terrestrial laboratory conditions using reactive MgO, MgSO₄, and simulated regolith powder (NUAA-1M) to prepare a BMSC-Martian regolith composite system (BMSC-MS). Two potentially in-situ obtainable additives, phosphoric acid and citric acid-based beverage powder, were introduced. Results show that phosphoric acid exhibits significantly better regulation performance. At an optimal dosage of 1 wt%, the BMSC-MS system achieved a 28-day compressive strength of 39.09 MPa.

## 1. Introduction
Cementitious materials are recognized as indispensable for the development of lunar and Martian outposts. To mitigate transportation costs, using Mars' abundant natural resources through ISRU is essential. Previous attempts include geopolymer Martian concrete, sulfur-regolith composite (Marscrete), and sintering/direct compaction. This study explores BMSC as a promising low-carbon binder capable of binding aggregates to produce robust structural concrete using Martian magnesium sulfate minerals.

## 2. Conceptual ISRU Pathway
- **Precursors:** Reactive MgO and water obtained from thermal decomposition of hydrated magnesium sulfate minerals. Sulfuric acid byproduct is used for leaching.
- **Admixtures:** Phosphoric acid extracted from Martian phosphate minerals via wet acid leaching. Citric acid recycled from astronaut rehydration beverages.
- **Aggregate:** Martian regolith (forsterite rich) participating in the cementitious reaction.

## 3. Materials and Methods
### 3.1. Raw Materials
- **Simulated Martian Regolith (NUAA-1M):** Sourced from volcanic scoria, processed to micropowder (<70 μm). High chemical similarity to Gale Crater data.
- **Binder:** Light-burned MgO (80 wt% active MgO) and magnesium sulfate heptahydrate.
- **Admixtures:** H₃PO₄ (85% purity) and commercial lemon powder (citric acid surrogate).

## 4. Results and Discussion
### 4.1. Fluidity and Setting Time
- Spread diameters ranged from 180 to 210 mm.
- Initial setting times: 20–32 min; final setting times: 35–70 min.
- Rapid setting is noted as potentially advantageous for additive manufacturing (3D printing) to ensure early-stage buildability and mitigate water loss in low-pressure environments.

### 4.2. Mechanical Properties
- Strength increased significantly with age.
- Optimized mix: 1 wt% Phosphoric acid at M/S molar ratio of 4.
- **Compressive Strength (28-day):** 39.09 MPa.
- **Flexural Strength (28-day):** ~10 MPa (interpolated from Figure 12).

### 4.3. Microstructure and Phase Analysis
- **Major Products:** 5·1·7 crystalline phase (5Mg(OH)₂·MgSO₄·7H₂O) and amorphous M-(A-)S-H gels.
- H₃PO₄ promotes the 5·1·7 phase formation and suppresses disordered Mg(OH)₂ precipitation.
- **Porosity:** Optimized system reduced total porosity to 20.89% (compared to 25.90% in low-dose/control samples).

## 5. Conclusions
1. Proposed an ISRU-oriented preparation pathway for Martian BMSC.
2. Phosphoric acid outperformed citric acid as an admixture for mechanical enhancement.
3. Optimal performance (39.09 MPa) supports structural load-bearing requirements for Martian habitats.
4. Microstructural densification is driven by synergistic crystal-gel interactions (5·1·7 phase and M-(A-)S-H gels).
