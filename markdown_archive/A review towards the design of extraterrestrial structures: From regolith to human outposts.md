# A review towards the design of extraterrestrial structures: From regolith to human outposts

## Abstract
The design of a permanent human habitat on a planetary body other than the Earth is an idea introduced many decades ago. This paper presents a meticulous selection of the most significant publications related to: (a) geotechnical engineering aspects of Lunar and Martian regolith; (b) ground motions from Moonquakes and meteoroid impacts; (c) concepts for extraterrestrial (ET) structures (generic, inflatable, deployable, 3D-printed).

## 1. Introduction
Civil engineering must expand towards the design and construction of Lunar and Martian structures. Key environmental challenges include low gravity, temperature fluctuations (up to 280 K), radiation, lack of atmosphere, meteoroid impacts, and Lunar dust.

## 2. Aspects of Geotechnical Engineering

### 2.5 Regolith-based landing pads
Foundations requiring extensive excavation are impractical. ISRU-based techniques include using Lunar concrete (KOHLS-1) or sintering regolith into tiles. Lee et al. (2012) and Kelso et al. (2016) demonstrated robotic construction of landing pads using basalt material.

## 3. Extraterrestrial Ground Motions
Seismic hazards include deep moonquakes (tide-related), shallow moonquakes (tectonic, up to magnitude 4.8 mb), thermal moonquakes, and meteoroid impacts. Moonquakes have very long durations (up to 30 mins) due to low attenuation.

## 4. Extraterrestrial Structures
### 4.1 Generic Structural Concepts
Concepts include flat 3D trusses supporting regolith shields (Benaroya & Ettouney, 1992) and arch-type structures using cast basalt (Aulesa et al., 2000).

### 4.2 Inflatable Structures
Inflatables (e.g., TransHab, NASA 1961 Toroid) offer high volume-to-mass ratios. Key materials include Kevlar 49, Nextel, and Nomex. Shielding with ~3m of regolith is often required for radiation protection.

### 4.4 3D-printed ET Structures
Additive Manufacturing (AM) using ISRU is a focus. Technologies include:
- **Contour Crafting (CC):** Extrusion of regolith mixed with binders (e.g., sulfur concrete).
- **Sintering:** Solar, Laser, or Microwave sintering of regolith.
- **Binder Jetting:** D-shape technology using regolith and a binding fluid (Cesaretti et al., 2014).

## 6. Discussion
Habitats have evolved from pre-fabricated Class I modules to ISRU-based Class III structures. While Lunar and Martian regolith samples show low cohesion (<6 kPa), they possess high internal friction angles (>40°), useful for load transfer. 3D printing is envisioned as the primary construction method for future outposts.
