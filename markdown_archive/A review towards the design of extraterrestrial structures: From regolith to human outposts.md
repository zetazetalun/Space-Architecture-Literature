# A review towards the design of extraterrestrial structures: From regolith to human outposts\n\n## Abstract\nThe design of a permanent human habitat on a planetary body other than the Earth is an idea introduced many decades ago, which became even more significant after the landing of the first humans on the Moon with the Apollo missions. The scope of this paper is to present a meticulous selection of the most significant publications within the scientific areas related to: (a) geotechnical engineering aspects, including the mechanical properties and chemical composition of Lunar and Martian regolith samples and simulants; (b) ground motions generated by Moonquakes and meteoroid impacts; (c) the different concepts and types of extraterrestrial (ET) structures (generic, inflatable, deployable, 3D-printed).\n\n## 1. Introduction\nThe concept of creating extraterrestrial habitats predates what is known as the \"space age\". It has become evident that there will soon be a need to expand civil engineering towards the design and construction of Lunar and Martian structures, habitats and outposts.\n\n## 2. Aspects of geotechnical engineering\nThis section covers a wide range of geotechnical engineering aspects that constitute the basis for further civil engineering analysis, including Lunar and Martian regolith mechanical and chemical properties, anchoring within regolith layers, and developing landing pads.\n\n## 3. Extraterrestrial ground motions and seismic hazard considerations\nGround motions on the Lunar/Martian surface, combined with microgravity, could pose a hazard for potential extraterrestrial structures. Key sources include deep moonquakes, shallow moonquakes, thermal moonquakes, and meteorite impacts.\n\n## 4. Extraterrestrial structures\nVarious researchers have proposed a plethora of concepts regarding different structural systems:\n- Inflatable structures\n- Deployable structures\n- 3D-printed structures\n\n## 5. Human-centered concepts for lunar/martian outposts\nFocuses on a more holistic conceptualization of a human habitat by employing a combination of large-scale additive manufacturing, ISRU, robotics, and inflatable structures.\n\n## 6. Discussion\nIn terms of shear strength, Lunar and Martian regolith samples do not exhibit high values of cohesion (generally less than 6 kPa). However, they can reach up to (and even more than) 40 degrees of internal friction angle... Today, there is a strong and clear need for a fresh civil engineering vision. There is a need for regolith-based ET structures that will exhibit resilience against natural hazards, also considering dynamic loading in the form of seismic ground motions and impacts, and constructed using large-scale additive manufacturing.
