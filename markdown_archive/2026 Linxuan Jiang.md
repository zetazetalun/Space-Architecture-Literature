# Evaluation of high and low temperature performance characteristics of bricks fabricated with lunar regolith simulant

## Abstract
This study investigates the microstructural evolution and thermal properties of lunar regolith simulant bricks fabricated via microwave sintering under three temperature cycling modes: high-temperature (25°C to 120°C), low-temperature (25°C to -120°C), and combined (±120°C). Results show that high-temperature cycling increases porosity by 11.7%, while low-temperature cycling enhances specific heat capacity by 15–20%. Combined cycling yields the best thermal insulation (0.82 W/m·K) despite a 20.8% reduction in elastic modulus. These findings suggest sintered lunar highland regolith is a promising material for thermal insulation in lunar base infrastructure.

## 1. Introduction
Lunar base construction using in-situ resource utilization (ISRU) is vital for sustainable exploration. The Moon's surface fluctuates from -173°C to 127°C, requiring materials that withstand thermal fatigue. Sintering, specifically microwave sintering, is identified as a promising technique for forming building materials from regolith due to its efficiency and volumetric heating capabilities.

## 2. Experimental Methods
### 2.1 Sample Preparation
- **Material:** HUST-2 (plagioclase lunar regolith simulant representing highland/polar regions).
- **Fabrication:** Microwave sintering at 1150°C for 2 hours.
- **Cycles:** 24 cycles under three modes: HUST-2-H (High), HUST-2-L (Low), and HUST-2-C (Combined ±120°C).

### 2.2 Characterization
Mechanical properties (compressive strength, elastic modulus), thermal properties (diffusivity, conductivity, heat capacity), and microstructure (XRD, SEM, MIP) were analyzed.

## 3. Results
### 3.1 Density and Quality
Mass loss was negligible (<0.1%). Combined cycling (HUST-2-C) showed a 0.56% decrease in density.
### 3.2 Microstructure
XRD showed no phase changes. MIP analysis indicated:
- **HUST-2-H:** Porosity increased from 16.74% to 18.70%; average pore size increased from 4.28 μm to 4.74 μm.
- **HUST-2-C:** Average pore size decreased to 3.56 μm.
### 3.3 Mechanical and Thermal Properties
- **Elastic Modulus:** HUST-2-C showed the highest degradation (20.8%).
- **Specific Heat Capacity:** HUST-2-L showed an increase of 15-20% across the range.
- **Thermal Conductivity:** HUST-2-C achieved the lowest value (0.82 W/m·K at 25°C), representing superior insulation performance.

## 5. Conclusion
Temperature cycling significantly affects microwave-sintered lunar regolith. High-temperature cycles drive pore evolution, while low-temperature cycles alter thermal properties. Combined cycling enhances thermal insulation, providing essential data for the design of lunar habitat envelopes.
