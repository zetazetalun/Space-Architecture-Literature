# Evaluation of high and low temperature performance characteristics of bricks fabricated with lunar regolith simulant

## Abstract
This study investigates the microstructural evolution and thermal properties of sintered lunar regolith (HUST-2) under three temperature cycling modes: high-temperature (25°C to 120°C), low-temperature (25°C to -120°C), and combined high-low (±120°C) for 24 cycles. Results show high-temperature cycling increases porosity (from 16.74% to 18.70%) and pore size. Low-temperature cycling enhances specific heat capacity by 15–20%. Combined cycling significantly improves thermal insulation, yielding a thermal conductivity of 0.82 W/m·K (18.6% lower than control), despite a 20.8% reduction in elastic modulus. These findings support the use of sintered lunar highland regolith for thermal insulation in lunar base infrastructure.

## 1. Introduction
Lunar surface temperatures range from -173°C to 127°C, posing challenges to material integrity. Microwave sintering is identified as an energy-efficient ISRU technology for lunar construction. Most studies focus on preparation; this study addresses service performance under thermal cycling.

## 2. Experimental Methods
- **Material:** HUST-2 plagioclase lunar regolith simulant (highland/polar composition).
- **Fabrication:** Microwave sintering at 1150°C for 2 hours.
- **Cycling Modes:**
  - HUST-2-H: 25°C to 120°C
  - HUST-2-L: 25°C to -120°C
  - HUST-2-C: ±120°C
  - HUST-2-B: Control (no treatment)
- **Characterization:** XRD (phase), SEM (microstructure), MIP (porosity), LFA (thermal diffusivity/conductivity), and universal testing machine (mechanical).

## 3. Results
### 3.1. Quality and Density
Mass loss was negligible (<0.56%). Density in combined-cycled samples showed a small but statistically significant downward trend.
### 3.2. Microstructure
XRD showed no phase transformation (primary phase: plagioclase feldspar). High-temp cycling increased average pore size from 4.28 μm to 4.74 μm.
### 3.3. Mechanical Properties
All cycled samples showed reduced compressive strength compared to control. HUST-2-C showed the most significant elastic modulus degradation (20.75%).
### 3.4. Thermal Performance
Combined cycling (HUST-2-C) achieved the lowest thermal conductivity. Low-temperature cycling (HUST-2-L) markedly increased specific heat capacity due to lattice strain effects.

## 4. Discussion
High-temperature periods dominate pore evolution (microcracking), while low-temperature periods dominate thermal property changes (residual stress). An insulation layer of 0.5–1.0 m of these bricks is estimated to maintain a 20°C differential in a lunar habitat.

## 5. Conclusion
Thermal cycling affects sintered regolith differently based on temperature range. Combined cycling provides the best thermal protection properties. These results provide a foundation for material optimization for lunar bases.
