# Additive manufacturing evolution in construction: From individual terrestrial to collective, aerial, and extraterrestrial applications

## Abstract
The third industrial revolution is marked by the fast-growing evolution of Additive Manufacturing (AM) in construction. This transformative shift is driving the automation of construction processes to bestow economic, sustainable, and functional habitats on earth and space. AM has enabled innovative manipulation and design of materials, architectures, and structures in the construction industry. To fulfill the automation in construction objectives, AM trajectory is advancing from off-site toward on-site construction by upscaling, collective robotic work, remote, aerial, and lunar construction. This study reviews the advancement of AM in the construction industry, tracing its evolution from individual terrestrial to collective, aerial and extraterrestrial construction.

## 3. Fundamental AM techniques (terrestrial construction)
### 3.1. Contour crafting (CC)
Developed by Khoshnevis in 2004, CC uses an XYZ gantry system with single or multiple nozzles with the capability of 6-axis positioning. The cementitious composite is extruded and each layer is printed by two passes of a large deposition head to increase the build-up rate and enhance structural integrity.
### 3.2. Binder jetting, powder bed printing or D-shape (monolite)
In binder jetting, a gantry equipped with multiple nozzles prints each layer that is generally made of granular powder materials (magnesium-based materials and sand) and binder.

## 4. Advanced AM techniques (terrestrial and non-terrestrial)
### 4.3. Lunar AM
Recently, the exploration and potential colonization of celestial bodies, such as Mars and the Moon, have garnered significant interest. The construction of structures on these extraterrestrial surfaces presents unique challenges, making AM an appealing approach. In 2019, NASA and the European Space Agency (ESA) introduced the concept of “Advanced 3D Printing Construction Systems for the Moon and Mars”. The goal is to establish habitats on the Moon or Mars before 2040. In 2022, NASA awarded a $57.2 million contract to ICON, a Texas-based AM company, to develop technology for AM roads, launchpads, and homes on the Moon’s surface. ICON partnered with NASA aimed at building AM habitats made of local materials on Mars using a printer that can print a 2000 ft² house in 24 h. Simultaneously, researchers from Louisiana State University secured funding from the NSF to advance robotic construction and AM capabilities on the Moon. AI SpaceFactory designed a cylindrical shape 3D-printed habitat on Mars called Marsha for NASA in 2018 with a double-layered shell for insulation and radiation protection.
