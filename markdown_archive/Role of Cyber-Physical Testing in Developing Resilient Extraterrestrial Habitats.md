**Authors:** Amin Maghareh, Ali Lenjani, Murali Krishnan, Shirley Dyke, and Ilias Bilionis  
**Affiliation:** School of Mechanical Engineering, Purdue University, West Lafayette, IN 47906  
**Publication:** Earth and Space 2021, ASCE

## Abstract
Extraterrestrial long-term habitat systems require groundbreaking technological advances to overcome extreme demands introduced by isolation and challenging environments. A habitat system must operate as intended under continuous disruptive conditions. Designing for demands such as wild temperature fluctuations, galactic cosmic rays, destructive dust, meteoroid impacts, vibrations, and solar particle events represents one of the greatest challenges. This study highlights the importance of system resilience and cyber-physical testing to address the grand challenge of developing habitat systems.

## Introduction
NASA's plan for establishing long-term settlements on Mars seeks the capacity for people to work, learn, operate, and sustainably live beyond Earth. Extraterrestrial habitat systems require advances to overcome unprecedented demands. Designing for extreme environments like wild temperature fluctuations, radiation, dust, and meteoroid impacts necessitates a resilience-oriented design framework. Currently, there is a lack of well-established design frameworks and technologies needed for habitat systems to successfully achieve and maintain performance over extended periods.

Key hazards include:
- **Radiation:** GCR and SPE affect human/plant survivability and component functionality.
- **Temperature:** Range on the Moon (111 °C to -171 °C) leads to thermal fatigue and expansion/contraction.
- **Dust:** Impacts mechanics, electronics, and solar power generation.
- **Seismic Activity:** Moonquakes can last for hours compared to minutes for Earthquakes.

## Resilience and System Failures
Habitat systems are complex and tightly coupled. Failures may rapidly cascade. While terrestrial systems benefit from reliability-based design, extraterrestrial habitats require a resilience-based approach focusing on absorptive, restorative, and adaptive capacities. 

### A Control-theoretic Approach
A resilient habitat system design translates to maintaining performance using:
1. **Passive Controls:** Preventive capability.
2. **Adaptive Controls:** Interventive and mitigative capabilities.

## Cyber-Physical Testing of Habitat Systems
Cyber-physical testing (hybrid simulation) partitions a system into:
- **Computational Domains:** Containing reliable models of known structural parts.
- **Physical Domains:** Novel or difficult-to-model physical specimens.

This approach allows for realistic examination of complex habitat interactions without the prohibitive cost of full-scale extraterrestrial testing. It enables modeling dynamic reconfiguration, representation of hazards (radiation/atmosphere loss), and development of health management frameworks.

## Resilient ExtraTerrestrial Habitats research institute (RETHi)
RETHi was established to develop technologies for resilient habitats. It uses a cyber-physical testing platform to validate control-theoretic approaches and evaluate integrated health management models. 

## Conclusion
Resilience is a key contributor to the success of habitat systems. Achieving breakthroughs requires innovative techniques like cyber-physical testing of interconnected habitat systems to manage disruptions through the design and operational phases.
