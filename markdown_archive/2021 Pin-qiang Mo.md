# Bearing capacity of surface circular footings on granular material under low gravity fields

**Authors:** Pin-Qiang Mo, Guoqing Zhou, Feng Gao, Ruilin Li  
**Journal:** Journal of Rock Mechanics and Geotechnical Engineering 13 (2021) 612–625  
**DOI:** 10.1016/j.jrmge.2020.11.009

## Abstract
Low gravity fields have been simulated through magnetic acceleration to conduct experimental study on bearing capacity of circular footings on a type of crushable planetary regolith simulant, which has comparable density and particle size distribution of lunar soil. The load–settlement responses of surface spread footings are obtained by investigating the relative density, footing size and gravity effects. The results show that the nonlinear gravity effect is not negligible, related to stress condition, soil dilatancy and mobilised friction angle. A cone penetration test (CPT)-based method for prediction of bearing capacity is proposed, providing new understandings for the design of shallow foundations on the Moon and Mars.

## 1. Introduction
Space exploration tends to progress towards the creation of lunar or Martian bases, human habitats, and other types of space architecture. The stability of these structures is significantly dependent on the interaction between foundations and planetary regolith under low gravity fields. Gravity on the Moon is about $1.625\text{ m/s}^2$ ($g/6$), and on Mars is around $3.711\text{ m/s}^2$ ($0.38g$). Although terrestrial shallow foundations are well-studied, the effect of low gravity fields on failure mechanisms has not been adequately investigated.

## 2. Experimental Methodology
### 2.1 Geotechnical Low Gravity Model Testing (GMMT)
To provide a stable low gravity field, GMMT was applied by using magnetic acceleration in the opposite direction to Earth's gravity. A uniform magnetic field (UMF) and combined gradient magnetic field (CGMF) provide stable magnetic force to a saturated-magnetized material.

### 2.2 Material: CUMT-1c Regolith Simulant
- **Base Material:** Cement (62.5R), iron oxide powder (BMMF-1), and water (1.25:1.25:1 ratio).
- **Properties:** Crushable, magnetic-sensitive granular material.
- **Sphericity:** ~0.69.
- **Roundness:** 0.27-0.56.
- **Angle of Repose:** ~42°.

### 2.3 Testing Parameters
- **Footing sizes:** 10 mm, 15 mm, 20 mm.
- **Relative density ($D_r$):** 59%, 66%, 70%.
- **Gravity levels ($N_g$):** $g/6, g/3, 2g/3, 1g$.

## 3. Results and Analysis
### 3.1 Load-Settlement Responses
Load tests used a hyperbolic asymptote method to describe the curve of footing pressure ($q_b$) vs. pseudo-strain ($s/B$).

### 3.2 Key Equations
Ultimate bearing capacity ($q_{ult}$) prediction:
$$\frac{q_{ult}}{P_{ref}} = D_1 D_r^{D_2} \left( \frac{B}{d_{50}} \right)^{D_3} \left( \frac{\gamma B}{P_{ref}} \right)^{D_4}$$
Where $P_{ref}$ is atmospheric pressure (100 kPa) and $D_1-D_4$ are fitting parameters.

## 4. Findings & Conclusion
1. **Gravity Effect:** The nonlinear gravity effect is significant. The ultimate bearing capacity on the Moon ($g/6$) is approximately 72% of that on Earth for the same soil density/footing size, while for Mars it is ~84%.
2. **Density Influence:** Relative density has a more important effect on the mobilised friction angle and soil compressibility than gravity alone.
3. **CPT Correlation:** Bearing capacity can be reliably estimated from CPT stiffness data ($k_{i,CPT}$), avoiding uncertainties in individual soil property estimates.
