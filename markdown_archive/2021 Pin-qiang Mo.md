# Bearing capacity of surface circular footings on granular material under low gravity fields

**Authors:** Pin-Qiang Mo, Guoqing Zhou, Feng Gao, Ruilin Li
**Journal:** Journal of Rock Mechanics and Geotechnical Engineering 13 (2021) 612–625
**DOI:** 10.1016/j.jrmge.2020.11.009

## Abstract
Low gravity fields have been simulated through magnetic acceleration to conduct experimental study on bearing capacity of circular footings on a type of crushable planetary regolith simulant, which has comparable density and particle size distribution of lunar soil. The results show that the nonlinear gravity effect is not negligible, related to stress condition, soil dilatancy and mobilised friction angle. A cone penetration test (CPT)-based method for prediction of bearing capacity is proposed, aiming to improve the design of shallow foundations on the Moon and Mars.

## 1. Introduction
Space exploration tends to progress towards the creation of lunar or Martian bases, human habitats, and other types of space architecture. The stability of these structures is significantly dependent on the interaction between foundations and planetary regolith under low gravity fields. Gravity on the Moon is ~1.625 m/s² (g/6) and on Mars is ~3.711 m/s² (0.38g).

## 2. Experimental Setup: GMMT Method
The Geotechnical Magnetic-Similitude-Gravity Model Testing (GMMT) method was adopted to simulate accurate and stable low gravity fields using magnetic force to counterweight Earth's gravity. A crushable magnetic planetary regolith simulant (CUMT-1c) was used.

## 3. Results
- **Gravity Effect:** The nonlinear gravity effect is significant. As gravity decreases, the bearing capacity factor Nγ increases relative to its Earth value, but the absolute bearing capacity decreases.
- **Footing Size Effect:** Bearing capacity increases with footing size, quantitatively described by the exponent b = 0.681.
- **Soil Density:** Foundation stiffness and ultimate load on dense soil (Dr = 70%) are over 2.5 times higher than on looser soil (Dr = 59%).

## 4. Conclusions
The ultimate bearing capacity of surface circular footings on the Moon is approximately 72% (based on the gravity reduction factor) of the same footing on Earth, while on Mars it is approximately 84%. CPT-based predictions provide a reliable way to avoid uncertainties in soil property estimations at extraterrestrial sites.
