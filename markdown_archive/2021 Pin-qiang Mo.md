# Bearing capacity of surface circular footings on granular material under low gravity fields

**Authors:** Pin-Qiang Mo, Guoqing Zhou, Feng Gao, Ruilin Li
**Journal:** Journal of Rock Mechanics and Geotechnical Engineering
**Volume:** 13 (2021) 612–625
**DOI:** 10.1016/j.jrmge.2020.11.009

## Abstract
Low gravity fields have been simulated through magnetic acceleration to conduct experimental study on bearing capacity of circular footings on a type of crushable planetary regolith simulant (CUMT-1c). The results show that the nonlinear gravity effect is not negligible, related to stress condition, soil dilatancy and mobilised friction angle. A cone penetration test (CPT)-based method for prediction of bearing capacity is proposed, improving the design of shallow foundations on the Moon and Mars.

## 1. Introduction
Space exploration tends to progress towards the creation of lunar or Martian bases and human habitats. The stability of these structures is significantly dependent on the interaction between foundations and planetary regolith under low gravity fields. Gravity on the Moon is about 1.625 m/s² (g/6) and on Mars is around 3.711 m/s² (0.38g). Shallow foundations are typically used for spacecraft landers and are expected to be used for future habitats.

## 2. Methodology
### Geotechnical Low Gravity Model Testing (GMMT)
A novel physical modeling approach using magnetic acceleration was adopted to simulate stable low gravity fields. By applying a magnetic force in the opposite direction to Earth's gravity, acceleration factors ($N_g$) from 0g to 6g can be simulated.

## 3. Findings and Results
### Bearing Capacity Factors
The study found that the bearing capacity factor $N_\gamma$ decreases with absolute footing size (scale effect) and is significantly influenced by gravity (gravity effect). 

### Key Formulas
The ultimate bearing capacity $q_{ult}$ is predicted by:
$$q_{ult}/P_{ref} = D_1 D_r^{D_2} (B/d_{50})^{D_3} (\gamma B/P_{ref})^{D_4}$$
Where $P_{ref}$ is atmospheric pressure (100 kPa), $D_r$ is relative density, and $B$ is footing width.

### Specific Comparisons
- **Moon:** The ultimate bearing capacity of a surface circular footing is approximately 72% of that on Earth (for equivalent stress conditions).
- **Mars:** The reduction ratio is approximately 84% compared to Earth.

## 4. Conclusions
1. Nonlinear gravity effects on foundation stiffness and bearing capacity are significant.
2. Relative density has a more important effect on the mobilised friction angle and soil compressibility than gravity alone.
3. A CPT-based prediction method avoids uncertainties in soil property estimations and provides a reliable tool for extraterrestrial foundation design.
