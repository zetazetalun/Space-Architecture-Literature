# Review article: Prospects for creating building materials based on regolith

## Abstract
The article discusses the prospects for using lunar regolith for space construction with a promising approach to ensuring a sustainable human presence on the Moon and beyond. The key concept involves the use of resources in place (ISRU). Regolith can be processed to create durable building materials using 3D printing, melting, or sintering. Structures protect against extreme temperatures, radiation, and micrometeorites.

## 1. Introduction
NASA’s Artemis program and ESA aim for permanent human presence on the Moon by 2040. This requires infrastructure built from local resources like lunar rocks and soil.

### 2.2 Properties of Regolith
- **Mechanical:** Compressive strength of molten regolith at room temperature is 67.1 ± 31.9 MPa; flexural strength is 100.7 ± 31.3 MPa. Sintered samples achieved 37.0 ± 4.8 MPa at 1120 °C.
- **Thermal:** CTE is approximately 5 × 10^-6 °C^-1. Thermal conductivity is ~1.1 W/m·K for laser-sintered samples.
- **Radiation:** Shielding requires >200 g/cm² to reduce galactic cosmic ray throughput by >50%.

## 2.4 Application of Additive Technologies
3D printing (additive manufacturing) allows for complex geometries without tools. Technologies include sintering (microwave, solar, laser), 3D printing with Sorel cement, and phosphoric acid binders for Mars.

## 3. Conclusions
Extraterrestrial construction based on regolith is achievable. 3D printing and geopolymers from local materials are promising for sustainable lunar bases, reducing Earth-dependence.
