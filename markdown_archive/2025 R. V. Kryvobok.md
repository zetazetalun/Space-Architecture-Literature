## Abstract
The article discusses the prospects for using lunar regolith for space construction with a promising approach to ensuring a sustainable human presence on the Moon and beyond. The key concept involves the use of resources in place (ISRU). As a building material, regolith can be processed using 3D printing, melting, or sintering. Regolith structures can protect astronauts from extreme temperatures, radiation, and micrometeorites. The study analyzes mining and processing methods, new composite materials, and the application of additive technologies for extraterrestrial infrastructure.

## 1. Introduction
National space agencies (NASA, ESA) aim to establish permanent human presence on the Moon and Mars by 2040. A key requirement is the creation of infrastructure using available local resources, primarily lunar soil (regolith), which covers the lunar surface up to 15 meters deep.

## 2. Analysis of Research and Publications

### 2.1 Chemical and Mineral Composition of Lunar Regolith
Regolith contains basalt fragments (pyroxene, olivine, ilmenite) and highland fragments (anorthosite, plagioclase). Unlike terrestrial soil, it is dry, lacks organic matter, and contains glass beads from impacts.

### 2.2 Properties of Regolith
*   **Physical:** Fine, abrasive dust with particle sizes from submicron to millimeters. Surface density is 1.5 g/cm³.
*   **Mechanical:** Compressive strength of sintered regolith reaches ~37.0 ± 4.8 MPa at 1120 °C. Molten regolith samples show compressive strength of 67.1 ± 31.9 MPa and flexural strength of 100.7 ± 31.3 MPa.
*   **Thermal:** Low coefficient of thermal expansion (CTE) ~5-6 × 10⁻⁶ °C⁻¹. Thermal conductivity is ~1.1 W/m·K for laser-sintered samples.
*   **Radiation:** >200 g/cm² of regolith can result in >50% reduction in galactic cosmic ray throughput.

### 2.3 Promising Areas for Use
*   **Concrete-like materials:** Geopolymers and sulfur concrete.
*   **Glass production:** Substrates for semiconductors, windows, and thermal insulation.
*   **Mirror production:** High-quality glass with aluminum coatings (~100 nm layer).

### 2.4 Application of Additive Technologies
Three main molding principles: concrete curing, additive manufacturing (3D printing), and regolith sintering/melting. Sorel cement (magnesia cement) and phosphoric acid binders are explored, though they require terrestrial additives.

## 3. Conclusions
Extraterrestrial construction based on regolith is achievable. 3D printing is the most promising technology due to its automation and efficiency. Geopolymers and multicomponent oxide systems are critical research areas for establishing permanent bases on the Moon and Mars.
