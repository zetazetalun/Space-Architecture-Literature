# Analytical Evaluation of Stress–Strain Behavior and Reaction Mechanism of Lunar Regolith Simulant (CQU-1) Geopolymer

## Abstract
Utilizing lunar regolith as a raw material for structural components offers significant potential for future lunar exploration. Direct manufacturing from unprocessed regolith reduces the need for specialized refining equipment. This study prepared and tested forty-eight CQU-1 lunar regolith simulant geopolymer (LRSG) cylindrical specimens under axial compression. The effects of curing temperature (60 °C and 80 °C), curing time (3 d, 7 d, 14 d and 28 d), and water–binder ratio (0.325 and 0.455) were investigated. The alkali-activated CQU-1 LRSG achieved a maximum compressive strength of 33.89 MPa. Elevated curing temperatures and extended curing times enhanced peak stress and elastic modulus while reducing peak and ultimate strains. Based on test results, a stress–strain model, elastic modulus model, and peak strain model of alkali-activated CQU-1 LRSG were proposed.

## 1. Introduction
The Moon is a strategically prime target for deep-space exploration. Leading space agencies like NASA and CNSA aim to establish sustainable human presence and permanent lunar bases. Lunar construction integrating materials science and aerospace engineering is a new research frontier. While various technologies like sintering and binder-based additive manufacturing exist, alkali-activated LRSG offers acceptable mechanical properties, excellent durability, and low energy consumption.

## 5. Conclusions
1. LRSG failure patterns are analogous to concrete; higher temperatures/longer curing lead to brittle failure.
2. Maximum compressive strength reached 33.89 MPa under optimal conditions (80 °C, 28 d, W/B 0.325).
3. Increasing W/B ratio reduces compressive strength and stiffness while increasing ductility.
4. Segmental models were developed to predict stress-strain, elastic modulus, and peak strain explicitly incorporating curing conditions.
