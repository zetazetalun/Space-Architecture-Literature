# Analytical Evaluation of Stress–Strain Behavior and Reaction Mechanism of Lunar Regolith Simulant (CQU-1) Geopolymer

**Journal:** Polymers (MDPI)  
**DOI:** 10.3390/polym18080998  
**Published:** 20 April 2026

## Abstract
Utilizing lunar regolith as a raw material for structural components offers significant potential for future lunar exploration. This study investigates forty-eight CQU-1 lunar regolith simulant geopolymer (LRSG) cylindrical specimens tested under axial compression. Key variables include curing temperature (60 °C and 80 °C), curing time (3–28 days), and water–binder ratio (0.325 and 0.455). The study proposes constitutive models for stress–strain relationships, elastic modulus, and peak strain.

## 1. Introduction
Space agencies aim to establish a sustainable human presence on the Moon. Lunar construction is a new frontier integrating materials science and aerospace engineering. Alkali-activated lunar regolith simulant geopolymer (LRSG) offers a compelling combination of mechanical properties, low energy consumption, and simple preparation compared to sintering or pure binder-based methods.

## 2. Materials and Methods
- **Simulant:** CQU-1 LRS (derived from Jilin Province volcanic ash), compositionally similar to Chang’E and Apollo 14 samples.
- **Activator:** Industrial-grade sodium silicate (Na₂SiO₃) with a modulus of 3.3.
- **Specimen Preparation:** Mixing LRS with activator at W/B ratios of 0.325 and 0.455. Specimens were 75 × 150 mm cylindrical molds.
- **Curing:** Initial 24h at 30 °C, followed by oven curing at 60 °C or 80 °C for 3, 7, 14, or 28 days.

## 3. Results and Discussion
- **Failure Modes:** Higher temperatures and longer curing times led to more brittle failure (longitudinal splitting), while short-term cured specimens showed more ductility.
- **Peak Stress:** Maximum compressive strength of 33.89 MPa was achieved at 80 °C, 28 days, W/B 0.325. 
- **Modulus:** Elastic modulus ranged from 0.65 GPa to 27.99 GPa. It significantly increased with curing time and temperature.
- **W/B Ratio Effects:** Increasing the W/B ratio from 0.325 to 0.455 reduced compressive strength by up to 43.4%.

## 4. Conclusions
1. LRSG failure patterns are analogous to conventional concrete.
2. Increased curing temperature and time enhance stiffness and brittleness due to accelerated polymerization.
3. A segmental constitutive model was developed that accurately predicts stress-strain behavior (EI = 8.53% for elastic modulus).
4. Optimal parameters for CQU-1 LRSG: 80 °C curing, 28 days duration, 0.325 water-binder ratio.
5. The work confirms the viability of LRSG for future extraterrestrial construction.
