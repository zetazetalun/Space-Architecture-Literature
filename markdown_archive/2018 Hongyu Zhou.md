## Thermal and Mechanical Properties of Cementitious Composites for Additive Construction of Energy-Saving Habitats

Hongyu Zhou, Adam L. Brooks, Dominic Hanna, and Babak Salarieh
Dept. of Civil and Environmental Engineering, Univ. of Alabama in Huntsville, Huntsville, AL 35758, USA

### ABSTRACT
Lightweight cementitious composites (LWCC) are cement based material combined with lightweight fillers. Because of its advantages in reducing both structural weight and thermal conductivity, it has the potential to be used in the construction of terrestrial and extraterrestrial habits. Furthermore, since it has relatively small and spherical shaped aggregates, LWCC is also suitable for extrusion based 3-D printing. In this research, a comprehensive and thorough study was carried out to investigate the effects of different lightweight fillers on both the mechanical and thermal properties of LWCC. Various lightweight fillers including three types of hollow glass microspheres (HGM) and two fly ash cenospheres (FACs) are studied in conjunction with various particle size, shell wall thickness, and volume fractions. Both mechanical and thermophysical properties were tested. The results indicated that the thermal property of LWCC is mostly governed by the volume fraction of LWFs whereas the mechanical properties are heavily affected by the type and property of LWF particles included.

### INTRODUCTION

#### Cementitious Materials for Additive Construction
3-D printing of cementitious materials has the potential to address major challenges in the concrete industry: (i) eliminating formwork; (ii) highly automated construction reducing waste and time; and (iii) allowing parametric variation of material properties for optimal structural and energetic performance.

#### Lightweight Cementitious Composites
LWCC incorporates millimeter and micrometer size lightweight functional fillers (LWFs) such as hollow glass microspheres (HGM) and fly-ash cenospheres (FAC). These materials provide two-fold advantages: reduced structural dead load and lower thermal conductivity for energy efficiency. Their spherical shape provides controlled rheological properties for 3-D printing and long-distance pumping.

### EXPERIMENTAL PROGRAM

#### Material Preparation
Materials included ASTM Type I-II Portland cement, silica sand, water (w/c = 0.43), superplasticizer, and LWFs. Volume fractions (vf) of 7%, 14%, 21%, and 28% were tested, replacing equivalent volumes of silica sand.

#### Testing
- **Mechanical:** Tested using a MTS-810 system at 0.01 mm/min loading rate.
- **Thermal:** Tested using the Transient Plane Source (TPS) method (ISO 22007-2) to determine thermal conductivity and volumetric heat capacity.
- **Dynamic:** Tested using Split Hopkinson Pressure Bar (SHPB) for strain rates ranging from 400 s⁻¹ to 1000 s⁻¹.

#### Findings
- **Compressive Strength:** Small volume fractions (<15%) of HGMs can actually increase strength by improving rheology and reducing air content. Thicker-shelled HGMs (S60) significantly outperform thinner-shelled ones (K25) at high concentrations.
- **Thermal Properties:** Conductivity is primarily dictated by the volume fraction of the filler rather than the filler type or size. Conductivity dropped by ~30% at 7% volume fraction and reached as low as 0.62 W/m·K at high fractions.
- **Dynamic Behavior:** LWCC is strain-rate sensitive; compressive strength at a strain rate of 920 s⁻¹ was 66.7% higher than static strength.

### CONCLUSIONS
1. Thermal properties are governed by volume fraction; mechanical properties are governed by filler particle properties (shell thickness/strength).
2. HGMs and FACs are suitable for structural load-bearing lightweight composites if volume fractions are controlled.
3. The spherical shape of micro-fillers improves rheology, making these composites ideal for extrusion-based additive construction (3D printing).
4. LWCC demonstrates significant strain-rate sensitivity under dynamic impact.
