# Thermal and Mechanical Properties of Cementitious Composites for Additive Construction of Energy-Saving Habitats

**Authors:** Hongyu Zhou, Adam L. Brooks, Dominic Hanna, Babak Salarieh
**Affiliation:** Dept. of Civil and Environmental Engineering, Univ. of Alabama in Huntsville
**Source:** Earth and Space 2018, ASCE

## Abstract
Lightweight cementitious composites (LWCC) are cement-based materials combined with lightweight fillers. They offer advantages in reducing structural weight and thermal conductivity, making them potential candidates for terrestrial and extraterrestrial habitats. Due to small, spherical aggregates, LWCC is suitable for extrusion-based 3D printing. This research investigates the effects of fillers (Hollow Glass Microspheres - HGM and Fly Ash Cenospheres - FAC) on mechanical and thermal properties.

## Introduction
3D printable concrete eliminates formwork, reduces labor/waste, and allows for functionally graded material design. LWCC, by incorporating micrometer-sized functional fillers, reduces dead loads and thermal conductivity, improving energy efficiency and fire resistance.

### Key Findings
- **Compressive Strength:** Strength is a function of LWF particle size and shell thickness. Smaller particles with thicker shells (like S60) yield higher mechanical strength at high volume fractions.
- **Thermal Properties:** Thermal conductivity is primarily dictated by the volume fraction of LWF rather than filler type. Increasing LWF to ~28% reduces conductivity from 2.3 to 0.65-0.8 W/m·K.
- **Dynamic Behavior:** LWCC is strain-rate sensitive; compressive strength increases significantly under high-velocity impact (up to 66.7% higher than static strength at 920 s⁻¹).

## Conclusions
LWCC incorporating high-strength microspheres (S60) is suitable for structural load-bearing in habitats while providing superior insulation. The spherical shape of fillers improves rheology, facilitating extrusion-based additive manufacturing.
