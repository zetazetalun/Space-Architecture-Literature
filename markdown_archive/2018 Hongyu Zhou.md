# Thermal and Mechanical Properties of Cementitious Composites for Additive Construction of Energy-Saving Habitats

**Authors:** Hongyu Zhou, Adam L. Brooks, Dominic Hanna, and Babak Salarieh
**Affiliation:** Dept. of Civil and Environmental Engineering, Univ. of Alabama in Huntsville, Huntsville, AL 35758, USA

## ABSTRACT
Lightweight cementitious composites (LWCC) are cement-based materials combined with lightweight fillers. Because of its advantages in reducing structural weight and thermal conductivity, it has the potential for terrestrial and extraterrestrial habitats. Suitable for extrusion-based 3D printing due to small, spherical aggregates, this study investigates hollow glass microspheres (HGM) and fly ash cenospheres (FAC) with varying particle sizes and volume fractions. Thermal properties are found to be governed by volume fraction, whereas mechanical properties are affected by particle type.

## INTRODUCTION
3D printable concrete offers potential for in situ construction by eliminating formwork, reducing labor costs, and allowing parametric material variation for optimal performance. LWCC incorporates millimeter and micrometer fillers to reduce dead load and building energy consumption. Spherical shapes ensure controlled rheology for 3D printing and long-distance pumping.

## EXPERIMENTAL PROGRAM
- **Binder:** ASTM Type I-II Portland cement, silica sand, water, superplasticizer.
- **W/C Ratio:** 0.43.
- **Volume Fractions (vf):** 7%, 14%, 21%, and 28% replacing silica sand.
- **Mechanical Testing:** Displacement-controlled MTS-810 system (0.01 mm/min).
- **Thermal Testing:** Transient Plane Source (TPS) method (ISO 22007-2).
- **Dynamic Testing:** Split Hopkinson Pressure Bar (SHPB) for strain rates 400 s⁻¹ to 1000 s⁻¹.

## RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
### Compressive Strength
Compressive strength is a function of particle size and shell thickness. Low volume fractions (<15%) of HGMs can actually increase strength due to improved rheology and reduced air content. Smaller size HGM particles with thicker shells (S60) yield higher strength compared to K25. At 28% vf, S60 mortar was 125% stronger than K25.

### Thermal Properties
Thermal conductivity decreased by ~30% at 7% vf and dropped to 0.65-0.8 W/m·K at 28% vf (compared to 2.2 W/m·K for control). Thermal conductivity is insensitive to particle size but highly sensitive to volume fraction.

### Dynamic Behavior
LWCC is strain-rate sensitive. Compressive strength at a strain rate of 400 s⁻¹ was 32.2 MPa (40% higher than quasi-static). At 920 s⁻¹, strength was 66.7% higher than static strength.

## CONCLUSIONS
1. Thermal properties are dictated by LWF volume fraction; mechanical properties by particle stiffness and size.
2. FAC and high-density HGM (e.g., S60) are suitable for structural load-bearing lightweight composites.
3. Small LWF particles distribute well and are suitable for extrusion-based additive construction.
4. LWCC demonstrates significant strain-rate sensitivity under impact loading.
