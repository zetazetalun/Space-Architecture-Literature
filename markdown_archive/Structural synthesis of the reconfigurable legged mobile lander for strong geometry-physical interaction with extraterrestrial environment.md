# Structural synthesis of the reconfigurable legged mobile lander for strong geometry-physical interaction with extraterrestrial environment

**Authors:** Youcheng Han, Weizhong Guo, Changjie Zhao, Ziyue Li, Ze Fu, Yinghui Li
**Journal:** Mechanism and Machine Theory 189 (2023) 105446
**Date:** Available online 31 July 2023

## Abstract
To design the engineering prototype of reconfigurable legged mobile lander (ReLML) for strong geometry-physical interaction with extraterrestrial environment, this paper proposes a tailored structural synthesis methodology unifying kinematic geometry and statics. The ReLML can serve the alien base construction and adjustable launch bracket.

## 1. Introduction
As extraterrestrial exploration develops, there is a need for innovative intelligent detection equipment. Current designs include propulsive braking+wheeled landers and legged passive buffering+wheeled mobile landers. This work proposes the ReLML, which integrates legged passive buffering and legged locomotion to build lunar bases or robotized lunar bases.

## 2. Methodology
### Operation Modes
1.  **Adjusting Mode (3R):** Three rotations for geometrical adaptation to hostile terrain.
2.  **Landing Mode (2R1T):** Two rotations and one translation to survive strong impact interactions.
3.  **Roving Mode (2R1T):** Two rotations and one translation for good motion/force interactivity during locomotion.

### Synthesis Steps
- Step 1: Define mission requirements and functional modes.
- Step 2: Extract interaction characteristics (motion, force, energy).
- Step 3: Derive output finite motion characteristics of leg end-effectors.
- Step 4: Derive instantaneous force characteristics.
- Steps 5-7: Unified topology synthesis of metamorphic execution, joint, and transmission mechanisms.
- Step 8-9: Assemble reconfigurable system and evaluate topology.

## 3. Results and Discussion
The paper provides detailed algebraic representations of the screw-based virtual work for different modes. It categorizes metamorphic limb layouts into three subcategories based on degrees of freedom (4-DoF, 5-DoF, 6-DoF) and their ability to restrict translations or rotations across modes.

## 4. Conclusion
The proposed methodology guarantees mission-oriented design demands by considering strong geometry-physical interaction. The ReLML integrates folding/deployment, foothold adjustment, landing buffering, roving, and orientation adjustment, making it suitable for serving alien base construction and sample return missions.
