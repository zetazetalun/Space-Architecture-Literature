# A new deployable pantographic lunar habitat

## Abstract
This research presents a deployable lunar habitat designed to overcome cargo volume limitations. The structure utilizes linear and planar elements connected via movable joints, specifically employing modified polar and angulated scissor-like elements (SLE). The design combines rigid plates for environmental protection with an inflatable membrane for pressure retention, aiming to reduce extravehicular activity (EVA) time through a simplified deployment mechanism.

## 1. Introduction
The study categorizes lunar habitats into three classes: Class-I (pre-integrated rigid), Class-II (prefabricated deployable), and Class-III (ISRU-derived). The authors focus on Class-II hybrid structures that offer a balance between low launch volume and high radiation shielding.

## 2. Design Considerations
Key design factors include radiation, micrometeoroids, and vacuum hazards. The design prioritizes crew safety, modularity for component replacement, and commonality to reduce maintenance complexity.

## 3. Concept Assessment
The authors evaluate three existing concepts:
* **Trigon:** Robotic modular system with triangular/square panels.
* **Inflated-Torus:** Hybrid rigid-core and inflatable fabric concept.
* **Moon Base Two:** Automatic inflatable dome with regolith shielding.

Based on scoring criteria (volume expansion, subsystem integration, protection, ease of assembly, and robotics), the hybrid inflated-torus approach was selected as the baseline for the new SLE-based design.

## 4. Scissor-Hinge Systems
The design employs Scissor Structural Mechanisms (SSM) consisting of:
* **Translational Units:** Pivot in the middle, parallel unit lines.
* **Polar Units:** Pivot off-center, intersecting unit lines.
* **Angulated Units:** Identical elements connected through a kink angle to form closed-loop structures.

## 5. Design and Parameters

### Habitat Dimensions
Based on NASA standards, the design aims for a minimum Net Habitable Volume (NHV) of 25 m³ per crew member (40 m³ suggested for 180-day missions). For a crew of four, the total required floor area is calculated at 45.6 m².

### Mechanism Design
The proposed habitat includes:
* Two angulated SLE loops.
* A central structural core (1.10 m diameter).
* Modified Polar SLE (MP-SLE) roof units.
* Carbon composite shielding panels.
* Multi-layer inflatable membrane.

### Material Selection
* **Rigid Panels:** Carbon composite for radiation shielding and thermal insulation.
* **Membrane:** Multi-layer approach including Vectran or Kevlar restraint layers, redundant pressure bladders (Combitherm), and Nomex scuff protection.

The design provides 169.2 m³ of volume for a 180-day mission for four astronauts. The central telescopic jack actuation system improves deployment efficiency compared to traditional complex assembly methods.
