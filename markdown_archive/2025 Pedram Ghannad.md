## ABSTRACT

This paper investigates the risks associated with autonomous robotic construction on Mars, emphasizing a comprehensive approach to identifying, assessing, and mitigating challenges unique to extreme extraterrestrial environments. Based on a systematic review of literature from 2014 to 2024, the study focuses on five critical risk areas: atmospheric conditions, topography, energy and power requirements, communication systems, and equipment reliability. These categories inform a quantitative risk assessment method that maps risks by likelihood and impact, supporting the prioritization of mitigation strategies. Key hazards, such as dust contamination, thermal inertia, and reduced mobility, are shown to significantly affect equipment performance, energy supply, and communication systems.

## INTRODUCTION

NASA's goal of establishing a sustained human presence on Mars requires the development of innovative construction methods capable of addressing the planet's unique and extreme conditions. Advancements in Martian habitat construction have addressed various technological and methodological areas, including autonomous robotics, in-situ resource utilization (ISRU), and additive manufacturing. Proposed habitat designs include terrestrial-style structures using local materials, inflatable bases, and mobile modular units.

## METHODOLOGY

This study employs a structured methodology to identify, categorize, assess, and mitigate risks associated with autonomous robotic construction on Mars. Risks were categorized within the **3M framework**: Mother Nature (environmental), Machine (technological), and Material (resource-related). A **5x5 risk matrix** was used to assess the likelihood and impact of each identified risk.

## RESULTS, ANALYSIS, AND DISCUSSION

### Table 1. Risk Likelihood Rating Criteria

| Rating | Likelihood | Description |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 5 | Definite | Is expected to frequently occur |
| 4 | Highly Probable | Has a good chance of occurring and is not unusual |
| 3 | Possible | Likely to occur sometime |
| 2 | Negligible | Has a slight chance of occurring |
| 1 | Remote | Not impossible but only in exceptional circumstances |

### Table 2. Risk Severity Rating Criteria

| Rating | Severity | Description |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 5 | Catastrophic/Disastrous | Irreversible failure leading to mission termination |
| 4 | Major | Severe disruption with prolonged downtime |
| 3 | Moderate | Noticeable impairment causing partial loss of capabilities |
| 2 | Minor | Limited impact with manageable reduction in performance |
| 1 | Insignificant | Minimal effect with quick recovery and no lasting impact |

### 5 x 5 Risk Matrix Analysis

High-priority hazards identified include:
- **Dust accumulation on solar panels/equipment**: Score of 25 (Highest risk).
- **Reduced traction and difficult terrain**: High-risk ratings due to loose regolith.
- **Communication signal interference and thermal inertia**: Moderate risks requiring redundant systems.

## CONCLUSION

The study provides a structured framework to prioritize hazards and guide the development of targeted mitigation strategies for Martian construction. "Substructure construction" was identified as carrying the highest total risk due to foundation stability and environmental extremes, while "Finishing" ranks the lowest. Future work should focus on practical simulations and prototype testing in controlled environments.
