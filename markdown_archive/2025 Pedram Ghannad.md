# Systematic Risk Assessment and Quantification for Autonomous Robotic Construction on Mars

**Authors:** Pedram Ghannad, Ph.D.; Saratu Terreno, Ph.D.; Pooja Podduturi; and Naila Mahaveen  
**Affiliation:** Dept. of Civil Engineering and Construction, Bradley Univ., Peoria, IL.  
**Publication:** Computing in Civil Engineering 2025, © ASCE, pp. 689–697.

## ABSTRACT
This paper investigates the risks associated with autonomous robotic construction on Mars, emphasizing a comprehensive approach to identifying, assessing, and mitigating challenges unique to extreme extraterrestrial environments. Based on a systematic review of literature from 2014 to 2024, the study focuses on five critical risk areas: atmospheric conditions, topography, energy and power requirements, communication systems, and equipment reliability. These categories inform a quantitative risk assessment method that maps risks by likelihood and impact, supporting the prioritization of mitigation strategies.

## INTRODUCTION
NASA's goal of establishing a sustained human presence on Mars requires innovative construction methods addressing radiation exposure, temperature fluctuations, and resource limitations. Advancements include autonomous robotics, in-situ resource utilization (ISRU), and additive manufacturing (3D printing). Current proposed habitat designs include inflatable bases, mobile modular units, and structures using local materials.

## LITERATURE REVIEW
Key focus areas identified in previous research include:
- **Atmospheric Conditions:** Temperature extremes (-195°F to 70°F), low pressure, and pervasive dust storms affecting solar efficiency and equipment.
- **Geological and Topographical Hazards:** Rugged terrain, loose regolith, and subterranean environments like lava tubes.
- **Equipment Limitations and Durability:** Dust contamination of mechanical joints and seals, and communication delays between Earth and Mars.
- **Energy Requirements:** Limitations of solar power due to irradiance and dust; potential for nuclear or hybrid systems.

## METHODOLOGY
The study uses a **3M framework** (Mother Nature, Machine, Material) and a **5x5 risk matrix** derived from MIL-STD-882B to categorize likelihood and severity.

### Table 1. Risk Likelihood Rating Criteria
| Rating | Likelihood | Description |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 5 | Definite | Is expected to frequently occur |
| 4 | Highly Probable | Has a good chance of occurring and is not unusual |
| 3 | Possible | Likely to occur sometime |
| 2 | Negligible | Has a slight chance of occurring |
| 1 | Remote | Not impossible but only in exceptional circumstances |

### Table 2. Risk Severity Rating Criteria
| Rating | Severity | Description |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 5 | Catastrophic/Disastrous | Irreversible failure leading to mission termination |
| 4 | Major | Severe disruption with prolonged downtime |
| 3 | Moderate | Noticeable impairment causing partial loss of capabilities |
| 2 | Minor | Limited impact with manageable reduction in performance |
| 1 | Insignificant | Minimal effect with quick recovery |

## RESULTS, ANALYSIS, AND DISCUSSION
- **High-Risk Hazards:** Dust accumulation on solar panels/equipment (scored 25), reduced traction in loose regolith, and difficult terrain traversal.
- **Risk Factor Analysis (RFA):** Hierarchical ranking of risks across construction phases: Site Exploration, Site Preparation, Substructure Construction, Superstructure Construction, and Finishing.
- **Substructure Construction:** Identified as the phase carrying the highest cumulative risk due to foundation stability and environmental exposure.

## CONCLUSION
The study provides a structured roadmap for targeted mitigation. Future work will focus on expert validation, simulations, and prototype testing in controlled environments mimicking Martian conditions.
