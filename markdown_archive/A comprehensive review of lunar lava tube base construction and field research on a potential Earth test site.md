# A comprehensive review of lunar lava tube base construction and field research on a potential Earth test site

## Abstract
Lunar lava tubes are considered ideal natural shelters and scientific laboratories for lunar base construction due to their underground nature. This paper reviews geological origins, exploration history, and distribution of these tubes. It presents advantages and concepts for base construction and introduces a field study on a potential Earth analog site in Hainan, China. Challenges in geotechnical engineering, including excavation, reinforcement, and in-situ testing, are discussed.

## 1. Introduction
Lunar lava tubes offer a stable temperature environment, low radiation levels, and protection from meteorite impacts. Recent research focuses on utilizing these natural caves for Class III habitats, often integrating Class II inflatable modules within the volcanic structures.

## 3. Lunar lava tube base
### 3.1. Advantages
- **Stable temperature:** Temperatures inside lunar lava tubes (near Mare Tranquillitatis) are estimated to be around 17°C, compared to the extreme fluctuations on the lunar surface (-170°C to 110°C).
- **Radiation protection:** A regolith thickness of 1–6 m makes detrimental radiation effects from SPEs and GCRs negligible. Lava tube roofs are often much thicker, providing robust protection.
- **Meteorite and Dust protection:** Subterranean passages shield habitats from cosmic micrometeorites and minimize movement of charged lunar dust.

### 3.2. Lava tube based lunar base concepts
Four major concepts are reviewed:
1. **"Green" habitat (Grandl, 1985):** In Mare Tranquillitatis Pit, accommodating 100 residents.
2. **Swarm Habitat (Mizuguchi and Ikeda, 2023):** 100 truncated octahedron modules designed for irregular terrain.
3. **Kalita et al. (2021) concept:** Spanning a skylight with living spaces and underground modules.
4. **Harbin Institute of Technology concept (2023):** Utilizes radar exploration, drilling projectiles, inflatable modules, and 3D printing of lunar soil bricks to create a living chamber.

## 4. Analog sites
### 4.2. Field mapping and laboratory testing of lava tubes in Haikou
The "Seventy-Two Caves" in Haikou, Hainan (19.925°N, 110.219°E) was surveyed. 
- **Dimensions:** Total length >780 m, passage height 3–4 m, width ~20 m.
- **Basalt Properties:** Density 2.152–2.680 g/cm³, Poisson's ratio 0.22–0.3.
- **Chemical Composition:** High similarity to Apollo 14 lunar regolith samples (SiO2, Al2O3, FeO, CaO).

## 5. Challenges and opportunities for geotechnical engineering
- **Cave exploration:** Developing rovers with 3D laser scanners and microseismic sensors.
- **In-situ testing:** Understanding the interaction between drilling equipment and rocks in microgravity/vacuum.
- **Excavation & Reinforcement:** Evaluating mechanical drilling vs. laser melting for tube entrance stabilization.

## 6. Conclusions
Lunar lava tubes provide a viable path for sustained human presence. Geotechnical engineering is critical for solving structural stability, excavation, and ISRU material utilization problems.
