## Geotechnical Engineering Considerations for Extraterrestrial Infrastructure Construction

James K. Mitchell, Sc.D., P.E., Dist. M. ASCE and W. David Carrier III, Sc.D., P.E., Fellow, Life M. ASCE

**ABSTRACT:** Detailed knowledge of soils and rocks is needed for the design of foundations, excavation, and underground space utilization on the Moon, Mars, and asteroids. In-situ resource utilization (ISRU) will play a major role due to transportation costs. This paper summarizes current knowledge of rocks and soils on the Moon and Mars, focusing on geotechnical properties and implications for infrastructure construction.

### THE NATURE AND GEOTECHNICAL PROPERTIES OF THE SOILS

#### Moon
Lunar soil (regolith) is a broadly-graded silty fine sand to sandy silt (SM or ML). Engineering properties depend primarily on relative density, which increases rapidly with depth:
- 0 to 5 cm: 0-30% (loose)
- 5 to 10 cm: >65% (medium to dense)
- Below 30 cm: >90% (very dense)

#### Mars
Mars regolith is more complex due to volcanic, hydro, and aeolian processes. It contains silty fine sands similar to the Moon but with higher variability, including gravel and boulder-sized rocks. Rover tracks suggest soil cohesion and densification under wheels.

### GEOTECHNICAL ENGINEERING CONSIDERATIONS
- **Trafficability:** Generally manageable, but soft zones (as seen with Spirit) and rocky areas pose risks.
- **Dust:** Creates health and equipment risks (mitigation required).
- **Foundations:** Lunar soil can support high loads on shallow foundations; Mars is site-specific.
- **Excavation:** Drill holes on the Moon remain open, allowing stable vertical walls for excavations.
- **ISRU:** Excavated soil exhibits bulking when recompacted; local materials will be used for oxygen extraction and parts fabrication.
