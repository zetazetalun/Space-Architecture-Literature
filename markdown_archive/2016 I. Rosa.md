# Multiscale Modeling and Testing of Protein-Bound Regolith and Soils\n\nI. Rosa, M. D. Lepech, and D. J. Loftus (ASCE 2016)\n\n## ABSTRACT\nExtraterrestrial construction presents challenges due to limited naturally occurring resources. This work focuses on protein-bound soils (regolith) produced by mixing soil, water, and a biopolymer binder (protein). The composite achieves compressive strength comparable to ordinary Portland cement concrete. This paper proposes a multi-scale framework (nano to macro) to model fracture properties and design durable materials for extreme environments.\n\n## INTRODUCTION\nRegolith is abundant on the Moon and Mars and can provide raw materials for protective structures against radiation and meteorite impacts. Protein-bound regolith offers a low-energy, in situ construction material. Previous studies showed bovine-derived polymers (BSA) can bind regolith simulant, resulting in strengths similar to concrete. However, high porosity (20-40%) and compliant binders (Young's modulus ~4 MPa) distinguish it from traditional cementitious materials.\n\n## MULTISCALE FRAMEWORK\n1. **Nanoscale**: Nanoindentation tests elastic response of the protein binder.\n2. **Microscale**: Statistically Equivalent Periodic Unit Cells (SEPUC) capture interactions between soil, protein, and voids based on MicroCT data.\n3. **Mesoscale**: Rigid Body Spring Model (RBSM) simulates crack propagation and predicts strength.\n4. **Macroscale**: Triaxial and uniaxial tests (aided by 3D Digital Image Correlation) validate numerical models.\n\n## KEY MECHANICAL PROPERTIES\n- **Compressive Strength**: ~12 MPa (compared to ~35 MPa for cement mortar).\n- **Stiffness (Young's Modulus)**: ~1.2 GPa (compared to ~22 GPa for mortar).\n- **Porosity**: 20-40% (compared to 7-20% for concrete).\n- **Binder content**: 5-15% by weight.\n\n## CONCLUSIONS\nThe multiscale approach allows for tailoring mix designs for varying environments and loading requirements. Future work aims to optimize mix designs and extend modeling to full-scale extraterrestrial structures.
