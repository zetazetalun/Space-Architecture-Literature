# Eco-friendly additive construction: Analysis of the printability of earthen-based matrices stabilized with potato starch gel and sisal fibers\n\n**Authors:** Guido Silva, Robert Ñañez, Diana Zavaleta, Valeria Burgos, Suyeon Kim, Gaby Ruiz, Miguel A. Pando, Rafael Aguilar, Javier Nakamatsu\n**Journal:** Construction and Building Materials 347 (2022) 128556\n**Publisher:** Elsevier\n\n## Abstract\n3D printing for construction purposes is a disruptive technology with the potential for rapid and massive applications making it a feasible alternative for social housing, temporary shelters after disasters, and, recently, for extraterrestrial habitats. However, most of the matrices used for construction by 3D printing are based on hydraulic cement as the main constituent... This article studies the printable capabilities of eco-friendly earthen-based matrices which uses potato starch as a natural stabilizer for raw soil mortars aiming to obtain 3D printed filaments with adequate fresh and hardened-states properties. The aqueous starch gel was combined with sisal fibers... The results indicate that printable earthen-based matrices reinforced with 1 % of sisal fibers by weight of soil and stabilized with aqueous starch gels with concentrations up to 5 % (w/w) showed improved workability and minor cracking and can be used for 3D printing.\n\n## 1. Introduction\n3D printing applied for construction purposes allows building physical assets from digital and versatile designs using layer-by-layer deposition of fresh extrudable materials... The current literature indicates that the use of this technology for construction would generate socio-environmental, economic, and design flexibility benefits. Regarding the socio-environmental impact, a construction process using 3D printing would produce less material waste...\n\n## 2. Additive construction using earthen-based matrices\n### 2.1. Summary of recent advances\nRecently, there has been an increased interest in using soil as the base material for 3D printing construction. This is because of its environmental and economic benefits: local materials can be used, and low-environmental impact industrial processes are required for making them suitable for building purposes.\n\n### 2.2. Colibri: A prototype 3D printing system for earthen-based construction\nColibri, a 3D printing system scaled prototype for earthen-based construction, was developed in-house at PUCP... The Colibri steel gantry provides a practical printing volume of 1 × 1 × 1 m, suitable for 3D printing of small to medium-size specimens...\n\n## 3. Analysis of printability capabilities of earthen-based filaments using Colibri 3D printing system\n### 3.1. Description of the materials\n* **Base soil:** Low plasticity sandy clay (CL) based on USCS. Contains 58.6% SiO2 and 15.6% Al2O3.\n* **Natural chemical stabilizer:** Food-grade potato starch.\n* **Fiber reinforcement:** Sisal fibers obtained from Agave sisalana (approx 10 mm length).\n\n### 3.2. Description of the experimental program\nMatrices included EW (soil + water), ES (soil + starch gel), and EFS (soil + starch gel + sisal fibers) series. Tests involved shrinkage cracking, buildability, open time (Vicat needle), and mechanical properties.\n\n## 4. Results and discussions\n### 4.1. Experimental results for earthen matrices in the fresh state\nFilament printing, shear vane, and cylinder stability tests confirmed extrudability and pumpability. Fibers allowed the continuous extrusion without issue, mitigating some viscosity challenges inherent to higher starch concentrations.\n\n### 4.2. Experimental results for earthen matrices during hardening and in the hardened-state\nShrinkage Cracking: The control EW(20) filament showed a critical central crack... none of the stabilized fiber reinforced filaments, EFS2.5 %(24) and EFS5.0 %(27), showed any cracks.\nMechanical Strength: Uniaxial compression tests revealed significant improvement. The results indicate increased strengths of up to 116%, 135%, and 157% at 3, 7, and 28 days of age, respectively, achieved when compared with the control matrices.\nCapillarity Absorption: Calculated water absorption coefficient for the control sample EW(20) was 0.611 kg/m2min0.5, while filaments incorporating starch gel exhibited much lower water absorption coefficients (0.1049 and 0.0903 kg/m2min0.5).\n\n## 5. Conclusions\nThis work presented the construction of the Colibri 3D printing prototype system... A composite matrix of soil that incorporates a 5 % starch gel and sisal fibers showed the best characteristics for construction applications. This composite evidenced a yield strength of 4.3 kPa, a compressive strength of 2.2 MPa, and a higher stiffening rate than soil–water matrices.
