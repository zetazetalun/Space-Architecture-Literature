# Multi-objective design optimization of clam-inspired drilling into the lunar regolith\n\n## Authors\nLiang Zhang, Lei Wang, Quan Sun, Jesus Badal, Qiushi Chen\n\n## Abstract\nCharacterization of the lunar regolith is critical for the extraction and utilization of in situ resources for building a permanent base on the Moon. This paper presents a two-stage multi-objective optimization design framework for a clam-inspired drill into lunar regolith. Using discrete element method (DEM) modeling calibrated with lunar highlands simulant (LHS-1), both geometry and controlling strategies are optimized. Geometry parameters include anchor height, shape, and cone apex angle, while controlling strategies include downward, rotation, and expansion velocities, and anchor-cone distance. A Pareto front is used to identify the most preferred designs to minimize construction cost and power consumption while maximizing drilling effectiveness and efficiency.\n\n## 1 Introduction\nArtemis missions highlight the necessity of using in situ resources on the Moon for long-term space exploration. Regolith characterization is essential for construction and habituation. Challenges include different soil properties, lack of human presence, high transportation costs, and extreme environmental conditions. Bio-inspired strategies, such as clam-inspired drilling, offer potential for high efficiency and low-cost sustainable designs.\n\n## 2 Two-stage multi-objective design optimization framework\n### 2.1 Numerical modeling\n- **Software**: Particle Flow Code (PFC3D) version 7.0.\n- **Simulant**: Lunar highlands simulant (LHS-1).\n- **Parameters**: Low gravity (1.625 m/s²), spherical balls (0.004 m diameter), linear contact model with rolling friction.\n\n### 2.2 Informed design decision\n- **Stage 1**: Optimize geometry (G) to minimize construction cost (C) and power consumption (W).\n- **Stage 2**: Optimize controlling strategies (CS) to maximize drilling effectiveness (E1) and efficiency (E2).\n\n## 3 Results\n- **Drill Geometry**: Cylinder-shaped anchors and sharp tips are effective in reducing penetration resistance. The optimal geometry identified is H=3r, b=5r, and alpha=60 degrees.\n- **Controlling Strategies**: Low downward velocity (VD), high rotation velocity (VR), and closer anchor-cone distance (L) contribute to decreased penetration resistance. The final optimal design is VD = 0.01 m/s, VR = 0, VE = 0.01, and L = 3r.\n\n## 4 Conclusion\n- Bio-inspired self-penetration robots are efficient for lunar regolith characterization.\n- Optimized geometry significantly reduces power consumption and construction costs.\n- The framework provides a guide for extraterrestrial exploration tools.
