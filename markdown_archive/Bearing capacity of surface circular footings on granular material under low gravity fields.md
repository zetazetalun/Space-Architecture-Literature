# Bearing capacity of surface circular footings on granular material under low gravity fields

**Authors:** Pin-Qiang Mo, Guoqing Zhou, Feng Gao, Ruilin Li  
**Journal:** Journal of Rock Mechanics and Geotechnical Engineering 13 (2021) 612–625  
**DOI:** 10.1016/j.jrmge.2020.11.009

## Abstract
Low gravity fields were simulated through magnetic acceleration to study the bearing capacity of circular footings on a crushable planetary regolith simulant. The study investigates relative density, footing size, and gravity effects. Results show that the nonlinear gravity effect is significant and relates to stress conditions, soil dilatancy, and mobilised friction angle. A CPT-based method for bearing capacity prediction is proposed for Moon and Mars foundation design.

## 1. Introduction
As space exploration moves towards creating lunar or Martian bases and human habitats, the stability of space architecture depends on foundation-regolith interaction under low gravity (Moon: 1.625 m/s²; Mars: 3.711 m/s²). Isolated bearing pads are currently the typical foundation for spacecraft (e.g., Lunar Module, InSight).

## 2. Geotechnical Low Gravity Model Testing
### 2.1 GMMT Method
The Geotechnical Magnetic-Similitude-Gravity Model Testing (GMMT) method applies a magnetic force counteracting Earth's gravity to simulate low-g environments accurately.

### 2.2 Material: CUMT-1c
- **Type:** Crushable magnetic-type planetary regolith simulant.
- **Composition:** Mixture of cement, iron oxide powder, and water, crushed to match lunar soil size distribution.
- **Properties:** Specific gravity 2.93, mean particle size 0.12 mm, angle of repose ~42°.

## 3. Results of Loading Tests
### 3.1 Bearing Capacity Factor (Nγ)
The bearing capacity factor decreases with absolute footing size ('scale effect'). Gravity has a more pronounced influence than footing size alone.

## 4. CPT-Based Prediction
A miniature penetrometer (8 mm diameter) was used. Penetration resistance (qc) increases with relative density and gravity. The gradient of qc (CPT stiffness) correlates strongly with the footing's ultimate bearing capacity (qult), allowing for field predictions without complex soil property estimations.

## 5. Conclusions
- Nonlinear gravity effects are not negligible in foundation design.
- Relative density affects soil compressibility and shear strength more significantly than footing size.
- The CPT-based method provides a robust tool for extraterrestrial geotechnical engineering and lunar/Martian base planning.
