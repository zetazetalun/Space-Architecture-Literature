# Kitchen waste bone-driven enzyme-induced calcium phosphate precipitation under microgravity for space biocementation

**Journal:** Biogeotechnics 4 (2026) 100156  
**Authors:** Zhen Yan, Kazunori Nakashima, Chikara Takano, Satoru Kawasaki  

## Abstract
This study validates the feasibility of extracting calcium and phosphorus from kitchen waste bones for crude enzyme-induced calcium phosphate precipitation (EICPP) under both normal and microgravity conditions. The experimental results demonstrate no significant differences in the degree of reaction and characteristics of precipitation between these environments. By leveraging local resources, reducing material transport costs, and addressing waste management challenges, this research underscores the potential for extraterrestrial construction, thereby enhancing sustainability in space environments.

## 1. Introduction
The advancement of human exploration necessitates In-Situ Resource Utilization (ISRU) to reduce dependence on Earth-transported materials. Biocementation, specifically Enzyme-Induced Calcium Phosphate Precipitation (EICPP), is explored as a sustainable method using kitchen waste bones as calcium/phosphorus sources and crude soybean enzymes as urease sources.

## 2. Materials and Methods
### 2.1 Extraction
*   **Bone Powder:** Kitchen waste pig bones ground to <150 μm, dissolved in 2 mol/L HCl.
*   **Crude Enzyme:** Soybeans ground to <150 μm, extracted in distilled water (50 g/L), resulting in a urease activity of 11.2 U.
*   **Reaction Equation:** 
    Ca10(PO4)6(OH)2 + 8 HCl → 10 Ca2+ + 6 HPO4^2− + 8 Cl− + 2 H2O

### 2.2 Microgravity Simulation
Microgravity ($10^{-3}$ g) was simulated using a Kitagawa Zeromo CL-1000 clinostat machine at 25 °C for 24 hours.

## 3. Results and Discussion
*   **Reaction Monitoring:** pH increased with urea content due to hydrolysis. Final pH values remained near-neutral, favoring ammonium recovery and ammonia control.
*   **Precipitation Analysis:** SEM images confirmed that morphology under microgravity was indistinguishable from normal gravity. XRD and EDS identified **hydroxyapatite** [Ca10(PO4)6(OH)2] as the dominant crystalline phase.
*   **Conclusion on Microgravity:** Gravity-driven convection is minimal for small precipitate particles, explaining the consistent reaction rates across gravity conditions.

## 4. Conclusion
EICPP is a viable solution for extraterrestrial construction, especially during settlement expansion where organic waste (bone/urea) increases. The process is effective in microgravity and offers biosecurity advantages over microbial methods.
