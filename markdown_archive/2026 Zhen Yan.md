# Vacuum effects on Lunar/Martian simulant consolidation via enzyme-induced carbonate precipitation

**Authors:** Zhen Yan, Satoru Kawasaki
**Journal:** Advances in Space Research 77 (2026) 12577–12587
**Received:** 16 April 2025; **Accepted:** 12 March 2026; **Available online:** 17 March 2026

## Abstract
As space exploration advances, the development of construction materials suitable for extraterrestrial environments has become a critical research focus. Biocement, due to its sustainability and potential applications with in-situ resource utilization (ISRU), has emerged as a promising approach for extraterrestrial construction. However, its feasibility under extreme space conditions, particularly in vacuum environments, remains largely unexplored. This study investigates the effects of vacuum conditions on the consolidation of lunar and Martian regolith simulants via enzyme-induced carbonate precipitation and compares the results with those under atmospheric pressure. Experimental results indicate that while vacuum conditions have little effect on precipitation content, they significantly reduce the unconfined compressive strength of the consolidated samples. This reduction is primarily attributed to the rapid volatilization of water and ammonia under vacuum, leading to increased porosity and a looser microstructure, which weakens the mechanical performance.

## 1. Introduction
ISRU technologies are essential to reduce the costs of transporting building materials from Earth. Biocementation can harness metabolic pathways such as urea hydrolysis to form calcium carbonate (CaCO3), enhancing soil properties. Enzyme-induced carbonate precipitation (EICP) offers advantages over MICP by eliminating bacterial contamination risks and not requiring oxygen.

## 2. Materials and Methods
### 2.1. Cementing solution and simulants
- **Urease source:** Crude soybean urease (50 g/L powder, ~11 U/mL activity).
- **Cementing solution:** 0.5 M Urea and 0.5 M Calcium Chloride (final concentration).
- **Simulants:** Lunar Highland Simulant (LHS-1) and Martian Global Simulant (MGS-1).

### 2.2. Consolidation of simulants
- **Conditions:** Vacuum (~83.89% vacuum level, -0.085 MPa gauge) vs. Atmospheric pressure.
- **Cycles:** 1, 2, 3, and 4 cycles.
- **Temperature:** 20 °C (reaction maintained).

## 3. Results and Discussion
- **Microstructure:** Vacuum-treated samples exhibit larger pores due to rapid volatilization of water and ammonia.
- **XRD Analysis:** No significant changes in the crystalline phase (Aragonite/Calcite) were observed between vacuum and atmospheric conditions.
- **Precipitation Content:** Variations were small; vacuum conditions resulted in slightly lower content (decrease < 1%).
- **UCS Performance:** UCS under vacuum was significantly lower than atmospheric pressure. For LHS-1 (4 cycles), UCS was ~120.80 kPa (vacuum) vs. ~581.48 kPa (atmospheric).

## 4. Conclusions
EICP remains chemically viable in vacuum, but consolidation effectiveness is limited by structural changes at the microscale (increased porosity). Future research should focus on additives to increase viscosity and reduce liquid evaporation.
