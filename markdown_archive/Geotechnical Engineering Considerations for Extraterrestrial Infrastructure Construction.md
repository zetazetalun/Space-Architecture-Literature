# Geotechnical Engineering Considerations for Extraterrestrial Infrastructure Construction

**Authors:** James K. Mitchell and W. David Carrier III

**Abstract:** Detailed knowledge of soils and rocks is needed for designing foundations, excavation, and underground space utilization on the Moon and Mars. ISRU will play a major role due to transport costs. This paper summarizes current knowledge of extraterrestrial soil compositions, geotechnical properties, and in-situ states.

## The Nature of Lunar Soils
Lunar soil (regolith) is a broadly-graded silty fine sand to sandy silt (SM or ML). Its engineering properties depend primarily on relative density, which increases rapidly with depth:
- 0-5 cm: 0-30% (loose)
- 5-10 cm: &gt;65% (medium to dense)
- 10-30 cm: &gt;80% (dense)
- &gt;30 cm: &gt;90% (very dense)

## The Nature of Martian Soils
Martian soil is more complex due to aeolian (wind), volcanic, and hydrological history. It includes gravel, cobbles, and indurated layers (duricrust).
- Bulk density: 1.0 to 1.8 g/cm3
- Internal friction angle: 30&deg; to 45&deg;
- Cohesion: 0.1 to 1.0 kPa

## Geotechnical Engineering Considerations
1. **Trafficability:** Generally feasible, but rocky areas and soft zones (like those encountered by Spirit) pose risks.
2. **Dust Mitigation:** Significant risk to health and equipment due to electrostatics and adhesion.
3. **Foundations:** Lunar soil can support high loads on shallow foundations. Martian bearing capacity is site-specific.
4. **Excavation:** Lunar boreholes remain open; vertical excavations to &gt;2m are possible.
5. **ISRU:** Essential for oxygen and structural material production.
