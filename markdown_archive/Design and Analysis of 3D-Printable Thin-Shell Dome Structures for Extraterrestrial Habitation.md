# Design and Analysis of 3D-Printable Thin-Shell Dome Structures for Extraterrestrial Habitation

**Authors:** Matthew Troemner, Elham Ramyar, Benton Johnson, and Gianluca Cusatis
**Affiliations:** Northwestern University; Skidmore, Owings & Merrill LLP
**Publication:** Earth and Space 2021, ASCE

## Abstract
Extreme environmental conditions and novel construction techniques dictate the form of Martian habitats. This paper presents the design and analysis of a dome-shaped Martian habitat developed for NASA’s 3D-Printed Habitat Challenge. The structure utilizes a composite hemispheric-parabolic dome optimized for 3D printing with 'Marscrete' (Martian regolith and sulfur) over an inflatable pressure vessel. Structural performance is assessed under Martian gravity, wind, regolith deposition, and meteorite impacts.

## Introduction
Martian missions require semi-permanent structures using in situ resource utilization (ISRU) to reduce transit mass. NASA's 3D-Printed Habitat Challenge (2015) set requirements for a 1-year mission for 4 astronauts, providing 93 m² of living space and including all necessary life support systems (ECLSS).

## Design Basis
- **Form:** Internal hemispherical dome with an outer parabolic dome structure.
- **Optimization:** Minimized overhanging regions and maximized continuity to reduce 'cold joints'.
- **Material:** Marscrete (sulfur-based concrete).
- **Thickness:** Variable shell thickness from 21 to 108 cm.

## Construction Method
1. **Site Preparation:** Autonomous rovers clear debris and prepare a shallow foundation.
2. **Foundation:** 3D-printed using an articulated arm-type printer.
3. **Deployment:** A collapsible inflatable pressure vessel is deployed and inflated to serve as a backing/support for the shell.
4. **Shell Printing:** A small crawler robot circumnavigates the pressure vessel to print the shell from bottom to top.
5. **Completion:** An oculus is affixed at the apex; the pressure vessel is slightly deflated to allow for thermal expansion/contraction.

## Environment and Loading
- **Gravity:** 3.71 m/s² (38% of Earth's).
- **Wind Load:** Calculated at 9.79 Pa (conservative estimate at 70 mph), increased to 60.0 Pa for analysis.
- **Sand/Regolith Buildup:** Static pressure load of 2,780 Pa (0.5 m depth, 1,500 kg/m³ density).
- **Meteorite Impact:** Critical load identified as a 7,390g meteorite at 6.29 km/sec.

## Load Analysis Results
- Finite element static analysis (FEA) using shell and solid elements.
- **Outcome:** Structural response remained in the elastic region under all loads (ASCE-7 combinations).
- **Deformation:** Less than 1.0 mm under expected loads.

## Meteorite Impact Performance
- **Simulation:** Lattice Discrete Particle Model (LDPM).
- **Critical Impact:** 7,390 grams mass (3.5 g/cm³) at 6.29 km/sec.
- **Risk Assessment:** The probability of a hazardous meteorite (>12,022g entry mass) impacting the 300 m² site during a one-year mission is approximately 6.9 × 10⁻⁹.

## Proposed Layout
- **Central Area:** Round common area for recreation/relaxation with a transparent oculus.
- **Perimeter:** Semicircular regions for kitchen/workspaces/lab and private bedrooms/lavatory.
- **Logistics:** Includes two external entries: one personnel airlock and one docking port for an MMSEV.

## Conclusions
The hemispheric-parabolic dome surpasses structural and functional requirements for a Martian habitat. The construction scheme eliminates the need for temporary printed supports by using an inflatable internal form.
