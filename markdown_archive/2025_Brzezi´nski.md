# Lunar Regolith Improvement by Inducing Interparticle Adhesion with Capillary Forces

**Authors:** Karol Brzeziński, Joanna Julia Sokołowska, Bartłomiej Przybyszewski  
**Journal:** Materials 2025, 18, 2390  
**Published:** 20 May 2025  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.3390/ma18102390

## Abstract
This paper concerns the assessment of the lunar regolith ability to consolidate in the presence of liquid water and develop and sustain cohesion after drying. This type of cohesion originates from interparticle adhesion and can be potentially improved through grading modification. The research was conducted using the lunar regolith simulant (EAC-1A). It was shown that simple mechanical grinding enabled the improvement of interparticle adhesion with capillary forces, resulting in improved flexural strength of the consolidated material (0.123 MPa).

## 1. Introduction
The exploration of the Moon requires the creation of a stationary base for people, a "lunar habitat". Scientists are increasingly opting for solutions that maximize the use of resources available locally on the Moon (ISRU), with minimal use of raw materials supplied from Earth. Concepts for modifying locally obtained lunar materials such as lunar regolith are being investigated. 

## 2. Materials and Methods
### 2.1. Material
EAC-1A lunar regolith simulant was used. It is a basanite material reproducing the particle size distribution (PSD) of real lunar soil.

### 2.2. Specimen Preparation and Testing
Specimens (20 mm × 20 mm × 80 mm) were prepared by:
1. Mixing 400 g LRS with 100 g distilled water.
2. Oven-drying at 127 °C for 22 h.
3. Testing flexural strength in a 3-point flexural test with 50 mm support spacing.

## 3. Results and Discussion
- **Original Simulant:** Developed a mean flexural strength of 0.029 MPa.
- **Ground Simulant:** Developed a mean flexural strength of 0.123 MPa.
- **Comparison:** Grinding the material increased the specific surface area (SPA) by over 2.5 times and improved the strength by four times. This is attributed to the creation of "solid bridges" between particles.

## 4. Conclusions
The research confirms it is possible to stabilize lunar regolith via capillary consolidation without additives. Grinding enables significant strength improvement. The water used in the process is recoverable and can be recycled, fitting within a closed-loop ISRU framework.
