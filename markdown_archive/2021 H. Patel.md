# Applying Terrestrial Geo-Material Science Methodology to Lunar ISRU Construction

**Authors:** H. Patel, P. Suermann, N. Shumaker, S. Deitrick, J. W. Bullard, and R. C. Ewing

**Published in:** Earth and Space 2021

## Abstract
Building on research for military and expeditionary construction, this research aims to establish methods for the design and testing of material mixtures derived from the lunar surface with minimal additives. The paper reviews opportunities and challenges of methods like concreting and sintering and discusses the need for highland-specific regolith simulants for the Artemis missions.

## Introduction
There is a pressing need for construction methods using in-situ materials for critical lunar infrastructure like landing pads, roads, and radiation shielding. The lack of in-situ geotechnical site assessment on the Moon necessitates predictive methodologies for geotechnical properties.

## Background: Lunar surface environment
*   **Mare Regions:** ~15% of surface, basaltic, relatively young, thinner regolith.
*   **Highlands:** Anorthositic, much older, thicker regolith, higher agglutinate content.
*   **Regolith Properties:** Fine-grained (avg 72 μm), bulk density 1.30 to 1.92 g/cm³. 95% of mass <1.37 mm.

### Agglutinates
Fused particles of impact glass and mineral fragments. Content ranges from 5% to 65% by volume. They introduce unique chemical and physical properties that affect construction material performance.

## Construction Methods and Considerations
1.  **Concreting:** Binding grains in a matrix. Challenges include scarcity of water and calcium on the Moon. Alternatives include geopolymers (aluminosilicate precursors) and sulfur binders (melting point ~115°C).
2.  **Sintering:** Fusing grains via heat. Lunar regolith mineral constituents (aluminosilicates) have relatively low melting temperatures (800°C to 1000°C). Challenges include the coarse particle size (70 μm) compared to industrial sintering powders.
3.  **Additive Construction:** Can be extrusion-based (concreting) or powder-bed based (sintering).

## Methodology: THAMES
The Thermodynamic Hydration and Microstructure Evolution Simulator (THAMES) is used to predict mineralogical components and simulate 3D virtual microstructures to calculate engineering properties of binders.

## Conclusion
The research aims to identify how existing terrestrial models can be adapted to evaluate regolith-based materials under lunar conditions, eventually leading to ASCE/ASTM standards for ISRU construction.
