# Applying Terrestrial Geo-Material Science Methodology to Lunar ISRU Construction

**Authors:** H. Patel, P. Suermann, N. Shumaker, S. Deitrick, J. W. Bullard, and R. C. Ewing  
**Publication:** Earth and Space 2021, pp. 171-180  
**Publisher:** ASCE

## Abstract
Building on research for military and expeditionary construction, this research aims to establish methods for the design and testing of material mixtures derived from the lunar surface with minimal additives. The paper reviews challenges of concreting and sintering using regolith simulants representative of the lunar highlands, particularly regarding mineralogical and agglutinated properties.

## Introduction
Critical infrastructure such as landing pads, roads, berms, and radiation shielding is required for lunar occupation. On Earth, geotechnical site assessments are standard; on the Moon, there is a knowledge gap in predicting geotechnical properties for engineering. ISRU is essential due to the cost of importing resources from Earth.

## Lunar Surface Environment
- **Terrain Classes:** Mare (15% of surface, basaltic flows, younger) and Highlands (Anorthosite, older, thicker regolith, higher crater density).
- **Regolith Composition:** Broadly-graded silty sands/sandy silts. ~95% by mass < 1.37 mm; ~50% < 50 μm. Bulk density: 1.30 to 1.92 g/cm³.
- **Agglutinates:** Fused particles of impact glass, rock, and mineral fragments. Content ranges from 5% to 65% by volume. They introduce unique chemical and physical properties affecting material mixtures.

## Construction Methods and Considerations

### Concreting
- **Challenges:** Scarcity of calcium and liquid water. 
- **Proposed Solutions:** Geopolymers (aluminosilicate precursors) and sulfur binders (melting point ~115 °C). Sulfur binders are volatile and have poor durability under thermal cycling.

### Sintering
- **Mechanism:** Fusing grains using heat (60% to 80% of melting temperature).
- **Lunar Advantages:** No water required; works in vacuum. 
- **Barriers:** Coarse particle size (70 μm vs. 7 μm for standard ceramics) requires higher temperatures or partial melting.
- **Advanced Methods:** Spark Plasma Sintering (SPS) uses pulsed electric current and uniaxial pressure to achieve high density quickly.

## Methodology: THAMES Model
The **Thermodynamic Hydration and Microstructure Evolution Simulator (THAMES)** is being adapted to predict stable mineralogical components and 3D microstructures for lunar materials. It uses geochemical thermodynamic methods to calculate engineering properties from virtual microstructures.

## Materials Characterization
- **Simulants:** Most studies use JSC-1A (mare-like). New simulants like those from Outward Technologies include agglutinates to better represent highland regions targeted by Artemis.
- **Basalt Glass Fiber:** Can be created by melting regolith; valuable for reinforcement without requiring polymer binders if directly sintered.

## Conclusion
The research aims to adapt existing models to evaluate regolith-based materials under lunar conditions, eventually leading to ASCE or ASTM standards for ISRU construction.
