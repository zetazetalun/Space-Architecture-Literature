# Exploration of in-situ utilization of extraterrestrial resources: Preparation of Chang’E-5 lunar regolith simulant geopolymers modified by Al2O3 and FeTiO3 under vacuum environment

**Authors:** Mengzhao Ding, Yanjie Shi, Huimin Han, Lida Luo, Qingwei Wang
**Journal:** Construction and Building Materials
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.conbuildmat.2025.143672

## Abstract
Geopolymer solidification is considered a promising strategy for in situ resource utilization (ISRU) in lunar base construction through the synthesis of building materials from lunar regolith. In this study, a Chang’E-5 lunar regolith simulant (CE-5 LRS) named DH-1 was used to prepare geopolymers under simulated lunar conditions. The effects of curing temperature, atmosphere, and additives (γ-Al2O3 and FeTiO3) were systematically investigated. Higher average curing temperatures (36.5–76.2 °C) improved mechanical performance. Vacuum-cured specimens exhibited a 62.7% increase in strength compared to air-controls. The addition of 5 wt% γ-Al2O3 increased strength by 36.5%.

## 1. Introduction
Lunar orbit delivery costs are approximately $3370/kg. ISRU is essential for building a permanent lunar base. Geopolymerization is a promising technique as it requires simple molds and reacts at low temperatures. CE-5 lunar regolith (LR) exhibits low Al2O3 content and elevated FeTiO3 concentration compared to Apollo-series samples.

## 3. Results and Discussion

### 3.1. Compressive Strength
- **Curing Temperature:** Higher thermal energy inputs correlate positively with strength. Early-stage curing (0-24h) is decisive.
- **Atmosphere:** Vacuum curing (V-II) achieved 9.6 ± 0.3 MPa, 62.7% higher than air-cured A-II (5.9 ± 0.8 MPa). This is due to the absence of CO2 (preventing carbonation) and effective degassing.
- **Additives:** 
    - **Al2O3:** 5 wt% increased strength to 13.1 ± 0.7 MPa. Excessive amounts (15 wt%) caused a severe drop to 2.6 ± 0.1 MPa due to amorphous Al(OH)3 precipitation.
    - **FeTiO3:** Displayed negligible chemical reaction, acting only as a physical filler with modest strength gains at high loadings.

## 4. Conclusion
1. Compressive strength strongly depends on the curing temperature of the first 24 hours.
2. Vacuum curing improves properties by eliminating carbonation and maximizing particle contact through degassing.
3. Optimal Al2O3 addition is ~5 wt% for the DH-1 simulant.
4. FeTiO3 behaves as an inert filler.
