# Exploration of in-situ utilization of extraterrestrial resources: Preparation of Chang’E-5 lunar regolith simulant geopolymers modified by Al2O3 and FeTiO3 under vacuum environment

## Abstract
Geopolymer solidification is considered a promising strategy for in situ resource utilization (ISRU) in lunar base construction. In this study, a Chang’E-5 lunar regolith simulant (CE-5 LRS) named DH-1 was used to prepare geopolymers under simulated lunar conditions. The effects of curing temperature, atmosphere, and additives (γ-Al2O3 and FeTiO3) were investigated. Thermal analysis demonstrated that higher average curing temperatures (36.5–76.2 °C) improved mechanical performance, with the optimal specimen achieving a compressive strength of 12.0 ± 0.5 MPa. Vacuum-cured specimens exhibited a 62.7 % increase in strength compared to air-controls. Incorporating 5 wt% γ-Al2O3 increased strength by 36.5 %, while FeTiO3 (5–15 wt%) displayed negligible influence on the reaction process.

## 1. Introduction
Building a permanent lunar base requires using local resources to avoid high delivery costs (~3370 $/kg). Geopolymerization is a promising technique requiring simple molds and low temperatures. This study addresses a research gap regarding geopolymers derived from Chang'E-5 regolith, which has lower Al2O3 and higher FeTiO3 than Apollo samples.

## 2. Experimental
### 2.1 Raw materials
DH-1 LRS was used, replicating CE-5 chemistry (11.2 wt% Al2O3, 6.0 wt% FeTiO3). 
### 2.2 Geopolymer preparation
NaOH was used as the alkaline activator (12 wt% ratio). Specimens were cured under vacuum (~0.01 MPa) and various temperature schemes (I-V) derived from LRO Diviner lunar surface data.

## 3. Results and Discussion
### 3.1 Compressive strength
- **Vacuum vs Air:** Vacuum curing increased strength by 62.7% (9.6 MPa vs 5.9 MPa for A-II) by eliminating carbonation and degassing microbubbles.
- **Additives:** 5 wt% γ-Al2O3 optimized strength to 13.1 MPa. 15 wt% caused a decline to 2.6 MPa due to amorphous Al(OH)3 formation. FeTiO3 acted as an inert filler.
- **Curing Temperature:** Early-stage temperature (first 24h) is the decisive factor for mechanical properties.

## 4. Conclusion
1. Strength depends strongly on early-stage curing temperatures.
2. Vacuum curing is superior to air curing for geopolymer strength.
3. 5 wt% Al2O3 addition optimizes N-A-S-H gel formation for low-Al regolith.
4. FeTiO3 is chemically inert but provides physical pore-filling densification.
