# Balancing Strength and Extreme Thermal Resilience in Lunar Regolith Composites: The Role of Multi-Walled Carbon Nanotubes

**Authors:** Andrea J. Hoe, Amirreza Tarafdar, Wenhua Lin, Michael R. Fiske, Jennifer E. Edmunson, and Yeqing Wang
**Journal:** Small (Wiley-VCH GmbH)
**Year:** 2025
**DOI:** 10.1002/smll.202502220

## Abstract
The Artemis program aims to establish sustainable lunar habitats using resilient materials with minimal Earth-based resources. This study explores reinforcing lunar regolith with multi-walled carbon nanotubes (MWCNTs), using minimal water and urea (locally sourced plasticizer) and phosphoric acid. Samples with 0.00, 0.25, 0.50, and 1.00 wt.% MWCNTs were tested under freeze-thaw cycles, vacuum pressures, and ambient environments.

## Methodology
- **Simulant:** LSP-1 Lunar South Pole Simulant.
- **Additives:** MWCNTs (>50 nm diameter), Urea (superplasticizer), Phosphoric acid (pH modifier/binder).
- **Particle Size Strategy:** 80:20 ratio of fine (<25 μm) to coarse (<250 μm) particles.
- **Curing Routes:**
    - **Route 1:** Ambient curing, no cycling.
    - **Route 2:** Ambient curing + thermal cycling (-80 °C to 117 °C).
    - **Route 3:** Vacuum curing + thermal cycling.

## Key Results
- **Ambient Strength:** 0.50 wt.% MWCNT reached 39 MPa (44.44% increase over control).
- **Vacuum Performance:** 0.00 wt.% MWCNT samples cured in vacuum achieved 47 MPa due to densification and optimal water retention (~35%).
- **Thermal Challenges:** MWCNTs have a negative Coefficient of Thermal Expansion (CTE), while regolith has a positive CTE. This mismatch causes microcracking and strength degradation during thermal cycling.

## Conclusion
MWCNTs significantly enhance compressive strength under stable temperatures, but thermal mismatch remains a critical barrier for materials subjected to extreme day-night lunar cycles. Vacuum-curing without nanotubes currently provides the highest strength (47 MPa) due to improved densification.
