# Bearing capacity of lunar soil ground under extraterrestrial environmental effects

**Authors:** Banglu Xi, Mingjing Jiang, Pinqiang Mo, Jiaqiang Yang, Zhenhua Zhang  
**Journal:** Computers and Geotechnics 165 (2024) 105923  
**Available Online:** 12 November 2023  

## Abstract
Lunar environment features a low gravity field and ultra-high vacuum which shows significant effects on the bearing behavior of lunar soil ground for future lunar habitation. Therefore, the 2D Discrete Element Method was employed to simulate the plate load tests under different gravity fields in non-vacuum and high-vacuum environments, analyzing the bearing behavior and soil response under extraterrestrial environmental effects. The results show that a bilinear line bounded by 1g can be employed to describe the gravity effect on the gravity factors for ultimate bearing capacity and coefficient of subgrade reaction.

## 1. Introduction
Lunar habitation plays an important role in the future of human society, working as a supply center for in-situ resources. Design and construction of future lunar habitation require an accurate understanding of lunar soil-tool interaction. The lunar environment presents low gravity and high vacuum, leading to an increase in internal friction angle and cohesion due to van der Waals forces.

## 3. Results and Discussion
### 3.1. Ultimate Bearing Capacity
The ultimate bearing capacity increases non-linearly with the increasing gravity field. Bearing capacity increases faster in low gravity fields than in high gravity fields. High vacuum increases bearing capacity significantly compared to non-vacuum conditions due to inter-particle adhesion.

### 3.2. Gravity Factor Equations
A bilinear relationship for the gravity factor ($s_{ng}^{qu}$) is proposed:
- For $n_g < 1$: $a_1 + (n_g - 1)a_1$
- For $n_g > 1$: $a_2 + (n_g - 1)a_2$

### 3.3. Failure Mechanisms
- **Non-vacuum:** Affected area becomes deeper in depth and narrower in width with decreasing gravity.
- **High-vacuum:** Affected area becomes deeper in depth and wider in width with decreasing gravity.

## 4. Conclusion
1. A bilinear line bounded by 1g describes gravity factors for ultimate bearing capacity and subgrade reaction.
2. Centrifuge tests (high-g) cannot be directly applied to predict low-g lunar soil behavior.
3. The low gravity field can bear a larger mass than Earth because gravity affects the base load more than it reduces bearing capacity.
