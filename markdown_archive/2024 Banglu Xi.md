# Bearing capacity of lunar soil ground under extraterrestrial environmental effects

**Authors:** Banglu Xi, Mingjing Jiang, Pinqiang Mo, Jiaqiang Yang, Zhenhua Zhang  
**Journal:** Computers and Geotechnics 165 (2024) 105923  
**DOI:** 10.1016/j.compgeo.2023.105923

## Abstract
Lunar environment features a low gravity field and ultra-high vacuum which shows significant effects on the bearing behavior of lunar soil ground for future lunar habitation. Therefore, the 2D Discrete Element Method was employed to simulate the plate load tests under different gravity fields in non-vacuum (i.e., excluding the van der Waals force) and high-vacuum (i.e., including the van der Waals force) environments, analyzing the bearing behavior and soil response under extraterrestrial environmental effects. The results show that a bilinear line bounded by 1g can be employed to describe the gravity effect on the gravity factors for ultimate bearing capacity and coefficient of subgrade reaction. The slopes are different in low and high-gravity fields, indicating that the high-gravity effect in a centrifuge cannot be directly applied to predict the bearing behavior of lunar soil ground in a low-gravity field. 

## 1. Introduction
Lunar habitation plays an important role in the future of human society, working as a supply center of in-situ resources and a base for deep space exploration. From a geotechnical perspective, accurate understanding of lunar soil-tool interaction is required for design and construction. The Moon features lower gravity (1/6g) and ultra-high vacuum, affecting soil-plate interaction due to low initial stress and increased internal friction/cohesion from van der Waals forces.

## 3. Findings and Concluding Remarks
1.  **Bilinear Gravity Effect:** A bilinear relationship bounded by 1g describes gravity factors for ultimate bearing capacity. This suggests centrifuge tests (high-g) cannot be linearly extrapolated to low-g environments.
2.  **Vacuum Influence:** High vacuum increases ultimate bearing capacity due to the introduction of apparent cohesion from van der Waals forces, which become dominant in low-gravity environments.
3.  **Soil Response:** The affected area under a foundation becomes deeper and narrower with decreasing gravity in non-vacuum, but deeper and *wider* in high-vacuum environments. 
4.  **Asteroid Feasibility:** Soil under 0.01g gravity still exhibits bearing capacity, supporting the feasibility of sampling or anchoring on asteroids.
