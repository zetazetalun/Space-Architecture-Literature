# Bearing capacity of lunar soil ground under extraterrestrial environmental effects

**Journal:** Computers and Geotechnics 165 (2024) 105923  
**Authors:** Banglu Xi, Mingjing Jiang, Pinqiang Mo, Jiaqiang Yang, Zhenhua Zhang  

## Abstract
Lunar environment features a low gravity field and ultra-high vacuum which shows significant effects on the bearing behavior of lunar soil ground for future lunar habitation. Therefore, the 2D Discrete Element Method was employed to simulate the plate load tests under different gravity fields in non-vacuum (i.e., excluding the van der Waals force) and high-vacuum (i.e., including the van der Waals force) environments, analyzing the bearing behavior and soil response under extraterrestrial environmental effects. The results show that a bilinear line bounded by 1g can be employed to describe the gravity effect on the gravity factors for ultimate bearing capacity and coefficient of subgrade reaction.

## 1. Introduction
Lunar habitation plays an important role in the future of human society, working as a supply center of in-situ resources and a platform for scientific research. Accurate understanding of lunar soil-tool interaction is vital for design and construction. The lunar environment features a lower gravity field and ultra-high vacuum, presenting differences from Earth. High vacuum leads to inter-particle van der Waals forces, increasing internal friction and cohesion.

## 4. Bearing behavior
### 4.2. Ultimate bearing capacity
The ultimate bearing capacity ($q_u$) increases non-linearly with the increasing gravity field. Bearing capacity in vacuum is higher than in non-vacuum environments due to higher shear strength. A bilinear line bounded by 1g describes the relationship between the gravity factor and gravity level.

## 6. Concluding remarks
1. A bilinear line bounded by 1g can describe the gravity effect on bearing capacity factors. High-gravity centrifuge effects cannot be directly applied to predict low-gravity behavior.
2. In high vacuum, soil maintains significant bearing capacity even at very low gravity (0.01g), supporting the feasibility of asteroid sampling or habitation.
3. Affected soil areas become shallower and wider with decreasing gravity in high-vacuum conditions.
