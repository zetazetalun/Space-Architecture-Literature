# Overview of the Lunar In Situ Resource Utilization Techniques for Future Lunar Missions

**Journal:** Space: Science & Technology (A Science Partner Journal)
**DOI:** 10.34133/space.0037
**Authors:** Peng Zhang, Wei Dai, Ran Niu, Guang Zhang, Guanghui Liu, Xin Liu, Zheng Bo, Zhi Wang, Haibo Zheng, Chengbao Liu, Hanzhe Yang, Yifan Bai, Yang Zhang, Dong Yan, Kefa Zhou, and Ming Gao.

## Abstract
Building a lunar base by in situ resource utilization (ISRU) will drastically reduce the reliance of supplies from Earth. This article provides a comprehensive review of the past developing processes of ISRU and the latest progress of several ISRU technologies, including in situ water access, in situ oxygen production, in situ construction and manufacture, in situ energy utilization, and in situ life support and plant cultivation on the Moon. A 3-step development plan for lunar ISRU technologies in the next decade is proposed.

## In Situ Lunar Construction Techniques

### Additive Manufacturing (AM)
- **Contour Crafting:** A gantry-based 3D printing technique using liquid-state binders. NASA has explored this for quick infrastructure build-up.
- **D-Shape:** Uses binder jetting to produce large-scale outer protective shells for habitats. Challenges include injection of fluids in low gravity.

### Sintering Techniques
- **Microwave Sintering:** Higher efficiency and more even heating compared to radiant heating. Experiments with Apollo 17 soil showed improved formability.
- **Solar Sintering:** Uses concentrated sunlight via Fresnel lenses. Suitable for dust mitigation and basic brick fabrication.

### Extrusion Printing
- **Regolith Inks:** Mixing regolith with photocurable resins or copolymers (e.g., PLGA). Samples showed average compressive strength of 428.1 MPa and flexure strength of 129.5 MPa.

### Continuous Basalt Fibers
- Fabricated from lunar soil simulants (CSA-1).
- Achieved tensile strength as high as ~1,400 MPa.

## Energy and Life Support
- **Thermal Energy Storage (TES):** Using lunar regolith as a medium for storing solar heat for night survival.
- **Artificial Ecosystems:** China's "Lunar Palace 1" and "Space 180" experiments achieved high regeneration rates: 100% for oxygen and water, and 55-83% for food.

## Roadmap Recommendations
1. **Before 2027:** Mineral resource exploration and assessment.
2. **Around 2030:** In situ experiments on lunar surfaces using payloads.
3. **Around 2035:** Pilot-scale replenishment of survival matters and medium-term residence.
4. **Around 2040:** Large-scale replenishment for long-term lunar/Martian missions.
