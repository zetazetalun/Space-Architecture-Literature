# Overview of the Lunar In Situ Resource Utilization Techniques for Future Lunar Missions

## Abstract
This article provides a comprehensive review of the past developing processes of ISRU and the latest progress of several ISRU technologies, including in situ water access, in situ oxygen production, in situ construction and manufacture, in situ energy utilization, and in situ life support and plant cultivation on the Moon. A 3-step development plan for lunar ISRU technologies in the next decade is proposed, which consists of providing technological solutions, conducting technical verification on payloads, and carrying out in situ experiments, with the ultimate aim of establishing a permanent lunar station.

## Introduction
Future lunar exploration trends indicate a shift from understanding the Moon to making use of it, moving from short-term exploration to long-term residence. ISRU includes the mining, treatment, and utilization of natural resources like minerals, water ice, volatiles, and solar energy to enable sustainable deep-space exploration.

## Natural Resources on the Moon
| Resource Type | Description/Components |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Mineral Resources** | Silicate minerals (pyroxene, plagioclase, olivine), oxide minerals (ilmenite, spinel), and native metals. |
| **Water/Ice** | Concentrated in polar Permanently Shadowed Regions (PSRs); estimated total mass ~2.9 × 10¹² kg. |
| **Volatiles** | H, ³He, ⁴He, C, N, F, Cl implanted by solar wind. |
| **Solar Energy** | Average radiant flux of 1.1 × 10¹³ ergs/cm² per year. |

## In Situ Lunar Construction Techniques
### Additive Manufacturing (AM)
- **Contour Crafting**: A gantry-based 3D printing technique using liquid-state binders to crystallize materials like sulfur concrete. NASA is exploring this for quick infrastructure build-up.
- **D-Shape**: Suitable for large-scale construction of outer protective shells using regolith and liquid ink, though fluid injection in vacuum/low gravity remains a challenge.

### Sintering Techniques
- **Microwave Sintering**: Offers high efficiency and even heating. Experiments with Apollo 17 soil demonstrated improved formability and strength.
- **Solar Sintering**: Uses concentrated sunlight (e.g., via Fresnel lenses) to sinter regolith for dust mitigation or building bricks. It is limited by the depth of heat penetration compared to microwaves.

### Extrusion Printing of Regolith Inks
- Uses premixed pastes of regolith simulant and binders (like PLGA or photocurable resins).
- **Results**: Slurries using photocurable resins achieved an average compressive strength of 428.1 MPa and flexure strength of 129.5 MPa.

## Energy and Life Support
- **Thermal Energy Storage (TES)**: Using lunar regolith as a medium for thermal mass to store solar heat for use during the lunar night.
- **Artificial Ecosystems**: Systems like BIOS-3 (Russia), "Lunar Palace 1" (China), and NASA's ALS for oxygen/water regeneration and food production. Lunar regolith can be used as a substrate after biological weathering/improvement.

## Development Roadmap
1. **Before 2027**: Mineral resource exploration and usability assessment.
2. **Around 2030**: In situ experiments on the lunar surface via experimental payloads.
3. **Around 2035**: Pilot-scale replenishment of survival matters for medium-term residence.
4. **Around 2040**: Large-scale replenishment for long-term lunar and future Martian missions.
