# Overview of the Lunar In Situ Resource Utilization Techniques for Future Lunar Missions

**Authors:** Peng Zhang, Wei Dai, Ran Niu, Guang Zhang, Guanghui Liu, Xin Liu, Zheng Bo, Zhi Wang, Haibo Zheng, Chengbao Liu, Hanzhe Yang, Yifan Bai, Yang Zhang, Dong Yan, Kefa Zhou, and Ming Gao
**Published:** June 1, 2023
**Journal:** Space: Science & Technology
**DOI:** 10.34133/space.0037

## Abstract
Building a lunar base by in situ resource utilization (ISRU) will drastically reduce the reliance on supplies from Earth. This article provides a comprehensive review of ISRU technologies, including water access, oxygen production, in situ construction, energy utilization, and life support systems. A 3-step development plan for lunar ISRU in the next decade is proposed.

## Introduction
Future lunar exploration modes will shift from short-term exploration to long-term residence, focusing on resource-rich areas like the South Pole. ISRU involves the mining, treatment, and utilization of natural resources including minerals, water/ice, volatiles, and solar energy.

## Natural Resources on the Moon
- **Mineral Resources:** Silicate minerals (pyroxene, plagioclase, olivine) make up >90% of lunar rocks. Oxide minerals (ilmenite, spinels) are concentrated in mare basalts.
- **Water/Ice Resources:** Primarily found in polar permanently shadowed regions (PSRs). Estimates suggest ~2.9 x 10^12 kg of water within the uppermost meter of PSRs.
- **Volatiles:** Implanted by solar wind (H, He-3, C, N, F, Cl).
- **Solar Energy:** Abundant in non-permanently shaded areas of polar regions.

## In Situ Lunar Construction Techniques
Extreme environments (high vacuum, low gravity) require specialized construction methods.

### 1. Additive Manufacturing (AM)
- **Contour Crafting:** Uses liquid-state binders to extrude material layer-by-layer. NASA explored this for infrastructure, but vacuum conditions pose pumping challenges.
- **D-Shape Method:** Suitable for large-scale protective shells for habitats. Drawbacks include the transport of liquid ink from Earth.

### 2. Sintering Techniques
- **Microwave Sintering:** Higher efficiency and even heating compared to conventional methods. Formability and strength of products are improved.
- **Solar Sintering:** Uses Fresnel lenses to concentrate sunlight. Effective for dust mitigation and basic brick production but has limited sintering depth.

### 3. Extrusion Printing of Regolith Inks
- Preparation of printing slurry by mixing lunar regolith simulant with photocurable resins. Samples have shown an average compressive strength of 428.1 MPa.

### 4. Lunar Soil Fibers
- Continuous basalt-like fibers can be fabricated from lunar soil. Fabricated continuous fibers have demonstrated tensile strengths up to 1,400 MPa.

## Energy and Life Support
- **Thermal Energy Storage (TES):** Using lunar regolith as a medium for thermal mass to store solar energy for the lunar night.
- **Artificial Ecosystems:** Closed-loop systems like China's 'Lunar Palace 1' achieved 100% regeneration of oxygen and water and 83% of food.

## Roadmap Proposals
- **2027:** Resource exploration and assessment.
- **2030:** In situ experiments on lunar surfaces.
- **2035:** Pilot-scale replenishment for residence.
- **2040:** Large-scale replenishment for lunar/Martian missions.
