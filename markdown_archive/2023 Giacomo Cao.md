# Recent advances on ISRU technologies and study of microgravity impact on blood cells for deep space exploration

**Type:** Review  
**Published:** 19 July 2023  
**DOI:** 10.3389/frspt.2023.1146461

## Abstract
The work analyzes current advancements in technology development for deep space exploration and colonization. Key topics include:
1. A method for producing tangible goods for industrial/civil installations on the Moon, Mars, or asteroids using SHS reactions.
2. A theoretical analysis of the impact of gravity on SHS front velocity.
3. A process using Martian atmosphere and regolith to produce oxygen, water, food (microalgae), fuels, and fertilizers.
4. Optical characterization of sintered lunar regolith for thermal energy storage and solar energy harvesting.
5. Impact of microgravity on red blood cells.

## 1. Introduction
In-Situ Resource Utilization (ISRU) and In-Situ Fabrication and Repair (ISFR) are essential for colonization. The Italian task force (University of Cagliari, INAF, CNR, CRS4) focus on:
- Using SHS to produce structural elements.
- ISRU for consumables (food, fuel, oxygen) on Mars.
- Optical characterization of lunar regolith as solar absorbers.
- Microalgae (Spirulina platensis) for biomass.
- Microgravity effects on erythrocytes.

## 2. Manufacturing physical assets in extraterrestrial environments
A new process based on self-propagating high-temperature synthesis (SHS) was developed for fabricating composite ceramics. 

### Process Stages:
1. **Extraction:** Regolith from lunar or martian soil.
2. **Enrichment:** Magnetic separation of iron oxide (Martian minerals).
3. **Mixing:** Mixing enriched regolith with aluminum powder (carried from Earth).
4. **Synthesis:** Local ignition triggers SHS (1,800–2,000 K) to produce ceramics/composites.

### Findings:
- Compressive strength of SHS samples: 25.8–27.2 MPa (higher than previous geothermite-based methods at 10–18 MPa).
- Parabolic flight tests (Airbus-300) confirmed synthesis of TiB2-xTiAl composites under microgravity.

## 4. Optical characterization of lunar regolith simulant
Sintered JSC-1A regolith was evaluated for thermal energy storage (TES) and solar harvesting using Spark Plasma Sintering (SPS).
- **Sintering conditions:** 700°C and 900°C, 20 Pa vacuum, 15 MPa pressure.
- **Density:** 86% (at 700°C) and 98% (at 900°C).
- **Optical properties:** Sintered samples showed superior solar absorptance (0.88–0.92) and lower thermal emittance compared to native powder, making them suitable for solar receivers.

## 5. Edible microalgae on Mars
Spirulina platensis was cultivated under simulated Martian conditions (microgravity and 40% Martian Medium under CO2 atmosphere).
- **Martian productivity:** ~11 g L⁻¹ day⁻¹ (higher than Earth conditions at 0.048 g L⁻¹ day⁻¹).

## 6. Concluding remarks
The work summarizes three pillars: SHS for structural elements, ISRU/ECLSS integration for consumables, and optical enhancement of regolith through sintering for energy applications.
