## Fabrication of Flexible Thermoelectric Energy Harvesting System

Guangxi Wu; Ferin Neff; and Xiong (Bill) Yu

### ABSTRACT
Thermoelectric energy harvesting is promising to provide sustainable energy source in the extraterrestrial environment. This paper describes procedures incurred in the fabrication of thermoelectric energy harvester on flexible substrates. An electrically parallel structure is proposed, which features higher energy efficiency than the counterpart electrically series structure based on theoretical analyses. As proof of concept, flexible thermoelectric harvesters in electrically parallel and series structures are prepared with thick film printing procedures. The performance of elements and device with different architectures are characterized. Both analytical and experimental comparison imply a promising improvement of energy conversion efficiency by using the electrically parallel structure.

### INTRODUCTION
Thermoelectric modules (TEMs) when used as generators (TEGs) are capable of providing continuous energy for extraterrestrial explorations, where sun light is not always available or intense enough for solar cells to work. For example, the solar brightness on Mars and Jupiter is as weak as 45% and 4%, respectively. TEGs driven by the decay of suitable radioactive materials, such as radioisotope thermoelectric generators (RTGs), have received the most interest. If the spacecraft or extraterrestrial vehicle body surface can be covered by TEGs, energy provided by the TEGs will be significantly boosted up.

### ELECTRICALLY SERIES vs PARALLEL STRUCTURES
Conventionally, TEMs use an electrically series structure where p-legs and n-legs are connected in series. This paper proposes an alternative TEM structure where the legs are connected in parallel both thermally and electrically. When the electrically parallel structure is utilized, the TEM’s figure-of-merit can match that of the higher-performing material, preventing the typical 24-32% efficiency loss seen due to material property mismatch in series structures.

### TEM FABRICATION PROCESS
- **Material preparation:** Bi2Te3 (n-type and p-type) coarse powder was ball-milled to create nanocomposite particles (max grain size 76 microns).
- **TE ink preparation:** Ground powder was mixed with an epoxy matrix system (EPON Resin 863, hardener MHHPA, catalyst AC-8) at a 4.5:1 ratio.
- **Printing:** Thick-film printing on polyimide substrates using mesh screens for gold electrodes and stencils for TE legs.
- **Curing:** Gold electrodes cured at 110°C for 10 minutes; TE inks cured at 110°C for 24 hours.

### ELECTRIC OUTPUT PERFORMANCE
Experimental testing under a 50°C temperature gradient demonstrated that the n-type electrically parallel structure achieved higher open-circuit voltage and maximum output power compared to both p-type parallel and traditional series structures.

### CONCLUSION
This study proves that electrically parallel TEM structures fabricated via thick-film printing on flexible substrates can improve energy conversion efficiency. The proposed sandwiched multilayered structure for parallel TEMs could simplify large-area fabrication for space applications.
