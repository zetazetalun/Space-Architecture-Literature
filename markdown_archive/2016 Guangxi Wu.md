# Fabrication of Flexible Thermoelectric Energy Harvesting System

**Authors:** Guangxi Wu, Ferin Neff, and Xiong (Bill) Yu

## ABSTRACT
Thermoelectric energy harvesting is promising to provide sustainable energy source in the extraterrestrial environment. This paper describes procedures incurred in the fabrication of thermoelectric energy harvester on flexible substrates. An electrically parallel structure is proposed, which features higher energy efficiency than the counterpart electrically series structure based on theoretical analyses. As proof of concept, flexible thermoelectric harvesters in electrically parallel and series structures are prepared with thick film printing procedures. The performance of elements and device with different architectures are characterized. Both analytical and experimental comparison imply a promising improvement of energy conversion efficiency by using the electrically parallel structure.

## INTRODUCTION
Thermoelectric modules (TEMs) when used as generators (TEGs) are capable of providing continuous energy for extraterrestrial explorations. For example, the solar brightness on Mars and Jupiter is as weak as 45% and 4%, respectively. TEGs driven by the decay of radioactive materials (RTGs) have proven reliability for space missions. However, conventional TEGs suffer from low efficiency. This paper explores leveraging the ubiquitous temperature gradients on spacecraft or vehicle bodies (e.g., -153°C to 123°C on the Moon) by covering surfaces with flexible TEGs.

## ELECTRICALLY SERIES VS. PARALLEL STRUCTURES

### Series Structure
Conventionally, TEMs use alternating p-legs and n-legs connected in series. The maximum efficiency is limited by the module figure-of-merit ($ZT_{module}$), which is often impaired by the property mismatch between p-type and n-type materials.

### Parallel Structure
This paper proposes an alternative where legs are connected in parallel. This allows the module to maintain the high performance of a single superior material, avoiding the 'attenuation' caused by pairing a strong material with a weaker counterpart. Theoretical analysis suggests a potential efficiency increase of 32% for specific $Bi_2Te_3$ material pairs.

## FABRICATION PROCESS

1.  **Material Preparation:** $Bi_2Te_3$ powder was ball-milled to achieve grain sizes below 76 microns to enhance $ZT$ via grain boundary scattering.
2.  **TE Ink Preparation:** Ground powders were mixed with an epoxy matrix (EPON Resin 863) at a 4.5:1 ratio.
3.  **Printing:** Stencil and mesh screen printing were used to deposit gold electrodes and TE legs on flexible polyimide substrates.
4.  **Curing:** Gold electrodes cured at 110°C for 10 mins; TE inks cured at 110°C for 24 hours.
5.  **Assembly:** The printed sheets were rolled to form the final module.

## RESULTS AND PERFORMANCE
- **Characterization:** Tested under a temperature difference of 50°C.
- **Output Power:** The n-type electrically parallel structure exhibited higher maximum output power compared to the p-type parallel and the conventional series structure.
- **Inner Resistance:** The series structure suffered from high internal resistance, which limited its effective output compared to the parallel design.

## CONCLUSION
The electrically parallel TEM structure is promising for improving energy conversion efficiency and simplifying large-area fabrication on flexible substrates. Sandwiched multilayer structures are suggested for future large-scale production to further optimize the fabrication of these harvesters for space environments.
