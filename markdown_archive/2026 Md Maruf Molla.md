# Damage evaluation of regolith-resin-composite (RRC) materials for lunar structures using linear and nonlinear ultrasonic waves

## Abstract
The growing interest in establishing permanent bases on the moon necessitates the development of durable construction materials that can withstand the extreme lunar environment. This paper investigates the viability of ultrasonic waves and their performance for non-destructive evaluation in a novel material fabricated using lunar regolith simulant and epoxy resin, known as regolith-resin-composite (RRC). An experimental framework involving nine loading stages was employed. The results indicate that the relative nonlinear parameter increases by 76 % at the maximum applied load, while the linear parameter (amplitude) changed by only 19.9 %. This signifies that second harmonic generation is highly sensitive to damage progression in RRC, offering a reliable method for inspecting lunar construction materials.

## 1. Introduction
### 1.1. Lunar construction
The establishment of a permanent presence on the Moon requires robust infrastructure using in-situ lunar materials to mitigate transportation costs from Earth. Lunar regolith is a promising candidate due to its abundance, radiation shielding properties, and resistance to thermal cycles. Construction materials based on regolith include sulphur concrete, sintered regolith, and regolith-resin-composites (RRC). RRC is particularly viable as it is waterless or nearly waterless and can be produced with relatively low energy compared to sintering.

## 2. Second harmonic generation of ultrasonic waves
Linear ultrasonic methods rely on wave velocity and amplitude, whereas nonlinear methods utilize harmonic generation (double frequency) to detect micro-damage. Micro-damage causes a 'clapping effect' that increases the relative nonlinearity parameter (beta), making it a sensitive indicator of early-stage structural degradation.

## 3. Methodology
Specimens were prepared using lunar highlands simulant (LHS-1E) mixed with 15% high-temperature thermosetting epoxy resin. The mixture was compacted at 20 MPa and post-cured at temperatures up to 220°C. Specimens were subjected to uniaxial compression loading in a UTM, with ultrasonic measurements taken after each unloading cycle.

## 4. Results and Discussion
- **Wave Velocity:** The average bulk wave velocity was 2870.8 m/s. Velocity showed minimal change at low loading levels but decreased more significantly after 50% of the ultimate load was applied.
- **Amplitude:** Signal amplitude decreased as load increased, with a maximum reduction of 19.9% at a 120 V power input.
- **Nonlinear Parameters:** The relative nonlinearity parameter (beta prime) showed an overall increasing trend, rising by 76% at the maximum load (90% of ultimate capacity). This parameter proved much more sensitive to damage progression than linear wave velocity (4% change).

## 5. Conclusions
The nonlinear ultrasonic parameter derived from second harmonic generation effectively distinguishes between different damage levels in RRC. It is more reliable and sensitive than linear parameters (velocity and amplitude) for early-stage damage detection, making it a viable candidate for structural health monitoring of lunar habitats.
