# Damage evaluation of regolith-resin-composite (RRC) materials for lunar structures using linear and nonlinear ultrasonic waves

**Authors:** Md Maruf Molla, Ching Tai Ng, Tafsir Tafsirojjaman, Scott T. Smith  
**Journal:** Construction and Building Materials  
**Volume:** 514  
**Year:** 2026  
**DOI:** 10.1016/j.conbuildmat.2026.145564

## Abstract
The growing interest in establishing permanent bases on the moon necessitates the development of durable construction materials that can withstand the extreme lunar environment. This paper investigates the viability of ultrasonic waves and their performance for non-destructive evaluation in a novel material fabricated using lunar regolith simulant and epoxy resin, known as regolith-resin-composite (RRC). The results indicate that the relative nonlinear parameter increases by 76% at the maximum applied load, compared to a 19.9% change in linear amplitude, signifying the reliability of nonlinear ultrasonic waves for inspecting lunar construction materials.

## 1. Introduction
### 1.1. Lunar construction
The establishment of a permanent presence on the Moon requires robust infrastructure using in-situ lunar materials to mitigate transportation costs. Lunar regolith is promising due to its abundance, resistance to freeze-thaw cycles, and radiation shielding capabilities. Various materials have been studied, including sulfur concrete, sintered regolith, and geopolymer-based concrete. Waterless or nearly waterless regolith-resin-composite (RRC) emerged as a viable candidate due to its high strength and low energy demand compared to sintering.

## 2. Experimental Investigation
### 2.1. Specimen Preparation
*   **Material:** Lunar Highlands Simulant (LHS-1E) and high-temperature thermosetting epoxy resin (R2600 resin and H2409 hardener).
*   **Mixture:** 15% epoxy resin by weight, blended with regolith simulant.
*   **Process:** Mixed for 3 minutes, poured into 40 × 40 × 160 mm³ moulds, and compacted at 20 MPa using a Universal Testing Machine (UTM).
*   **Curing:** 24h at room temperature, followed by a post-curing thermal profile (up to 220°C).
*   **Compressive Strength:** Average of 134.3 MPa.

### 2.2. Ultrasonic Testing Setup
*   **Equipment:** NI PXIe-1073 system, NI PXI-5412 waveform generator, Ciprian Amplifier HVA-800-A.
*   **Transducers:** ULTRAN direct contact transducers (GC200-D13 and GC350-D13).
*   **Procedure:** A step-loading scheme was used (nine stages up to 90% of ultimate capacity) with ultrasonic evaluations at the end of each unloading stage.

## 3. Results and Discussion
### 3.1. Wave Velocity and Amplitude
*   The average bulk wave velocity in the intact RRC specimen was 2870.8 m/s.
*   Wave velocity showed minimal change (approx. 4% reduction) at maximum load.
*   Amplitude showed a more significant reduction of up to 19.9% at the 120 V input level.

### 3.2. Nonlinear Ultrasonic Parameters
*   Second harmonic generation (nonlinear parameter β') proved highly sensitive to damage progression.
*   The relative nonlinearity parameter increased by 76% (FFT analysis) and 65% (STFT analysis) at the highest applied load.
*   The parameter exhibited a consistent increase after applying 20% of the ultimate load, indicating its effectiveness in capturing early-stage micro-damage.

## 4. Conclusions
*   Nonlinear ultrasonic analysis is significantly more effective than linear analysis for monitoring damage in RRC.
*   The nonlinearity parameter derived from second harmonic generation distinguishes different loading levels effectively.
*   Future work is needed to investigate RRC behavior under actual lunar conditions, including vacuum and extreme thermal cycles.
