# Dynamic mechanical properties of lunar regolith based geopolymer: Coupled effects of composition and temperature

**Journal:** Case Studies in Construction Materials 23 (2025) e05417  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cscm.2025.e05417  
**Authors:** Zihan Zhou, Xiyao Zheng, Jun Wu

## Abstract
This study developed a high-fidelity lunar regolith simulant (GCD-2) that closely replicated the physicochemical properties of lunar samples retrieved by the Chang’e-6 mission. A lunar regolith based geopolymer (LRG) was subsequently synthesized using "one-part" method, in which a dry mixture of GCD-2 (as precursor) and solid sodium silicate (as activator) was combined with water. The dynamic mechanical properties of the LRG were then evaluated using the Split Hopkinson Pressure Bar (SHPB) apparatus. The results demonstrated that increasing Ca/Si ratio to 0.35 markedly enhanced the degree of geopolymerization, promoting the formation of abundant C-(A)-S-H gel and resulting in a denser matrix with significantly improved dynamic elastic modulus and peak compressive strength. A synergistic effect between Ca and Al was observed, yielding a highly compact microstructure with a peak dynamic compressive strength of 70.77 MPa.

## 1. Introduction
Establishing a permanent lunar base is a central priority for international space exploration (NASA's Artemis/Lunar Outpost and CNSA's 2030 base plans). The critical challenge is the economically prohibitive cost of transporting materials from Earth, necessitating in-situ resource utilization (ISRU). Geopolymers, which are amorphous aluminosilicate gels formed via polycondensation, are promising lunar construction materials due to their rapid setting and high early strength.

## 2. Materials and Methods
### 2.1 Materials
- **Precursor:** GCD-2 lunar regolith simulant (fidelity to Chang'e-6 samples). Median particle diameter ($D_{50}$) of 6.21 µm.
- **Activator:** Solid sodium silicate granules (modulus = 1.0, containing $Na_2O$ = 25% and $SiO_2$ = 50%).
- **Admixtures:** Analytical-grade $CaO$ and $Al_2O_3$ to adjust Ca/Si and Al/Si ratios.

### 2.2 Sample Preparation: "One-part" Method
Dry mixture of simulant and solid activator combined with water. This method avoids the risk of corrosive liquid reagent handling in extraterrestrial environments. Alkali activator-to-precursor mass ratio was 0.18; water-to-binder ratio was 0.25.

### 2.3 Thermal Curing Regimes
- **First Batch:** Curing started at the onset of the heating phase ($73^{\circ}C \rightarrow 113^{\circ}C \rightarrow -178^{\circ}C \rightarrow 73^{\circ}C$).
- **Second Batch:** Curing started during the cooling phase ($73^{\circ}C \rightarrow -178^{\circ}C \rightarrow 113^{\circ}C \rightarrow 73^{\circ}C$).

## 4. Conclusions
1. Curing during the lunar daytime temperature rise phase yields superior mechanical performance compared to the cooling phase.
2. Optimal composition for impact resistance: Ca/Si ratio of 0.35 and Al/Si ratio of 0.50.
3. The "one-part" method is highly feasible for ISRU construction, reducing logistical complexity and environmental risk.
4. Peak dynamic compressive strength achieved: 70.77 MPa.
