# Development of lunar regolith-based composite for in-situ 3D printing via high-pressure extrusion system

**Authors:** Hua ZHAO, Jihong ZHU, Shangqin YUAN, Shaoying LI, Weihong ZHANG
**Journal:** Frontiers of Mechanical Engineering
**Year:** 2023
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1007/s11465-022-0745-8

## Abstract
To fully utilize the in-situ resources on the moon to facilitate the establishment of a lunar habitat is significant to realize the long-term residence of mankind on the moon and the deep space exploration in the future. In this work, we proposed a cost-effective melting extrusion system for lunar regolith-based composite printing, and engineering thermoplastic powders are employed as a bonding agent for lunar regolith composite. The high-performance nylon and lunar regolith are uniformly pre-mixed in powder form with different weight fractions. The high-pressure extrusion system is helpful to enhance the interface affinity of polymer binders with lunar regolith as well as maximize the loading ratio of in-situ resources. Mechanical properties such as tensile strength, elastic modulus, and Poisson’s ratio were evaluated. The maximum tensile strength and impact toughness reach 36.2 MPa and 5.15 kJ/m², respectively. High-pressure melt extrusion can increase the effective loading fraction up to 80 wt.%. 

## 1. Introduction
The construction of the lunar habitat needs to protect mankind from the terrible environment of the moon, such as large temperature differences, high vacuum, meteorite impact, and radiation. Transportation of construction materials from the earth to the moon is economically unaffordable, making in-situ resource utilization (ISRU) technology essential. Lunar regolith is an ideal material for construction due to its environmental suitability.

## 2. Experimental
### 2.1 Materials
Basalt was used as a lunar regolith simulant, with particle sizes mainly distributed between 50 and 200 μm. PA12 powder was used as the thermoplastic binder. Composites were prepared with 40, 50, and 60 wt.% basalt fractions for initial testing, later reaching 80 wt.% in validation.

### 2.2 Fabrication Systems
Two types of extruders were used:
1. **Piston-based pellet extruder:** Used for small-batch material performance experiments.
2. **Screw-based pellet extruder:** Robot-assisted system developed for printing large-scale or complex curved components.

## 3. Results and Discussion
- **Thermal Properties:** The addition of basalt particles promotes the recrystallization of PA12, which is beneficial for melt extrusion molding.
- **Mechanical Properties:** 
  - Tensile strength decreases as regolith weight fraction increases (due to crack generation at the interface).
  - Elastic modulus increases as regolith fraction increases (regolith acting as a hard phase).
  - Maximum tensile strength: 36.2 MPa (at 40 wt.% loading).
  - Impact resistance: Improved with higher basalt content; maximum impact toughness of 5.15 kJ/m² reached at 60 wt.%.
- **Large-scale Verification:** A lunar habitat architectural model was successfully printed using the robot-assisted screw-based system with 80 wt.% basalt loading.

## 4. Conclusions
The high-pressure melt extrusion molding process provides an effective approach for lunar ISRU construction. It minimizes energy and time consumption compared to direct melt/sintering methods while ensuring adequate mechanical properties. The system enables the use of up to 80 wt.% lunar regolith in the composite feedstock.
