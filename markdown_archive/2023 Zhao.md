# Development of lunar regolith-based composite for in-situ 3D printing via high-pressure extrusion system\n\n**Authors:** Hua Zhao, Jihong Zhu, Shangqin Yuan, Shaoying Li, Weihong Zhang\n**Journal:** Frontiers of Mechanical Engineering\n**Year:** 2023\n**DOI:** 10.1007/s11465-022-0745-8\n\n## Abstract\nTo fully utilize the in-situ resources on the moon to facilitate the establishment of a lunar habitat is significant to realize the long-term residence of mankind. In this work, a cost-effective melting extrusion system for lunar regolith-based composite printing was proposed, using engineering thermoplastic powders (PA12) as a bonding agent. High-pressure extrusion was shown to enhance interface affinity and maximize the loading ratio of in-situ resources. Mechanical properties including tensile strength, elastic modulus, and impact performance were evaluated. Maximum tensile strength reached 36.2 MPa and impact toughness 5.15 kJ/m². The system can increase the effective loading fraction of regolith up to 80 wt.% and adapt to extreme conditions for in-situ manufacturing.\n\n## 1. Introduction\nThe construction of a lunar habitat must protect mankind from temperature differences, vacuum, meteorite impact, and radiation while utilizing in-situ resources (ISRU) to reduce transportation costs. Lunar regolith is an ideal material due to its abundance and radiation protection properties. Additive manufacturing (AM) provides a novel way to build habitats with complex geometries.\n\n## 2. Experimental Materials and Methods\n### 2.1 Materials\n- **Lunar Regolith Simulant:** Basalt powder with particle sizes primarily between 50 and 200 μm (mean: 101.084 μm).\n- **Binder:** Polyamide 12 (PA12) thermoplastic polymer.\n- **Composites:** PA12 mixed with basalt at 40, 50, and 60 wt.% for testing, and up to 80 wt.% for final verification.\n\n### 2.4 Specimen Fabrication\nTwo types of printers were used:\n1. **Piston-based pellet extruder:** For small-batch material performance experiments.\n2. **Screw-based pellet extruder:** Robot-assisted system for large-scale component fabrication.\n\n### Table 1: Material composition of lunar regolith and simulants (wt.%)\n| Sample | SiO2 | TiO2 | Al2O3 | FeO | MgO | CaO | Na2O | K2O |\n| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |\n| Apollo 14 | 48.10 | 1.70 | 17.40 | 10.40 | 9.40 | 10.70 | 0.70 | 0.55 |\n| CE-5 | 42.20 | 5.00 | 10.80 | 22.50 | 6.48 | 11.00 | 0.26 | 0.19 |\n| Basalt-1# | 54.48 | 1.39 | 14.89 | 8.66 | 6.31 | 7.16 | 2.71 | 0.39 |\n\n## 3. Results and Discussion\n### 3.3 Mechanical Properties\n- **Tensile Strength:** Reached a maximum of 36.16 MPa for PA12-Basalt40wt.% at 225 °C and 7 mm/s.\n- **Impact Toughness:** Increased with regolith content; basalt particles act as a hard phase absorbing impact energy. Impact toughness reached 5.15 kJ/m² for the 60 wt.% composite with a 45°/-45° infill pattern.\n- **Regolith Loading:** Successfully demonstrated printing with up to 80 wt.% regolith using the screw-based extruder.\n\n### Table 7: Process parameters of screw-based pellet extrusion\n| Parameter | Value |\n| :--- | :--- |\n| Basalt weight fraction | 80 wt.% |\n| Layer height | 4 mm |\n| Nozzle diameter | 5 mm |\n| Printing temperature | 235 °C |\n| Robot motion speed | 8–9 mm/s |\n\n## 4. Conclusions\nThe study verifies that melt extrusion molding of lunar regolith-based composites is an effective approach for lunar ISRU. High-pressure melt extrusion enables high loading fractions (80 wt.%) and provides sufficient mechanical strength and impact resistance for structural components. A robot-assisted screw-based system was successfully used to print a lunar habitat architectural model.
