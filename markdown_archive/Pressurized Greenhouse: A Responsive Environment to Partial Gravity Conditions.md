# Pressurized Greenhouse: A Responsive Environment to Partial Gravity Conditions

**Authors:** Mahsa Moghimi Esfandabadi¹ and Olga Bannova, Ph.D.²
**Affiliation:** Sasakawa International Center for Space Architecture, Cullen College of Engineering, Houston, TX.

## Abstract
The greenhouse is a vital module for a long-duration mission to support fresh nutrition for the crew. This paper discusses two concepts of extraterrestrial greenhouses: a habitable greenhouse (focused on human factors and psychological health) and an industrial greenhouse (focused on maximum production efficiency and low pressure). It proposes a "hybrid approach"—a pre-breathe greenhouse module that accommodates a spectrum of pressure, oxygen, and carbon dioxide levels. This responsive design serves as a pre-breathing chamber for EVAs, reducing decompression sickness risks while providing psychological benefits through interaction with plants. The proposed design is evaluated using figures of merit (FOM) regarding its impact on mission planning and success.

## Introduction
For long-duration Mars missions, greenhouses are essential for food production. Current designs fall into two categories:
1. **Industrial-scale:** Prioritizes efficiency via low-pressure environments.
2. **Habitable:** Prioritizes crew wellness via high-pressure environments.

## Habitable Greenhouses
Assumed to connect directly to habitats (often Class II inflatables), these operate under equal pressure (approx. 101.3 kPa). While simplifying ECLSS and providing psychological "backyard" benefits, they require high launch mass and structural support.

## Industrial Greenhouse Approach
Designed to maintain low-pressure environments (< 60 kPa) to minimize resource use and launch mass. 
- **Mass Savings:** Natural/hybrid lighting can reduce inflatable mass by 32.5% to 39.9%.
- **Insulation:** Low pressure allows for SiO₂ aerogel-based thermal insulation or vacuum insulation panels (VIPs).
- **Shell Mass:** A low-pressure shell with natural/hybrid lighting is only 50.1% the mass of a high-pressure greenhouse using artificial lighting.

## The Middle Path: The Pre-Breathe Greenhouse
This concept allows internal pressure to fluctuate between 34.48 and 56.5 kPa. It serves as a transition zone for crewmembers preparing for EVA, replacing the time spent in cramped airlocks with time spent in a productive, psychologically beneficial garden environment.

## Conclusions
A pre-breathe greenhouse approach offers a compromise between high-pressure habitable gardens and low-pressure industrial modules. It provides multifunctional capabilities, lower launch/landing mass requirements, and 24/7 availability for the crew while serving as a critical component of the life support and psychological wellness system.
