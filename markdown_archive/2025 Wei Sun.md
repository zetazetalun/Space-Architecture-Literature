# Advanced Martian construction: High-strength ISRU bricks, robust sulfur bonding, modular assembly, and FEA-verified pyramid habitats

**Authors:** Wei Sun, Jiabao Zhao, Handong Yan, ShaoFei Jiang, Tiejiong Lou  
**Journal:** Journal of Building Engineering 111 (2025) 113356  
**Published:** June 2025 (Elsevier Ltd.)  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jobe.2025.113356

## Abstract
Sustainable habitat construction on Mars faces challenges including low atmospheric pressure, reduced gravity, and large pressure differentials. This study presents an integrated In-Situ Resource Utilization (ISRU) approach combining high-strength regolith bricks, hydration-free sulfur bonding, and a modular pyramid habitat design validated by Finite Element Analysis (FEA). Optimized mechanical compaction (40 MPa) of nano-SiO2-enhanced Martian regolith simulant achieved compressive strengths exceeding 20 MPa at ambient temperatures and up to 44.5 MPa with thermal treatment (1000 °C). A sulfur-based mortar for modular assembly yielded bond strengths exceeding 2.0 MPa. FEA confirmed structural integrity under Martian loads (101.3 kPa internal pressure and 3.71 m/s² gravity).

## 1. Introduction
Mars presents obstacles: a tenuos atmosphere (~636 Pa), drastic temperature fluctuations (−143 °C to 20 °C), and the absence of stable liquid water. Conventional construction reliant on hydration is hindered. Structures must maintain ~101 kPa internal pressure. This study advocates for pre-fabricating structural elements (bricks) within a controlled environment, which are then assembled on-site using interlocking or sulfur-based bonding.

## 3. Results and Discussion
- **Compaction:** Increasing pressure from 10 MPa to 40 MPa improved strength from 3.4 MPa to 4.9 MPa. Beyond 40 MPa, gains were marginal.
- **Nano-SiO2 Effect:** Using 200 nm SiO2 particles instead of micron-sized ones boosted ambient compressive strength to >20 MPa due to denser packing and enhanced surface area.
- **Thermal Treatment:** Sintering at 1000 °C increased compressive strength to 44.5 MPa.
- **Sulfur Bonding:** Flat-cut interfaces reached 2.0 MPa bond strength, causing failure to occur in the brick material rather than the bond, indicating a reliable joint.

## 4. Habitat Design and FEA
A modular pyramid geometry was chosen for its aerodynamic stability and efficiency in handling pressure differentials. 
- **Internal Pressure:** 101.3 kPa.
- **Mars Gravity:** 3.71 m/s².
- **FEA Results:** Maximum predicted principal tensile stress was 1.15 MPa, which is below the demonstrated 1.2 MPa tensile limit of the sulfur bond/brick system.

## 5. Conclusions
1. Novel high-strength ISRU bricks can be fabricated via 40 MPa compaction and optional 1000 °C sintering.
2. Sulfur mortar provides a robust bonding agent for modular assembly.
3. Pyramid habitat geometry is structurally viable for Martian environmental loads.
