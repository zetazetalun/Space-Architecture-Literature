# Advanced Martian construction: High-strength ISRU bricks, robust sulfur bonding, modular assembly, and FEA-verified pyramid habitats

**Journal:** Journal of Building Engineering 111 (2025) 113356  
**Authors:** Wei Sun, Jiabao Zhao, Handong Yan, ShaoFei Jiang, Tiejiong Lou

## Abstract
Sustainable habitat construction on Mars faces significant challenges, including low atmospheric pressure hindering hydration, reduced gravity complicating compaction, and large habitat pressure differentials. This study presents an integrated In-Situ Resource Utilization (ISRU) approach combining high-strength regolith bricks, hydration-free sulfur bonding, and a modular pyramid habitat design validated by Finite Element Analysis (FEA). Optimized mechanical compaction (40 MPa) of nano-SiO2-enhanced Martian regolith simulant effectively bypasses hydration constraints, achieving compressive strengths exceeding 20 MPa even at ambient temperatures. 

## 1. Introduction
Mars presents formidable obstacles: an extremely tenuous atmosphere (~636 Pa), drastic temperature fluctuations (−143 °C–20 °C), and a water phase diagram that precludes stable liquid water on the surface. This severely hinders conventional construction materials reliant on hydration reactions. Furthermore, structures must maintain a pressurized internal atmosphere (~101 kPa). One viable approach is to employ 3D printing technology or pre-fabricating structural elements within a pressurized environment and subsequently transporting/assembling them. This study advocates for the separation of the fabrication of structural elements from the 3D printing process, favoring a modular masonry approach.

Experimental procedures involved:
- **Mixing:** Dry ingredients agitated for 2 min, followed by water incorporation.
- **Compaction:** Consistent pressure applied for 1.5 hours using steel molds (25.4 mm cubes).
- **Thermal Treatment:** Specimens incrementally heated (up to 1000 °C) for 6 h.
- **Bonding:** Elemental sulfur used as mortar. Interfaces tested: cut-free, flat-cut, and cross-cut.

## 3. Key Results
- **Compaction Pressure:** 40 MPa was identified as optimal, yielding strengths around 22 MPa for nano-SiO2-enhanced bricks without thermal treatment.
- **Aggregate Size:** Utilizing nano-scale SiO2 (200 nm) dramatically increased strength from <5 MPa (micron-sized) to >20 MPa due to denser packing.
- **Temperature:** Post-compaction thermal treatment at 1000 °C increased compressive strength to 44.5 MPa (sintering effect).
- **Water Content:** Optimal strength plateau reached at 25-35% water content (acting as lubricant and hydration reactant).
- **Bonding:** Flat-cut interfaces with sulfur mortar achieved average bond strengths of 2.0 MPa, shifting failure from the interface to the brick material itself.

## 4. Habitat Design & FEA
A modular pyramid habitat was designed to withstand a 101.3 kPa internal pressure differential and 3.71 m/s² Martian gravity. FEA using experimentally derived properties (22 MPa compressive, 1.2 MPa tensile/bond) showed a maximum principal tensile stress of 1.15 MPa, which is below the 1.2 MPa capacity of the sulfur-bonded ISRU bricks.

## 5. Conclusions
This research successfully validates an integrated approach—from material development to structural application—for resource-efficient Martian infrastructure. Mechanical compaction combined with sulfur bonding provides a robust alternative to hydration-dependent construction.
