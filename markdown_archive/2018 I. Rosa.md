# Multiscale Modeling and Testing of Protein-Bound Regolith and Soils\n\nI. Rosa; M. D. Lepech; and D. J. Loftus\n\n## ABSTRACT\nExtraterrestrial construction presents many interesting and new challenges. Unlike Earth, there are very limited naturally occurring resources readily available in extraterrestrial environments, such as the Moon or Mars. Transporting large amounts of materials from Earth is cost prohibitive. Thus, this work focuses on a novel class of composites for use in limited resource environments: protein-bound soils. The composite is produced by mixing soil, water, and a biopolymer binder (protein or other biopolymer) to create a versatile composite with compressive strength comparable to that of ordinary Portland cement concrete. This paper proposes a multi-scale framework to model the composite’s fracture properties in order to design a durable material that can resist extreme environments.\n\n## INTRODUCTION\nIn 2015, NASA announced its Journey to Mars Roadmap, describing how in situ resource utilization was key to reduce the cost of space exploration. Regolith, abundant on both the Moon and Mars, has the potential of providing the raw materials needed to build protective structures. Protein-bound regolith could provide a low-energy, in situ construction material for roads and landing pads. The protein binder (less than 13% of mass) could be produced terrestrially or via engineered microorganisms grown locally.\n\n## DEVELOPMENT OF A MULTISCALE FRAMEWORK\nIn creating a multiscale framework for protein-bound soils, the microstructure was the central focus. At 1 µm resolution, protein features, soil particles, and voids are visible. The framework consists of:\n1. **Nanoscale**: Nanoindentation for mechanical response of desiccated protein.\n2. **Microscale**: MicroCT imaging and micromechanical modeling using SEPUCs (Statistically Equivalent Periodic Unit Cells).\n3. **Mesoscale**: Rigid Body Spring Model (RBSM) for crack propagation modeling.\n4. **Macroscale**: Triaxial and uniaxial testing for bulk properties validation.\n\n## RESULTS\n- **Stiffness**: Protein-bound regolith has a stiffness of approximately 1.2 GPa.\n- **Compressive Strength**: Approximately 12 MPa.\n- **Porosity**: Ranges from 20-40%.\n- **Comparison**: While cement mortar has ~22 GPa stiffness and ~35 MPa strength, protein-bound soils exhibit larger deformations at failure.\n\n## CONCLUSIONS\nThe framework uses micromechanics and imaging to capture heterogeneity, aided by nanoindentation of dry proteins. This approach accelerates the development of protein-bound soil composites, allowing for optimized mix designs for varying environments and load requirements.
