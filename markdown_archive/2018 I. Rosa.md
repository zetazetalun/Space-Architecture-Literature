# Multiscale Modeling and Testing of Protein-Bound Regolith and Soils\n\n**Authors:** I. Rosa, M. D. Lepech, D. J. Loftus\n**Publication:** Earth and Space 2018\n\n## Abstract\nExtraterrestrial construction presents many challenges due to limited naturally occurring resources. Transporting materials from Earth is cost prohibitive. This work focuses on protein-bound soils, a novel class of composites produced by mixing soil, water, and a biopolymer binder. The paper proposes a multiscale framework to model fracture properties across nano, micro, meso, and macro scales to design durable structures for the Moon, Mars, or other celestial bodies.\n\n## Introduction\nNASA’s Journey to Mars Roadmap identifies In Situ Resource Utilization (ISRU) as critical. Regolith is abundant on the Moon and Mars and can provide raw materials for radiation shielding and impact protection. Protein-bound regolith offers a low-energy, in situ alternative to sintering for building roads and landing pads.\n\n## Multiscale Framework\n\n### 1. Nanoscale: Nanoindentation\nUsed to obtain mechanical properties of the dry protein binder. Preliminary results for desiccated protein estimate a Young’s modulus of approximately 4 MPa.\n\n### 2. Microscale: MicroCT Imaging\nUses virtual 3-D periodic unit cells to capture interactions between soil particles, protein, and voids. This allows for the homogenization of mechanical properties like Young’s modulus (Ehom) and Poisson’s ratio (vhom).\n\n### 3. Mesoscale: Crack Propagation\nEmploys the Rigid Body Spring Model (RBSM) to simulate fracture using homogenized properties from the microscale. The model captures non-linear behavior and the large deformations characteristic of this material.\n\n### 4. Macroscale: Experimental Testing\nUses triaxial testing and 3-D Digital Image Correlation (DIC) to validate numerical models. This scale characterizes the yield surface using Mohr-Coulomb criteria: \u03c4 = c + \u03c3 tan \u03c6.\n\n## Comparison of Material Properties\n\n*   **Cement Mortar:** Stiffness ~22 GPa; Compressive Strength ~35 MPa; Porosity 7-20%.\n*   **Protein-Bound Regolith:** Stiffness ~1.2 GPa; Compressive Strength ~12 MPa; Porosity 20-40%.\n*   **Binder Content:** 5-15% by weight (protein binder) vs up to 25% (cement paste).\n\n## Conclusions\nThis multiscale approach accelerates the design of protein-bound soil composites. The framework enables tailoring of mix designs for specific environments and load requirements, facilitating faster implementation for extraterrestrial infrastructure.
