# Multiscale Modeling and Testing of Protein-Bound Regolith and Soils

## Abstract
This research addresses the challenges of extraterrestrial construction by proposing a novel composite material made from regolith, water, and a biopolymer binder (protein). The study introduces a multiscale framework to model the fracture properties and mechanical behavior of this material, aiming to provide a durable and low-energy alternative to traditional cement for structures on the Moon or Mars.

## Introduction
The authors highlight the necessity of In Situ Resource Utilization (ISRU) to overcome the high costs of transporting materials from Earth. Protein-bound regolith is presented as a candidate for radiation shielding and infrastructure like landing pads. This material uses small amounts of biopolymers that could potentially be produced extraterrestrially using microorganisms.

## Multiscale Framework
The study utilizes a four-tiered modeling and testing approach:
1. **Nanoscale**: Uses nanoindentation to characterize the mechanical properties of the desiccated protein binder.
2. **Microscale**: Employs MicroCT imaging and Statistically Equivalent Periodic Unit Cells (SEPUC) to model interactions between soil particles, protein bridges, and voids.
3. **Mesoscale**: Implements a Rigid Body Spring Model (RBSM) to simulate crack propagation and fracture response based on homogenized properties from the microscale.
4. **Macroscale**: Conducts triaxial and uniaxial compression tests, alongside Digital Image Correlation (DIC), to validate the numerical models and determine bulk material properties.

## Material Comparison
The paper compares the microstructure and performance of protein-bound soils to Portland cement mortar. It notes that while the protein-bound material can achieve significant strength, it has higher porosity (20-40%) and lower stiffness than traditional concrete, requiring specialized modeling to account for larger deformations at failure.

## Conclusions
The multiscale framework provides a foundation for designing and optimizing protein-bound regolith mixes for specific environmental loads. Future work aims to extend this to system-scale modeling for full extraterrestrial structures.
