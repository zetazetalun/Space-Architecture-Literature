# Review of research into bioregenerative life support system(s) which can support humans living in space\n\n## Abstract\nLong-term survival in deep space requires Bioregenerative Life Support Systems (BLSS) to regenerate oxygen, water, and food in situ while recycling waste. This review covers historical and current BLSS research by major space-faring nations and discusses the transition to extraterrestrial applications, specifically for lunar and Martian missions.\n\n## 1. Introduction\nBLSS are artificial closed ecosystems comprising producers (plants), consumers (humans/animals), and decomposers (microorganisms). They are essential for autonomous human existence in deep space, reducing dependence on Earth-based supplies.\n\n## 2. Research Progress of Earth-Based BLSS\n### 2.1 Soviet Union/Russia (BIOS Systems)\n- **BIOS-1/2**: Early experiments using microalgae for gas and water circulation.\n- **BIOS-3**: Achieved >90% closure in gas and water and met 40-70% of food needs for a crew of 2-3.\n\n### 2.2 United States (CELSS and Biosphere 2)\n- **LMLSTP**: Integrated biological and physical/chemical technologies for missions of up to 91 days.\n- **Biosphere 2**: The largest closed ecosystem facility, providing insights into system-level balance and unexpected atmospheric challenges (e.g., CO2 absorption into concrete).\n\n### 2.3 Europe (MELiSSA)\n- Managed by ESA, focusing on a multi-compartment model involving anaerobic bacteria, nitrifying bacteria, and microalgae for waste processing and food production.\n\n### 2.4 Japan (CEEF/EEF)\n- Notable for integrating animal units (goats) into the closed ecosystem to study material circulation.\n\n### 2.5 China (Lunar Palace)\n- **Lunar Palace 1**: Achieved 105-day survival for 3 crew members with 97% closure.\n- **Lunar Palace 365**: A 370-day experiment with four crew members, achieving 98.2% material closure and 100% regeneration of oxygen and water.\n\n## 3. Prospects for Spatial BLSS\nFuture research must move from Earth-based simulations to extraterrestrial environments to account for gravity, radiation, and lunar night cycles. A three-stage strategy is proposed:\n1. **Stage 1**: Hydroponics combined with processed in situ resources (lunar/Martian soil) for substrates.\n2. **Stage 2**: Expansion using large amounts of ISRU-based substrates and the introduction of insects for animal protein.\n3. **Stage 3**: Full-scale habitats with recycled inorganic salts and complex aquatic units (e.g., fish).\n\n## 4. Evaluation Indices for BLSS\nKey metrics include regeneration rates, system stability, energy consumption per person, equipment weight, and labor intensity.
