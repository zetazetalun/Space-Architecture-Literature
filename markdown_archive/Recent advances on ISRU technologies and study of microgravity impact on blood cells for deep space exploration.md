# Recent advances on ISRU technologies and study of microgravity impact on blood cells for deep space exploration

## Abstract
This work analyzes current advancements in technology development for deep space exploration and colonization. It focuses on producing tangible goods for civil or industrial installations on the Moon, Mars, or asteroids using in situ available regolith. Key technologies discussed include Self-propagating High-temperature Synthesis (SHS) for composite ceramics, production of oxygen and fuels from Martian feedstock, and cultivation of *Spirulina platensis* for nourishment. Additionally, it addresses Spark Plasma Sintering (SPS) of regolith simulants for energy storage and the impact of microgravity on human health (red blood cells).

## 1. Introduction
In-Situ Resource Utilization (ISRU) and In Situ Fabrication and Repair (ISFR) are critical for providing life support, propellants, and construction materials in space. The Italian task force (University of Cagliari, INAF, CNR, CRS4) is developing these technologies, specifically using SHS to produce structural elements and integrated ISRU/ECLSS systems for Mars missions.

## 2. Manufacturing physical assets in extraterrestrial environments
The authors developed an ISFR process to manufacture structural elements from lunar and martian regolith using SHS reactions. 

### The SHS Manufacturing Process:
1. **Extraction:** Regolith collection from the surface.
2. **Enrichment:** Magnetic separation to increase iron oxide content.
3. **Mixing:** Combining enriched regolith with aluminum powder (carried from Earth).
4. **Compaction:** Forming cylindrical or parallelepiped pellets.
5. **Ignition:** High-temperature redox reactions (above 1,526°C) that propagate through the pellet to form ceramic composites.

### Mechanical Properties:
- **Compressive Strength:** 25.8–27.2 MPa.
- **Significance:** These values are significantly higher than the 10–18 MPa achieved by previous geothermite-based processes.

## 5. Edible microalgae on Mars
*Spirulina platensis* was cultivated under simulated Martian conditions (microgravity and CO2-rich atmosphere). 
- **Finding:** Productivity was found to be approximately 11 g L⁻¹ day⁻¹ on Mars simulation, compared to 0.048 g L⁻¹ day⁻¹ under terrestrial conditions. The CO2-rich atmosphere and microgravity (facilitating nutrient diffusion) are key factors.

## 6. Optical characterization of lunar regolith simulant
Spark Plasma Sintering (SPS) was used to densify JSC-1A lunar regolith simulant at 700°C and 900°C.
- **Density:** Relative densities reached 86% and 98% respectively.
- **Optical Results:** Solar absorptance increased from 0.88 (700°C) to 0.92 (900°C), while thermal emittance ranged from 0.87 to 0.96. This makes sintered regolith an excellent material for solar receivers and thermal energy storage.

## 8. Concluding remarks
The technologies reviewed (SHS manufacturing, integrated ISRU/ECLSS, and microalgae cultivation) demonstrate the feasibility of using in-situ resources to sustain human presence and infrastructure on the Moon and Mars.
