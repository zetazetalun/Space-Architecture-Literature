# An assumption of in situ resource utilization for “bio-bricks” in space exploration

**Authors:** Hongyan Zuo, Shuisong Ni, Meiying Xu  
**Journal:** Frontiers in Materials  
**Type:** Mini Review  
**Published:** 06 April 2023  
**DOI:** 10.3389/fmats.2023.1155643

## Abstract
Microbially induced carbonate precipitation (MICP) mimics natural cementation by using bacterial metabolic pathways to produce bicarbonate minerals as cementing agents. These can manufacture "bio-bricks" rivaling regular bricks in strength. This process is energy-saving and cost-effective for in situ resource utilization (ISRU) in extraterrestrial settlements like lunar bases.

## 1 Introduction
NASA estimates it costs ~$10,000 to put one pound of material in Earth orbit, making ISRU an economic imperative. Common ISRU methods like sintering or melting are energy-intensive. Bio-cementation offers a 'green' alternative using microorganisms to induce carbonate mineral precipitation for construction.

## 2 Microbial carbonate precipitation by different metabolic activities
Metabolic pathways include:
- **Aerobic:** Photosynthesis, ureolysis, and ammonification.
- **Anaerobic:** Sulfate reduction and denitrification.
- **Methane Oxidation:** Both aerobic and anaerobic.

### 2.1 Aerobic processes
Ureolysis (enzymatic degradation of urea) is the most studied due to its efficiency and high calcite precipitation. Species like *Sporosarcina pasteurii* (formerly *Bacillus pasteurii*) are primary study models.

### 2.2 Anaerobic processes
Given the Moon's lack of oxygen, anaerobic processes like sulfate reduction and denitrification are significant. Denitrification is considered highly suitable for soil reinforcement.

## 3 Properties of sand-sized material on the moon
Lunar regolith consists of fragmental and unconsolidated rock material (2-15 m thick). 
- **Specific gravity:** 2.9–3.5 g/cm³.
- **Mean particle size (D50):** ~72 μm (significantly finer than terrestrial masonry sand).
- **Mineralogy:** Plagioclase, pyroxene, ilmenite, olivine, and impact glass.

## 4 Characteristics of artificial bio-bricks
Bio-bricks produced via MICP have shown Unconfined Compressive Strengths (UCS) ranging from **1 MPa to 23 MPa**. This makes them comparable to conventional bricks or cement-treated beams.

## 5 Research needs and future directions
Challenges include the fine grain size of lunar regolith (72 μm vs 360-730 μm used in labs), the need for low-gravity testing, and sourcing urea (potentially from human urine) and water ice in-situ.
