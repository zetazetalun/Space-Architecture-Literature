# Robotic 3D printed lunar bionic architecture based on lunar regolith selective laser sintering technology

## Abstract
This research presents an integrated fabrication process for a bionic lunar base, focusing on 3D printing mechanisms using lunar regolith, real-time robotic control, and a specialized tool end system. It addresses in situ fabrication through a hybrid powder strategy (lunar soil combined with PAEK) and dual-light field laser printing.

## 1. Introduction
The study outlines the stages of lunar base construction, emphasizing the transition from Earth-prefabricated modules to in situ resource utilization (ISRU). It highlights the challenges of the lunar environment, such as vacuum, radiation, and extreme temperature fluctuations.

## 2. State-of-the-Art Lunar Construction
### 2.1 In Situ Construction Methods
The paper compares several international projects: 
- **Sinterhub (NASA):** Selective microwave sintering.
- **Lunar Habitation (ESA):** Solar sintering over inflatable membranes.
- **Chinese Super Mason (HZUST):** Autoclaved lunar bricks.

## 3. Architectural Design and Workflow
### 3.1 Bionic Design Prototype
The authors propose a bionic shell structure inspired by mollusks to handle low gravity and provide structural protection. The workflow includes environmental analysis, geometric optimization (using BESO), and real-time robotic obstacle avoidance.

### 3.2 3D Printing and Fabrication
A robotic system is described for path generation and real-time optimization to ensure precise deposition of materials on complex lunar terrain.

## 4. Materials and Technology
The study focuses on a hybrid powder consisting of lunar regolith and Polyaryletherketone (PAEK). This combination is chosen to lower the sintering temperature and improve bonding efficiency. The dual-light field laser strategy uses a large spot for pre-heating and a small spot for high-precision sintering.

## 5. Experiments
Experimental verification was conducted using JSC-1A simulant. 
- **Thermal Analysis:** The hybrid powder melts at 310°C and decomposes at 510°C. 
- **Mechanical Testing:** Compressive strength increased with higher PAEK content and processing pressure.
- **Scaling:** Large-scale decimeter-level samples were successfully printed to verify horizontal and vertical structural integrity.

## 6. Conclusion
The paper concludes that in situ 3D printing using hybrid regolith-polymer powders is a viable and cost-effective method for lunar habitat construction, though further research into robotic self-healing and extreme environment testing is required.
