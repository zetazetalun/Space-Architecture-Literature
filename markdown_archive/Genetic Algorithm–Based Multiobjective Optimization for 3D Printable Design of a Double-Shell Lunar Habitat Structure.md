# Genetic Algorithm–Based Multiobjective Optimization for 3D Printable Design of a Double-Shell Lunar Habitat Structure\n\n**Authors:** Quanyao Chen, Yuyue Gao, Lieyun Ding, Cheng Zhou, Wenbin Han, Yan Zhou, and Yusheng Shi\n\n**Abstract:** The establishment of lunar habitats is significant for humans to explore the Moon. Constructing lunar habitats through additive manufacturing using lunar in situ resource is a promising solution. This study proposed a parametric design and multiobjective optimization approach based on genetic algorithms for the structure of lunar habitats. Three kinds of genetic algorithm–based multiobjective optimization methods were used and compared. The optimized Pareto solution set was obtained, and finite-model analysis was performed to determine the optimal structure based on maximum stress and displacement.\n\n## 1. Introduction\nEstablishing a habitat on the Moon is essential for long-term exploration. Robotic construction based on in situ resource utilization (ISRU) is seen as the most feasible way to overcome high transportation costs. Additive manufacturing offers advantages in speed and accuracy.\n\n## 2. Related Work\nLunar habitats must withstand low gravity, vacuum, temperature variations, radiation, and meteorite impacts. Proposed designs include inflatable membrane structures, hybrid inflatable/rigid structures, and 3D printed regolith shields. Additive manufacturing techniques like D-shape, contour crafting (CC), and concrete printing (CP) are primary candidates.\n\n## 3. Methodology\n### 3.1. Lunar Ampullae Design\nThe proposed habitat is a double-shell structure named \"Lunar Ampullae.\" It features:\n- Inner shell for pressure containment.\n- Outer shell for radiation and meteorite protection.\n- Middle rib for structural connection.\n- Catenary roof for stability.\n\n### 3.2. Optimization Parameters\n| Parameters | Symbol | Description | Range (m) |\n| :--- | :---: | :--- | :---: |\n| Surface major axis | A | Major axis of shell | [12, 14] |\n| Surface minor axis | B | Minor axis of shell | [4, 6] |\n| Inner shell thickness | ti | Thickness of inner shell | [0.2, 0.3] |\n| Outer shell thickness | to | Thickness of outer shell | [0.2, 0.3] |\n| Distance | d | Distance between shells | [0.4, 0.6] |\n\n### 3.3. Objectives\n1. **Minimize Mass (W):** To reduce material consumption.\n2. **Maximize Volume (V):** To ensure sufficient habitable space.\n3. **Maximize Thermal Resistance (T):** For environmental protection.\n\n## 4. Results and Discussion\n### 4.1. Algorithm Comparison\nNSGA-II was compared against SPEA2 and PESA-II. NSGA-II demonstrated the best convergence and diversity with the highest Hypervolume (HV) index (0.8022 at PM=0.06).\n\n### 4.2. Structural Analysis\nFinite-element analysis (FEA) was conducted using Karamba 3D. Solution 25 was identified as optimal with:\n- Maximum Tensile Stress: 3.33 MPa.\n- Maximum Displacement: 2.73 x 10^-2 cm.\n\n### 4.3. Physical Model\nA scaled physical model was successfully produced using concrete additive manufacturing to demonstrate construction potential.\n\n## 5. Conclusion\nThe study successfully integrated parametric design with multiobjective genetic algorithms to optimize lunar habitats. NSGA-II proved most effective for this design problem. Future work will include thermal stress analysis and seismic performance optimization.
