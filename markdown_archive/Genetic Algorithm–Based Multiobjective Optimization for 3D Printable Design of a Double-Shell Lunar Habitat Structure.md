# Genetic Algorithm–Based Multiobjective Optimization for 3D Printable Design of a Double-Shell Lunar Habitat Structure\n\n## Abstract\nThe establishment of lunar habitats is significant for humans to explore the Moon and carry out scientific work. Constructing lunar habitats through additive manufacturing using lunar in situ resource is a promising solution. This study proposed a parametric design and multiobjective optimization approach based on genetic algorithms for the structure of lunar habitats. Structural shape was translated into design parameters, which were optimized during the design phase. The optimization objectives were determined considering the material mass consumption, space efficiency, and resistance ability for extreme extraterrestrial environments of the structure. Three kinds of genetic algorithm–based multiobjective optimization methods were used and compared to optimize the structural parameters. The optimized Pareto solution set containing multiple solutions based on the hypervolume index was obtained. Finite-model analysis was performed on the structures of the Pareto solution set and the optimal structure was determined based on the results of maximum stress and displacement. Finally, partially scaled-down physical models were produced through additive manufacturing to demonstrate the optimized double-shell habitat structure.\n\n## Methodology\nThe research proposed a conceptual design of a lunar habitat structure named 'Lunar Ampullae'.\n- **Design Strategy**: Double-shell structure built by additive manufacturing using lunar ISRU (outer shell) and a pressurized module (inner shell).\n- **Parametric Modeling**: Developed using Rhino and Grasshopper (GH) with the 'Karamba 3D' plug-in. Nine design parameters (A, B, a, b, d, Hb, Ht, ti, to) defined the geometry.\n- **Optimization**: Compared NSGA-II, SPEA2, and PESA-II genetic algorithms. Objectives included maximizing habitable volume and thermal insulation while minimizing structural mass.\n- **Verification**: Used Finite Element Analysis (FEA) to evaluate the stress and displacement of Pareto-optimal solutions. A scaled physical model was produced using additive manufacturing with putty powder/concrete.\n\n## Key Findings\n- **Algorithm Performance**: NSGA-II provided the best Pareto solution set diversity and convergence performance, specifically with a crossover probability of 0.9 and mutation probability of 0.06.\n- **Optimal Structure**: Solution #25 was identified as optimal, featuring a maximum tensile stress of 3.33 MPa and maximum displacement of 0.0273 cm, which are well within structural safety limits for 0.1 MPa internal pressure.\n- **Design Insights**: A positive correlation exists between habitat mass, volume, and thermal insulation; the double-shell design effectively provides radiation and thermal protection through structural redundancy.\n\n## Conclusion\nThe integrated parametric design and GA-based optimization approach allows for the efficient development of structural solutions that meet the complex requirements of lunar habitation. Future work should incorporate more environmental factors like cosmic radiation and seismic impacts into the optimization objectives.
