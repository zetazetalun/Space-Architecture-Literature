# 3D printed sulfur-regolith concrete performance evaluation for waterless extraterrestrial robotic construction

**Journal:** Automation in Construction 165 (2024) 105571  
**Authors:** Ilerioluwa Giwa, Mary Dempsey, Michael Fiske, Ali Kazemian

## Abstract
By leveraging the capabilities of construction 3D printing, building structures in harsh extraterrestrial environments is conceivable. Sulfur concrete is a waterless construction material that offers great potential to replace Portland cement concrete (PCC) in extraterrestrial construction. The shape stability of 3D printed Martian sulfur-regolith concrete (SRC) was found to benefit from a lower substrate layer temperature. However, this comes at the cost of flexural strength, resulting in up to 53% strength loss. The printed SRC specimens demonstrated a significantly faster strength development rate (gaining about 85% of the ultimate strength after only 12 h) compared to the printed PCC. The printed SRC specimens also outperformed the PCC specimens in vacuum conditions at higher temperatures. Furthermore, modifying the SRC materials with Dicyclopentadiene resulted in up to 44% strength increase and minimized the sublimation rate of the printed specimens in vacuum, especially at an elevated temperature.

## 1. Introduction
NASA’s Artemis program aims to land astronauts on the Moon to establish a sustained human presence. Supporting infrastructure such as landing pads, habitats, and protective shields are needed. Extrusion-based Construction 3D Printing (C3DP) using in-situ resources (ISRU) on the Moon and Mars is critical to avoid the prohibitive cost of transporting materials from Earth.

## 4. Results and Discussion
### 4.2.1 Effects of process parameters on shape stability
Layer deformations occurred at the shortest interlayer time gap (45 s). With a 45 s delay, layers printed at 130 °C and 150 °C exhibited 10% and 23% deformation, respectively. Longer delays (120 s and 180 s) allowed the bottom layer to solidify, preventing visible deformation.

### 4.2.4 Recyclability
Printed SRC and MSRC specimens were recycled by remelting and reprinting. No significant changes were observed in flowability or flexural strength, highlighting a cradle-to-cradle sustainability approach.

### 4.3.2 Performance under vacuum
SRC specimens showed negligible weight loss at room temperature in vacuum (0.016% after 7 days). At 55 °C, weight loss increased to 0.92%. DCPD modification reduced weight loss/sublimation by up to 85% compared to unmodified SRC.

## 5. Conclusions
- Martian SRC was successfully 3D printed, proving the feasibility of waterless off-world construction.
- SRC exhibits faster strength development (85% in 12 h) compared to PCC (19% in 12 h).
- SRC specimens outperformed PCC in vacuum and high-temperature conditions.
- DCPD modification increases strength by up to 44% and significantly reduces sublimation.
