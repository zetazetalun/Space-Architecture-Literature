# Construction Technology of the Curved ‘Mars House’

**Authors:** Xiqiang Lin, Hailong Wang, Xiaoyan Sun, Wael Kikhia, Zhennan Wu, Lu Lan, and Jiawei Zhao
**Publisher:** Springer Nature Switzerland AG (3DcP 2023, STAM)
**DOI:** 10.1007/978-3-031-64269-2_30

## Abstract
3D printing technology is one of the most likely construction technologies to be used when building extraterrestrial bases in the future. The Mars 1 Base project in Gansu Province, China simulates the Martian landscape and uses concrete 3D printing technology to construct houses, creating a unique cultural tourism industry project. This article introduces the structural design, 3D printing process and overall installation and construction of the Mars house. The 3D-printed hotel is egg-shaped, with a total height of 9 m and a floor area of 46 square meters, divided into two floors with triangular windows and a skylight on the top.

## 1 Introduction
Advancements in Mars exploration have sparked interest in Mars colonization and habitat development. Concrete 3D printing technology presents a promising approach to constructing Martian habitats, addressing challenges such as extreme environments and limited resources.

## 2 Architectural Design and Structural Design of Mars House
The egg-shaped structure was chosen to withstand harsh Martian conditions (strong winds, radiation, temperature differences). The house is 9 m high with a floor area of 46 square meters.

### Structural System
- **Outer Shell:** Concrete 3D printed wall used as a permanent mould.
- **Reinforcement:** Horizontal steel mesh placed every 25 cm; vertical φ12 mm reinforcement inserted inside the printed shell.
- **Core:** C40 self-compacting concrete poured into the printed mould to form a composite reinforced concrete structure.
- **Segmentation:** The structure is divided into four rings for printing and assembly.

## 3 3D Printing Construction Technology and Process

### 3.1 Mars House 3D Printing Process
The process includes: Model refinement -> Preparation -> Factory printing -> Concrete pouring -> Transportation -> On-site assembly -> Decoration.

### 3.2 Personnel, Materials, and Machines
- **Printers:** 2 industrial-grade concrete 3D printers.
- **Dimensions:** 14.8 m × 8.6 m × 5.5 m.
- **Print Parameters:** Layer height: 15 mm; Print speed: 120 mm/s; Extrusion width: 40 mm.
- **Material Consumption:** 10.6 m³ of 3D printing material; 17.76 m³ of internal filling concrete (Total: 28.36 m³).

### 3.3 3D Printed Concrete Mix Proportion and Performance

**Table 1. The concrete mix ratio for 3D printing of the Mars house**
| Raw materials | OPC42.5 cement/% | Artificial sand/% | Admixture/% | Water/ % |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Mix proportion | 38 | 42 | 4 | 16 |

**Mechanical Performance (at 28 days):**
- **Compressive Strength (Standard Moulding):** 47.9 MPa
- **Compressive Strength (3D Printed):** 40.3 MPa (16% reduction)
- **Interlayer Tensile Strength:** 3.2 MPa
- **Interlayer Bonding Strength:** 2.7 MPa
- **Permeability Grade:** P12

### 3.4 3D Printing Prefabrication of Components
Components are printed as ring-shaped structures in the factory. It takes 6 days to complete 4 ring-shaped structures. Interface agents are used between layers to ensure bonding.

## 4 Mar House On-Site Assembly and Construction
Printed components are transported to the site via truck. A large crane assembles the four ring-shaped structures. Structural columns are formed by welding reinforcement cages at reserved positions and pouring mortar.

## 5 Summary
The Mars habitat hotel project demonstrated that concrete 3D printing improves construction speed and accuracy. This egg-shaped structure serves as a technical baseline for additive intelligent construction in extreme environments.
