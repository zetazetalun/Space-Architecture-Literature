# Sulfur-based materials for martian construction: Pioneering sustainable extraterrestrial building solutions

**Wei Sun, Jiabao Zhao, Handong Yan, Zijin Xiao, Tiejiong Lou**

**Journal:** Alexandria Engineering Journal 117 (2025) 27–44  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.aej.2025.01.008

## Abstract
The overarching purpose of this study is to devise sustainable construction materials and techniques for Martian infrastructure, addressing challenges such as water scarcity and extreme temperatures. The study investigates Martian bricks fabricated with varying components (Martian soil simulant and different aggregates), bond thicknesses, and recycling iterations. Compressive tests on cube specimens and four-point bending tests for bond strength reveal that MS simulant enhances compressive strength (25.2 MPa at 20% content). Fe2O3 aggregates achieved the highest compressive strength (39.2 MPa). The study proposes pyramid-shaped structures for load distribution and highlights the recyclability of sulfur-based bricks.

## 1. Introduction
The construction of habitats on Mars is crucial for providing shelter and protection. Martian conditions (low atmospheric pressure, temperature extremes, and lack of liquid water) necessitate water-free construction methods. Elemental sulfur (ES) is an appealing binder as it can be extracted from Martian sulfides and used without water.

## 2. Experiments
Martian bricks were fabricated using MS simulant (analogous to JSC-1m) and sulfur. Sulfur powder was heated to 140°C to reach a molten state, mixed with aggregates, and cast into molds. Compressive tests used 25.4 mm cube molds. Bond strength was tested using four-point bending tests on two cubic bricks joined with sulfur-based binder.

## 3. Results and Discussion
*   **Compressive Behavior:** Bricks with 20% MS simulant achieved an average strength of 25.2 MPa. 
*   **Bond Thickness:** A 3 mm thickness was found optimal, yielding a bond strength of 1.42 MPa. Thicknesses above 3 mm led to decreased strength due to shrinkage and cracking.
*   **Aggregate Types:** Fe2O3 aggregates provided the highest compressive strength (39.2 MPa), while SiO2 aggregates provided stronger interfacial bonds (1.32 MPa).
*   **Recyclability:** Initial recycling reduced strength from 33.4 MPa to 26.3 MPa, but subsequent cycles showed partial recovery to 29.9 MPa.

## 4. Pyramid-shaped Structures
The study proposes modular pyramid-shaped designs to manage Martian loads. These structures offer stable bases, efficient load distribution (including negative pressure from internal pressurization), and resistance to wind and dust storms. Martian ice blocks are suggested as temporary supports during construction.

## 5. Conclusions
1. MS simulant improves sulfur-brick compressive strength from 16.8 MPa to 25.2 MPa.
2. Optimal bond thickness is 3 mm (1.42 MPa strength).
3. Fe2O3 aggregates yield highest compressive strength; SiO2 gives superior bonding.
4. Sulfur-based bricks are recyclable with manageable strength loss.
5. Pyramid geometry is highly effective for Martian environmental resilience.
