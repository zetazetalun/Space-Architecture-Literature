# A Survey on Extraterrestrial Habitation Structures with a Focus on Energy-Saving 3D Printing Techniques

**Authors:** Mitra Khalilidermani and Dariusz Knez  
**Publisher:** MDPI (Applied Sciences)  
**Published:** 2 December 2023  
**DOI:** 10.3390/app132312913

## Abstract
In the past two decades, various space agencies have shown great enthusiasm for constructing habitable structures on lunar and Martian surfaces. The objective is to evaluate different habitat construction techniques from technical, economic, and energy-consumption perspectives. The primary focus is on the 3D printing technique. It was found that the combination of 3D-printed components along with an internal breathable inflatable module is the most promising technique. Microwave sintering was identified as the most energy-saving and reliable approach for melting regolith.

## 1. Introduction
Future space habitation is envisioned for the sustainability of mankind in the solar system. NASA's Mars Exploration Program and the Artemis program are actively pursuing permanent extraterrestrial habitats. Key engineering challenges include unfavorable physics, risky regolith characteristics, and uncertain construction techniques.

## 2. Physics-Based Challenges
Primary challenges include:
- **Microgravity:** Imposes instability; necessitates foundations fastened to regolith.
- **Cryogenic Temperatures:** Reach -230°C on the Moon and -140°C on Mars; requires thermal insulation and materials resistant to thermal stress.
- **Low Atmospheric Pressure:** 0.6 kPa on Mars and nearly vacuum on the Moon; curtails fluid applications and necessitates protective layers against micrometeoroids.
- **Radiation:** Solar winds, flares, and cosmic rays require shielding (1.5 m to 2 m of regolith).
- **Micrometeoroids:** High-speed impacts require resilient composites (Kevlar) or 3D-printed regolith shells.

## 2.2. Aggregate Ability of the Regolith
Regolith can be used in raw form (stored in bags as shielding) or synthesized form (combined with binders or sintered). 
- **Sulfur Concrete:** Reaches compressive strengths of ~55.16 MPa.
- **Sintering Methods:** Laser sintering, solar sintering, and microwave sintering.

## 2.4. 3D Printing Technology
Three main techniques are discussed:
1. **Monolite (D-shape):** Selective activation/solidification of material layers.
2. **Contour Crafting (CC):** Extrusion based; uses mixtures similar to concrete.
3. **Freeform Construction:** Extrusion of material with binders.

### Comparison of Sintering Methods
- **Laser Sintering:** Extremely high energy consumption.
- **Solar Sintering:** Unlimited energy but dependent on location (poor at poles) and lens cleaning.
- **Microwave Sintering:** 33–44 times less energy than laser; greater heat penetration depth.

## 3. Results and Discussion
- **Inflatables:** Best for breathable environments; lightweight, high tensile strength, and air-tight.
- **3D Printing:** Best for outer protection; reduces transportation of raw materials (though binders may still need transport).
- **Regolith Properties:** Lunar regolith density increases with depth (1.30 to 1.69 g/cm³), requiring different energy levels for excavation compared to Mars.

## 4. Conclusions
- Cryogenic temperature is the most restricting factor for habitat infrastructure.
- Safe outposts are most constructible using 3D-printed regolith shells with internal inflatable modules.
- Binders constitute 60% of total interplanetary transportation costs; developing in-situ binders is a research priority.
