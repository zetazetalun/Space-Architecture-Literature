# Lunar base infrastructure construction: Challenges and future directions

**Authors:** Yifeng Xia, Yuyue Gao, Wenbin Han, Xinyi Li, Cheng Zhou, Yan Zhou, Lieyun Ding
**Journal:** Automation in Construction 176 (2025) 106251
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.autcon.2025.106251](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.autcon.2025.106251)

## Abstract
As deep space exploration advances, agencies such as NASA and ESA have developed mid-to-long-term plans for lunar habitation. This article reviews existing lunar construction concepts and designs, summarizing research in transportation, navigation, energy, and habitation infrastructure. It addresses site selection, building materials, and structural design while highlighting future challenges in lunar infrastructure development.

## 1. Introduction
Lunar infrastructure is a focal point for global space initiatives due to resources like helium-3 and the Moon's role as a stepping stone to Mars. Key programs include the US Artemis Program, China's International Lunar Research Station (ILRS), and ESA's Terrae Novae roadmap. Construction on the Moon faces extreme challenges: high vacuum, microgravity, drastic temperature fluctuations (-170°C to 110°C), and abrasive regolith.

## 2. Transportation Infrastructure
### 2.1 Landing and Launching Pads
*   **Methods:** Robotic teleoperation and in-situ manufacturing using lunar regolith.
*   **Technologies:** Vacuum sintering, laser selective sintering for interlocking bricks, and lightweight metal meshes.

### 2.2 Roadbed and Pavement
*   **Methods:** Microwave heating (penetration depth benefits), solar sintering, and laser melting for regolith stabilization.

### 2.3 Elevators
*   **Concepts:** Galactic Harbor, climber-type and counterweight-type elevators. Current focus on carbon nanotubes and graphene for cables.

## 3. Energy Infrastructure
*   **Solar Power:** Proposes tall lunar towers (TLT) for the South Pole. Foldable panels can reduce transport weight by 56%.
*   **Nuclear Power:** Fission Surface Power (FSP) units (approx. 40 kW) utilizing regolith for radiation shielding (horizontally circular and vertically conical layers).
*   **Energy Storage:** Regolith-based thermal storage using helium as a working fluid. Insulation layers (20mm) can reduce night loss to 5%.

## 4. Habitation Infrastructure
### 4.1 Launched Habitation
*   **Class I (Rigid):** Prefabricated modules (e.g., T-shaped layouts).
*   **Class II (Deployable/Inflatable):** Scissor-like elements (SLE) and inflatable volumes (e.g., TransHab).

### 4.2 In-situ Habitation (Class III)
*   **3D Printing:** Examples include 'Lunar Lantern' and 'Lunar Ampullae'.
*   **Masonry Assembly:** 'Xuan Wu' plan involving robotic assembly of sintered regolith bricks.

### 4.3 Lava-tube Habitation
Natural shielding against radiation and micro-meteorites. Concepts include 'Modern Ark' for biological storage and lunar farms within tubes.

## 5. Conclusions and Challenges
1.  **Material Scaling:** Difficulty in producing large-scale structural components under microgravity.
2.  **Transportation:** Lack of regular cargo/crew transport.
3.  **Energy:** Current designs (40 kW) are insufficient for industrial-scale needs.
4.  **Communication:** Topographical interference and lack of international interoperability standards.
