# Design of an Autonomously Deployable Mars Habitat

**Conference Paper** · July 2021  
**Authors:** Rudolf Walther Erdem Neumerkel, Miruna Vecerdi, Sandra Häuplik-Meusburger  
**Conference:** 50th International Conference on Environmental Systems (ICES-2021-398)

## Abstract
This paper presents a proposal for the design of an autonomously deployable habitat for a long-duration manned mission on Mars. Key drivers in the design were the payload and volume constraints of Starship, the heavy launch vehicle developed by SpaceX and addressing habitability challenges implied by Martian conditions. The habitat is conceived to be transported in compacted form and to expand to a habitable volume by autonomously deploying after being placed on the Martian surface. For this, the integration of several different kinetic structures is proposed: a protective casing, a vertically sliding core, radially expanding girders and an inflatable membrane.

## I. Introduction
The Martian environment requires addresses to low atmospheric pressure, extreme temperatures, radiation, and MMOD impacts. A solution to transportation constraints is deployable architecture. The proposed design fits within the fairing of the SpaceX Starship and autonomously expands to a habitable volume once placed on the Martian surface.

## II. Design Method
The design integrates four main elements:
1.  **Casing:** A protective rigid shell (aluminum) divided into three identical parts for transport and docking.
2.  **Telescopic Core:** The primary rigid structure featuring a hexagonal base and a water-filled cupola for radiation protection and natural light.
3.  **Expanding Girders:** Foldable titanium girders that push the casing parts radially outward using torsional/compressive springs.
4.  **Inflatable Membrane:** A multi-layered structure (Kevlar, Nomex) with an outer chamber designed to be filled with regolith.

### Membrane Layers
- **Interior:** Nomex finish for puncture/flame resistance; redundant air bladders (CepacHD200).
- **Middle:** Restraint layer of woven Kevlar belts.
- **Exterior:** Dust protection and a fillable chamber for up to 100 cm of ground-up regolith.

## III. Arrival and Deployment Choreography
The Starship can carry two stacked habitat units. Total weight per unit is approximately 28t. After placement, the casing unlocks, and the girders expand. Internal pressure (1 bar) pushes the casing outwards and doubles the core's height. 

## IV. Architectural Quality
The habitat supports 4–6 crew members for a 2-year stay. It features sanitary units, a kitchen, hydroponic greenhouses, and crew quarters. The central atrium acts as a circulation distributor and provides vertical mobility via stairs.

## V. Conclusion
The design achieves an expandability factor of 5, transforming from a 180 m³ stowed volume to 921 m³ habitable volume. The regolith-filling strategy offers a lower-risk ISRU approach compared to energy-intensive 3D sintering.
