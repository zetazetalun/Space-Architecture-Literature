# In-depth analysis of sintering, exposure time, and layer height (um) in LRS 3D printed devices with DLP

**Journal:** Journal of Manufacturing Processes 133 (2025) 576–591  
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.jmapro.2024.11.060](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmapro.2024.11.060)  
**Authors:** Shenggui Chen, Sadaf Bashir Khan, Nan Li, Chuang Xiao

## Abstract
This study showcases a quantitative assessment of the mechanical properties of moon dust (Lunar Regolith Simulants, LRS) printed through vat polymerization. The research investigates the effects of layer height ($L_H$), exposure time ($E_T$), and sintering temperatures on the mechanical properties. A 55% volume suspension of LRS was utilized to print rod and block configurations via digital light printing (DLP). Results show that a 30 μm $L_H$ with 11,000 ms $E_T$ exhibits maximum compressive and flexural strength of 330 MPa and 100 MPa respectively at 1085 °C.

## 1. Introduction
Space agencies are exploring In-Situ Resource Utilization (ISRU) to create building blocks on planets like Mars and the Moon. Lunar regolith is an abrasive powder containing silicates and oxide minerals. While powder-based approaches like laser powder bed fusion (LPBF) and microwave sintering have been studied, vat photopolymerization offers high precision and favorable mechanical properties.

## 2. Experimental
### 2.1 Materials
- **LRS:** CLRS-2 (basaltic simulant mimicking Apollo 14 samples).
- **Resin:** Photocurable slurry (HDDA, TMP3EOTA, and TPO photoinitiator).
- **Slurry:** 55% volumetric ratio of regolith to resin.

### 2.2 Parameters
- **Layer Heights ($L_H$):** 10, 20, 30, 40, 50, 60 μm.
- **Exposure Times ($E_T$):** 3000, 5000, 7000, 11,000 ms.
- **Sintering Temperatures:** 1050 °C to 1090 °C.
- **Sintering Furnace:** SQFL-1700C (Precision ±1 °C).

## 3. Results and Discussion
### 3.1 Mechanical Strength (Green Bodies)
- Compressive strength and flexural strength correlate positively with exposure time.
- Higher layer heights (above 30 μm) tend to decrease mechanical resilience due to reduced interlayer adhesion.

### 3.2 Sintering Effects
- Sintering causes dimensional shrinkage and a color shift from gray to reddish (due to iron oxidation).
- 1085 °C was identified as the optimal sintering temperature. Above this temperature, strength slightly declines due to over-sintering/deformation.
- **Fast Sintering (3 °C/min):** Results in higher strength (330 MPa compressive) compared to slow sintering due to reduced grain growth and enhanced densification.

## 4. Conclusion
The study identifies 30 μm layer height, 11,000 ms exposure time, and 1085 °C sintering temperature as the optimal parameters for DLP-printed lunar regolith. This combination produces high-density structures suitable for lunar infrastructure, achieving compressive strengths up to 330 MPa.
