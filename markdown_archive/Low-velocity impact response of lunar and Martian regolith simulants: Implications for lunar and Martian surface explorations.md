# Low-velocity impact response of lunar and Martian regolith simulants: Implications for lunar and Martian surface explorations

**Journal:** Physics of Fluids 36, 113318 (2024)
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1063/5.0233884
**Authors:** Takuma Ishii, Arata Kioka, Jyh-Jaan Steven Huang, Yoshiki Tabuchi, Yasuhiro Yamada

## ABSTRACT
The granular mechanics of lunar and Martian regolith remain inadequately understood, impeding progress in successful exploration, landing, drilling, sampling, and construction activities on extraterrestrial surfaces. This study investigates the granular behavior of the lunar and Martian regolith under impact conditions using high-fidelity simulants (LHS-1, LMS-1, and MGS-1) and terrestrial silica sand. Findings demonstrate that crater depths in lunar highland and Martian simulants are significantly greater (up to two times) than in terrestrial sand, highlighting unique granular responses that influence future resource utilization and infrastructure development.

### Experimental Setup
- **Projectile:** Alumina sphere (10 mm diameter).
- **Drop Heights:** 10.0, 15.0, 20.0, and 25.0 mm.
- **Impact Velocity:** 0.44 to 0.70 m/s.
- **Analysis:** High-resolution 3D topographical mapping (0.05 mm accuracy) and X-ray computed tomography (X-CT).

## RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
- **Crater Depths:** LHS-1 and MGS-1 exhibited the greatest crater depths, nearly identical to each other. T-8 (terrestrial sand) showed the smallest depth, indicating higher resistance to penetration.
- **Velocity Scaling:** Crater depth increases almost linearly with impact velocity (0.44–0.70 m/s).
- **Power-law Scaling:** The relationship between kinetic energy (Ek) and crater depth (d) was expressed as:
    - LHS-1: d ~ Ek^0.553
    - LMS-1: d ~ Ek^0.417
    - MGS-1: d ~ Ek^0.473
    - T-8: d ~ Ek^0.672
- **Geotechnical Factors:** Differences in cratering efficiency are attributed to internal friction angles, cohesion (307-393 Pa in simulants vs 0 Pa in sand), and porosity.

## CONCLUSIONS
Lunar highlands and Martian surfaces may exhibit reduced resistance to impact due to higher porosity and specific grain morphology compared to lunar mare surfaces. These findings are crucial for optimizing landing strategies, drilling systems, and foundation stability for future space infrastructure.
