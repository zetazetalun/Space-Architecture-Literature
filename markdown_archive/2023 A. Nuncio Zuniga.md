# SUITABILITY OF SILICONE FOR SOFT-ROBOTIC EXPLORATION OF TERRESTRIAL AND EXTRATERRESTRIAL OCEAN WORLDS

**Journal:** Space Science and Technology (Космічна наука і технологія)  
**Volume/Issue:** 2023. Vol. 29. No. 3  
**Authors:** A. Nuncio Zuniga, W. Fink  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.15407/knit2023.03.047

## ABSTRACT
This work revisits relevant mechanical and chemical properties of silicone rubber — Ecoflex in this study — to assess its suitability and viability for use in soft-robotic explorer construction and subsequent deployment and as a sealant for communication beacons, sensor pods, and other electronics in extreme planetary liquid environments, such as the depths of Earth’s oceans and extraterrestrial ocean worlds. Strain at a range of temperatures, as an indicator for operational durability, was tested under various endpoint clamp forces for several compound ratios. The temperature range at which silicone rubber remains pliable was assessed to determine its deployability. The re-binding property of cured silicone rubber samples with newly curing samples was investigated for its potential for additive manufacturing in soft robotics. Finally, the dissolution resistance, non-polarity, and electrical non-conductivity of silicone rubber were studied to assess its suitability for sealing electronics to be submerged in the salt water of both ocean and saturated salinity, as well as in hydrocarbon liquids.

## 1. INTRODUCTION
Subsurface oceans on planetary bodies known as ocean worlds (e.g., Europa, Titan, Enceladus) are prime candidate locales for astrobiological exploration. Current underwater autonomous vehicles are dominated by rigid frames, which are limited in their ability to navigate small apertures. Soft-robotics presents a more effective paradigm for liquid environment exploration. This paper re-examines silicone rubber (Ecoflex) for constructing soft-robotic explorers and sealing electronic components in extreme planetary liquid environments.

## 2. MATERIALS AND METHODS
### 2.1. Ecoflex Overview
Ecoflex™ 00-30 was chosen as a stand-in for other polymers. It is a proprietary silicone rubber made by mixing two compounds, A and B. It cures in approximately 4 hours at room temperature and is known for its high elasticity and ease of casting.

### 2.2. Testing Procedures
*   **Strain Testing:** Conducted at various temperatures (-5 °C to 40 °C) and A:B mixing ratios (20:80, 50:50, 80:20).
*   **Low Temperature Testing:** Samples were exposed to -7.5 °C, -20 °C, -80 °C, and -196 °C (liquid nitrogen).
*   **Re-bonding Ability:** Investigated by pouring fresh Ecoflex against already cured pieces to test structural integrity after curing.
*   **Chemical/Electrical Tests:** Submersion in salt water (3.5% and saturated) and mineral oil; testing for non-conductivity by submerging a Raspberry Pi directly in the liquid silicone before curing.

## 4. RESULTS
### 4.1. Strain Testing
Increased amounts of compound A led to a stiffer material. Strain results were largely temperature-independent within the measured range (-5 °C to 40 °C).

### 4.2. Stretching to Failure
The 50:50 mix exhibited a breaking elongation of 553.3 ± 20.5% at room temperature.

### 4.3. Very Low Temperature Testing
*   Samples remained pliable at 0 °C and -20 °C.
*   All ratios froze at -80 °C but returned to pre-freezing pliability upon thawing.
*   At -196 °C, samples froze and shattered; however, they regained pliability upon returning to room temperature.

### 4.4. Re-bonding Ability
New Ecoflex cured successfully against pieces up to 6 days old, creating a bond nearly indistinguishable from a single curing session. This supports potential use in additive manufacturing.

### 4.5. Dissolution and Conductivity
*   **Salt Water:** No degradation after 21 days of submersion.
*   **Mineral Oil:** No degradation after two months.
*   **Non-conductivity:** A Raspberry Pi encapsulated in Ecoflex functioned perfectly for 6 hours while powered by a 5000 mAh bank.

## 5. DISCUSSION & CONCLUSIONS
Ecoflex 00-30 is an excellent encapsulation option for soft-robotics. It maintains pliability down to at least -20 °C and likely functional down to -50 °C. While it freezes at the extremely low temperatures found on Titan (-179 °C), it remains a robust candidate for aqueous planetary environments (Europa/Enceladus) where brines may depress the freezing point. The material's ability to facilitate additive manufacturing and protect electronics in conductive liquid environments makes it a prime candidate for future space exploration missions.
