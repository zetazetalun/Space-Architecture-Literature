# Materials and design concepts for space-resilient structures

**Authors:** Mohannad Z. Naser, Alaa I. Chehab  
**Journal:** Progress in Aerospace Sciences  
**Volume/Issue:** 98 (2018) 74–90  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.paerosci.2018.03.004

## Abstract
Space exploration and terraforming nearby planets have been fascinating concepts for the longest time. Nowadays, that technological advancements with regard to space exploration are thriving, it is only a matter of time before humans can start colonizing nearby moons and planets. This paper presents a state-of-the-art literature review on recent developments of “space-native” construction materials, and highlights evolutionary design concepts for “space-resilient” structures (i.e., colonies and habitats). This paper also details effects of harsh (and unique) space environments on various terrestrial and extraterrestrial construction materials, as well as on space infrastructure and structural systems. The feasibility of exploiting available space resources in terms of “in-situ resource utilization” and “harvesting of elements and compounds”, as well as emergence of enabling technologies such as “cultured (lab-grown)” space construction materials are discussed. Towards the end of the present review, number of limitations and challenges facing Lunar and Martian exploration, and venues in-need for urgent research are identified and examined.

## 1. Introduction
Key motivations include the high cost of transporting materials from Earth ($20,000/kg to the Moon) and the abundance of useful elements in lunar/Martian regolith. Research focuses on tailoring construction materials from local resources while overcoming challenges of microgravity and vacuum conditions.

## 2. Space Environment and Loading
### 2.1 Space Environment
- Lack of atmosphere causes temperature fluctuations (-173 to 127°C on Moon).
- Meteorite/micrometeorite impacts (20–70 km/s speeds).
- Vacuum leads to outgassing and accelerated water loss in hydraulic materials.
- Radiation (Solar flares, Galactic Cosmic Radiation).

### 2.2 Loading and Mechanics
Gravity on the Moon is 1/6th of Earth's; Mars is roughly 1/3rd. Foundations must be placed below the depth of thermal cycling to control settlement.

## 3. Space Resources (ISRU)
ISRU is defined as operations harnessing raw resources to produce products/services for robotic and human exploration. Lunar regolith is rich in alumina, calcium oxide, and silicate.

## 4. Properties of Space Construction Materials
### 4.1 Concrete Derivates
- **Sulfur Concrete:** Non-hydraulic (waterless). Sulfur melts at 120–150°C. Lunar sulfur concrete strengths: 39–75.7 MPa.
- **Geopolymers:** Produced by reaction between aluminosilicate precursors and alkaline solutions. Strengths: 16.6 to 33.1 MPa.
- **Waterless Concrete:** Use of resins, epoxies, or thermoplastic polymers. Polymer-bound simulant (10% polymer) reached 12.75 MPa.

### 4.2 Metals and Alloys
Aluminum, magnesium, iron, and titanium are prioritized. Magnesium provides 30x the vibration damping of aluminum, useful for impact protection.

### 4.3 Advanced Materials
- **Composites:** Graphite/epoxy (Gr/E), Ceramic Matrix Composites (CMC).
- **Shape Memory Materials:** Shape Memory Alloys (SMA) and Polymers (SMP) for self-deployable mechanisms.
- **Bio-inspired Materials:** Harvesting spider silk or lab-grown fibers for structural reinforcement.

## 5. Processing Methods
- **Solar and Laser Sintering:** Heating powder below melting point using concentrated sunlight or lasers.
- **Microwave Sintering:** Volumetric heating (2.45 GHz). Can penetrate ~13.4 mm in lunar simulant.
- **Dry-Mix/Steam-Injection (DMSI):** Using steam to hydrate cement particles in vacuum; produces 50mm cubes with 69 MPa strength.

## 7. Structural Concepts
- **Class I (Pre-fabricated):** Rapidly deployed, small crews (2–6 personnel).
- **Class II (Deployable/Inflatable):** High packing coefficient, redundant structural systems (e.g., Kevlar envelopes).
- **Class III (ISRU-based):** Constructed using local resources; often terrestrial-like "rigid" structures (concrete).
- **Underground Bases:** Utilizing lava tubes (30m+ diameter) for natural shielding against radiation and meteorites.

## 9. Summary and Conclusions
1. Harsh environment necessitates specialized materials.
2. Concrete and its derivatives (sulfur, geopolymer) are high-potential candidates.
3. Habitats categorized into stationary/mobile and surface/underground (lava tubes).
4. Logistics, energy, and robotics remain critical bottlenecks.
