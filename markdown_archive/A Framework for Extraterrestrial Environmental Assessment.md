# A Framework for Extraterrestrial Environmental Assessment

**Author:** William R. Kramer
**Affiliation:** Outer Space Consulting, Frederick, MD, 21701, USA
**Journal:** Space Policy 53 (2020) 101385
**DOI:** 10.1016/j.spacepol.2020.101385

## Abstract
Many of the proposed and foreseeable activities on the Moon, Mars, and elsewhere in our solar system will require construction and similar actions. Most will, by intent, alter those natural environments in a variety of ways. This article advocates the need for an appropriate assessment and review process and proposes a simple framework as a starting point for discussion. It suggests that space industries and other space actors themselves should consider developing and using an appropriately structured assessment process rather than relying on terrestrial frameworks like NEPA which may be counterproductive.

## 1. A rapidly evolving problem in space development
Nearly all reasonably foreseeable physical activities on the Moon, Mars, and elsewhere, such as construction, mining, energy production, and human habitation, may adversely affect those extraterrestrial environments. Alterations to alien landscapes could have longer-lasting implications than anthropogenic changes on Earth due to the lack of tectonic movements and vegetation.

## 2. The neglected need for extraterrestrial environmental assessment
Regardless of space industries, government agencies, and other space actors, such as scientific missions, not recognizing the critical need for extraterrestrial environmental assessments (EEAs), once proposed actions on celestial bodies are underway, it is predictable that interest groups and perhaps governments will demand them.

### Potential Impacts of Mining and Construction:
- **Ejected Rocks:** Blasting could damage nearby infrastructure.
- **Vibrations:** Could invalidate data collected by distant seismic studies.
- **Dust:** Could coat solar panels, interfere with telescopes, or erode gaskets.
- **Chemical Leaks:** Lubricants and compounds could seep into subsurface water ice resources.

## 3. National Environmental Policy Act (NEPA)
NEPA is currently limited to actions with a US federal nexus and is not recommended for direct application to extraterrestrial actions. Direct application would be impractical and counterproductive due to lack of international authority and the cumbersome nature of the tool.
