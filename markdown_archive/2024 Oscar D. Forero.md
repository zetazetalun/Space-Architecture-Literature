# Seismic Vulnerability Assessment of Non-Structural Elements Inside an Inflatable Lunar Habitat

**Authors:** Oscar D. Forero, Julio A. Ramirez, Ph.D., and Shirley J. Dyke, Ph.D.
**Publication:** Earth and Space 2024, ASCE, pp. 625-636

## Abstract
The existence of moonquakes is often overlooked in habitat design. This study develops a procedure for vulnerability assessment for essential non-structural elements (NSE), specifically the environmental control and life support system (ECLSS), inside an inflatable habitat. Findings suggest that due to epistemic uncertainty, significant disturbances may result in long-lasting vibrations requiring effective mitigation measures.

## Introduction
Under NASA's Artemis program, plans are underway for surface missions focusing on inflatable habitats. Inflatables offer significantly increased payload volume upon deployment. This study compares the performance of habitats under launch vibrations versus shallow moonquakes.

## Case Study
- **Habitat Type:** Torus-shaped inflatable structure mounted atop a lander.
- **Height:** 6 meters, two floors.
- **Support:** Cantilever hydraulic jack system.
- **Internal Element:** ECLSS universal pallet (0.60m x 0.60m x 1.80m) located on the second floor.
- **Material:** Aluminum core and pedestal.

## Launch Dynamic Environment
Payloads face dynamic loads from engine ignition, liftoff, and aerodynamic pressure. Tests include:
- **Sine Sweep Test:** Specifications matching Atlas V and Falcon vehicle requirements.
- **Random Vibration Test:** Based on NASA-STD-7001B workmanship criteria (2-minute duration).

## Lunar Seismic Motion Environment
- **Data Source:** Apollo Passive Seismic Experiment (APSE).
- **Key Events:** 28 shallow moonquakes (High-Frequency Teleseismic events - HFT).
- **Observations:** Ground acceleration near sources can reach 1.6 gL to 4 gL. Moonquakes operate primarily at low frequencies (< 10 Hz).

## Numerical Simulation and Results
A time-stepping technique (Newmark's method) with 5% critical damping was used.

### Findings
- **Displacement:** Under a scaled value of 1.0 gL, the deployed torus exhibited a maximum displacement of 2.4 cm.
- **Duration:** Lunar seismic responses can last over an hour, whereas launch effects persist for only a few minutes.
- **Resonance:** The low-frequency nature of moonquakes (< 10 Hz) can resonate with specific surface habitat designs, a factor not accounted for in launch vibration criteria.
- **Scale Factor:** A scale factor of 3.0 gL was identified as the ground acceleration producing deformation responses similar to those of the launch environment.

## Conclusion
Ignoring the risk of the lunar seismic environment in favor of only launch environment design could result in undesired outcomes for habitats during extended missions. Future work must refine probabilistic methods for lunar seismic assessment.
