# Seismic Vulnerability Assessment of Non-Structural Elements Inside an Inflatable Lunar Habitat

**Authors:** Oscar D. Forero, Julio A. Ramirez, Ph.D., and Shirley J. Dyke, Ph.D.
**Affiliation:** Purdue University, West Lafayette, IN.
**Source:** Earth and Space 2024 (ASCE), pp. 625–636.

## Abstract
Moonquakes are often overlooked in surface habitation design. This study develops a vulnerability assessment procedure for essential non-structural elements (NSE), specifically the Environmental Control and Life Support System (ECLSS), inside an inflatable habitat based on NASA’s Moon to Mars Architecture requirements. Findings suggest that significant disturbances and long-lasting vibrations from seismic events require effective mitigation measures, as launch environment designs may not fully account for low-frequency lunar seismic hazards.

## Introduction
NASA’s Artemis program aims for a short-term surface mission by the end of this decade. Inflatable habitats (e.g., BEAM, Sierra Space LIFE) are preferred for their high payload-volume-to-mass ratio. This paper compares Class I (pre-fabricated/lander-mounted) inflatable torus habitat performance under launch vibrations and shallow moonquakes.

## Case Study
- **Habitat:** Torus-shaped inflatable structure mounted atop a lander.
- **Dimensions:** 6m height, two floors, aluminum central core.
- **Target Element:** ECLSS universal pallet (0.60m x 0.60m x 1.80m) on the second floor.
- **Simulation:** Newmark's method for dynamic equations of motion with 5% critical damping.

## Launch and Lunar Environments
- **Launch Dynamic Environment:** Includes sine sweep and random vibration tests (Atlas V, Falcon 9 standards). Durations are typically short (minutes).
- **Lunar Seismic Motion Environment:** Based on Apollo Passive Seismic Experiment (APSE) data. Focuses on 28 "High-Frequency Teleseismic" (HFT) shallow moonquakes.
- **Key Observation:** Ground acceleration can reach 1.6 gL to 4.0 gL near the source. Seismic events on the Moon can last for over an hour due to the highly fractured crust causing low attenuation.

## Numerical Simulation Results
- **Primary Structure Response:** At a scaled 1.0 gL, the deployed inflatable habitat exhibited a maximum displacement of 2.4 cm (compared to 0.6 cm in the undeployed/stowed configuration).
- **Comparison Factor:** A scale factor of 3.0 gL was identified as the point where lunar seismic deformation closely approximates the design loads of the launch environment.
- **Time Factor:** Lunar seismic responses last over an hour, whereas launch vibrations persist for only a few minutes.

| Parameter | Launch Environment | Lunar Seismic Environment |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Frequency** | High Frequency | Low Frequency (< 10 Hz) |
| **Duration** | Minutes | > 1 Hour |
| **Max Displacement (1.0gL)** | 0.6 cm (undeployed) | 2.4 cm (deployed) |

## Discussion and Conclusion
Lunar seismic activity operates at lower frequencies (<10Hz) which can resonate with certain surface habitat designs. The prolonged duration of these events introduces risks of material fatigue and fracture not accounted for in standard launch-centric vibration testing. Future long-term surface settlements must incorporate these seismic effects into their structural design optimization.
