# Micromechanical testing and property upscaling of planetary rocks: A critical review

**Authors:** Yiwei Liu, Guoping Zhang, Jiangmei Qiao, Xuhai Tang
**Journal:** International Journal of Mining Science and Technology
**Volume:** 34 (2024)
**Pages:** 1217–1241
**DOI:** 10.1016/j.ijmst.2024.08.002

## Abstract
Knowledge of the mechanical behavior of planetary rocks is indispensable for space explorations. The scarcity of pristine samples and the irregular shapes of planetary meteorites make it difficult to obtain representative samples for conventional macroscale rock mechanics experiments (macro-RMEs). This critical review discusses recent advances in microscale RMEs (micro-RMEs) techniques and the upscaling methods for extracting mechanical parameters. Methods of mineralogical and microstructural analyses, along with non-destructive mechanical techniques, have provided new opportunities for studying planetary rocks with unprecedented precision and capabilities. Finally, the potential applications of planetary rock mechanics research to guiding the design and execution of space missions are envisioned, ranging from sample return missions and planetary defense to extraterrestrial construction.

## 1. Introduction
Understanding the mechanical properties of planetary rocks is crucial for advancing deep space exploration missions. These properties are essential for tasks such as planetary sampling, establishing space research stations, planetary defense, and future space mining endeavors. They provide valuable insights into the compressive strength, elasticity, and toughness of meteorites, which guide in-situ resource utilization (ISRU) technologies, influencing material selection and processing methods.

## 3. Methods of Nondestructive Microscale Mechanical Experiments
### 3.1. Nanoindentation
Nanoindentation is used to measure hardness and elastic modulus by applying a controlled load through a sharp indenter. It is particularly beneficial for precious samples like carbonaceous chondrites and lunar/Martian regolith, as the samples can be reused after testing.

### 3.2. Atomic Force Microscopy (AFM)
AFM offers atomic-level resolution to map surface topography and mechanical properties (adhesion, Young’s modulus). Tapping mode and PeakForce QNM are highlighted as non-destructive mapping modes.

## 4. Upscaling of Microscale Mechanical Properties
The paper reviews homogenization schemes used to relate micro-measurements to the strength of the intact planetary body:
- **Mori-Tanaka (M-T) scheme:** Considers interactions among mineral inclusions.
- **Self-consistent scheme (SCS):** Accounts for stress and strain inhomogeneity.
- **Voigt-Reuss-Hill Average:** Provides bounds for effective elastic modulus.
- **Accurate Grain-Based Modeling (AGBM):** A numerical simulation incorporating detailed microstructural data.

## 5. Prospective Applications
### 5.3. Extraterrestrial Construction
ISRU is the most promising technique for sustainable and cost-effective construction. Regolith consolidation methods include:
- Cold sintering
- Laser sintering
- Geopolymerization
- Crosslinking
- 3D printing (additive manufacturing)

## 6. Conclusions
1. Non-destructive micro-RMEs (nanoindentation, AFM) provide high-precision research tools for studying planetary rocks.
2. Upscaling methods like AGBM are essential for predicting macroscale behavior from microscale observations.
3. Future research must focus on building a comprehensive database of planetary rock mechanical properties to support space colonization.
