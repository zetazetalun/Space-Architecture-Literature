# Numerical Simulation of 3D Printing of Infrastructure Materials

**Authors:** E. Ramyar, Z. Xinwei, and G. Cusatis  
**Publication:** Earth and Space 2021, ASCE

## ABSTRACT
3D printing is poised to become a disruptive force for the terrestrial construction industry, and it is also the most likely technology that will make possible the safe and reliable development of extraterrestrial infrastructure. The main objective of this paper is to highlight the formulation of the discrete fresh concrete model (DFCM). DFCM is a particle-based fresh cementitious material model that simulates the rheological and mechanical behavior of material accurately before, during, and after printing to optimize the printing process and the material flow.

## INTRODUCTION
The emergence of automation and control processes is revolutionizing the construction industry, particularly concrete 3D printing. The challenge is twofold: adapting technology to rheological characteristics and developing new materials. Computational approaches are crucial for deep understanding of both the printing process and the behavior of printed products.

## DISCRETE FRESH CONCRETE MODEL (DFCM)
The DFCM adopts rigid composite spherical particles of different sizes to simulate coarse stiff aggregate surrounding a layer of soft mortar (the "excess paste theory"). Interaction among particles is governed by visco-plastic constitutive equations based on stress-strain relationships, enabling a seamless transition from fresh to hardened concrete descriptions. The mortar layer is considered a Bingham fluid with specific rheological characteristics.

### Key Parameters
- Tensile strength ($\sigma_t$)
- Mortar normal modulus ($E_N$)
- Shear yield stress ($\sigma_{\tau0}$)
- Plastic viscosity ($\mu_\infty$)
- Mortar thickness ($h$)

## REPRODUCING EXPERIMENTAL RESULTS
The model was validated using Slump and ICAR rheometer tests:
- **Self-Consolidating Concrete (SCC):** Spread range of 45.5 to 81.0 cm. Simulation showed 60 cm diameter and 25.0 cm slump.
- **Printable Concrete:** Showed minimal slump (decrease in height and horizontal spread of only a couple centimeters).
- **Rheometer Tests:** DFCM reproduced torque vs. rotational speed results, identifying that printable concrete is less rate-dependent than standard concrete.

## NUMERICALLY 3D PRINTING OF THE NORTHWESTERN MARTIAN HABITAT
Northwestern University, with Skidmore, Owings & Merrill (SOM), designed a dome-shaped Martian habitat for NASA’s 3D-Printed Habitat Challenge. The DFCM was used to simulate the layer-by-layer printing process, monitoring material flow and final configuration. This model is essential for long-term extraterrestrial landing where construction must be accomplished by autonomous remote operations.

## CONCLUSION
DFCM is an accurate tool for modeling the behavior of self-compacting, ordinary, and printable concrete. It can be used successfully to simulate the 3D printing process of concrete structures on any planet.
