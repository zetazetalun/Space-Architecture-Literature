# Multi-scale modeling of hydrogel-based concrete formed under the ambient environment and the extremely harsh environment of Mars

## Metadata
- **Journal:** Journal of the Mechanics and Physics of Solids
- **Volume:** 196 (2025) 105969
- **Authors:** Ning Liu, Tianju Xue, Jishen Qiu
- **Affiliation:** Hong Kong University of Science and Technology
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmps.2024.105969

## Abstract
Hydrogel-based concretes (HBCs) are an emerging class of load-bearing composite materials consisting of inert particles joined together by micro-hydrogel joints. As HBCs can harden via sol-gel process and H2O phase changes under a freezing temperature and vacuum, they are suitable for future extraterrestrial constructions. This study presents a new multi-scale model that quantitatively predicts the mechanical properties of HBC formed under different curing environments including Martian atmosphere. On the micro-scale, four representative joint microstructures are studied: tubular, foamy, honeycomb, and tube-cased-foam joints. On the macro-scale, the study simplified the constitutive joint relationships in a linear parallel bond model (LPM) and employed them in a discrete element method (DEM)-based HBC model.

## Key Findings
1. **Microstructural Evolution:** Curing under Martian conditions (-55 C, 10 Pa) results in foamy interparticle joints due to water freezing/sublimation, whereas ambient curing results in tubular joints due to evaporation.
2. **Mechanical Performance:** HBC incorporating minimal polymer (20 kg/m3) and inert sand achieved a compressive strength of 5 MPa, which is structurally adequate for Martian gravity (3.7 m/s2).
3. **Modeling Accuracy:** The proposed multi-scale DEM model accurately predicts compressive stress-strain curves and the influence of mix designs (sand-to-aggregate ratio) and curing history on bulk material properties.

## Conclusion
The study validates that HBC structures could be built with significantly less energy input than high-power laser or microwave sintering methods. The multi-scale DEM model successfully captures the influence of s/a ratios and environmental conditions, providing a framework for future microstructural design in extreme environments.
