# The Sample Analysis Laboratory (SAL) at the German Aerospace Center (DLR) Berlin – a cutting edge laboratory for extraterrestrial material analysis

**S. P. Garland, J. Helbert, A. van den Neucker, E. Bonato, C. Hamann, A. Greshake, A. Maturilli, G. Alemanno, L. Hecht, S. Adeli, I. Büttner, and H. Rauer**

## ABSTRACT
Sample return missions provide the ultimate ground truth for interpretation of remote sensing data and enable an unprecedented level of analysis compared to in-situ instruments. In response to the explosion of interest in this field in recent years the German Aerospace Center (DLR) in Berlin is constructing a Sample Analysis Laboratory (SAL) for the analysis and curation of returned samples in close cooperation with the Museum für Naturkunde (MfN), and in collaboration with colleagues at NASA and JAXA. Key topics to which SAL will contribute are the geochemical history of hydration, oxidation and alteration of planetary surfaces through analysing natural and artificial samples; detection of organics and possible traces or signs of extinct life; the formation and evolution of planetary bodies. In addition, SAL will extend the capabilities of the existing on-site Planetary Spectroscopy Laboratory facilities with complementary instrumentation for material analysis, supporting preparation for interpretation of NIR and MIR data from instruments on board the VERITAS, EnVision and BepiColombo missions as well as sample curation in the context of the MMX mission.

The laboratory will provide a cleanroom environment of approximately 80 m². The major analytical instruments have already been purchased for SAL: a JEOL iHP200F Field Emission Electron Microprobe Analyzer, a JEOL JSM-IT800 Field Emission Scanning Electron Microscope and a Malvern Panalytical Empyrean X-ray Diffraction system. A vis-IR microscope (Bruker Hyperion 2000) interfaced with a Bruker Vertex 80V FTIR spectrometer is already in operation and has been used for measurements on Ryugu grains from the Hayabusa2 mission, and spectral analysis of the Ribbeck aubrite. The SAL cleanroom construction is planned to be finished in January 2026, with operation and commissioning due to be well under way by Spring of 2026. This contribution will give an overview of the DLR Sample Analysis Laboratory, the time plan and major goals for the coming years.

**Keywords:** Extraterrestrial sample return, asteroids, infrared spectroscopy, microscopy, planetary research.

## 1. INTRODUCTION
The field of extraterrestrial sample return is growing in importance in the planetary science community. Robotic sample return missions have brought samples from asteroids Itokawa, Ryugu and Bennu, from comet Wild 2 and in the near future will bring samples from the Martian moon Phobos and possibly from Mars itself. In order to preserve pristine returned material special protocols for sample storage, manipulation and transport are required.

## 2. THE LABORATORY LAYOUT AND INSTRUMENTATION
The Sample Analysis Laboratory (SAL) aims to serve the dual purpose of sample curation, as well as sample preparation and analysis within the same laboratory space. The main analytical instruments include:
- **FE-SEM:** Field-emission scanning electron microscope (JEOL JSM-IT800).
- **FE-EMPA:** Field-emission electron microprobe analyser (JEOL iHP200F).
- **XRD:** X-ray diffractometer (Malvern Panalytical Empyrean).
- **FTIR Microscope:** Fourier Transform Infrared microscope (Bruker Hyperion 2000).
- **Digital Microscope:** Keyence VHX-7000 3D microscope.

The layout includes an 80 m² ISO 8 cleanroom with an airlock. Technical equipment (pumps, power, cooling) is housed in the basement to minimize contamination. Two ISO-5 glove boxes are planned for sample storage and preparation, using low-outgassing vacuum compatible components (Viton seals, Hypalon gloves) and a dry nitrogen atmosphere.

## 3. CURRENT RESEARCH
Research is already being conducted on Ryugu samples from Hayabusa2. Work focuses on space weathering effects, specifically geochemical and spectral changes in microcraters and melt splashes. Samples are handled in a provisional vacuum storage unit and glovebox. Micro-FTIR reflectance measurements on grains A0112, A0383, and C0404 allow spectral characterization in VNIR to MIR ranges (2–7 µm), enabling direct comparison with remote sensing data.

## 4. CONSTRUCTION STATUS AND OUTLOOK
Construction of the SAL cleanroom on the ground floor of the DLR Institute of Space Research in Berlin is underway, with completion expected in January 2026. Full operation as a curation facility, particularly for the MMX (Martian Moons eXploration) mission samples, is planned from 2027 onwards.
