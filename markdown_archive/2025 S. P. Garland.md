# The Sample Analysis Laboratory (SAL) at the German Aerospace Center (DLR) Berlin – a cutting edge laboratory for extraterrestrial material analysis

**Authors:** S. P. Garland, J. Helbert, A. van den Neucker, E. Bonato, C. Hamann, A. Greshake, A. Maturilli, G. Alemanno, L. Hecht, S. Adeli, I. Büttner, H. Rauer

**Affiliations:** German Aerospace Center (DLR), ESTEC, TESCAN GROUP, Museum für Naturkunde Berlin, Freie Universität Berlin

## ABSTRACT
Sample return missions provide the ultimate ground truth for interpretation of remote sensing data and enable an unprecedented level of analysis compared to in-situ instruments. In response to the explosion of interest in this field in recent years the German Aerospace Center (DLR) in Berlin is constructing a Sample Analysis Laboratory (SAL) for the analysis and curation of returned samples in close cooperation with the Museum für Naturkunde (MfN). Key topics to which SAL will contribute are the geochemical history of hydration, oxidation and alteration of planetary surfaces through analysing natural and artificial samples; detection of organics and possible traces or signs of extinct life; the formation and evolution of planetary bodies. In addition, SAL will extend the capabilities of the existing on-site Planetary Spectroscopy Laboratory facilities with complementary instrumentation for material analysis. The laboratory will provide a cleanroom environment of approximately 80 m². The major analytical instruments have already been purchased: a FE-EMPA, a FE-SEM, and an XRD system. The SAL cleanroom construction is planned to be finished in January 2026.

## 1. INTRODUCTION
The field of extraterrestrial sample return is growing, with missions bringing samples from asteroids Itokawa, Ryugu, and Bennu, and future missions targeting Phobos and Mars. To preserve pristine material, special protocols for sample storage, manipulation, and transport are required. SAL will enable storage and manipulation of sensitive material under a protective dry nitrogen atmosphere to minimize contamination.

## 2. THE LABORATORY LAYOUT AND INSTRUMENTATION
The SAL serves a dual purpose: sample curation and sample preparation/analysis within the same space. 

### Major Analytical Instruments:
- **FE-EMPA:** Field-emission electron microprobe analyzer (JEOL iHP200F) with 5 WDS spectrometers and EDS for elemental mapping.
- **FE-SEM:** Field-emission scanning electron microscope (JEOL JSM-IT800) with EDS, BSE, and SE detectors for high-resolution imaging.
- **XRD:** X-ray diffractometer (Malvern Panalytical Empyrean) for mineralogical analysis of powders or solid objects (spot size down to 140µm).
- **FTIR Microscope:** Bruker Hyperion 2000 interfaced with a Bruker Vertex 80V spectrometer for spectral mapping down to 50 µm resolution.
- **3D Microscope:** Keyence VHX-7000 for rapid detailed imaging and quasi-3D representation.

### Cleanroom Facilities:
- **Area:** ~80 m² ISO 8 cleanroom.
- **Gloveboxes:** Two ISO-5 gloveboxes for storage and preparation, using low-outgassing components (Viton seals, Hypalon gloves) and dry nitrogen environments.

## 3. CURRENT RESEARCH
Research is currently being conducted on samples from the Hayabusa2 mission (asteroid Ryugu). Work focuses on space weathering effects, specifically geochemical and spectral changes in the infrared within microcraters. High-resolution SEM images of Ryugu sample A0383 have revealed phyllosilicate matrices, magnetite, pyrrhotite, dolomite, and pentlandite.

## 4. CONSTRUCTION STATUS AND OUTLOOK
Construction is occurring on the ground floor of the DLR Institute of Space Research in Berlin. Instruments are decoupled from the floor on separate supports. Commissioning is expected in 2026, with the facility becoming a European center for extraterrestrial sample curation and handling training by 2027. A major goal is to curate a portion of the Martian Moons eXploration (MMX) samples due to return in 2031.
