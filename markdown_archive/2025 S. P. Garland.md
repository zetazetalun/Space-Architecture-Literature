# The Sample Analysis Laboratory (SAL) at the German Aerospace Center (DLR) Berlin – a cutting edge laboratory for extraterrestrial material analysis

**S. P. Garland, J. Helbert, A. van den Neucker, E. Bonato, C. Hamann, A. Greshake, A. Maturilli, G. Alemanno, L. Hecht, S. Adeli, I. Büttner, and H. Rauer**

## ABSTRACT
Sample return missions provide the ultimate ground truth for interpretation of remote sensing data and enable an unprecedented level of analysis compared to in-situ instruments. In response to the explosion of interest in this field in recent years the German Aerospace Center (DLR) in Berlin is constructing a Sample Analysis Laboratory (SAL) for the analysis and curation of returned samples in close cooperation with the Museum für Naturkunde (MfN), and in collaboration with colleagues at NASA and JAXA. Key topics to which SAL will contribute are the geochemical history of hydration, oxidation and alteration of planetary surfaces through analysing natural and artificial samples; detection of organics and possible traces or signs of extinct life; the formation and evolution of planetary bodies. In addition, SAL will extend the capabilities of the existing on-site Planetary Spectroscopy Laboratory facilities with complementary instrumentation for material analysis, supporting preparation for interpretation of NIR and MIR data from instruments on board the VERITAS, EnVision and BepiColombo missions as well as sample curation in the context of the MMX mission.

The laboratory will provide a cleanroom environment of approximately 80 m². The major analytical instruments have already been purchased for SAL: a JEOL iHP200F Field Emission Electron Microprobe Analyzer, a JEOL JSM-IT800 Field Emission Scanning Electron Microscope and a Malvern Panalytical Empyrean X-ray Diffraction system. A vis-IR microscope (Bruker Hyperion 2000) interfaced with a Bruker Vertex 80V FTIR spectrometer is already in operation and has been used for measurements on Ryugu grains from the Hayabusa2 mission, and spectral analysis of the Ribbeck aubrite. The SAL cleanroom construction is planned to be finished in January 2026, with operation and commissioning due to be well under way by Spring of 2026.

## 1. INTRODUCTION
The field of extraterrestrial sample return is growing in importance in the planetary science community. Returned samples provide the ultimate ground truth to better understand and interpret remote sensing data and allow for far more detailed analysis of extraterrestrial material than what can be achieved using in-situ instruments. In order to preserve pristine returned material special protocols for sample storage, manipulation and transport are required.

## 2. THE LABORATORY LAYOUT AND INSTRUMENTATION
The SAL aims to serve the dual purpose of sample curation, as well as sample preparation and analysis within the same laboratory space. 

### Main Analytical Instruments:
- **FE-SEM**: Field-emission scanning electron microscope.
- **FE-EMPA**: Field-emission electron microprobe analyser.
- **XRD**: X-ray diffractometer.
- **FTIR Microscope**: Fourier Transform Infrared microscope.
- **Digital Microscope**: Keyence VHX-7000.

### Facilities and Specifications:
- **Area**: Approximately 80 m² ISO 8 cleanroom.
- **Gloveboxes**: Two ISO-5 gloveboxes (one for storage, one for preparation) using dry nitrogen and low outgassing components (Viton seals).
- **Integration**: Technical equipment (pumps, power) housed in the basement to minimize contamination and heat.

## 3. CURRENT RESEARCH
Research is already being conducted on samples from the Hayabusa2 mission (asteroid Ryugu). Work focuses on the effect of space weathering, specifically the geochemical and spectral changes in the infrared within microcraters and melt splashes. 

### Key Findings on Ryugu Sample A0383:
- Geochemical analysis using SEM backscattered electron (BSE) images identified Phyllosilicate matrix, Magnetite, Pyrrhotite, Dolomite, and Pentlandite.
- Micro-FTIR reflectance measurements allow spectral characterization at the microscale (VNIR to MIR).

## 4. CONSTRUCTION STATUS AND OUTLOOK
- **Construction Completion**: Planned for January 2026.
- **Commissioning**: Spring 2026.
- **Curation Operations**: Expected from 2027 onwards, specifically targeting MMX samples (Martian moon Phobos).
