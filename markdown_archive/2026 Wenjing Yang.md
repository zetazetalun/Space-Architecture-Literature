# Innovative applications of microbial induced carbonate precipitation (MICP) for extraterrestrial construction

## Abstract
The idea of in-situ resource utilization (ISRU) for building extraterrestrial bases on the moon and Mars has become increasingly popular. This review outlines the latest developments in fabrication of space construction materials using microbial induced carbonate precipitation (MICP) technology. This innovative approach uses the urease of *Sporosarcina pasteurii* to promote the precipitation of calcium carbonate from external carbon and nitrogen sources. By using lunar and Martian stardust as raw materials, this method presents a sustainable alternative to the difficult and costly process of transporting building materials from Earth to space.

## 1. Introduction
A major barrier to lunar base construction is the exorbitant cost of transporting materials from Earth. In-situ resource utilization (ISRU) has emerged as a key solution. Three main types of lunar construction materials have been developed: sintered materials, 3D-printed structures, and bio-bricks.

## 2. MICP feasibility in lunar and Martian surface environments
### 2.1. The feasibility of MICP on lunar surface
The Moon's surface is covered in regolith rich in SiO2, Al2O3, and CaO. MICP uses urease-active bacteria (e.g., *Sporosarcina pasteurii*) to catalyze CaCO3 precipitation. Bacteria produce urease to hydrolyze urea into CO2 and NH4+; CO2 dissolves in water to form CO3^2-, which reacts with dissolved calcium salts (from regolith like CaO) to precipitate CaCO3, binding soil particles.

## 5. Progress in bio-bricks research
### 5.1. Progress in the study of lunar bio-bricks
Researchers have developed techniques for printing biomaterials in vitro by combining 3D printing with MICP to create porous CaCO3-based composites. Lyophilized bacteria are dispersed in gelatin and triggered by adding CaCl2 and urea. This method can produce composites capable of withstanding compressive forces of up to 3.5 MPa.

### 5.2. Progress in the study of Martian bio-bricks
Dikshit et al. employed a casting method to prepare bio-bricks from Mars-simulated soil, achieving a maximum compressive strength of 3.3 MPa by adding 10 mM NiCl2 and 1% guar gum.

## 6. Summary and outlook
1. The Moon is rich in ilmenite (TiO2), which can be used to produce H2, O2, and water for MICP processes.
2. Bio-bricks can be strengthened with unique materials like guar gum or glass fibers.
3. Research is needed for bacterial strains that can adapt to high vacuum and radiation.
4. MICP supports Class III habitat construction using ISRU materials.
