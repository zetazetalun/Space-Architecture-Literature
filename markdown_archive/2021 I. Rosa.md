## Abstract
Extraterrestrial construction presents many interesting and new challenges. Transporting large amounts of construction materials from Earth is cost prohibitive. Thus, to take advantage of limited in situ extraterrestrial resources, this work focuses on a novel class of biopolymer-bound soil composite (BSC) materials. These quasi-brittle composites are produced by desiccating a mixture of soil, water, and a biopolymer binder to create a versatile material with uniaxial compressive strength comparable to concrete. This paper expands upon a BSC design methodology for regolith soils. Nineteen BSC mixes using JSC-1A regolith were tested with varying biopolymer solution concentrations and biopolymer to soil ratios. A maximum compressive strength of 25 MPa was obtained at a biopolymer concentration of 48% and a biopolymer to soil ratio of 12.4%. 

## 1. Introduction
While reinforced concrete is a critical material for economic development, its manufacture accounts for 5% of global CO2 emissions. For space exploration, NASA is developing closed-loop building material systems. Biopolymer-bound Soil Composites (BSC) leverage natural binders and locally sourced aggregates to provide suitable mechanical performance with reduced resource and energy use.

## 2. Experimental Design
The maximum compressive strength of BSC materials is a function of the soil maximum bulk density, the biopolymer solution concentration, and density. Dried bovine blood plasma proteins (BSA and immunoglobins) were used as the biopolymer. Mixes with concentrations of 30%, 38%, 43%, 48%, and 53% were investigated.

## 3. Materials and Methods
- **Inorganic Aggregate:** JSC-1A Lunar Regolith Simulant.
- **Binder:** AP920 Bovine Plasma.
- **Manufacturing:** Dry-mixing of soil and protein, followed by water addition. Samples were compacted in a 25.4 mm cylindrical mold under 5 MPa pressure and desiccated at 60°C for 24 hours.

## 4. Results
A linear relationship between compressive strength, dry bulk density, and biopolymer-to-soil ratio was observed for saturation ratios ≤ 1. Oversaturation (ratios > 1) results in decreased density and strength due to shrinkage-induced damage in the biopolymer matrix.

## 5. Conclusions
JSC-1A/AP920 BSC materials achieve compressive strengths comparable to concrete (up to 25.4 MPa). The design theory developed for sand-based BSC is applicable to lunar regolith. Future work will explore 3D printing applications for these materials.
