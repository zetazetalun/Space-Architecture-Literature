# Designing Biopolymer-Bound Regolith Composites for Maximum Compressive Strength

**Authors:** I. Rosa, A. Coto, M. I. Allende, M. D. Lepech, and D. J. Loftus
**Affiliations:** Dept. of Civil and Environmental Engineering, Stanford Univ.; Space Bioscience Research Division, NASA Ames Research Center.
**Conference:** Earth and Space 2021 (ASCE)

## Abstract
Extraterrestrial construction presents challenges due to the cost of transporting materials from Earth. This work focuses on a novel class of biopolymer-bound soil composite (BSC) materials produced by desiccating a mixture of soil, water, and a biopolymer binder (bovine blood plasma proteins). Nineteen BSC mixes using JSC-1A regolith were tested. A maximum compressive strength of 25 MPa was obtained. A design tool is introduced for future structural design on the Moon, Mars, or other bodies.

## 1. Introduction
The study addresses the need for sustainable construction materials that use local resources and low energy. BSC materials leverage natural binders and locally sourced aggregates to create a circular material system suitable for long-duration space missions.

## 2. Experimental Design
The study translates BSC design methodology to lunar regolith using JSC-1A simulant. Key parameters include soil maximum bulk density, biopolymer solution concentration, and biopolymer solution density. Dried bovine blood plasma proteins (BSA and immunoglobins) were used as the binder.

## 3. Materials and Experimental Methods
- **Inorganic Aggregate:** JSC-1A simulant.
- **Binder:** AP920 Bovine Plasma.
- **Manufacturing:** Dry-mixing of aggregate and protein, adding water, blending, and compacting at 5 MPa in a cylindrical mold (2:1 height-to-diameter ratio). Specimens were desiccated at 60°C for 24 hours.
- **Testing:** Unconfined uniaxial compression testing using an MTS Criterion Model 43.

## 5. Models for Predicting Bulk Density and Compressive Strength
Linear and mixed-effects models were developed to predict dry bulk density and compressive strength as functions of biopolymer concentration and soil ratio. The linear model for compressive strength showed an R-squared value of 0.967.

## 6. Discussion
Saturation ratios above unity (oversaturation) result in decreased compressive strength due to shrinkage-induced damage in the biopolymer matrix. Strength follows a linear relationship with bulk density and biopolymer-to-soil ratio when the saturation ratio is ≤ 1.0.

## 7. Conclusions & Future Work
BSC materials exhibited a linear relationship between strength and density/binder ratio. Future work will explore 3D printing and alternate manufacturing methods to achieve higher bulk densities closer to dry compaction levels.
