# Additive manufacturing evolution in construction: From individual terrestrial to collective, aerial, and extraterrestrial applications

## Abstract
The third industrial revolution is marked by the fast-growing evolution of Additive Manufacturing (AM) in construction. This study reviews the advancement of AM in the construction industry, tracing its evolution from individual terrestrial to collective, aerial, and extraterrestrial construction. It elaborates on bio-inspired technology, topology optimization, and preferred materials for extraterrestrial surfaces.

## 1. Introduction
Automation in construction via AM can expedite timelines, enhance safety, and contribute to economic efficiency. The trajectory is moving from off-site to on-site, including remote, aerial, and lunar construction.

## 2. Extraterrestrial AM (Section 4.3)
- **Mars & Moon:** Exploration and potential colonization have garnered interest. NASA and ESA introduced the 'Advanced 3D Printing Construction Systems for the Moon and Mars' in 2019, targeting habitats by 2040.
- **Marscrete:** A Martian concrete primarily composed of sulfur and regolith. It can be deposited by 3D printers with minimal human supervision.
- **In-Situ Resources:** Leveraging lunar soil and Martian dust as raw materials is critical to avoid the exorbitant cost of transport from Earth.
- **Class III Technology:** Use of laser technology for melting regolith and providing local materials (sintering) is noted alongside extrusion-based methods like CC and CP.

## 3. Environmental Challenges (Table 1)
- **Moon:** Temperature (-171 to 127 °C), Gravity (1.625 m/s²), Oxygen (45%), Relative Humidity (~0%).
- **Mars:** Temperature (-143 to 17 °C), Gravity (3.721 m/s²), Oxygen (0.13%), Relative Humidity (0-100%).

## 4. Advanced AM Techniques
- **Collective Robotic Construction (CRC):** Coordinated groups of autonomous robots working simultaneously to build larger structures.
- **Aerial AM (UAV AM):** Drones equipped with 3DP technology for inaccessible environments.

## 5. Summary of Materials and Testing
The paper highlights printability (pumpability, extrudability, buildability) as key characteristics. For extraterrestrial use, simulated regolith serves as a precursor for geopolymerization or sulfur-based binders.
