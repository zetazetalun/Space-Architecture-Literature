# Unity of terrestrial and extraterrestrial soils in granular configuration

**Journal:** Earth and Planetary Science Letters  
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.epsl.2025.119239](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.epsl.2025.119239)  
**Authors:** Jun Zhang, Yong Li, Yifei Cui, et al.

## Abstract
Grain size distribution (GSD) is crucial for understanding soil properties and surface processes. This study finds that both terrestrial and lunar soils are subjected to a unified GSD function: $P(D) = g(\mu)D^{-\mu}\exp(-D/D_c)$. This function reduces textural fractions to a parameter pair ($\mu, D_c$), unifying soils across different environments and genesis mechanisms. The study introduces a stochastic framework for soil formation and generates 'numerical simulants' to aid in-situ resource utilization (ISRU) and lunar base construction.

## 1. Introduction
Both Earth and Moon are covered by soil 'skins' that dominate surface processes. While mineral constituents differ, the study of lunar soil (LS) often adopts the paradigm of terrestrial soil (TS) research. High fidelity in mineralogy and GSD for material simulants is difficult to achieve; thus, the authors propose a GSD-based paradigm to facilitate data harmonization for ISRU and extraterrestrial engineering.

## 2. Materials and Methods
### 2.1 Variety in Textures
The study utilizes thousands of samples, including TS from five continents, LS from Apollo, Luna, and Chang’E missions, and Martian soil (MS) from the Curiosity rover. While TS samples cover the USDA triangle, LS and MS are primarily bimodal due to specific provenances like meteorite impacts.

### 2.2 Unity in GSD
All soils satisfy a unified GSD function: 
$P(D) = g(\mu)D^{-\mu}\exp(-D/D_c)$
Where:
- $P(D)$ is the exceedance percentage.
- $\mu$ and $D_c$ are fitting parameters.
- $g(\mu)$ is a coefficient function.

## 3. Results and Application
### 3.1 Numerical Simulants
The unified GSD allows for the creation of 'numerical simulants,' providing a cost-effective way to derive mechanical and thermal properties of lunar regolith by establishing properties as functions of ($\mu, D_c$).

### 3.2 GSD-Fields
The study creates $\mu$-fields for Apollo 11 (mare), Apollo 15 (basin margin), and Apollo 16 (highland) sites, depicting local fluctuations in soil parameters at high resolutions. This facilitates upscaling local geotechnical properties to regional scales for construction planning.

## 4. Conclusion
Soils across terrestrial bodies are unified in granular configuration. The derived GSD parameters ($\mu, D_c$) offer a low-dimensional coding for soil data, supporting future ISRU, 3D regolith mapping, and base construction on the Moon and Mars.
