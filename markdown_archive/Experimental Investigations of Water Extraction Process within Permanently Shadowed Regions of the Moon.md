# Experimental Investigations of Water Extraction Process within Permanently Shadowed Regions of the Moon\n\n## Abstract\nHeat and mass transfers occurring on the surface and within the subsurface of extraterrestrial bodies during natural and future industrial heating processes require knowledge of thermal properties of porous dry and icy materials. A series of experiments have been employed to simulate borehole heating of icy regolith samples under low vacuum (0.1 to 1.5 Torr) and very low temperatures (>93 K), using JSC-1A analog prepared as 'mud-pies' with water contents from 0 to 20% wt. Real-time measurements of temperatures in an array of points and vacuum chamber pressures were recorded. To decipher thermal properties data in this inverse heat equation problem, a 3D finite element method modelling has been used to simulate the behaviour of both the samples and the vacuum chamber. The determined thermal conductivities vary with temperature, pressure, ice content, and porosity, while heat capacities vary with temperature, ice content, and porosity.\n\n## Introduction\nThe current paradigm in research of rocky planets and moons is to 'follow the water'. Of all material resources in Space, water has the most promising and possibly fastest commercial utilization potential. This paper investigates both thermal conductivities and heat capacities of icy JSC-1A materials of ice contents from 0 to 20% wt based on experimental measurements.\n\n## Experimental Method and Setup\n- **Facilities:** Center for Space Resources at the Colorado School of Mines.\n- **Hardware:** Medium vacuum chamber (0.3375 m³), base for the In Situ Experimental Probe (IEP).\n- **Analog:** JSC-1A regolith analog, baked at 93°C for 24 hours.\n- **Sample Prep:** Mud-pies (~500g, 2.5 in height, 3.28 in diameter), frozen to -30°C then cryo-cooled in liquid nitrogen.\n- **Heater:** OMEGA CSH-1011001 cartridge heater providing ~7W of power.\n\n## Data Processing and Modeling\nA 3D model of the chamber interior was created in COMSOL Multiphysics 5.2 to simulate the heat transfer process governed by the 3D heat equation accounting for phase change. The phase change material is divided into two bulk materials: icy regolith and desiccated regolith.\n\n### Table 1. Lower permeability results in higher pore pressure and potential transition from sublimation to fusion-based phase change.\n- **Phase Change:** Occurs between -20 and 20°C, with rapid sublimation around 0°C causing pressure spikes.\n- **Heating Rates:** Low ice content surfaces are easier to heat to specific temperatures, while high ice content surfaces (e.g., 14-16% wt) are significantly harder to heat up, showing higher thermal inertia.\n\n## Conclusions\nHeat and mass transfer within icy planetary deposits is computationally intensive and non-linear. The study proved that understanding volumetric mixtures with ice requires accounting for the dynamism of ice phase change and the pressure domain.
