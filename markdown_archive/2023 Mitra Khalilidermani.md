# A Survey on Extraterrestrial Habitation Structures with a Focus on Energy-Saving 3D Printing Techniques

**Authors:** Mitra Khalilidermani and Dariusz Knez
**Published in:** *Applied Sciences*, 2023, 13, 12913
**DOI:** 10.3390/app132312913

## Abstract
In the past two decades, various space agencies have shown great enthusiasm for constructing habitable structures on lunar and Martian surfaces. The objective is to evaluate different habitat construction techniques from technical, economic, and energy-consumption perspectives. The primary focus is on the 3D printing technique. It was found that the combination of 3D-printed components along with an internal breathable inflatable module is the most promising technique for habitat development on the Moon and Mars. Moreover, the microwave sintering method was identified as the most energy-saving and reliable approach for melting on-site regolith.

## 1. Introduction
Future space habitation is envisioned for the sustainability of mankind in the solar system. Large space agencies like NASA and ESA are tracing the construction of permanent habitats on the Moon and Mars. Engineering challenges include extraterrestrial physics, risky regolith characteristics, and uncertain construction techniques.

## 2. Physics-Based Challenges
Primary challenges include:
- **Microgravity:** Causes instability and requires specific foundations.
- **Cryogenic Temperatures:** Reaches −230 °C on the Moon and −140 °C on Mars.
- **Airless Atmosphere:** Lack of pressure and protection from micrometeoroids.
- **Radiation:** Solar winds and cosmic rays requiring protective regolith shielding (1.5 m to 2 m thick).
- **Seismic Events:** Meteoroid impacts and ground motions (quakes).

### Sintering Methods Comparison
- **Laser Sintering:** High energy consumption, small volume processed.
- **Solar Sintering:** Unlimited energy but location-dependent (poor at poles).
- **Microwave Sintering:** Requires 33–44 times less energy than laser sintering; deeper heat penetration.

## 4. Key Findings
- **Inflatable + 3D Printing:** The most viable approach is a Class II inflatable module for life support sheltered by a Class III 3D-printed regolith shell.
- **Material Performance:** Sulfur concrete using troilite minerals (FeS) from local regolith achieved compressive strengths of ~55 MPa.
- **Logistics:** Interplanetary transportation is estimated at EUR 200,000 per kg; using local regolith dramatically reduces costs.

## 5. Conclusions
Cryogenic temperature is identified as the most restricting factor for habitat infrastructure. Fully ISRU-based operations with autonomous 3D printing are essential for long-term habitation.
