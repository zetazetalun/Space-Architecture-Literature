# Earthmoving for the extraterrestrial

**Author:** Jeffrey S Nesbit  
**Affiliation:** Temple University, USA  
**Publication:** EPD: Society and Space, 2025, Vol. 43(2) 245–261  
**DOI:** 10.1177/02637758231204701

## Abstract
The United States spaceport, and more importantly, its technical landscape, operate in the background for the technological and political progress in pursuit of the extraterrestrial. Throughout the construction of the launch complexes on the Florida coastline, earthmoving became standard practice to elevate rocket pads above sea level and protect against rocket blasts. This article reveals a critical history of place-based science on Cape Canaveral through an evolution of earthmoving practices, from cultural commemoration, extraterrestrial imagination, and contemporary environmental crises.

## Technical History of Launch Infrastructure

### The German Influence
The earliest rocket launch sites required the design of earthmoving techniques. The launch pads and facilities at Peenemunde, under the direction of Albert Speer, utilized dense series of mechanical conduit lines and underground networks. Bounded by an "embankment" of built-up earthwork, Peenemunde became the model for Cape Canaveral under the guidance of Wernher von Braun and Dr. Kurt Debus.

### Construction and Earthmoving (1950–1961)
In 1950, the US Air Force selected the Central Florida coastline for missile operations. Key infrastructure data includes:
- **Rapid Development:** 70% of all launch complexes at Cape Canaveral were constructed between 1950 and 1961.
- **Pre-NASA Era:** By 1958, 23 of the 36 launch complexes were already in place.
- **Engineering Methods:** Dredging hydraulic fill from the Banana River and pumping it into construction sites was used to elevate pads above sea level. 
- **Sandpiles:** Two large pyramid-like sandpiles were constructed to elevate pads for the Saturn V rocket.

### Technology Adaptation
The mobile launch complex was supported by the **Missile Crawler Transporter**. This technology was directly adapted from terrestrial mining equipment:
- **Origin:** Steam-shovel crawlers used for raw extraction and surface coal mining in Kentucky.
- **Application:** Used as the primary vehicle for transporting rockets from the Vertical Assembly Building to the elevated launch pad.

## Environmental Context
- **Natural Buffers:** 20-foot high dunes act as natural dikes against hurricanes.
- **Hydrological Impact:** The construction of the crawler way blocked channels between the Banana River and Banana Creek, causing significant hydrological disruption.
- **Current Threats:** Rising sea levels and hurricane-induced erosion (e.g., Hurricane Dorian in 2019) put historically significant launch facilities at risk.

## Historical Typologies of Landforms at Cape Canaveral
1. **Burial Mounds:** Indigenous Ais people's mounds (up to 25 ft high, 100 ft wide).
2. **Launch Infrastructure:** Militarized earthworks and semi-buried concrete blockhouses.
3. **Dredging History:** Artificial manipulation of ground and water courses.
