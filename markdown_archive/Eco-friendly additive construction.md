# Eco-friendly additive construction: Analysis of the printability of earthen-based matrices stabilized with potato starch gel and sisal fibers

## Abstract
3D printing for construction is a feasible alternative for social housing, temporary shelters, and recently, for extraterrestrial habitats. This article studies the printable capabilities of earthen-based matrices using potato starch as a natural stabilizer and sisal fibers to control shrinkage cracking. Results indicate that matrices with 1% sisal fibers and 5% starch gel show improved workability, minor cracking, and adequate strength for 3D printing.

## 1. Introduction
3D printing (additive construction) allows for layer-by-layer deposition of extrudable materials, reducing waste and improving safety. While most matrices use cement, this study explores raw soil as a base component to minimize environmental impact and transportation costs.

## 2. Additive construction using earthen-based matrices
### 2.1. Summary of recent advances
A literature review reveals that 46% of publications on soil-based 3D printing focus on regolith-based 3D printing for extraterrestrial construction (Lunar and Martian habitats). Technologies mentioned include microwave, solar, and laser sintering, as well as thermoplastic binders with regolith.

## 3. Analysis of printability
### 3.1. Materials
- **Base soil**: Low plasticity sandy clay (CL) from Carabayllo, Lima.
- **Stabilizer**: Food-grade potato starch gel (gelatinized at 90°C).
- **Reinforcement**: Sisal fibers (10 mm length, 137 μm diameter).

### 3.2. Experimental program
Two stages: 1) Evaluating extrudability/pumpability of various liquid/starch concentrations. 2) Assessing hardening, shrinkage, buildability (stacking), and mechanical properties.

## 4. Results and discussions
- **Yield Strength**: Optimized printable matrices (e.g., EFS5.0%) showed yield strengths around 4.3 kPa.
- **Shrinkage**: Sisal fibers (1%) reduced maximum crack width from 8.49 mm (control) to 0.60 mm.
- **Buildability**: The control mixture allowed 16 layers (75x150 mm tower), while stabilized mixtures with fibers (EFS5.0%) allowed 12 layers without deformation.
- **Compressive Strength**: Increased up to 157% at 28 days compared to control (reaching ~2.2 MPa).

## 5. Conclusions
The study presents the Colibri 3D printing system and validates that starch-stabilized, fiber-reinforced earthen matrices are suitable for additive construction with improved strength and durability.
