# Karst Cave as Terrestrial Simulation Platform to Test and Design Human Base in Lunar Lava Tube

**Authors:** Jinghang Ding, Gengxin Xie, Linli Guo, Xin Xiong, Ya Han, and Xi Wang
**Publication:** Space: Science & Technology (AAAS Science Partner Journal), Volume 2022
**DOI:** 10.34133/2022/9875780

## Abstract
Developing efficient approaches to building a suitable environment for humans on the moon plays a key role in future long-term sustainable lunar exploration. Lunar lava tubes offer potential sites for habitats due to their stable structures and protective environments. This paper proposes using Earth’s karst caves as terrestrial analogs to simulate these tubes, specifically focusing on cave sealing technology, efficient daylight systems, and internal circulation research of artificial ecosystems.

## 1. Introduction
Lunar lava tubes are hollow pipe-shaped caves formed by volcanic activity. Their hard basalt roofs provide natural protection against extreme temperature changes, cosmic radiation, and meteorite impacts, making them ideal candidates for human lunar habitats.

## 2. Rationality of Using a Lunar Lava Tube for a Base

### 2.1 Energy Estimation
Energy consumption for a habitat of 8 astronauts in a 50m high semicircular tunnel cave (8,000 m²) was calculated. Results indicate that maintaining a 20°C environment in a lava tube requires significantly less energy than on the surface.

### 2.2 Safety Assessment
*   **Radiation:** Tubes buried >6m deep provide total protection from cosmic rays and solar flares.
*   **Meteorites:** Burial depth protects against impacts unless the thickness-to-crater diameter ratio is below 0.87.
*   **Dust:** Interiors are permanent shadow zones where charged moon dust is minimal.

## 3. Terrestrial Simulation: Karst Caves
Karst caves in Chongqing, China, are proposed as analogs. While formation mechanisms differ (dissolution vs. volcanic), they provide comparable sizes and internal environmental stability.

### 3.3 Simulation Research Directions
1.  **Automatic Construction:** Sealing the cave using in situ resource utilization (ISRU) materials.
2.  **Artificial Ecosystems:** Studying interactions between humans, plants, and microorganisms within sealed natural rock environments.
3.  **Light Pipes:** Using daylight systems to provide sunlight for photosynthesis.

## 4. Challenges and Roadmap
Building extraterrestrial bases faces challenges including low-gravity equipment adaptation, psychological impacts of long-term underground survival, and low-cost sealing solutions. The roadmap suggests an autonomous stage using ISRU and robots to automatically build bases by 2045.

## 5. Conclusion
Lunar lava tubes are excellent candidates for lunar bases. Terrestrial karst caves provide a viable simulation platform to evaluate sealing technologies and artificial ecosystem stability before actual lunar deployment.
