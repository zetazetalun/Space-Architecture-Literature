# 3D printed sulfur-regolith concrete performance evaluation for waterless extraterrestrial robotic construction

**Journal:** Automation in Construction  
**Volume:** 165 (2024) 105571  
**Authors:** Ilerioluwa Giwa, Mary Dempsey, Michael Fiske, Ali Kazemian

## Abstract
By leveraging the capabilities of construction 3D printing, building structures in harsh extraterrestrial environments is conceivable. Sulfur concrete is a waterless construction material that offers great potential to replace Portland cement concrete (PCC) in extraterrestrial construction. This study evaluates the shape stability, mechanical performance, and recyclability of 3D-printed Martian sulfur-regolith concrete (SRC), particularly under vacuum conditions and temperature variations.

## 1. Introduction
Conventional construction methods relying on manual effort are unsuitable for Lunar and Martian environments due to ionizing radiation, extreme temperatures, and limited human workforce. Extrusion-based Construction 3D Printing (C3DP) using in-situ resource utilization (ISRU) materials is a promising alternative. Sulfur concrete (SC) is especially suitable as it is waterless and can be produced from sulfur minerals found on the Moon and Mars.

## 3. Materials and Methodology

### 3.1 Materials
- **Binder:** Elemental sulfur powder (99.9% purity).
- **Fine Aggregate:** Mars Global Simulant (MGS-1), a basaltic soil analog.
- **Modifier:** Dicyclopentadiene (DCPD) at 10% of sulfur weight.

## 4. Results and Discussion

### 4.1 Mechanical Performance
- **Strength Development:** SRC gained 85% of its ultimate strength within 12 hours. In contrast, PCC only gained 19% of its 28-day strength in the same period.
- **DCPD Modification:** Adding 10% DCPD increased compressive strength by 44% and flexural strength by 34%.
- **Vacuum Performance:** At 55°C in vacuum, PCC lost ~78% of its flexural strength due to moisture outgassing. SRC showed much better resilience, with strength reduction limited to 7-12%.

### 4.2 Recyclability
Printed SRC specimens were remelted and reprinted without compositional modification. The recycled specimens (SRC_R) showed no significant loss in flexural strength compared to the original prints, demonstrating a cradle-to-cradle sustainability approach.

## 5. Conclusions
- Martian SRC was successfully 3D printed, proving the feasibility of waterless off-world additive construction.
- Substrate temperature is critical; higher temperatures improve interlayer adhesion but can lead to plastic deformation.
- SRC outperforms PCC in vacuum conditions and offers rapid strength development.
- Modifying SRC with DCPD reduces sublimation rates in vacuum by an order of magnitude.
