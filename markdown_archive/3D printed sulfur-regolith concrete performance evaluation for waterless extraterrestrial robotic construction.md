# 3D printed sulfur-regolith concrete performance evaluation for waterless extraterrestrial robotic construction\n\n## Authors\nIlerioluwa Giwa, Mary Dempsey, Michael Fiske, Ali Kazemian\n\n## Abstract\nBy leveraging the capabilities of construction 3D printing, building structures in harsh extraterrestrial environments is conceivable. Sulfur concrete is a waterless construction material that offers great potential to replace Portland cement concrete (PCC) in extraterrestrial construction. The shape stability of 3D printed Martian sulfur-regolith concrete (SRC) was found to benefit from a lower substrate layer temperature. However, this comes at the cost of flexural strength, resulting in up to 53% strength loss. The printed SRC specimens demonstrated a significantly faster strength development rate (gaining about 85% of the ultimate strength after only 12 h) compared to the printed PCC. The printed SRC specimens also outperformed the PCC specimens in vacuum conditions at higher temperatures. Furthermore, modifying the SRC materials with Dicyclopentadiene resulted in up to 44% strength increase and minimized the sublimation rate of the printed specimens in vacuum, especially at an elevated temperature.\n\n## 1. Introduction\nNASA's Artemis program aims to land astronauts on the Moon to establish a sustained human presence and enable missions to Mars. Supporting infrastructure like habitats and launch pads are needed. Conventional construction methods cannot be used due to limited human workforce and harsh conditions. Extrusion-based Construction 3D Printing (C3DP) holds potential for autonomous construction. ISRU is essential to avoid the cost of transporting materials from Earth.\n\n## 2. Methodology\nSRC materials were prepared using elemental sulfur powder (99.9% purity) and Mars Global Simulant (MGS-1). A sulfur content of 33% was selected. Modified SRC (MSRC) included 10% Dicyclopentadiene (DCPD). Printing was performed at 130\u00b0C and 150\u00b0C with interlayer time gaps of 45s, 120s, and 180s. Comparison specimens used Type I/II Portland Cement Concrete (PCC).\n\n## 3. Key Results\n- **Strength Development:** SRC gained 75-85% of ultimate strength in 12h, whereas PCC gained only 19%.\n- **Vacuum Performance:** SRC showed 0.016% weight loss in vacuum at 25\u00b0C. PCC lost 30% strength in vacuum due to moisture evaporation.\n- **Shape Stability:** Lower substrate temperatures improved stability but reduced interlayer bond strength.\n- **Recyclability:** SRC specimens were remelted and reprinted without losing mechanical strength.\n- **Additives:** 10% DCPD increased compressive strength by 44% and reduced sublimation rates by an order of magnitude.\n\n## 4. Conclusion\nMartian SRC is a feasible material for off-world additive construction, offering superior vacuum resilience and rapid strength development compared to cementitious materials. The findings highlight the importance of process optimization (extrusion and substrate temperature) and demonstrate the potential for a cradle-to-cradle sustainability approach in space.
