# DESIGN OF COMPOSITE SAMPLING SYSTEM FOR SURFACE SOIL OF EXTRATERRESTRIAL BODIES

**By:** Qi-Chen SUN, Zeng ZHAO, Jun-Shan GONG, Zhong-Wang YIN, Xiao-Ming LAI, Hai-Bin YU, Zhong-Xian ZHAO, Chao WANG, and Hui LV

**Journal:** Thermal Science
**DOI:** [10.2298/TSCI2502267S](https://doi.org/10.2298/TSCI2502267S)

## Abstract
Space resource development has become a frontier hot topic in international space exploration. The utilization of asteroid resources necessitates extensive regolith mining operations. This paper presents the design and simulation analysis of an extraterrestrial surface regolith composite sampling system. The findings demonstrate that the composite approach, combining spiral drilling and transport with kinetic energy directional excitation, is highly effective, collecting over 500 g within 5 seconds at 3000 rpm with a reaction force below 5 N.

## Introduction
Asteroids are rich in precious metals and rare elements with significant economic potential. Large-scale mining and transportation technology for regolith particles is essential for subsequent extraterrestrial construction and resource development, ensuring feasibility, sustainability, and scalability.

## Scheme Design of Sampling System
The system utilizes a **Composite Sampling Principle**:
1.  **Kinetic Energy Directional Sampling:** High-speed brushes interact with particles to impart velocity and direct them into the sample channel.
2.  **Auger Drilling Transport:** Subsurface excavation via a spiral flange for collecting and transporting regolith.

## Conclusion
The system integrates spiral drilling with kinetic energy directional acquisition, demonstrating superior adaptability to asteroid surfaces. It achieves peak efficiency at 3000 rpm, collecting ~730 g in 5 seconds with a peak reaction force below 7 N, satisfying low reaction force requirements for microgravity environments.
