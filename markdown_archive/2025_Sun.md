# DESIGN OF COMPOSITE SAMPLING SYSTEM FOR SURFACE SOIL OF EXTRATERRESTRIAL BODIES

**By:** Qi-Chen SUN, Zeng ZHAO, Jun-Shan GONG, Zhong-Wang YIN, Xiao-Ming LAI, Hai-Bin YU, Zhong-Xian ZHAO, Chao WANG, and Hui LV

**Journal:** THERMAL SCIENCE: Year 2025, Vol. 29, No. 2B, pp. 1267-1272
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.2298/TSCI2502267S

## Abstract
Space resource development has become a frontier hot topic in international space exploration. The utilization of asteroid resources necessitates extensive regolith mining operations. Catering to the demands of extensive space activities, extraterrestrial construction, and resource development, this paper presents the design and simulation analysis of an extraterrestrial surface regolith composite sampling system. The findings demonstrate that the composite sampling approach, which combines spiral drilling and transport with kinetic energy directional excitation, is highly effective. It is capable of collecting no less than 500 g within a mere 5 seconds at a rotation speed of 3000 rpm, while the reaction force exerted by the brush remains below 5 N (later clarified as 7 N in text), thus satisfying the requirement for a low reaction force.

## 1. Introduction
Space resource development is key for projects like planetary exploration and lunar development. Asteroids are rich in precious metals and raw materials for space exploration. This paper focuses on large-scale mining and transportation technology for regolith on asteroids to provide raw materials for extraterrestrial construction.

## 2. Scheme Design of Sampling System
Asteroids have weak surface gravity and irregular shapes. The proposed system uses a **compound sampling method** combining:
- **Kinetic Energy Directional Sampling:** High-speed brushes impart velocity to particles, directing them into a sample channel.
- **Auger Drilling Transport Sampling:** Uses a spiral flange for subsurface drilling, excavation, and transporting extracted regolith.

## 3. Results and Simulation
Simulation was conducted at a constant feed speed of 0.01 m/s across various brush rotation speeds.

### Reaction Force Analysis:
- Maximum reaction force in Z-axis: 6.6 N
- Maximum reaction force in X-axis: 0.78 N
- Maximum reaction force in Y-axis: < 1.3 N
- Peak value remains below 7 N, making it suitable for microgravity environments.

## 4. Conclusion
The composite sampling system demonstrates superior adaptability to asteroid surface conditions. It achieves peak efficiency at 3000 rpm, collecting over 700g in 5 seconds with minimal reaction forces, supporting large-scale asteroid resource extraction and construction needs.
