# Fiber-reinforced concrete for complex engineering conditions: material properties, nonlinear response and sustainable applications in spaceflight

**Journal:** High Temperature Materials and Processes 2026; 45(1): 20250102  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1515/htmp-2025-0102  
**Authors:** Junyu Liu and Xi Liu*

## Abstract
As space missions evolve toward longer durations and deeper missions, structural materials face unprecedented challenges in extreme extraterrestrial environments, such as extreme temperature swings, intense radiation, cyclic loading, and crack propagation. Fiber-reinforced concrete (FRC) has gained increasing attention for its ability to enhance material ductility, energy dissipation, and structural resilience under nonlinear conditions. This review summarizes recent research on FRC, focusing on its mechanical properties, nonlinear response mechanisms, and potential for sustainable construction. The inclusion of natural, synthetic, or hybrid fibers significantly improves compressive strength, flexural performance, and crack control. Future research should focus on the performance evolution and adaptive design strategies of FRCs in complex nonlinear service environments (including high strain rates and low gravity in aerospace).

## 1 Introduction
Concrete is the most widely used building material globally. With the increasing need for environmentally conscious progress, infrastructure is being improved to meet the needs of a growing population. Recycling and reusing construction solid waste (C&D) are crucial strategies. Waste materials can be utilized as filler, fibers, or substitutes for fine aggregate in concrete. Recycled aggregate concrete (RAC) enhances durability but presents performance challenges like increased porosity and reduced strength. Incorporating fibers serves as a bridging mechanism to dramatically enhance interfacial properties.

## 2 Fibers
Fibers are categorized into natural and synthetic, and either short/discontinuous strands (5 to 50 mm) or uninterrupted filaments.

### 2.1 Natural fibers
Natural fibers (straw, hemp, kenaf, jute, coconut, bamboo) exhibit low density, improved shock absorption, and sustainability.

### 2.2 Synthetic fibers
Synthetic fibers (carbon, aramid, glass, basalt) can be organic or inorganic based. They significantly reduce initial cracks (up to 96.8% reduction in nominal area) and enhance long-term structural integrity.

## 3 Fiber applications
### 3.1 Natural fiber applications
Natural fibers like hemp and flax are used in bioplastics and 3D printing filaments. They improve tensile strength and water resistance.

### 3.2 Synthetic fiber applications
Carbon fiber-reinforced polymer (CFRP) is widely used in aerospace. Recycled CFRP-reinforced mortar increased flexural and tensile strength by 20.3% to 44.6%. Polyethylene fiber-reinforced concrete (PEFRC) shows superior resistance to shattering and impact.

## 4 Conclusion and future prospective
Natural and synthetic fibers offer pathways to sustainable, high-performance materials for extreme environments. Future directions include the optimization of 3D printing polymer materials combined with microwave heating technology for post-processing in aerospace applications.
