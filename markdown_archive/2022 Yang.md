# Laboratory construction and curation scheme for returned samples of the chang’e-5 mission

## Abstract
This research establishes protocols for the curation and analysis of lunar materials returned from the Moon. It defines requirements for laboratory environments, focusing on non-damaging analysis technologies to protect the scientific integrity of samples for present and future research.

## Mission Context
The Chang’e-5 mission successfully landed in Oceanus Procellarum and retrieved 1731 g of lunar material, including a one-meter drill core. This provided the first Chinese opportunity for direct extraterrestrial sample analysis.

## Laboratory Environmental Standards
- **Atmospheric Control:** Samples are stored in glove boxes with nitrogen atmospheres where oxygen is maintained under 20 ppm and water under 50 ppm.
- **Contamination Prevention:** Only specific materials like stainless steel, aluminum, and Teflon are permitted to contact the samples to prevent chemical alteration.
- **Thermal Management:** Facilities include frozen storage designed to mimic the lunar temperature environment and prevent chemical reactivity.
- **Sub-Labs:** The facility includes specialized areas such as Change Rooms, Pristine Sample Labs, Core and Saw Rooms, and various secure vaults.

## Curation and Research Procedures
- **Initial Processing:** Sealed containers undergo X-ray and CT imaging for 3D modeling of interior structures before unsealing.
- **Management Strategy:** A significant portion of the material is kept in long-term preservation for future study using next-generation technologies.
- **Administrative Oversight:** The China National Space Administration (CNSA) manages the distribution of samples to the international scientific community via a peer-reviewed application process.
