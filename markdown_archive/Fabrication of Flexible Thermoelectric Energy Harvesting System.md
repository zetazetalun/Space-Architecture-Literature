# Fabrication of Flexible Thermoelectric Energy Harvesting System\n\n**Authors:** Guangxi Wu, Ferin Neff, Xiong (Bill) Yu  \n**Source:** ASCE (American Society of Civil Engineers)\n\n## ABSTRACT\nThermoelectric energy harvesting is promising to provide sustainable energy source in the extraterrestrial environment. This paper describes procedures incurred in the fabrication of thermoelectric energy harvester on flexible substrates. An electrically parallel structure is proposed, which features higher energy efficiency than the counterpart electrically series structure based on theoretical analyses. As proof of concept, flexible thermoelectric harvesters in electrically parallel and series structures are prepared with thick film printing procedures. Both analytical and experimental comparison imply a promising improvement of energy conversion efficiency by using the electrically parallel structure.\n\n## INTRODUCTION\nThermoelectric modules (TEMs) are capable of providing continuous energy for extraterrestrial explorations (e.g., Mars and Jupiter) where solar intensity is low. Radioisotope thermoelectric generators (RTGs) have been used for decades. This paper proposes flexible TEGs to cover spacecraft or vehicle surfaces to leverage extreme temperature gradients (e.g., -153°C to 123°C on the Moon). Traditional TEMs use series connections, but this paper evaluates an electrically parallel structure to improve efficiency and simplify large-area fabrication via printing.\n\n## ELECTRICALLY SERIES VS. PARALLEL STRUCTURE\n- **Series:** Efficiency is impaired by material property mismatch between p-type and n-type legs (attenuation factor A < 1). Potential of high-performance materials is wasted.\n- **Parallel:** Derived derivations show the module figure-of-merit (ZT_module) can reach the maximum of the better material. Theoretically, overall efficiency can be increased by ~32% for Bi2Te3 modules.\n\n## FABRICATION PROCESS (FILM PRINTING)\n1. **Material Preparation:** Bi2Te3 (p and n-type) ball-milled to limit grain size < 76 microns.\n2. **TE Ink Preparation:** Ground powder mixed with an epoxy matrix (EPON Resin 863, hardener, catalyst) at a 4.5:1 ratio.\n3. **Screen/Stencil Design:** Mesh screens for electrodes (gold ink) and stainless steel stencils for TE legs.\n4. **Printing:** Performed on polyimide substrate using an MPM TF-100 Thick Film Printer. Layers included gold electrodes followed by TE legs, cured at 110°C.\n5. **Finishing:** Rolled up to form the final flexible device.\n\n## RESULTS AND PERFORMANCE\n- Characterization using a trans-impedance amplifier and heat source/sink setup.\n- **I-V and W-V Curves:** n-type electrically parallel structure produced higher open-circuit voltage and maximum output power compared to p-type and series structures under a 50°C temperature gradient.\n- **Conclusion:** Parallel structures are a prospective solution for large-area implementation and higher energy conversion efficiency in space environments.\n\n## REFERENCES\n- El-Genk, M.
