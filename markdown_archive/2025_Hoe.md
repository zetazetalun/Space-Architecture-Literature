# Balancing Strength and Extreme Thermal Resilience in Lunar Regolith Composites: The Role of Multi-Walled Carbon Nanotubes## AbstractThis study explores the feasibility of reinforcing lunar regolith with multi-walled carbon nanotubes (MWCNTs) using minimal water and additives (urea and phosphoric acid) for Moon base construction. Composites with 0.00, 0.25, 0.50, and 1.00 wt.% MWCNTs were tested under ambient curing, vacuum curing, and freeze-thaw cycles (-80 to 117°C). Results show that 0.50 wt.% MWCNT composites achieved 39 MPa compressive strength in ambient conditions (44% increase over control). However, thermal cycling revealed limitations due to mismatched coefficients of thermal expansion (CTE) between MWCNTs and regolith, causing microcracking. Vacuum-cured MWCNT-free samples reached 47 MPa, demonstrating the significant influence of curing protocols on material densification.## IntroductionSustainable lunar habitats require in situ resource utilization (ISRU) to reduce the cost of transporting materials from Earth (~$10,000/lb). This research focuses on combining lunar regolith with MWCNTs, urea (superplasticizer), and phosphoric acid to create resilient building materials.## Results and Discussion### Mechanical Strength- Route 1 (Ambient): 0.50 wt.% MWCNT samples reached 39 MPa (41.43% increase in modulus).- Route 3 (Vacuum + Cycle): 0.00 wt.% MWCNT control samples achieved 47 MPa. Higher MWCNT content (1.00 wt.%) performed poorly (28 MPa) due to thermal mismatch.### Thermal Cycling EffectsThermal fluctuations (-80 to 117°C) induced stresses at the MWCNT-regolith interface because MWCNTs have a negative CTE (-1.2 x 10^-5/°C) while regolith components (SiO2, Al2O3, CaO) have positive CTEs. Simulation (ABAQUS) confirmed interfacial stresses exceeding 207 MPa at -171°C.### MicrostructureSEM analysis showed MWCNTs wrapping regolith particles in Route 1 but separation/delamination in Route 2 and 3 after thermal cycling.## ConclusionWhile MWCNTs enhance regolith strength under stable temperatures, CTE mismatch remains a critical challenge for extreme lunar environments. Optimized curing and CTE compatibility are essential for robust ISRU construction.
