# Advances in in-situ resources utilization for extraterrestrial construction

**Authors:** Shanshan Cheng, Yuyue Gao, Yusheng Shi, Long Xiao, Lieyun Ding, Cheng Zhou, Yan Zhou
**Journal:** Advances in Space Research 74 (2024) 3297–3325
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.asr.2024.06.029

## Abstract
To establish foundational support for forthcoming deep space exploration and settlement endeavors, the significance of extraterrestrial construction has become paramount. Recent developments underscore a growing acknowledgment of the imperative role played by in situ resource utilization, attributed to its potential for cost efficiency and facilitation of sustainable progress. This paper compiles and categorizes the advancements in this domain, focusing on three distinctive categories of in situ resources: regolith, water–ice, and energy resources.

## 1. Introduction
Early human space exploration included the Apollo and Luna programs. The second round of deep space exploration includes NASA’s Artemis Plan, ESA’s Terrae Novae 2030+, JAXA’s SLIM, and China's Tianwen-1. ISRU is prioritized for cost-effectiveness and sustainability.

## 2. Regolith Resources for Construction
Regolith refers to the layer of unconsolidated rocks and fines over primordial bedrock. On the Moon, this layer is 3-20m deep; on Mars, it is projected to be 90-113m deep.

### 2.1. Components
- **Moon:** Lunar highlands (Anorthositic rocks, Ca, Al, Si); Lunar maria (Mg, Fe, Ti).
- **Mars:** Predominantly basalt, silicate minerals (olivine, pyroxene), sulfates, and oxides.

### 2.2. Mining and Processing
- **Discrete Excavation:** Backhoe, scraper, front-loader types.
- **Continuous Excavation:** Pneumatic, bucket wheel, and bucket chain systems.
- **Beneficiation:** Electrostatic and magnetic separation.

### 2.3. Utilization
- **Direct Utilization:** Radiation shielding (Regishell) and thermal insulation.
- **Metals Extraction:** Pyrolysis, electrolysis (MRE/MSE), and chemical reduction (carbothermal/biomining).
- **Anhydrous Concrete:** Sulfur concrete (regolith + sulfur) and polymer concrete.
- **Sintered Bricks:** SLM/SLS, Solar Sintering (SSLS), Microwave Sintering (SMWS), DLP, and LENS.

## 3. Water-ice Resources for Construction
Presence confirmed in polar regions (Moon) and subsurface mid/high latitudes (Mars).

### 3.1. Utilization
- **Direct:** Water layers in inflatable habitats for neutron radiation shielding.
- **Concrete:** Magnesian, calcareous, geopolymer, and biopolymer (StarCrete) concretes.

## 4. Energy Resources for Construction
- **Atmosphere:** Martian CO2 capture via freezing, temperature swing, or compression adsorption.
- **Solar:** Essential for sintering and thermal storage in regolith.

## 5. Applications for Construction

### 5.1. 3D Printing Technology
- **Concrete-based:** Extrusion (ink), Contour Crafting (CC), and Binder Jetting.
- **Water-based:** 3D printing with subsurface ice (e.g., Mars Ice House).

### 5.2. Prefabrication and Assembly
- **Interlocking Structures:** Modular bricks (e.g., 'Xuanwu' habitation).
- **Folding Structures:** Miura origami and honeycomb structures (expansion rates up to 750%).

### 5.3. Inflatable and Deployable Technology
Reduction in launch volume; uses atmospheric resources for inflation (e.g., LARC bowling habitat).

### 5.4. Regolith Bagging
Stacking automated bags (sandbags) for structural forms; low energy consumption.

### 5.5. Hybrid Construction
Combining multiple technologies (e.g., 'Clover' and 'China Star' schemes).

## 6. Challenges and Future Directions
- Integration of diverse resources rather than individual application.
- Deep processing of byproducts (e.g., slag from metallurgy).
- Sustainable facilities for long-term habitation.
