# Genetic Algorithm–Based Multiobjective Optimization for 3D Printable Design of a Double-Shell Lunar Habitat Structure

**Authors:** Quanyao Chen, Yuyue Gao, Lieyun Ding, Cheng Zhou, Wenbin Han, Yan Zhou, and Yusheng Shi
**Publisher:** American Society of Civil Engineers (ASCE)
**Journal:** Journal of Aerospace Engineering, Vol. 36, Issue 6, 2023
**DOI:** 10.1061/JAEEEZ.ASENG-4755

## Abstract
The establishment of lunar habitats is significant for humans to explore the Moon and carry out scientific work. Constructing lunar habitats through additive manufacturing using lunar in situ resource is a promising solution. This study proposed a parametric design and multiobjective optimization approach based on genetic algorithms for the structure of lunar habitats. Structural shape was translated into design parameters, which were optimized during the design phase. The optimization objectives were determined considering the material mass consumption, space efficiency, and resistance ability for extreme extraterrestrial environment of the structure. Three kinds of genetic algorithm–based multiobjective optimization methods were used and compared to optimize the structural parameters. The optimized Pareto solution set containing multiple solutions based on the hypervolume index are obtained. Finite-model analysis was performed on the structures of the Pareto solution set and the optimal structure was determined based on the results of maximum stress and displacement. Finally, partially scaled-down physical models were produced through additive manufacturing to demonstrate the optimized double-shell habitat structure.

## Introduction
Robotic construction based on in-situ resource utilization (ISRU) appears to be the most feasible way for lunar habitation. Additive manufacturing offers advantages including fast construction speed and high forming accuracy. This study applies a parametric design and a genetic algorithm (GA)–based multiobjective optimization (MOPT) approach to a 3D printable double-shell lunar habitat named “Lunar Ampullae.”

## Methodology
### Habitat Structure: "Lunar Ampullae"
The proposed habitat is a 3D printable double-shell structure including four parts:
1. Inner shell
2. Middle rib
3. Outer shell
4. Cenary roof

The shape is modeled based on eggshell geometry to achieve high internal space utilization and uniform force distribution.

### Optimization Framework
- **Parameters:** 9 design variables (axes of the shell curve, heights, axes of cross-section, layer thicknesses, and cavity distance).
- **Objectives:** Maximize available volume, minimize material mass, and maximize thermal insulation performance.
- **Algorithms:** Comparison of NSGA-II, SPEA2, and PESA-II.
- **Finite Element Analysis (FEA):** Used to evaluate structural performance under 0.1 MPa internal pressure and 1/6 G lunar gravity.

## Results and Discussion
### Algorithm Comparison
- **NSGA-II** achieved the best performance in terms of solution set diversity and convergence (Hypervolume index of 0.8022) with a crossover probability (PC) of 0.9 and mutation probability (PM) of 0.06.
- **Running Time:** NSGA-II was significantly faster (approx. 77s) than PESA-II (~598s) and SPEA2 (~477s).

### Optimal Solution (Solution #25)
- **Max Tensile Stress:** 3.33 MPa
- **Max Displacement:** 2.72 × 10⁻² cm
- **Conclusion:** These values are much smaller than the ultimate bearing capacity of concrete/regolith-based materials, indicating a low risk of structural failure.

### Physical Demonstration
A scaled-down physical model was successfully printed using concrete-based additive manufacturing (extrusion) to demonstrate the feasibility of the double-shell geometry.

## Conclusions
The GA-based parametric design approach successfully identifies optimized structural forms that balance conflicting lunar habitat requirements. The double-shell design provides inherent protection: if one shell is damaged, the other remains functional, and the cavity can be filled with radiation shielding material.

## Limitations
1. Did not address cosmic radiation or meteorite impact directly in the optimization loops.
2. Thermal stress analysis under extreme temperature cycles (-183°C to 127°C) was not included.
3. Physical testing used putty/concrete rather than true lunar regolith geopolymer.
