# Role of Cyber-Physical Testing in Developing Resilient Extraterrestrial Habitats

**Authors:** Amin Maghareh, Ali Lenjani, Murali Krishnan, Shirley Dyke, and Ilias Bilionis
**Publisher:** Earth and Space 2021 (ASCE)

## Abstract
Extraterrestrial long-term habitat systems require groundbreaking technological advances to overcome extreme demands (temperature fluctuations, cosmic rays, dust, meteoroids). This paper highlights the importance of system resilience and cyber-physical testing—integrating physical testing and computational modeling—to address the challenges of developing safe and resilient habitat systems.

## Key Concepts
- **Resilience:** Defined by three capacities: absorptive, restorative, and adaptive. The paper argues for moving beyond reliability-based design (passive capacity) to resilience-based design (active/adaptive control).
- **Cyber-Physical Testing:** Also known as hybrid simulation, it partitions systems into computational domains (models) and physical domains (specimens) to examine complex interactions cost-effectively.
- **Control-Theoretic Approach:** Proposes a design framework to maximize system plasticity (ability to prevent loss), system rapidity (restoration speed), and minimize lifecycle cost.

## Conclusion
Resilience is critical for habitat success in extreme environments. The Resilient ExtraTerrestrial Habitats research institute (RETHi) is developing a cyber-physical testing platform to validate control-theoretic approaches and evaluate integrated health management models for future space habitats.
