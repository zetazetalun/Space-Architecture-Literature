**Authors:** Amin Maghareh, Ali Lenjani, Murali Krishnan, Shirley Dyke, and Ilias Bilionis  
**Affiliation:** School of Mechanical Engineering, Purdue University  
**Publication:** Earth and Space 2021, ASCE

## Abstract
Extraterrestrial long-term habitat systems require groundbreaking technological advances to overcome extreme demands like isolation and challenging environments. A habitat system must operate as intended under continuous disruptive conditions. This study highlights the importance of system resilience and cyber-physical testing to address the grand challenge of developing resilient habitat systems.

## Introduction
NASA’s plan for long-term settlements on Mars (NASA 2015) seeks the capacity for people to live beyond Earth sustainably. Designing for extraterrestrial demands (temperature fluctuations, cosmic rays, dust, meteoroid impacts, vibrations, and solar particle events) is a significant engineering challenge. We lack a well-established design framework and technologies needed for habitat systems to successfully achieve and maintain performance over extended periods.

## Resilience and System Failures
Habitat systems are complex and tightly coupled; failures may rapidly cascade. Resilience is defined by three conceptual capacities: absorptive, restorative, and adaptive. Current conceptual habitat designs are often grounded in reliability-based design (passive capacity), which may be insufficient for unanticipated disruptive events. 

### Control-Theoretic Approach
Resilience translates to maintaining system performance using:
1. **Passive controls** (preventive capability)
2. **Adaptive controls** (interventive and mitigative capabilities)

## Cyber-Physical Testing of Habitat Systems
Cyber-physical testing (hybrid simulation) integrates physical testing and computational modeling. This approach allows researchers to evaluate different design approaches with a significant reduction in time and cost. The system is partitioned into:
- **Computational domains:** Containing reliable and accurate models of the majority of the system.
- **Physical domains:** Comprising specimens of novel or difficult-to-model parts.

## Conclusion
Resilience is a key contributor to the success of habitat systems. Achieving breakthroughs requires innovative techniques like cyber-physical testing to validate design approaches under extreme environmental and hazardous conditions.
