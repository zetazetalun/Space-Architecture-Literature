# Development of lunar regolith-based composite for in-situ 3D printing via high-pressure extrusion system

**Authors:** Hua ZHAO, Jihong ZHU, Shangqin YUAN, Shaoying LI, Weihong ZHANG
**Journal:** Frontiers of Mechanical Engineering (Front. Mech. Eng.)
**Year:** 2023
**DOI:** 10.1007/s11465-022-0745-8

## Abstract
To fully utilize the in-situ resources on the moon to facilitate the establishment of a lunar habitat is significant to realize the long-term residence of mankind. This work proposes a cost-effective melting extrusion system for lunar regolith-based composite printing, using engineering thermoplastic powders as a bonding agent. High-performance nylon (PA12) and lunar regolith (basalt simulant) are mixed with weight fractions up to 80 wt.%. Mechanical properties (tensile strength, elastic modulus, impact performance) were evaluated. Maximum tensile strength and impact toughness reached 36.2 MPa and 5.15 kJ/m², respectively. High-pressure melt extrusion can increase the effective loading fraction to 80 wt.% and adapt to extreme conditions for in-situ manufacturing.

## 1 Introduction
Establishing an extraterrestrial habitat on the moon requires utilizing abundant lunar regolith (ISRU) to produce architectural structures because transporting materials from Earth is economically unaffordable. The study explores additive manufacturing (AM) using lunar regolith-based composites to overcome the complexity and high energy consumption of traditional sintering methods.

## 2 Experimental
### 2.1 Materials
- **Binder:** PA12 powder (thermoplastic polymer).
- **Regolith Simulant:** Basalt powder (particle size 50–200 μm), closely matching Apollo 14 and CE-5 lunar samples.
- **Mixtures:** PA12-Basalt composites at 40, 50, and 60 wt.% (standard tests) and up to 80 wt.% (large-scale verification).

### 2.4 Specimens Fabrication
Two types of printers were used:
1. **Piston-based pellet extruder:** For small-batch material performance experiments.
2. **Screw-based pellet extruder:** Robot-assisted system for large-scale component fabrication.

## 3 Results and Discussion
- **Thermal Analysis:** DSC tests showed that basalt particles promote the recrystallization of PA12, benefiting melt extrusion.
- **Mechanical Properties:** 
    - **Tensile Strength:** Decreases as regolith weight fraction increases (Max: 36.2 MPa at 40 wt.% loading).
    - **Elastic Modulus:** Increases with regolith fraction, indicating improved deformation resistance.
    - **Impact Toughness:** Excellent resistance achieved at 60 wt.% basalt loading (5.15 kJ/m²).
- **Aging:** Stress and strain decrease significantly (15%–30% and 45%–66% respectively) after 30 days of placement.

## 4 Conclusions
- Developed an effective approach using high-pressure melt extrusion for lunar regolith-based composites.
- Successfully printed a lunar habitat architectural model and honeycomb structures.
- The system allows for high regolith loading (up to 80 wt.%), maximizing ISRU efficiency.
