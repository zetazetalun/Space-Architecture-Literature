# To boldly go where no one has gone before: Sulfur concrete, a promising construction material fulfilling the demands for a sustainable future on celestial objects: A review

## Authors
Q. Wang, D. Snoeck (Université Libre de Bruxelles)

## Abstract
The need to build long-term or permanent bases on the Moon and Mars necessitates reliable construction materials using local resources. Sulfur concrete is highlighted as a promising candidate due to its waterless mixing, high early strength, and recyclability. This review summarizes the history, environmental suitability, and future challenges of sulfur concrete in space architecture.

## Key Comparison of Construction Materials (Table 1 Summary)
- **Calcium Aluminate concrete:** Lunar application; 25–45 MPa; high water demand.
- **Magnesia Silica Concrete:** Martian application; 20–64 MPa; high water demand.
- **Polymer Concrete:** Lunar application; 12.6–71.3 MPa; waterless but requires transported polymers.
- **Geopolymer Concrete:** Lunar application; 10–80 MPa; low water consumption.
- **Sulfur Concrete:** Lunar/Martian application; 13–115 MPa; waterless, fast curing, fully recyclable.

## Technical Properties (Table 2 Summary)
- **Compressive strength:** 13–115 MPa
- **Tensile strength:** 5–7 MPa
- **Freeze-thaw durability:** 300% (significantly higher than PC-based concrete)
- **Permeability:** Negligible
- **Thermal conductivity:** 0.128 W/m·K

## Extraterrestrial Application Challenges
- **Sublimation:** In a vacuum, sulfur can sublimate. A 1 cm thick layer could disappear in 2 hours at 120 °C. However, burial under 30 cm of regolith mitigates this.
- **Thermal Constraints:** Sulfur melts at 120 °C, posing risks at lunar equatorial daytime temperatures (up to 123 °C).
- **Phase Transformation:** Transition from monoclinic to orthorhombic sulfur during cooling causes 7% volume shrinkage and internal cracking.

## Advanced Manufacturing & Strategies
- **3D Printing:** Technologies like Contour Crafting, Fused Deposition Modeling (FDM), and Binder Jetting are proposed for sulfur-based habitats.
- **Self-Healing:** Approaches similar to asphalt (microwave heating) or using polymers (DCP) are investigated to repair micro-cracks.
- **PCMs:** Phase-change materials are suggested to regulate thermal cycles and protect the structure's integrity.
