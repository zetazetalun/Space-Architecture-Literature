# Integrated Ground–Air–Space Exploration Technology of Jingpo Lake Lava Tubes

**Date:** March 27, 2026

## Abstract
Lava tubes are critical for planetary base construction and understanding geological development. This study presents a multi-platform investigation of a lava tube near Jingpo Lake, China, using space-, air-, and ground-based techniques (SAR, UAV-LiDAR, GPR, and Hyperspectral LiDAR). The integrated approach provides comprehensive data on terrain, tube orientation, cave distribution, and internal environments, serving as a robust reference for future lunar exploration.

## Introduction
Lava tubes form underground conduits that offer natural shielding from cosmic radiation and thermally stable conditions, making them ideal for future bases on the Moon and Mars. Recent research identifies evidence of these tubes on celestial bodies, and the Jingpo Lake site serves as a terrestrial analogue for the "Simulated Lunar Underground Space" base. 

## Exploration Technologies and Methods

### 1. Satellite-based SAR (Spaceborne)
- **Instrument:** HISEA-1 C-band SAR.
- **Function:** Large-scale detection and localization of potential skylights within the lava tube region.
- **Method:** Uses spotlight mode to enhance azimuthal resolution and Refined Lee Filter to mitigate speckle noise.

### 2. UAV LiDAR and Photogrammetry (Airborne)
- **Instrument:** DJI Phantom 4 RTK and DJI Zenmuse L1 LiDAR sensor.
- **Function:** Acquisition of high-resolution topographic data and colored point cloud data of skylight inner walls.
- **Method:** 3D flight planning and close-range photogrammetry to resolve interior wall morphology.

### 3. Ground-Penetrating Radar (Ground)
- **Instrument:** Multi-frequency GPR (50, 100, 200, 400 MHz).
- **Function:** Subsurface structural analysis to detect roof thickness, cavity distribution, and strata sequences.
- **Method:** Cross-verification of GPR reflections against 3D point cloud models.

### 4. Hyperspectral LiDAR (Internal/In-tube)
- **Instrument:** Principal prototype using Acousto-Optical Tunable Filter (AOTF).
- **Function:** Classification of rock materials and mineral composition in total darkness.
- **Spectral Range:** 500 to 1,000 nm (adjustable bands).

## Key Results
1. **Integrated Modeling:** Multi-sensor fusion enabled the creation of a 3D model with an error of less than 0.1 m.
2. **GPR Capabilities:** 50 MHz and 100 MHz radar successfully identified floor contours and internal heights (typically 2-3 m), while 400 MHz resolved sub-meter bedding structures.
3. **Hyperspectral Material Analysis:** The HSL system successfully classified rock compositions (feldspar, pyroxene, and olivine) in lightless conditions, providing a foundation for future lunar resource mapping.

## Conclusion
The integrated ground–air–space detection technology provides a critical reference for designing exploration payloads for lunar habitats. The methodology overcomes the limitations of single-sensor approaches, establishing a robust scientific foundation for geological research and habitat site selection in extreme environments.
