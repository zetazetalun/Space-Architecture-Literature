# Integrated Ground–Air–Space Exploration Technology of Jingpo Lake Lava Tubes

**Authors:** Teng Hu, Haiting Yang, Zhizhong Kang, et al.
**Journal:** Space: Science & Technology
**Date:** 2026
**DOI:** 10.34133/space.0438

## Abstract
Lava tubes play an important role in base construction and understanding planetary geological development on extraterrestrial bodies. This paper presents a multi-platform investigation of a lava tube near Jingpo Lake, China, employing space-, air-, and ground-based techniques (SAR, LiDAR, GPR, Hyperspectral LiDAR) to assist in the comprehensive exploration of lunar lava tubes.

## Introduction
Lava tubes are recognized as prime locations for future habitats on Mars and the Moon due to natural shielding from cosmic radiation and thermally stable conditions. Terrestrial analogs like Jingpo Lake are essential for validating robotic technologies and exploration methods for these subterranean environments.

## Key Findings
- **Multi-Sensor Fusion:** Combining SAR, UAV-LiDAR, GPR, and Hyperspectral LiDAR provides a comprehensive model of lava tube morphology, overcoming limitations of single-sensor approaches.
- **3D Modeling Accuracy:** The integrated approach constructed a realistic 3D model of the lava tube with an error of less than 0.1 m.
- **Material Classification:** Hyperspectral LiDAR successfully differentiated material components on the internal walls in complete darkness, proving its utility for future autonomous lunar exploration.
- **GPR Effectiveness:** Multi-frequency GPR (50-400 MHz) identified tube roof thickness (1-3 m) and internal height (2-3 m) from the surface.

## Discussion and Application to Space Architecture
Lava tubes offer natural protection from radiation and meteoroids. The study demonstrates that multi-sensor payloads can accurately characterize these spaces for habitability. Future development focuses on miniaturizing hyperspectral LiDAR for lunar rovers and optimizing real-time GPR processing for autonomous mission planning.
