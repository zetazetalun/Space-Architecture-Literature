# Silicate-lunar regolith composite: A vacuum self-hardened and comprehensively durable extraterrestrial construction material

## Metadata
- **Journal:** Construction and Building Materials 449 (2024) 138517
- **Authors:** Zifan Geng, Lizhi Zhang, Zhiwen Wu, Jiale Huang, Xiangyu Wang, Ming Jen Tan, Wei She, Hao Zhou, Guoqing Geng
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.conbuildmat.2024.138517
- **Keywords:** Extraterrestrial construction, Lunar regolith, Sodium silicate, Self-hardening, Vacuum dehydration, Durability

## Abstract
Extraterrestrial exploration, represented by Lunar base construction, has been promoted worldwide as a multi-disciplinary, cutting-edge and strategic issue. Herein we proposed a novel extraterrestrial construction material composed of Lunar/Martian regolith simulants and only 4 wt% of silicate adhesive. This silicate-regolith composite can be directly self-hardened in vacuum within 4.5 h without any curing to achieve 29 MPa of hardening strength. Meanwhile, it shows comprehensive durability with stable microstructures and >70 % of residual strength against long-term vacuum exposure, huge temperature difference and intense γ-ray and proton radiations. Its in-situ solidification and durability originate from vacuum dehydration, where silica clusters in solution spontaneously cohere into a solid network with strong connections of silica tetrahedra and bound water.

## 1. Introduction
Lunar construction faces rigorous environments including extreme temperature differences (-200 °C to 120 °C), high vacuum ($10^{-10}$ to $10^{-9}$ Pa), and intense radiation. In-situ resource utilization (ISRU) of lunar regolith is essential to reduce transportation costs.

## 2. Materials and Methods
### 2.1 Materials
- **Lunar Regolith Simulants (LRS):** LRS-1 (Apollo-17 type, 44 μm mean diameter), LRS-2 (Apollo-14), LRS-3 (Apollo-12), and MRS (Martian regolith simulant).
- **Binder:** Silicate solution (sodium silicate) with varying moduli (2.0, 2.5, 3.0).

### 2.2 Sample Preparation
- Mass ratio of silicate solution to regolith simulant: 0.14 : 1.
- Mixing at 6000 rpm for 20 s.
- Cold-pressing under 9.5 MPa to create green bodies (40×10×10 mm³).
- Hardening in a vacuum environment below 10 Pa.

## 3. Results and Discussion
### 3.1 Mechanical Performance
- **Hardening Strength:** 29 MPa (compressive) achieved after 4.5 h in vacuum.
- **Cryogenic Performance:** Hardening successfully achieved at -40 °C (freeze-dried SLRC) achieving 19.54 MPa compressive strength after 15 h.
- **Durability:** Residual flexural strength of 71%-81% and compressive strength of 88%-98% after 108 days of simulated lunar exposure (4 lunar cycles).

### 3.2 Microstructure and Mechanism
- Hardening is driven by vacuum dehydration, causing silica tetrahedra to condense into amorphous networks.
- Silicate binders act as bridges between regolith particles.

## 4. Conclusion
SLRC represents a facile, durable, and versatile solution for in-situ extraterrestrial construction. It eliminates the need for artificial heating during curing and is compatible with diverse regolith types, including cryogenic polar regions and sunless lava tubes.
