# Silicate-lunar regolith composite: A vacuum self-hardened and comprehensively durable extraterrestrial construction material

**Journal:** Construction and Building Materials 449 (2024) 138517  
**Authors:** Zifan Geng, Lizhi Zhang, Zhiwen Wu, Jiale Huang, Xiangyu Wang, Ming Jen Tan, Wei She, Hao Zhou, Guoqing Geng  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.conbuildmat.2024.138517

## Abstract
Extraterrestrial exploration, represented by Lunar base construction, has been promoted worldwide as a multi-disciplinary issue. This study proposes a novel construction material composed of Lunar/Martian regolith simulants and only 4 wt% of silicate adhesive. This composite can be directly self-hardened in vacuum within 4.5 h to achieve 29 MPa of hardening strength. It shows comprehensive durability against long-term vacuum exposure, high temperature differences, and radiation. The solidification originates from vacuum dehydration, where silica clusters cohere into a solid network.

## 1. Introduction
Future lunar habitats require infrastructure for loading and shielding against rigorous environments (temperature cycles from -200 °C to 120 °C, high vacuum, and radiation). High transport costs necessitate the use of in-situ lunar resources and automated construction methods. Current methods like sintering require high energy (>1000 °C), while geopolymers require specific curing conditions (>60 °C). This study explores a vacuum self-hardening approach using sodium silicate binders.

## 2. Materials and Methods
- **Regolith Simulants:** LRS-1 (Apollo-17 simulant), LRS-2 (Apollo-14), LRS-3 (Apollo-12), and MRS (Martian regolith simulant).
- **Binder:** Silicate solution (sodium silicate) with modulus adjusted between 2.0 and 3.0.
- **Preparation:** Mixing regolith with 4 wt% silicate binder, cold-pressing at 9.5 MPa, and exposure to vacuum (<10 Pa).
- **Simulated Environments:** Lunar diurnal cycles (-196 °C to 78 °C), mimic Martian atmosphere, and radiation exposure (γ-ray and protons).

## 3. Results and Discussion
- **Self-hardening:** Samples reached 20.34 MPa compressive strength within 4.5 hours of vacuum exposure.
- **Durability:** Retained 71-81% of flexural strength and 88-98% of compressive strength after 4 lunar diurnal cycles (108 days).
- **Microstructure:** SEM and EDS mapping showed stable silicate bridges between regolith particles even after long-term exposure.
- **Versatility:** Successfully tested with Martian regolith simulants and under cryogenic conditions below -40 °C.

## 4. Conclusion
The silicate-regolith composite utilizes low binder content (4 wt%) and leverages vacuum dehydration for rapid solidification. This method avoids high-energy heating and is viable for cryogenic polar regions or sunless lava tubes, providing a facile and durable solution for in-situ extraterrestrial construction.
