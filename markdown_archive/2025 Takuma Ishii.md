# Distinctive impact responses of extraterrestrial regolith simulants: Insights from crater morphology classification and granular dynamics through machine learning and x-ray computed tomography

**Cite as:** Phys. Fluids 37, 023358 (2025); **doi:** 10.1063/5.0252502
**Published Online:** 12 February 2025
**Authors:** Takuma Ishii, Arata Kioka, Jyh-Jaan Steven Huang, Takeshi Tsuji, and Yasuhiro Yamada

## Abstract
Impact responses of granular materials remain poorly understood, posing significant challenges to extraterrestrial exploration activities such as landing, sampling, drilling, and construction. We studied the crater morphology formed in the granular media and their granular behavior on low-velocity impact cratering by integrating three-dimensional surface scanning, machine learning-based classification, and x-ray computed tomography (X-CT) imaging. Our laboratory experiments focused on lunar (LHS-1 and LMS-1) and Martian regolith simulants (MGS-1) and terrestrial fine silica sand (T-8) as studied granular materials.

## I. Introduction
The physical and geomechanical properties of lunar and Martian surfaces remain a fundamental scientific question. The missions have revealed the inherent physical and geomechanical properties posing significant challenges data interpretation, landing and drilling operations, and constructing infrastructure, highlighting the necessity of a thorough understanding of the granular properties of regolith. This study aims to establish a connection between surface characteristics in crater morphology and geomechanical properties of the given granular material, such as porosity and cohesion.

## II. Material and Methodology
### A. Studied granular materials
This paper studied four granular materials with different physical properties:
- Terrestrial silica sand Tohoku Keisa No. 8 (T-8)
- Lunar highlands regolith simulant LHS-1
- Lunar mare regolith simulant LMS-1
- Martian global regolith simulant MGS-1

## III. Results and Discussion
### A. Classification of target granular materials with LR models
Machine learning using logistic regression (LR) models was applied to classify materials based on crater morphology. Condition (a) showed the best training score (0.841) and accuracy (0.643). The models well-distinguished terrestrial sand (T-8) from the simulants, but often confused LHS-1 with MGS-1 due to similar aspect ratios.

### B. Slice analysis of CT datasets
X-CT imaging revealed that when kinetic energy was 0.3 mJ, the porosity beneath the crater bottom decreased by 0.20 on average in LHS-1 and 0.03 in LMS-1. Higher cohesion in LMS-1 (393 Pa) significantly enhanced its resistance to impact compared to the nearly noncohesive T-8.

## IV. Conclusion
This study bridges the gap between surface crater morphology and subsurface granular material behavior. The findings establish a baseline for understanding the behavior of fine-grained regoliths under impact, providing insights for the strategies and preliminary design of future exploration, drilling, and resource utilization on extraterrestrial terrains.
