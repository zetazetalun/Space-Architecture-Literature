# Distinctive impact responses of extraterrestrial regolith simulants: Insights from crater morphology classification and granular dynamics through machine learning and x-ray computed tomography. Authors: Takuma Ishii, Arata Kioka, Jyh-Jaan Steven Huang, Takeshi Tsuji, Yasuhiro Yamada. Journal: Physics of Fluids 37, 023358 (2025). DOI: 10.1063/5.0252502. ## Abstract: Impact responses of granular materials remain poorly understood, posing significant challenges to extraterrestrial exploration activities such as landing, sampling, drilling, and construction. We studied the crater morphology formed in the granular media and their granular behavior on low-velocity impact cratering by integrating three-dimensional surface scanning, machine learning-based classification, and x-ray computed tomography (X-CT) imaging. ## I. Introduction: The impact behavior of granular materials has been extensively studied to gain crucial insights into their response to external forces. Impact cratering experiments provide valuable data on crater characteristics, including depth, diameter, and morphology. ## II. Material and Methodology: Four granular materials with different physical properties were studied: T-8 (Terrestrial Silica Sand, Cohesion 0 Pa), LHS-1 (Lunar Highlands Simulant, Cohesion 311 Pa), LMS-1 (Lunar Mare Simulant, Cohesion 393 Pa), and MGS-1 (Martian Global Simulant, Cohesion 307 Pa). Spherical alumina projectiles were dropped to generate kinetic energy ranging from 0.1 mJ to 0.3 mJ. CR-Scan Lizard 3D Scanner acquired crater data, and Geotek Rotating X-CT imaged internal microstructures. ## III. Results and Discussion: Multiclass Logistic Regression achieved up to 0.643 accuracy in classifying the media. T-8 was consistently well-classified, underscoring distinctiveness from regolith. X-CT revealed subsurface changes in porosity and particle distribution; for example, porosity beneath the crater decreased by 0.20 in LHS-1 but only 0.03 in LMS-1 due to higher cohesion. ## IV. Conclusion: The classification differentiated regolith simulants from terrestrial sand based on crater profiles. X-CT imaging revealed that significant subsurface changes in porosity and particle distribution are dictated by cohesion, providing insights for future exploration, drilling, and resource utilization.
