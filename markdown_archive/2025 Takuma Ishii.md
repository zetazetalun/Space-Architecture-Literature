# Distinctive impact responses of extraterrestrial regolith simulants: Insights from crater morphology classification and granular dynamics through machine learning and x-ray computed tomography

**Journal:** Physics of Fluids  
**DOI:** 10.1063/5.0252502  
**Published:** February 12, 2025

## Abstract
Impact responses of granular materials remain poorly understood, posing significant challenges to extraterrestrial exploration activities such as landing, sampling, drilling, and construction. We studied the crater morphology formed in the granular media and their granular behavior on low-velocity impact cratering by integrating three-dimensional surface scanning, machine learning-based classification, and x-ray computed tomography (X-CT) imaging. Our laboratory experiments focused on lunar (LHS-1 and LMS-1) and Martian regolith simulants (MGS-1) and terrestrial fine silica sand (T-8).

## Materials and Methodology

### Studied Granular Materials
Four granular materials were analyzed with different physical properties:

**Table I: Physical properties of studied granular materials.**
| Target material | Mean particle density ρg (g/cm³) | Mean particle diameter (μm) | Cohesion (Pa) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| LHS-1 (Lunar Highland) | 3.23 | 90 | 311 |
| LMS-1 (Lunar Mare) | 3.34 | 91 | 393 |
| MGS-1 (Mars Global) | 3.40 | 90 | 307 |
| T-8 (Silica Sand) | 2.78 | 120 | 0 |

### Experimental Setup
- **Projectiles:** Spherical alumina (3.6 g/cm³), 8 mm and 10 mm diameter.
- **Drop Heights:** 10.0 mm and 15.0 mm.
- **Impact Velocities:** 0.44–0.54 m/s.
- **Kinetic Energy:** 0.1–0.3 mJ.
- **Analysis:** 3D surface scanning (CR-Scan Lizard), Machine Learning (Logistic Regression), and X-Ray Computed Tomography (Geotek Rotating X-CT).

## Results and Discussion

### Classification with Machine Learning
Machine learning models were trained to classify the material based on crater profiles (curvature, roughness, and polynomial coefficients). 
- **Condition (a):** Model trained on craters from 10 mm drop and tested on 15 mm drop. Accuracy: 0.643.
- **Distinctiveness:** Terrestrial sand (T-8) was consistently well-distinguished from regolith simulants due to its lack of cohesion and different morphology.

### X-CT Porosity Analysis
- **LHS-1:** Showed substantial porosity decline (compaction) beneath the crater bottom (average reduction of 0.20 at 0.3 mJ impact).
- **LMS-1:** High cohesion (393 Pa) enhanced resistance to impact, resulting in significantly less porosity decline (0.03 on average).
- **T-8:** Porosity actually increased at the "center" region upon impact due to ambient gas displacement in noncohesive sand.

## Conclusion
This study establishes a link between surface crater morphology and subsurface geomechanical behavior. The findings highlight the critical role of cohesion in regolith simulants, which significantly influences their resistance to impact and subsurface deformation. These insights are vital for developing strategies for exploration, excavation, and resource utilization on extraterrestrial surfaces.
