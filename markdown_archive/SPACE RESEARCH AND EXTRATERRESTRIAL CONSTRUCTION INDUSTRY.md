## Abstract
Advance of civilization has been realized through the ages by exploration of unknown parts of the Earth and by exploiting new resources thus acquired. There are currently practically no new areas on this planet to be explored and exploited. This is achieved by the completion of the period called globalization. As a natural continuation of this process, humans will now explore and exploit new areas beyond Earth, i.e. in space. Moon has already been explored partly, it awaits a complete exploration and colonization. The next step will be Mars and beyond. Habitation on these new space bodies will firstly necessitate construction of outposts on these bodies. Thus construction on the Moon, Mars, and other space bodies can be considered to be an important component of space research. In the present article, an overview of space research is presented together with the innovations awaited in space construction industry and the importance of feedback of these studies to construction industry in general.

## I. INTRODUCTION
Humans are transitioning from global exploration to "spatialization." The loop of enlarging the human world involves resources, nature, economy, and technology. As resources on Earth are nearly fully exploited, the next frontier is extraterrestrial.

## II. SPATIALIZATION - WORLD 2050
Mankind's reach has expanded from surface travel to high altitude, peaking with the Apollo Moon landings (400,171 km). It is predicted that by 2050, humans will reach Mars, extending the human sphere to over 200 million km.

## III. PIONEERS OF GOING TO MOON AND MARS
Currently, 10 entities have launch capability, but only 3 (US, Russia, China) have sent humans to space. Manned missions to Mars are expected around 2050. Settlements will transition from short-term visits to long-term outposts.

## IV. HABITATION / CONSTRUCTION ON SPACE BODIES
Construction is a multi-dimensional task. Transporting material from Earth is prohibitively expensive ($20,000/kg to the Moon), necessitating ISRU.

### B. A better understanding of structures
Space structures must handle internal pressure more than external gravitational loads. Key structural concepts include:
- Constructions in lava tubes.
- Habitats embedded in regolith.
- Tensegric structures.
- Inflatable structures.
- Extensive use of prestressing.

### C. Automated construction
Due to hazardous environments, construction will be fully automated, involving mining, earthwork, and robotics.

### D. Special construction machines
Machines must be electrically operated, lightweight, and dust-resistant. Their reduced weight in low gravity requires design innovations to maintain effectiveness.

### E. Building Information Systems
Structures will be "smart" or mechatronic, controlled by a central management system monitoring pressure, temperature, and CO2, with actuators for automated response.

## CONCLUSIONS
Civil engineering is moving from globalization to spatialization. The success of space colonization depends on the development of ISRU, automated construction, and smart habitat management, which will provide feedback to improve terrestrial construction.
