# Bearing capacity of surface circular footings on granular material under low gravity fields

**Journal:** Journal of Rock Mechanics and Geotechnical Engineering 13 (2021) 612–625

## Abstract
Low gravity fields have been simulated through magnetic acceleration to conduct experimental study on bearing capacity of circular footings on a type of crushable planetary regolith simulant, which has comparable density and particle size distribution of lunar soil. The load–settlement responses of surface spread footings are obtained by investigating the relative density, footing size and gravity effects. The results show that the nonlinear gravity effect is not negligible, related to stress condition, soil dilatancy and mobilised friction angle. A cone penetration test (CPT)-based method for prediction of bearing capacity is proposed, improving the design of shallow foundations on the Moon and Mars.

## 1. Introduction
Space exploration tends to progress towards the creation of lunar or Martian bases and human habitats. The stability of these structures is significantly dependent on the interaction between foundations and planetary regolith under low gravity fields (Moon ≈ 1.625 m/s², Mars ≈ 3.711 m/s²). Isolated bearing pads are typical foundations for spacecraft (e.g., Lunar Module). This study uses Geotechnical Magnetic Similitude-Gravity Model Testing (GMMT) to simulate accurate low gravity fields.

## 2. Methodology
### 2.1 Materials
*   **Simulant:** CUMT-1c (crushable planetary regolith simulant).
*   **Properties:** Specific gravity $G_s = 2.93$, mean particle size $d_{50} = 0.12$ mm, angle of repose $\approx 42^\circ$.
*   **Preparation:** Magnetic sensitivity achieved by mixing cement, iron oxide powder, and water.

### 2.2 Testing Apparatus (GMMT)
*   Uses magnetic acceleration in the opposite direction to Earth gravity to simulate reduced gravity levels ($0g$ to $1g$).
*   Testing container: 86 mm inner diameter.
*   Footing sizes: 10 mm, 15 mm, 20 mm diameter circular footings.

## 3. Results
### 3.1 Gravity and Size Effects
*   Ultimate bearing capacity ($q_{ult}$) and foundation stiffness ($k_i$) generally increase with footing size, relative density, and gravity level.
*   The nonlinear gravity effect is significant. For example, $q_{ult}$ on the Moon is approximately 72% of that on Earth for the same footing.
*   The reduction ratio on Mars is approximately 84% compared to Earth.

### 3.2 Influence Zones
*   The normalized influence zone increases with relative density and decreases with footing size.
*   Gravity's effect on the influence zone is non-negligible; higher $g$-levels increase the influence zone.

### 3.3 CPT-based Prediction
*   A correlation is established between $q_{ult}$ and shallow penetration stiffness from miniature CPTs.
*   Formula for foundation stiffness: $k_i/P_{ref} = C_1 D_r^{C_2} (B/d_{50})^{C_3} (\gamma B/P_{ref})^{C_4}$.

## 4. Conclusion
Gravity fields have significant nonlinear relations with foundation stiffness and ultimate bearing capacity. Relative density has a more dominant effect on the mobilized friction angle than the gravity-induced stress gradient. The proposed CPT-based method provides a reliable way to estimate bearing capacity without extensive soil property estimation uncertainties.
