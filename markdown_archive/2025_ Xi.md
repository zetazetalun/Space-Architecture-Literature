# A modified Prandtl’s model for predicting the bearing capacity of lunar soil ground under extraterrestrial gravitational environment

**Authors:** Banglu Xi, Mingjing Jiang, Lin Qi, Jiaqiang Yang, Mingliang Chen  
**Journal:** Acta Astronautica 234 (2025) 59–72  
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.actaastro.2025.04.054](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.actaastro.2025.04.054)

## Abstract
The prediction of the bearing capacity of regolith on the Moon is vital for the design and construction of future lunar bases. Classical analytical methods developed on Earth often exclude gravity effects on soil bearing behavior. This study examines Prandtl’s bearing capacity theory using DEM simulations under various gravity fields (0.01g to 10g) to develop a modified model. The results show that the modified Prandtl’s model predicts bearing capacities within 50% error of experimental data, capturing the gravity effect on lunar soil and its simulants.

## 1. Introduction
Lunar habitation designs (inflatable, concrete, deployable, and lava tubes) require foundation systems to safely transfer structural loads to lunar soil. Classical theories like Prandtl (1921) and Terzaghi (1943) were developed for 1g environments and assume bearing capacity is directly proportional to gravity, which contradicts experimental findings from parabolic flights and centrifuge tests.

## 2. Modified Prandtl’s Model

### 2.1 Hypotheses and Examination
Discrete Element Method (DEM) was used to observe failure mechanisms. Parameters included:

| Parameter | Value |
| :--- | :--- |
| Void ratio | 0.22 |
| Dry density | 2600 kg/m³ |
| Frictional coefficient | 1.0 |
| Rolling resistance coefficient | 1.3 |

### 2.2 Model Modifications
1. **Soil Weight:** Introduced into the limit equilibrium analysis via integral calculus.
2. **Active Earth Pressure:** Replaced with the sum of earth pressure at rest and additional pressure from the applied load.
3. **Passive Earth Pressure:** Introduced a gravity-dependent coefficient ($k_p$).
4. **Failure Surface:** Accounted for stress-dependent internal friction angles (friction increases as gravity/confining pressure decreases).

## 3. Results and Comparison
- **Bearing Capacity Factors:** $N_c$ and $N_q$ values in the modified model are smaller than classical theories because the model uses earth pressure at rest rather than active pressure.
- **Gravity Effect:** The model demonstrates that bearing capacity increases non-linearly with decreasing gravity relative to soil weight effects.
- **Validation:** Compared against centrifuge tests (KLS-1) and parabolic flight data (FJS-1, Toyoura sand). The model stays within a 50% error margin, whereas classical models show significant divergence in low-gravity environments.

## 4. Conclusions
The modified Prandtl’s model effectively captures the non-linear relationship between gravity and bearing capacity. It serves as a more reliable tool for the design of foundations for future extraterrestrial bases compared to classical terrestrial models.
