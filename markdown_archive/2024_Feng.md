# A comprehensive review of lunar lava tube base construction and field research on a potential Earth test site

**Authors:** Yujie Feng, Peng-Zhi Pan, Xuhai Tang, Zhaofeng Wang, Yuxin Li, Altaf Hussain  
**Journal:** International Journal of Mining Science and Technology 34 (2024) 1201–1216  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijmst.2024.06.003

## Abstract
The Moon plays a pivotal role in deep space exploration. Lunar lava tubes, formed by volcanic eruptions, are ideal natural shelters for bases. This paper reviews geological origins, exploration history, and distribution of these tubes. It presents advantages and concepts for construction, summarizes ground-based attempts, and details a field study on a lava tube in Hainan, China. Challenges in geotechnical engineering, including in-situ testing and structural stability assessment, are discussed.

## 1. Introduction
Lunar lava tubes offer unique environmental advantages: stable temperatures, low radiation, and protection from meteorite impacts. Inflatable modules and ISRU-based construction are being considered for these underground spaces.

## 2. Lunar Lava Tubes
### 2.1. Geological Origins
Lava tubes form when the outer layer of a lava flow solidifies while the interior remains molten and continues to flow, leaving behind a hollow conduit. Lunar gravity (1/6th of Earth) allows for much larger tubes than those on Earth, potentially spanning hundreds of meters in width.

### 2.2. Exploration History
Key evidence comes from 'skylights' (pits). Major missions include:
- **SELENE (2009):** Identified the Marius Hills Hole (MHH), a 65m diameter skylight.
- **LRO:** Discovered over 300 pits.
- **GRAIL (2013):** Identified mass deficits suggesting a 4km-wide tube in Rima Sharp.

## 3. Advantages of Lava Tube Bases
- **Temperature:** Surface temperatures range from -170°C to 110°C; internal tube temperatures remain stable at approximately -20°C.
- **Radiation:** Regolith thickness of 1-6m negates most SPE and GCR effects. Lava tube roofs (often >10m) provide total protection.
- **Dust/Meteorites:** Tubes are shielded from the solar wind and micrometeorite impacts.

## 4. Analog Sites and Hainan Field Research
Earth analogs like CHILL-ICE (Iceland) and Lava Beds (USA) are used to test equipment. The study analyzed the "Seventy-Two Caves" in Hainan, China:
- **Composition:** High similarity to Apollo 14 lunar regolith (SiO2, Al2O3, FeO, CaO).
- **Mechanics:** Average compressive strength of dense basalt is 76.40 MPa; porous basalt is 63.25 MPa.
- **Morphology:** Features prominent skylights and intact tube segments over 780m long.

## 5. Geotechnical Challenges
1. **Exploration:** Localization in pitch-black environments.
2. **In-situ Testing:** Drilling and sampling under microgravity and vacuum.
3. **Stability:** Assessing the load-bearing capacity and reinforcement of tube walls.
4. **Construction:** Development of cave lining materials and automated excavation techniques.
