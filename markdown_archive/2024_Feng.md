# A comprehensive review of lunar lava tube base construction and field research on a potential Earth test site

**Authors:** Yujie Feng, Peng-Zhi Pan, Xuhai Tang, Zhaofeng Wang, Yuxin Li, Altaf Hussain  
**Journal:** International Journal of Mining Science and Technology 34 (2024) 1201–1216  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijmst.2024.06.003

## Abstract
Lunar lava tubes are underground caves formed by volcanic eruptions and are considered ideal natural shelters for lunar base construction. This paper provides an overview of geological origins, exploration history, and distribution. It highlights four advantages of lava tubes: stable temperature, radiation protection, protection from lunar dust/meteorites, and structural volume. It discusses geotechnical challenges including cave exploration, in-situ testing, and excavation/reinforcement techniques.

## 1. Introduction
Lunar lava tubes offer unique environmental advantages: stable temperatures, low radiation, and large internal volumes. They serve as natural shields against external hazards. Current research focuses on using these features for inflatable or ISRU-based habitats.

## 2. Lunar Lava Tubes
### 2.1 Geological Origins
Lava tubes (pyroducts) form during volcanic eruptions as the surface of lava flows cools and solidifies, forming a sturdy shell. On the Moon, lower gravity (1/6 of Earth) allows for much larger tubes, with diameters spanning hundreds to thousands of meters.

### 2.2 Exploration Evidence
- **SELENE/Kaguya:** Identified skylights (e.g., Marius Hills Hole) with diameters ~65m.
- **GRAIL:** Gravity deficit data indicated large subsurface cavities.
- **GPR:** Ground-penetrating radar on missions like Chang'e-3 detected buried underground structures.

## 3. Lunar Lava Tube Base Advantages
- **Stable Temperature:** While surface temperatures fluctuate by ~300°C, lava tube environments (at depth) are estimated to remain stable around -20°C.
- **Radiation Protection:** 1-6 meters of regolith thickness is sufficient to negate the detrimental effects of Galactic Cosmic Rays (GCRs) and Solar Particle Events (SPEs).
- **Dust and Meteorites:** Underground locations are shielded from charged dust movement and have near-zero risk of micrometeorite impact.

## 4. Construction Concepts
Several concepts are reviewed:
1. **"Green" Habitat (Grandl, 1985):** Modules inside pits with transparent dome seals.
2. **Swarm Habitat (Mizuguchi & Ikeda, 2023):** Using 100 truncated octahedron modules that slide into tubes.
3. **Harbin Institute Concept (2023):** A multi-step plan using radar scanning, inflatable core modules, and robots to construct 3D-printed lunar soil bricks for external shells.

## 5. Earth Analog Site: Haikou "Seventy-Two Caves"
Field research in Hainan, China, identified a site with basalt composition (SiO2, Al2O3, FeO, CaO) remarkably similar to Apollo 14 samples. 
- **Basalt Properties:** 
  - Dense basalt compressive strength: 76.40 MPa.
  - Porous basalt compressive strength: 63.25 MPa.
  - Elastic modulus: 22.75–26.98 GPa.

## 6. Challenges in Geotechnical Engineering
- **Exploration:** Need for precise 3D scanning and autonomous rovers in pitch-black environments.
- **In-situ Testing:** Difficulty of drilling and sampling in extreme vacuum/temperature conditions.
- **Excavation:** Requirements for laser melting or mechanical drilling under low gravity.

## 7. Conclusions
Lunar lava tubes are optimal for human habitation. Future work requires interdisciplinary cooperation in geotechnical engineering, mining, and materials science to develop excavation, reinforcement, and simulated Earth-based testing methods.
