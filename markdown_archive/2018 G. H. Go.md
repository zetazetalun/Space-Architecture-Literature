## ABSTRACT
The dust vacuum chamber is an essential infrastructure for the verification of space exploration equipment, especially extraterrestrial missions for in situ resource utilization (ISRU). This study presents a new approach in soil mechanics that is particularly applicable to the dust vacuum chamber. One of the main issues in operating the dust vacuum chamber is soil disturbance problems during the evacuation. Thus, the appropriate vacuum rate was suggested to minimize the soil disturbance in the dust vacuum chamber using theoretical and numerical approaches. Firstly, simple analytical solutions provided approximate maximum discharge velocity and evacuation time. Then, a two-dimensional finite element model (FEM) was developed to simulate the hydro-mechanical behavior. From the numerical analysis results, optimum guidelines have been proposed to ensure that soil disturbance is minimized.

## INTRODUCTION
The trend in space exploration is shifting toward surface missions using rovers and landers. Verification of equipment for lunar surface missions requires performance tests in a dirty vacuum chamber (DVC) simulating lunar surface conditions. However, soil disturbance caused by depressurization causes difficulties in maintaining the initial condition of the soil bin. This study suggests a new operating guideline to minimize soil disturbance through theoretical solutions and finite element analyses.

## POROELASTICITY THEORY
A "poroelasticity theory" proposed by Biot (1941) was applied to evaluate the hydro-mechanical behavior under depressurization. The constitutive equation governing poroelastic behavior relates to stress, strain, and pore air pressure:

$$\sigma = C\epsilon - \alpha_B u_a I$$

Where $\sigma$ is total stress, $\epsilon$ is strain tensor, $u_a$ is air pressure, and $\alpha_B$ is the Biot-Willis coefficient. The maximum air velocity limit ($q_{max}$) occurs when seepage force exceeds the soil's dead load:

$$q_{max} = \frac{K \cdot \rho_d \cdot g}{\mu}$$

## FINITE ELEMENT MODEL
A 2D FEM model coupled fluid flow and geomechanics for a soil specimen with a depth of 0.047m. Initial pressure was set to atmospheric pressure. Depressurization was applied at the top boundary while other boundaries were undrained. The top boundary was "free" for solid mechanical movement, the bottom was "fixed", and walls were "rollers".

## RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
As absolute pressure approaches vacuum, air velocity increases rapidly due to the exponential increase in the pressure gradient. Theoretical calculations determined $q_{max}$ to be 0.000235 m/s. For a vacuum rate of -0.33 kPa/sec, soil disturbance occurs 282.5 seconds after depressurization begins (at approximately -94 kPa).

Reducing the vacuum rate from -0.33 kPa/sec to -0.11 kPa/sec approximately 10 seconds before reaching the critical limit effectively drops the air velocity and prevents soil disruption. This implies that vacuum rates must be controlled adequately until reaching maximum allowable values (e.g., 2.5 Torr).

## SUMMARY AND CONCLUSION
1. A new theoretical approach provides insight into hydro-mechanical soil behavior in DVCs during depressurization.
2. The FEM model can provide appropriate vacuum rates to prevent disturbance.
3. Vacuum rates should be controlled to maintain the initial soil condition until the target vacuum pressure is reached.
