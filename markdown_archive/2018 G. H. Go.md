# Soil Mechanics in Vacuum Chamber

**Authors:** G. H. Go, J. Lee, H. S. Shin, B. H. Rhu, and H. W. Jin  
**Publication:** Earth and Space 2018, pp. 804–809  
**Publisher:** ASCE

## Abstract
The dust vacuum chamber is an essential infrastructure for the verification of space exploration equipment, especially extraterrestrial missions for in situ resource utilization (ISRU). This study presents a new approach in soil mechanics that is particularly applicable to the dust vacuum chamber. One of the main issues in operating the dust vacuum chamber is soil disturbance problems during the evacuation. Thus, the appropriate vacuum rate was suggested to minimize the soil disturbance in the dust vacuum chamber using theoretical and numerical approaches. From the numerical analysis results, optimum guidelines have been proposed to ensure that soil disturbance is minimized.

## Introduction
Recent space exploration trends have shifted toward surface missions using rovers and landers. Verifying exploration equipment requires performance tests in a Dirty Vacuum Chamber (DVC) that simulates lunar surface conditions. A major challenge in DVC operation is soil disturbance caused by depressurization, which alters the initial condition of the soil bin. This study suggests operating guidelines to minimize such disturbance through theoretical solutions and finite element analysis (FEA).

## Poroelasticity Theory
Biot's poroelasticity theory (1941) was applied to evaluate the hydro-mechanical behavior of soil under depressurization. The constitutive equation relates stress, strain, and pore air pressure:

$$\sigma = C\epsilon - \alpha_B u_a I$$

Where:
- $\sigma$: Total stress
- $\epsilon$: Strain tensor
- $u_a$: Pore air pressure
- $\alpha_B$: Biot-Willis coefficient
- $C$: Elastic matrix

Soil disturbance begins when effective stress becomes zero due to seepage stress. The maximum air velocity limit ($q_{max}$) occurs when the seepage force exceeds the soil's dead load.

## Finite Element Model
A 2D model coupled fluid flow and geomechanics. The modeling area was a soil specimen with a depth of 0.047m. Depressurization was applied at the top boundary, while others were undrained. The top boundary was set to "free" for solid mechanics, and the bottom was "fixed."

## Results and Discussion
As absolute pressure approaches vacuum, air velocity increases rapidly due to the exponential rise in the pressure gradient, inducing high drag forces. 
- Theoretical $q_{max}$: 0.000235 m/s.
- With a vacuum rate of -0.33 kPa/sec, disturbance occurred at 282.5 seconds (vacuum pressure ~ -94 kPa).
- By reducing the vacuum rate to -0.11 kPa/sec before reaching $q_{max}$, the air velocity dropped, effectively maintaining soil stability.

## Summary and Conclusion
1. The new theoretical approach provides insight into soil hydro-mechanical behavior in DVCs.
2. FEA can determine appropriate vacuum rates to prevent disturbance.
3. Vacuum rates should be controlled adequately until the pressure reaches a maximum allowable value (e.g., 2.5 Torr).
