# Soil Mechanics in Vacuum Chamber

**Authors:** G. H. Go, J. Lee, H. S. Shin, B. H. Rhu, and H. W. Jin

## Abstract
The dust vacuum chamber is an essential infrastructure for the verification of space exploration equipment, especially extraterrestrial missions for in situ resource utilization (ISRU). This study presents a new approach in soil mechanics that is particularly applicable to the dust vacuum chamber. One of the main issues in operating the dust vacuum chamber is soil disturbance problems during the evacuation. Thus, the appropriate vacuum rate was suggested to minimize the soil disturbance in the dust vacuum chamber using theoretical and numerical approaches. First, simple analytical solutions were used to provide approximate maximum discharge velocity and evacuation time. Then, a two-dimensional finite element model was developed to simulate the hydro-mechanical behavior of the soil specimen. From the numerical analysis results, optimum guidelines have been proposed to ensure that soil disturbance is minimized.

## Introduction
Recent space exploration trends emphasize surface missions using rovers and landers. Verification of equipment, such as that used in the LCROSS mission or for ISRU, requires performance tests in a dirty vacuum chamber (DVC) that simulates lunar surface conditions. A key issue in DVC operation is soil disturbance caused by depressurization, which can alter the initial condition of the soil bin.

## Poroelasticity Theory
Biot's poroelasticity theory was applied to evaluate the hydro-mechanical behavior. The constitutive equation relates stress, strain, and pore air pressure:
$$\sigma = C\epsilon - \alpha_B u_a I$$
where $\sigma$ is total stress, $\epsilon$ is strain, $u_a$ is pore air pressure, and $\alpha_B$ is the Biot-Willis coefficient. The maximum air velocity limit is defined as the moment when the seepage force exceeds the soil's dead load:
$$q_{max} = \frac{K}{\mu} \cdot \rho_d \cdot g$$

## Finite Element Model
A 2D FEM model was set up coupling fluid flow and geomechanics for a soil specimen depth of 0.047m. Boundary conditions included a free top boundary for solid mechanics and an undrained bottom for fluid flow, simulating the depressurization process from the top.

## Results and Discussion
As absolute pressure approaches vacuum, air velocity increases rapidly due to exponential pressure gradients, inducing high drag forces. Soil disturbance occurs when effective stress becomes zero due to seepage stress. For the given properties, the maximum air velocity ($q_{max}$) causing disturbance was calculated as 0.000235 m/s. At a vacuum rate of -0.33 kPa/sec, this occurs at 282.5 seconds. Reducing the vacuum rate to -0.11 kPa/sec before reaching this threshold prevents disruption.

## Summary and Conclusion
1. New theoretical approach provides insight into soil behavior in DVC.
2. FEM model can determine appropriate vacuum rates.
3. The approach is applicable for optimizing DVC operations to minimize disturbance.
4. Vacuum rates must be controlled adequately until the pressure reaches a stable threshold (e.g., 2.5 Torr).
