# Optimization of geopolymer mix composition for structural civil engineering applications

**Authors:** Janusz Kobaka, Jacek Katzer, Alberto Gallina, Karol Seweryn
**DOI:** 10.24425/ace.2026.157476
**Journal:** Archives of Civil Engineering, Vol. LXXII, Issue 1, 2026

## Abstract
Geopolymers are inorganic non-metallic cementitious materials serving as an alternative to ordinary Portland cement (OPC). This paper proposes a method of geopolymer design using a ternary plot to select proportions of ingredients (sand/fly ash/lunar regolith simulant and alkaline activators) to achieve high workability and strength. The research specifically considers the lack of OPC on the Moon, utilizing Lunar Highlands Simulant (LHS-1) to justify the use of geopolymers for extraterrestrial bases.

## 1. Introduction
Lunar programs such as NASA's Artemis and ESA's Terrae Novae require sustainable footprints. In-Situ Resource Utilization (ISRU) is essential due to the lack of clinker resources for concrete. Geopolymers, formed through alkali activation of aluminosilicate materials, offer superior strength, fire resistance, and environmental durability. This study focuses on achieving high strength using LHS-1 lunar regolith simulant.

## 3. Results and Conclusions
The highest compressive strength achieved for sand-based geopolymer was 30.1 MPa. For LHS-1 based geopolymer, the maximum compressive strength was 26.7 MPa (nearly equivalent to the sand-based version). Geopolymers reached full strength after only 3 days, compared to 28 days for standard concrete. The highest results were achieved using a 50% water solution of sodium hydroxide or sodium metasilicate. Higher densities (up to 2.0 g/cm³) correlated with higher strength.
