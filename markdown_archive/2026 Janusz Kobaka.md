# Optimization of geopolymer mix composition for structural civil engineering applications

**Authors:** Janusz Kobaka, Jacek Katzer, Alberto Gallina, Karol Seweryn  
**DOI:** 10.24425/ace.2026.157476  
**Date:** 2026  
**Journal:** Archives of Civil Engineering (Warsaw University of Technology)

## Abstract
Recently, geopolymers, a type of inorganic non-metallic cementitious materials, have attracted considerable attention as an alternative to ordinary Portland cement (OPC) and as an effective pathway to mitigate energy consumption and minimize CO2 emissions. This paper proposes a method of geopolymer design to achieve best mechanical properties for civil engineering perspective. The study utilizes lunar soil simulant (LHS-1) to evaluate suitability for extraterrestrial base construction.

## 1. Introduction
Civil engineering for lunar bases (Artemis, Terrae Novae) must deal with ISRU (In-Situ Resource Utilization). Due to the lack of clinker resources on the Moon, geopolymers are considered a viable alternative to concrete. Geopolymers utilize aluminosilicate materials (precursors) and alkali activation to produce three-dimensional gel structures.

## 3. Results
Tests showed that compressive strength of geopolymers based on sand and fly ash ranged from 1 to 30 MPa. Best results were achieved with a solution of NaOH with a molarity of 12.5 M. Compressive strength for LHS-1 based geopolymers ranged from 0.2 to 27.2 MPa.

## 4. Conclusions
- Geopolymer strength equivalent to ordinary concrete was achieved for both materials.
- Highest compressive strength: 30.1 MPa (sand-based) and 26.7 MPa (LHS-1 based).
- Geopolymers reached full strength in only 3 days, compared to 28 days for OPC.
- The study confirms the feasibility of using lunar-simulant-based geopolymers for structural extraterrestrial applications.
