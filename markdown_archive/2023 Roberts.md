# StarCrete: A starch-based biocomposite for off-world construction

**Authors:** Aled D. Roberts and Nigel S. Scrutton
**Journal:** Open Engineering 2023; 13: 20220390
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1515/eng-2022-0390

## Abstract
Robust and affordable technology capabilities are needed before a sustained human presence on the lunar and Martian surfaces can be established. A key challenge is the production of high-strength structural materials from *in situ* resources to provide spacious habitats with adequate radiation shielding. Here, we demonstrate the use of ordinary starch as a binder for simulated extraterrestrial regolith to produce a high-strength biocomposite material, termed StarCrete. After optimisation, lunar and Martian StarCrete achieved compressive strengths of 91.7 and 72.0 MPa, respectively, which is well within the domain of high-strength concrete (>42 MPa).

## 1 Introduction
Sustained human presence requires habitats with thick walls for radiation and meteoroid protection. Bulk construction materials should be produced from locally available resources (ISRU). Biopolymers are proposed as low-energy binders. Previous work investigated chitosan and blood proteins (BSA/HSA), but this study focuses on starch (amylose/amylopectin) as a surplus food-system resource.

## 3 Conclusions and outlook
StarCrete offers a high-strength, low-energy solution for ISRU construction. It integrates food and construction systems, utilizes simple processing (heat and compression), and achieves strengths surpassing standard concrete. Future work involves environmental testing (radiation, thermal cycling) and 3D printing feasibility.
