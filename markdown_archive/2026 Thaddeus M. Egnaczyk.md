# Effects of low-earth orbit exposure on geopolymer material properties

**Journal:** Advances in Space Research 77 (2026) 10583–10598
**Authors:** Thaddeus M. Egnaczyk, William H. Hartt V, Jake George, Norman J. Wagner

## Abstract
The construction of a lunar base camp including landing pads, radiation shielding, and transportation infrastructure is an integral part of NASA’s upcoming Artemis missions. Geopolymer binders formed from locally available aluminosilicate-rich regolith on the moon and Mars are proposed as a robust solution for extraterrestrial construction materials. The goal of this work is to establish the durability of potential extraterrestrial geopolymer construction materials at low-Earth orbit conditions as part of NASA’s MISSE-20 mission. Four compositions of geopolymer binders were exposed to LEO on the International Space Station for six months. Results show that geopolymers formed from lunar regolith simulants (LHS-1, BP-1) and Martian simulant (MGS-1C) show no degradation, with LHS-1 even showing a ~35% increase in compressive strength compared to Earth-control samples.

## 1. Introduction
Durable extraterrestrial construction materials are required for habitats and infrastructure. In-situ resource utilization (ISRU) is necessary to reduce payload costs. Geopolymer binders are a focus because they require little energy and allow for water recovery post-cure.

## 2. Materials and Methods
### 2.1 Geopolymer Compositions
- **LHS-1:** Lunar Highlands Simulant 1 (Exolith)
- **BP-1:** Black Point 1 lunar mare simulant
- **MGS-1C:** Mars Global Hydrated Clay Simulant
- **MK:** High purity metakaolin (Control)

### 2.2 Curing and Exposure
Samples were cured at 60 °C for 72 hours, then exposed on the ISS zenith position for 201 days. Pre-flight bakeout testing was performed to simulate ISS integration requirements.

## 3. Results
### 3.1 Mechanical Properties
- **LHS-1:** MISSE sample strength: 60.3 ± 2.8 MPa vs. Earth-control: 44.7 ± 2.0 MPa.
- **BP-1:** MISSE sample strength increased by ~20% compared to controls.
- **MGS-1C:** MISSE sample strength increased by ~25% compared to controls.
- **Metakaolin:** Experienced macro-cracking due to pre-flight bakeout/vacuum conditions.

### 3.2 Chemical Stability
XRD analysis indicated no significant chemical changes or new phase formations due to LEO exposure.

## 4. Discussion
The increase in compressive strength for regolith-based samples is attributed to continued curing at elevated temperatures during bakeout and orbit. Metakaolin's failure was linked to its lower solids mass fraction and thermal expansion differences compared to regolith-based geopolymers.
