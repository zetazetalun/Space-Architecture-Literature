# Effects of low-earth orbit exposure on geopolymer material properties

**Authors:** Thaddeus M. Egnaczyk, William H. Hartt V, Jake George, Norman J. Wagner
**Journal:** Advances in Space Research 77 (2026) 10583–10598
**Available online:** 28 February 2026

## Abstract
The construction of a lunar base camp including landing pads, radiation shielding, and transportation infrastructure is an integral part of NASA’s upcoming Artemis missions. Geopolymer binders formed from locally available aluminosilicate-rich regolith on the moon and Mars are proposed as a robust solution for extraterrestrial construction materials. This work establishes the durability of potential extraterrestrial geopolymer construction materials at low-Earth orbit conditions (ISS) for six months. Geopolymers formed from lunar regolith simulants (LHS-1, BP-1) and Martian regolith simulant (MGS-1C) show no signs of degradation. LHS-1 sample compressive strength increased by ~35% (60.3 MPa vs 44.7 MPa control). Results demonstrate the viability of geopolymers as extraterrestrial construction materials.

## 1. Introduction
Durable extraterrestrial construction materials are required for habitats and infrastructure. In-situ resource utilization (ISRU) reduces payload costs. Geopolymer binders are a low-energy alternative to sintering or melting, synthesized through the reaction of an aluminosilicate precursor and an alkaline solution. Water used in synthesis is not chemically sequestered and can potentially be recovered post-cure.

## 3. Results

### 3.1. Mechanical Properties
LHS-1 samples exposed to LEO showed a compressive strength of 60.3 MPa (SD 2.8), significantly higher than the Earth control (44.7 MPa, SD 2.0). BP-1 and MGS-1C also showed increases of 20% and 25% respectively.

## 5. Conclusions
Geopolymer binders synthesized from lunar and Martian simulants maintain excellent mechanical stability after six months in LEO. The findings motivate the use of geopolymers for Artemis infrastructure, including vertical takeoff/landing (VTVL) pads. Further research is needed to validate performance under the more extreme temperature swings of the lunar south pole.
