# Thermal and Mechanical Properties of Cementitious Composites for Additive Construction of Energy-Saving Habitats

**Authors:** Hongyu Zhou, Adam L. Brooks, Dominic Hanna, Babak Salarieh
**Publisher:** ASCE (American Society of Civil Engineers)

## ABSTRACT
Lightweight cementitious composites (LWCC) are cement-based materials combined with lightweight fillers (LWFs) like hollow glass microspheres (HGM) and fly ash cenospheres (FAC). They offer advantages in reducing structural weight and thermal conductivity for terrestrial and extraterrestrial habitats. The spherical shape of the aggregates makes LWCC suitable for extrusion-based 3D printing. This research investigates the mechanical and thermal properties of various LWCC mixes.

## EXPERIMENTAL PROGRAM
- **Materials:** ASTM Type I-II Portland cement, silica sand, water (w/c = 0.43), superplasticizer.
- **Fillers:** 
  - Hollow glass microspheres (HGM): K25, S38HS, S60.
  - Fly ash cenospheres (FAC): E106, E200/600.
- **Volume Fractions:** 7%, 14%, 21%, and 28% replacements for silica sand.
- **Testing:** Compressive strength (MTS-810), Thermal properties (Transient Plane Source method), and dynamic behavior (Split Hopkinson Pressure Bar).

## RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
### Compressive Strength
- LWCC compressive strength is a function of particle size and shell thickness. 
- Smaller particles with thicker shells (e.g., S60) yielded higher strength.
- At 28% volume fraction, S60 mortar was 125% stronger than K25 mortar.

### Thermal Properties
- Thermal conductivity decreases as LWF volume fraction increases.
- At 28% volume fraction, conductivity dropped to 0.65-0.8 W/m K (from ~2.2 W/m K in control mixes).
- Thermal properties are primarily dictated by volume fraction rather than filler type.

### Dynamic Testing (SHPB)
- LWCC is strain-rate sensitive.
- Dynamic compressive strength at 920 s⁻¹ was 66.7% higher than quasi-static strength.

## CONCLUSIONS
1. Thermal properties are governed by LWF volume fraction; mechanical properties are governed by LWF particle size and shell thickness.
2. LWCC materials with high strength-to-weight ratios (like S60 HGMs) are suitable for structural load-bearing.
3. The spherical shape of HGMs/FACs improves rheology for extrusion-based additive construction.
