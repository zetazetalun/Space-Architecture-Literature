# Designing sustainable built environments for Mars habitation: Integrating innovations in architecture, systems, and human well-being

**Authors:** Hongli Sun, Mengfan Duan, Yifan Wu, Yunyi Zeng, Hengxin Zhao, Shuangdui Wu, Borong Lin, Ronggui Yang, Gang Tan
**Journal:** Nexus (Cell Press Partner Journal)
**Date:** September 17, 2024
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ynexs.2024.100030

## Summary
Migration to Mars and building livable habitation has become a priority for human progress. This review explores a systemic outlook of Mars habitation environment control technologies, emphasizing the transition from "habitation design to operation." It combines resource sustainability (energy and materials) with human health (physiology and psychology) to propose a human-centered technical system for residential environments in outer space.

## Mars Habitation Technologies

### Design of Mars Habitation Module
Mars habitation is categorized by location and construction type:
- **Above-ground modules:** Similar to terrestrial structures, often using prefab pressurized (inflatable) cores with outer shells (e.g., Mars Ice House, Marsha).
- **Underground/Semi-underground modules:** Designed to utilize Martian lava tubes to withstand harsh space risks and radiation.

### Construction Materials & ISRU
- **Basalt:** Abundant on the Martian surface; offers radiation resistance and high specific heat.
- **Martian Soil/Regolith:** Can be used for concrete. High sulfur levels (avg. 6 wt%) make sulfur-based concrete suitable due to ease of operation and fast solidification in cold environments.
- **Metal Oxides:** Weathered layers are rich in iron minerals (up to 15 wt%), which can be used for advanced Martian materials.
- **Material Strength:** Sulfur-based Martian materials have shown compressive strengths of approximately 4.62 kgf/mm².

### Automated Construction
- **3D Printing:** Offers the ability to use in-situ materials autonomously.
- **Robotic Systems:** Examples include "Team Zopherus" (autonomous printing chamber) and "Chinese Super Mason" (robotic assembly of planetary LEGO bricks).

### Operation and Life Support
- **Thermal Control:** Use of multi-layer insulation, heat pipes, and phase change materials.
- **Air Generation:** Electrolyzed water and pressurized oxygen tanks; regenerative life support systems (ECLSS).
- **Lighting:** Rhythmic artificial lighting to support human circadian rhythms in the absence of a standard Earth-like day/night cycle.

## Conclusion
Achieving sustainable Mars habitation requires the synergistic integration of architecture, material science, and engineering. Future focus must remain on the five critical issues: module form, construction methods, material usage, energy systems, and human-centered indoor environments.
