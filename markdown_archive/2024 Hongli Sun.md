# Designing sustainable built environments for Mars habitation: Integrating innovations in architecture, systems, and human well-being

**Journal:** Nexus (Cell Press)
**Date:** September 17, 2024
**Authors:** Hongli Sun, Mengfan Duan, Yifan Wu, Yunyi Zeng, Hengxin Zhao, Shuangdui Wu, Borong Lin, Ronggui Yang, Gang Tan

## Summary
Migration to Mars and building livable habitation are arduous roads for human progress. This review provides a new angle of a developmental path from 'habitation design to operation,' illustrating a systemic outlook of promising Mars habitation environment control technologies for the sustainability of both habitation resources and occupant health.

## Mars Habitation Technologies

### Design of Mars Habitation Module
- **Forms:** Above-ground (pressurized inflatable cores with radiation shells), underground (lava tubes), and semi-underground.
- **Materials (ISRU):** 
  - **Basalt:** Abundant on surface; radiation resistant; high specific heat.
  - **Martian Soil/Clay/Steel:** Explored for Martian concrete.
  - **Sulfur-based Concrete:** Suitable due to ease of operation, fast solidification, and stability in dry/cold environments.
- **Construction:** 3D printing and robotic construction (e.g., Team Zopherus' autonomous printer; Chinese Super Mason mission for LEGO brick assembly).

### Habitation Operation
- **Thermal Control:** Multi-layer insulation, PU polyfoam, heat pipes, and polymer aerogels. 
- **Life Support:** Regenerative systems for air (electrolyzed water) and water (condensation/recycling).
- **Energy Management:** Solar (PV/BIPV), nuclear energy, and wind turbines.

### Human Well-being
- **Physiological:** Dynamic indoor temperature regulation and rhythmic artificial lighting to align with circadian cycles.
- **Psychological:** Use of private spaces, dynamic interior elements, and bionic environment regulation to combat isolation effects.

## Challenges and Future Opportunities
1. Developing module forms that mitigate radiation and temperature variations.
2. Rapid, high-quality assembly using local materials.
3. Integrated risk management from a life cycle perspective.
4. Synergistic integration across architecture, engineering, and physiology.
