# The principle of topological interlocking in extraterrestrial construction

**Authors:** A.V. Dyskin, Y. Estrin, E. Pasternak, H.C. Khor, A.J. Kanel-Belov  
**Journal:** Acta Astronautica 57 (2005) 10–21  
**Received:** 2 October 2003; **Accepted:** 1 December 2004  
**Available online:** 17 March 2005

## Abstract
Applications of a newly established principle of *topological interlocking* to different types of extraterrestrial construction are considered. Topological interlocking arises when elements of special shapes (usually convex or nearly convex, such that no stress concentration develops) are arranged in such a way that neither of them can be removed from the assembly without disturbing the neighbouring elements. Two types of extraterrestrial structures are considered. The first type represents mortar-free structures built from specially engineered interlocking bricks, called *osteomorphic bricks*. The self-adjusting property of these bricks permits erecting structures which tolerate low precision of production and assembly, thus making the proposed method suitable for in situ produced bricks and low cost assembling machinery. The structures of the second type are modular extraterrestrial bases or space ships organised in topologically interlocking assemblies.

## 1. Introduction
Extraterrestrial structures are categorized into two major classes:
1. Structures built from basic elements (bricks) manufactured in situ.
2. Modular structures based on Earth-manufactured units (assembled in orbit or on surface).

Traditional interlocking bricks require precise manufacturing and maintenance of gaps, which is difficult in the presence of lunar dust. This paper introduces topological interlocking which avoids keys and connectors and imposes low demands on manufacturing precision.

## 2. Principle of Topological Interlocking
Topological interlocking is achieved by a special choice of geometry and arrangement such that elements cannot be removed without disturbing neighbors. Examples include:
- Plate-like assemblies of tetrahedra.
- Osteomorphic bricks with curved surfaces that prevent displacement along the normal to the assembly in either direction.

## 3. Extraterrestrial construction based on osteomorphic bricks
### 3.1. Mortar free structures
Osteomorphic bricks allow structures without mortar. They are self-adjusting, relaxing the need for high-precision robotic positioning. They can be produced in situ (e.g., from regolith) via sintering in moulds (microwave/external) or selective laser sintering.

### 3.2. Sensitivity to manufacturing imperfections
The interlocking is insensitive to surface imperfections or planetary dust. Wall height variations can be compensated by adjusting the tension in post-tensioning cables.

### 3.3. Radiation protection
Mortar-free joints can create radiation leakage paths. Solutions include double-skin walls with shifted patterns or concave-convex planar faces.

## 4. Protective cladding with osteomorphic tiles
Osteomorphic bricks can be used for shielding pre-manufactured modules or spacecraft. Experiments showed that osteomorphic assemblies have high fracture resistance.

## 5. Interlocking modular structures
Modular bases or spaceships can be assembled using topological interlocking. This allows for "express assembly" on uneven ground. Hexagon-tiling based structures allow for individual module removal/servicing, whereas square-tiling based structures maintain higher global integrity if elements are lost.

## 6. Conclusion
1. Mortar-free structures can be built from self-adjusting osteomorphic bricks suitable for ISRU.
2. Protective cladding can be made from interlocking tiles.
3. Modular structures benefit from self-adjustment on uneven terrain.
4. Spacecraft can be built as flexible interlocking assemblies for long-distance travel.
