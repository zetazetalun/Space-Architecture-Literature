# Adaptive recycled plastic architecture: Vacuum-Sealed Chainmail Structures Through Computational Design

**Authors:** Yi Xu, Farzin Lotfi-Jam and Mustafa Faruki

**Key words:** recycled plastic, vacuum-sealed structures, lightweight, extreme conditions, circular economy, flexibility, geometric exploration, composed chainmail structures

## Abstract
The construction industry is a major consumer of raw materials, accounting for nearly half of global material usage annually, while generating significant waste that poses sustainability challenges. This paper explores the untapped potential of recycled plastics as a primary construction material, leveraging their lightweight, flexible, and customizable properties for advanced applications in modular chainmail systems. Through a computational workflow, the study optimizes the design, testing, and fabrication of vacuum-sealed chainmail structures composed of recycled plastic filaments, demonstrating their adaptability and structural performance for architectural use.

Key contributions include a novel methodology for integrating recycled plastic filaments into chainmail geometries, validated through 2D sectional testing, 3D shell structure generation, and physical modeling under vacuum constraints. The research identifies the rectangular chainmail configuration as the most efficient and adaptable, achieving superior deformation capacity, material efficiency, and load-bearing performance. Optimization strategies for temporary structures highlight practical deployment potential, balancing material savings, usable area, and water drainage efficiency.

The findings offer a foundation for innovative applications in extreme conditions, including disaster-prone areas, high-altitude environments, underwater platforms, and extraterrestrial habitats.

## Methodology
The research employs a multi-phase computational design workflow:
1. **Chainmail System Setup:** Establishing solid-to-gap ratios (8%) and uniform part dimensions (1mm diameter).
2. **2D Sectional Testing:** Investigating deformation behavior and curvature adaptability.
3. **3D Shell Structure Generation:** Using amplitude and frequency parameters to create complex geometries.
4. **Filtering Process:** Removing redundant geometries based on perimeter and surface area.
5. **Physical Modeling and Vacuum Constraints:** Validating outcomes using 1:20 scaled prototypes in vacuum-sealed environments.
6. **Load Analysis:** Using Karamba3D to evaluate structural integrity under dead, live, snow, and wind loads.

## Findings
- **Material Efficiency:** The rectangular chainmail system demonstrated the best material efficiency, achieving the greatest deformation capacity with the least material usage (5g per sample compared to 14g for triangular).
- **Structural Enhancement:** Vacuum-sealed chainmail systems can attain stiffness enhancements of up to 25 times compared to their unsealed state, withstanding pressures reaching 2 MPa.
- **Adaptability:** The rectangular system exhibited enhanced flexibility due to its linear form, which ensures uniform load distribution and minimizes stress concentrations.
- **Load Performance:** All tested iterations met the deflection criteria (limit of 8mm for a 2m span), with optimized shells achieving displacements as low as 0.93mm to 3.56mm depending on configuration.

## Discussion: Future Work in Space
The paper identifies **Space Debris Shielding** as a key future application. The chainmail structure could serve as a lightweight, adaptable shield. However, future deployment requires addressing radiation resistance and thermal durability through additive processes that integrate radiation-blocking materials or thermal stabilizers.
