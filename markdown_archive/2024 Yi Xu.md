# Adaptive recycled plastic architecture: Vacuum-Sealed Chainmail Structures Through Computational Design

**Authors:** Yi Xu, Farzin Lotfi-Jam and Mustafa Faruki  
**Key words:** recycled plastic, vacuum-sealed structures, lightweight, extreme conditions, circular economy, flexibility, geometric exploration, composed chainmail structures

## Abstract
The construction industry is a major consumer of raw materials, accounting for nearly half of global material usage annually, while generating significant waste that poses sustainability challenges. This paper explores the untapped potential of recycled plastics as a primary construction material, leveraging their lightweight, flexible, and customizable properties for advanced applications in modular chainmail systems. Through a computational workflow, the study optimizes the design, testing, and fabrication of vacuum-sealed chainmail structures composed of recycled plastic filaments, demonstrating their adaptability and structural performance for architectural use.

Key contributions include a novel methodology for integrating recycled plastic filaments into chainmail geometries, validated through 2D sectional testing, 3D shell structure generation, and physical modeling under vacuum constraints. The research identifies the rectangular chainmail configuration as the most efficient and adaptable, achieving superior deformation capacity, material efficiency, and load-bearing performance. Optimization strategies for temporary structures highlight practical deployment potential, balancing material savings, usable area, and water drainage efficiency.

The findings offer a foundation for innovative applications in extreme conditions, including disaster-prone areas, high-altitude environments, underwater platforms, and extraterrestrial habitats. These applications leverage the lightweight, adaptable, and durable properties of recycled plastics and modular chainmail systems, bridging the gap between waste management and high-performance design while addressing unique challenges in harsh and resource-constrained environments.

## Introduction
This study specifically examines the architectural adaptability of vacuum-sealed chainmail structures, a system demonstrated to possess variable stiffness, flexibility, and outstanding mechanical properties. The primary aim is to examine how this experimental material can be modified and incorporated into architectural structures, fulfilling requirements for structural integrity, sustainability, and practical application.

## Methodology
### Computational Design Workflow
The procedure commences with the Chainmail System Setup, establishing essential parameters including solid-to-gap ratios and uniform part dimensions. 
- **Solid-to-gap ratio (Rsg):** Defined as $Rsg = \frac{Vs}{Vg + Vs}$, where $Vs$ is the total solid area and $Vg$ is the total gap area. A ratio of 8% was used to enhance flexibility and interlocking.
- **Testing:** 2D Sectional Testing (assessing curvature using $y(x) = A \cdot \sin(\frac{2\pi f}{L}x)$), 3D Shell Structure Generation, and Physical Modeling under vacuum constraints.
- **Load Analysis:** Evaluated using Karamba3D for dead, live, snow, and wind loads based on SIA 262 and SIA 261 standards.

## Findings and Results
### Chainmail Geometry Performance
- **Rectangular configuration:** Demonstrated the best material efficiency and greatest deformation capacity. It exhibited the lowest overall weight (5g for a sample compared to 14g for triangular).
- **Triangular configuration:** Increased stiffness but reduced flexibility due to rigid connections.
- **Circular configuration:** Showed diminished performance due to overlapping material and uneven stress distribution.

### Structural Performance
- **Stiffness Enhancement:** Vacuum-sealed topologies attain stiffness enhancements of up to 25 times.
- **Pressure Capacity:** Systems demonstrated remarkable mechanical qualities under pressures reaching 2 MPa.
- **Load Analysis Result:** An optimized 4m² shell structure supported calculated total loads (TL = DL + LL + SL + WL) with a maximum displacement of only 3.56 mm, well within the 8 mm permissible deflection limit.

## Discussion & Future Work
The modular nature of the chainmail structure makes it suitable for deployment in remote locations where transportation costs are high. 
- **Space Debris Shielding:** Potential to serve as a lightweight, adaptable shield.
- **Extraterrestrial Habitats:** Future work must address performance under cosmic radiation and extreme temperature fluctuations. Additive processes could integrate radiation-blocking materials.

## Conclusion
The study establishes a foundation for scalable applications of adaptive materials in sustainable construction by integrating computational design and material circularity. The structural adaptability and recyclability of vacuum-sealed chainmail fabrics expand potential applications in extreme environments, including underwater habitats and extraterrestrial construction.
