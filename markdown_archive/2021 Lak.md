# A new deployable pantographic lunar habitat

**Authors:** Amin Lak, Maziar Asefi
**Journal:** Acta Astronautica 192 (2022) 351–367
**DOI:** 10.1016/j.actaastro.2021.12.049

## Abstract
Deployable lunar structures are proposed as a solution for cargo volume limitations. This study introduces a habitat consisting of linear and planar elements connected through movable joints using scissor-like elements (SLE). The design combines rigid plates with a membrane to provide environmental protection and reduce EVA time through a simple deployment mechanism.

## 1. Introduction
The study classifies lunar habitats into Class I (rigid), Class II (deployable), and Class III (ISRU-derived). It focuses on Class II, highlighting the advantages of low packaging volume and the potential of hybrid structures that combine inflatable membranes with rigid protective panels.

## 2. Design Considerations
Key hazards include micrometeoroids, radiation, and vacuum. Design criteria emphasize simple mechanical connections, common components, modularity, and stable footprints.

## 4. Scissor-Hinge Systems
The design utilizes three basic Scissor Unit (SU) types: translational, polar, and angulated. Angulated units allow for the formation of closed-loop deployable structures.

## 5. Design

### 5.1. Habitat Dimensions
Based on NASA standards, a minimum net habitable volume (NHV) of 25 m³/crewmember is recommended. For a 180-day mission with 4 crewmembers, the study aims for a total floor area of 45.6 m².

### 5.3. Mechanism Design
The structure consists of:
- Two loops of angulated SLE units.
- A central structural core (1.10 m diameter).
- Modified Polar Scissor-Like Element (MP-SLE) units for the roof.
- Rigid panels for shielding and flooring.
- A pressure barrier (multi-layer inflatable membrane).

### 5.3.9. Material Selection
- **Rigid Panels:** Carbon composite for radiation shielding and thermal insulation.
- **Pressure Barrier:** Multi-layer membrane including MLI thermal protection, Vectran or Kevlar restraint layers, and redundant pressure bladders.

## 6. Conclusions
The proposed system provides approximately 170 m³ of internal volume for four astronauts. It combines quick deployment with the protection characteristics of rigid structures, significantly reducing the volume needed during transit.
