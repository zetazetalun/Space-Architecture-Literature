# A new deployable pantographic lunar habitat

**Authors:** Amin Lak, Maziar Asefi
**Journal:** Acta Astronautica 192 (2022) 351–367
**DOI:** 10.1016/j.actaastro.2021.12.049

## Abstract
Deployable lunar structures address the volumetric limitations of cargo flights. This paper introduces a habitat using linear and planar elements connected via movable joints, utilizing modified polar and angulated scissor-like elements (SLE). An experimental study assessed deployment strategies and dimensional properties for a four-person crew.

## 1. Introduction
Habitat design approaches range from rigid hard-shell modules (Class I) to deployable units (Class II) and ISRU-based structures (Class III). Deployable structures are preferred due to low packaging volume.

## 2. Design Considerations
Key hazards include micrometeoroids, radiation, and vacuum. Design criteria emphasize simple mechanical connections, modularity, and common components for ease of maintenance and replacement.

### Table 1: Different types of class-II structures

| Inflatable | Multi-layered membrane inflated after landing. |

## 4. Scissor-hinge systems
Planar scissor structural mechanisms (SSM) are formed by connecting scissor units (SU). Types include translational, polar, and angulated units defined by the pivot point and bar geometry.

## 5. Design

### 5.1 Habitat Dimensions
Based on NASA standards, a minimum net habitable volume (NHV) of 25 m³/crewmember is recommended. For a 180-day mission, 40 m³/crewmember is suggested, leading to a total needed floor area of 45.6 m² for four people.

### 5.3 Mechanism Design
The proposed structure uses:
- Two identical loops of angulated SLE units.
- A central structural core.
- Modified polar SLE (MP-SLE) units for the roof.
- Rigid panels for shielding and flooring.

### 5.3.9 Material selection
- **Shielding:** Carbon composite for rigid panels.
- **Membrane:** Multi-layer including Vectran or Kevlar restraint layers, redundant pressure bladders, and MLI thermal protection.

### 5.3.10 Actuating mechanism
Deployment is driven by a central telescopic jack that slides the movable intermediate connection elements (MICE) along the z-axis.

## 6. Conclusions
The design provides a nominal internal volume of ~170 m³ for a 180-day mission. The hybrid approach combines the quick deployment of inflatable structures with the protection of rigid systems.
