# SUITABILITY OF SILICONE FOR SOFT-ROBOTIC EXPLORATION OF TERRESTRIAL AND EXTRATERRESTRIAL OCEAN WORLDS

**Authors:** A. Nuncio Zuniga, W. Fink  
**Journal:** Space Science and Technology, 2023, Vol. 29, No. 3, pp. 47–56  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.15407/knit2023.03.047

## ABSTRACT
This work revisits relevant mechanical and chemical properties of silicone rubber — Ecoflex in this study — to assess its suitability and viability for use in soft-robotic explorer construction and subsequent deployment and as a sealant for communication beacons, sensor pods, and other electronics in extreme planetary liquid environments, such as the depths of Earth’s oceans and extraterrestrial ocean worlds. 

## 1. INTRODUCTION
Subsurface oceans on planetary bodies known as ocean worlds, such as Jupiter’s moon Europa and Saturn’s moons Titan and Enceladus, are prime candidate locales for astrobiological exploration. Rigid-frame underwater vehicles have limited ability to navigate small apertures. Soft-robotics offer a more effective paradigm for liquid environment exploration. This paper experimentally revisits the suitability of silicone rubber for constructing soft-robotic explorers and sealing associated electronics.

## 2. MATERIALS AND METHODS
### 2.1. Ecoflex Overview
Ecoflex™ 00-30 was chosen as a stand-in for other rubbers/polymers. It is a silicone rubber made by mixing two compounds (A and B). It cures in approximately 4 hours at room temperature.

### 2.2. Experimental Setup
- **Strain Testing:** Rectangular prisms were cast at ratios of 20:80, 50:50, and 80:20 (A:B). Testing occurred at -5°C to 0°C, ~22.4°C, and 39°C to 40°C.
- **Low Temperature Testing:** Samples were tested in freezers at -7.5°C, -20°C, -80°C, and submerged in liquid nitrogen (-196°C).
- **Re-bonding Ability:** Cured Ecoflex was placed in molds with fresh liquid Ecoflex to test the strength of the interface.
- **Chemical/Electrical Testing:** Resistance to dissolution in salt water (3.5% and saturated) and mineral oil (hydrocarbon stand-in) was tested. Electrical non-conductivity was tested by submerging a Raspberry Pi directly in liquid silicone before curing.

## 4. RESULTS
### 4.1. Strain and Temperature
Increased amounts of compound A led to a stiffer material. Strain results were largely temperature-independent within the measured range (-5°C to 40°C).

### 4.2. Stretching to Failure
At a 50:50 mix ratio, prisms reached a breaking point at an elongation percent of 553.3 ± 20.5%.

### 4.3. Low Temperature Results
- **-20°C:** Remained pliable.
- **-80°C:** All tested ratios froze but returned to pre-freezing pliability once thawed.
- **-196°C:** Samples shattered but recovered pliability after returning to room temperature.

### 4.4. Re-bonding and Sealant Performance
- **Re-bonding:** Successfully fused new silicone to pieces up to 6 days old, creating an indistinguishable interface suitable for additive manufacturing.
- **Electrical:** Encapsulated electronics (Raspberry Pi) operated perfectly without short-circuiting.
- **Hermeticity:** Communication beacons remained functional and water-tight in saturated salt water for at least 21 days.

## 5. DISCUSSION & CONCLUSIONS
Ecoflex 00-30 is an excellent encapsulation and construction option for temperatures above -50°C. While suitable for Earth-based and water-based extraterrestrial oceans (e.g., Europa/Enceladus subsurface), the extremely low temperatures of Titan’s hydrocarbon lakes (-179°C) would require alternative polymers that remain flexible at those ranges. However, its additive manufacturing potential through re-bonding and its insulation properties make it a robust candidate for extreme environment soft-robotics.
