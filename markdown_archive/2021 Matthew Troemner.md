# Design and Analysis of 3D-Printable Thin-Shell Dome Structures for Extraterrestrial Habitation

**Authors:** Matthew Troemner, Elham Ramyar, Benton Johnson, and Gianluca Cusatis
**Venue:** Earth and Space 2021 (ASCE)

## Abstract
Extreme environmental conditions and novel construction techniques dictate the form of Martian habitats. This paper presents the design and analysis of a dome-shaped Martian habitat developed for NASA’s 3D-Printed Habitat Challenge. The structure features a composite hemispheric-parabolic dome optimized for self-weight and environmental loads, intended to be 3D-printed on an inflatable pressure vessel using 'Marscrete' (Martian regolith and sulfur). The study assesses structural performance under wind, gravity, sand loading, and meteorite impact.

## Introduction
Martian missions require semi-permanent structures utilizing in situ resource utilization (ISRU) and automated fabrication. NASA's 3D-Printed Habitat Challenge (2015) set requirements for a 93 m² living space for four astronauts for one year, including ECLSS and MEP systems.

## Design Basis
- **Form:** Internal hemispherical dome with an outer parabolic dome.
- **Material:** Marscrete (quasi-brittle behavior).
- **Optimization:** Thickness ranges from 21 to 108 cm with internal cross beams to ensure compressive stress under load.

## Construction Method
1. Site preparation by autonomous rovers.
2. 3D-printing the foundation with an articulated arm.
3. Deployment and inflation of a collapsible pressure vessel.
4. Printing over the pressure vessel using a 'crawler' robot to avoid large printer size requirements.
5. Deflation of the vessel to allow for independent thermal deformation.

## Environment and Loading
- **Gravity:** 3.71 m/s² (38% of Earth).
- **Wind Load:** Calculated at 9.79 Pa (increased to 60.0 Pa for analysis).
- **Sand Buildup:** Static pressure load of 2,780 Pa (unbalanced).
- **Impact:** Assessment of meteorite risk.

## Load Analysis
Finite element static analysis showed the structural response remained in the elastic region with deformation less than 1.0 mm under all typical Martian load combinations (ASCE-7).

## Meteorite Impact Performance
- **Critical Impact:** 7,390 g mass at 6.29 km/sec velocity.
- **Entry Mass:** 12,022 g (accounting for ablation).
- **Probability:** Hazardous impact probability estimated at 6.9 &#215; 10⁻⁹ for a one-year mission.

## Proposed Layout
- **Concept:** Inspired by 'tiny houses' and HI-SEAS.
- **Central Area:** Round common area with a transparent oculus.
- **Zones:** Two semicircular regions (Public: kitchen/workspaces; Private: bedrooms/lavatory).
- **Access:** Personnel airlock and MMSEV docking port.

## Conclusions
The design surpasses structural and functional requirements for a Martian habitat. The unique hemispheric-parabolic dome eliminates the need for printed supports and performs well under environmental and meteorite loads.
