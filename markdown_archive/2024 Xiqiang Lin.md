# Construction Technology of the Curved ‘Mars House’ Digital Hotel Project in China Using Concrete 3D Printing

**Authors:** Xiqiang Lin, Hailong Wang, Xiaoyan Sun, Wael Kikhia, Zhennan Wu, Lu Lan, and Jiawei Zhao  
**Publication:** 3DcP 2023, STAM, pp. 241–250, 2024.  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-64269-2_30

## Abstract
3D printing technology is one of the most likely construction technologies to be used when building extraterrestrial bases in the future. The Mars 1 Base project in Gansu Province, China simulates the Martian landscape and uses concrete 3D printing technology to construct houses. In June 2022, the 3D printing construction of five Martian hotel buildings began. The 3D-printed hotel is egg-shaped, 9 m high, with a floor area of 46 square meters. Components are 3D printed in a factory and transported to the site for assembly.

## 1. Introduction
Research into Mars exploration and habitat development aims to improve the feasibility of long-term human inhabitation. Concrete 3D printing presents a promising approach due to its potential for in-situ resource utilization and rapid construction in extreme environments.

## 2. Architectural and Structural Design
The egg-shaped structure was chosen for its ability to withstand harsh conditions (strong winds, radiation, and temperature differences). 

### Structural Scheme:
- **Outer Shell:** Concrete 3D printed wall acting as a permanent mould.
- **Reinforcement:** Steel mesh (horizontal and vertical) placed inside the mould.
- **Core:** C40 self-compacting concrete poured into the 3D-printed mould.
- **Segmentation:** The 9 m structure is divided into four rings for factory printing.

## 3. 3D Printing Construction Technology and Process

### 3.1 Process Flow
1. Model refinement and design.
2. Material/Equipment preparation.
3. Component printing in factory.
4. Pouring and embedding lifting points.
5. Hoisting and transportation to site.
6. On-site assembly and decoration.

### 3.2 Technical Parameters
- **Printers:** 2 industrial-grade concrete 3D printers.
- **Dimensions:** 14.8 m × 8.6 m × 5.5 m.
- **Layer Height:** 15 mm.
- **Print Speed:** 120 mm/s.
- **Extrusion Width:** 40 mm.
- **Total Material Volume:** 28.36 m³ (10.6 m³ printed, 17.76 m³ filling).

### 3.3 Material Performance (Concrete Mix)
| Raw Materials | Mix Proportion (%) |

| OPC42.5 cement | 38 |

| Admixture | 4 |

**Performance Metrics:**
- **Workability:** 200 mm
- **Setting Time:** 41 min
- **Compressive Strength (Standard Mould):** 47.9 MPa
- **Compressive Strength (Printed Sample):** 40.3 MPa (16% reduction)
- **Interlayer Bonding Strength:** 2.7 MPa
- **Permeability Grade:** P12

## 4. On-Site Assembly
Components were transported 1800 km to the Gobi Desert site. A large crane was used to assemble the four ring-shaped sections. The assembly of the four structures took one day to complete per house. Structural columns were formed by welding reinforcement cages at reserved positions and pouring mortar.

## 5. Summary
The project demonstrates that concrete 3D printing significantly improves construction speed and quality control through factory prefabrication. It serves as a technical baseline for future additive intelligent construction in extreme environments.
