# Overall Material Flow Scheme Design of Controlled Ecological Life Support System for Lunar Base

## Abstract
Recently, the Moon has once again become the focus of the aerospace field. The lunar base is likely to be the next long-term extraterrestrial base for humans after the near-Earth orbit space station. This paper, based on fully inheriting the physicochemical regeneration technologies of the present near-Earth orbit space station, gradually introduced biological components such as plants, microalgae, and microorganisms in 4 stages to establish an upgraded CELSS with a total material closure degree of 98%.

## Introduction
The life support system is the fundamental premise of manned space missions. A controlled ecological life support system (CELSS) can achieve the simultaneous regeneration of oxygen, water, and food, improve the material closure of the system, and provide psychological support by creating a green and fresh natural environment. This study focuses on the transition from physicochemical systems to bio-regenerative systems on the Moon.

## Methods
### Basic Concepts and Rationale of CELSS
The concept is based on the ecological principles of "producer–consumer–decomposer," constructing an artificial ecosystem of "human–(animal) plant–microbe–environment."

### Application Strategy (4 Stages)
1.  **Stage I—Prophase lunar base**: Physicochemical regeneration technology dominant; small-scale vegetable cultivation (~1 m²).
2.  **Stage II—Preliminary lunar base**: Expanded vegetable production (~4.18 m²); introduction of microalgae and wastewater bioreactors.
3.  **Stage III—Medium lunar base**: Introduction of grain crops (total area ~17.55 m²); oxygen regeneration entirely through photosynthesis.
4.  **Stage IV—Upgrade lunar base**: Further expansion of plant components (total area ~25.68 m²); solid waste biological treatment introduced; food closure >60%.

## Technical Configuration of Subsystems

| ID | Subsystem | Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | CO2 removal | Adsorption or absorption of CO2 from the atmosphere |
| 2 | Electrolysis oxygen generation | O2 generation by H2O electrolysis |
| 3 | CO2 reduction | Chemical catalytic process (Sabatier) producing CH4 and H2O |
| 4 | Urine processing | Distillation for water recovery |
| 5 | Water recovery | Deep purification (catalytic oxidation, ion exchange) |
| 6 | Food–vegetable production | Plant photosynthesis for fresh vegetables and O2 |
| 7 | Food–grain production | Photosynthesis for staple food and O2 |
| 8 | Solid waste dewatering | Drying and crushing of inedible plant parts |
| 9 | Wastewater bioreactor | Microbial metabolism for pretreatment and nitrogen oxidation |
| 10 | Solid waste bioreactor | Biological fermentation into organic fertilizer and CO2 |
| 11 | Brine/feces processing | Membrane distillation for further water recovery |
| 12 | Microalgae reactor | CO2 fixation, O2 regeneration, and algal protein food |

## Results

### Material Closure Performance
- **Stage I (Prophase)**: Total closure 83.4%; Food closure 0.8%.
- **Stage II (Preliminary)**: Total closure 95.2%; Food closure 3.4%.
- **Stage III (Medium)**: Total closure 96.9%; Food closure 40.3%.
- **Stage IV (Upgrade)**: Total closure 98%; Food closure 62.7%.

### Water and Gas Circulation
- In Stage IV, the total material closure degree reaches 98%.
- Water recovery rate in urine and feces reaches approximately 98% and 90%, respectively.
- Atmospheric regeneration rate in Stage II is approximately 1.59 times human metabolism.

## Discussion
Water recovery is highlighted as the critical factor for system closure. The paper suggests an application sequence where fresh vegetables take precedence over grain crops due to lower energy consumption and higher psychological/nutritional benefits per unit of investment. Microalgae are noted for their high photosynthetic efficiency and environmental adaptability.

## Conclusion
This study establishes a reference for the phased construction of lunar base life support systems. Future work needs to verify these systems in actual space environments (space stations) and integrate emerging technologies like biosynthesis and in situ resource utilization (ISRU).
