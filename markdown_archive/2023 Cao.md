# Recent advances on ISRU technologies and study of microgravity impact on blood cells for deep space exploration

**Authors:** Giacomo Cao, Alessandro Concas, Roberto Orrù, Roberta Licheri, Elisa Sani, Aldo Dell’Oro, Giacomo Fais, Cristina Manis, Alessia Manca, Giuseppe Uras, Pierluigi Caboni, Antonio Mario Locci, Alberto Cincotti, Nicola Lai, Terenzio Congiu, Gavino Faa, Massimo Pisu, Gavin Brelstaff and Antonella Pantaleo

**Published:** 19 July 2023
**Journal:** Frontiers in Space Technologies
**DOI:** 10.3389/frspt.2023.1146461

## Abstract
The long-term solution to problems like overcrowding and resource depletion could be overcome through space colonization and the exploitation of extraterrestrial natural resources. This work analyzes advancements in technology for deep space exploration, specifically focusing on: 
1. Self-propagating high-temperature synthesis (SHS) for fabricating composite ceramics from in situ regolith for construction.
2. ISRU processes using Martian atmosphere and regolith to produce oxygen, water, food (microalgae), and fuels.
3. Sintering of lunar regolith for thermal energy storage.
4. Impact of microgravity on red blood cells.

## 1. Introduction
In-Situ Resource Utilization (ISRU) and In Situ Fabrication and Repair (ISFR) are essential for providing life support consumables, propellants, construction materials, and energy to extraterrestrial crews. This paper reviews the work of an Italian task force between 2012 and 2023.

## 2. Manufacturing physical assets in extraterrestrial environments
A new process based on SHS reactions was developed for the fabrication of composite ceramics. The process stages include:
1. Regolith extraction.
2. Enrichment (e.g., iron oxide via magnetic separation).
3. Mixing with aluminum powder (carried from Earth).
4. Ignition of self-propagating high-temperature combustion reactions (generally 1,800–2,000 K).

### Findings
- Compressive strength of SHS samples: 25.8–27.2 MPa (higher than geothermite-based processes).
- Gravity affects combustion velocity; bottom-side ignition under terrestrial conditions leads to higher velocities due to free convection.
- Microgravity experiments performed during parabolic flights (Airbus-300) validated the process stages.

## 4. ISRU process to sustain human missions on Mars
A synergistic combination of ISRU and ECLSS is proposed to ensure crew self-sufficiency. The system includes a physico-chemical section (producing O2, water, and propellants) and a biological section (greenhouses/photobioreactors for biomass).

## 6. Optical characterization of lunar regolith simulant
Sintered regolith is investigated for Thermal Energy Storage (TES). 
- Simulant used: JSC-1A.
- Process: Spark Plasma Sintering (SPS) at 700°C and 900°C.
- Results: Relative densities reached 86% (at 700°C) and 98% (at 900°C).
- Optical properties: Sintered samples showed superior solar absorptance compared to powder form, making them efficient solar absorbers for cavity-like receivers.

## 8. Concluding remarks
The study concludes that SHS reactions can successfully produce structural elements from regolith. Integrated ISRU-ECLSS systems are capable of producing necessary consumables, and sintered regolith blocks are suitable for energy harvesting architectures.
