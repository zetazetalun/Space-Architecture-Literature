# Wettability characteristics of lunar soil particles from the Chang’E-5 mission: A single-particle surface analysis

**Journal:** Applied Surface Science  
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.apsusc.2025.163331](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.apsusc.2025.163331)  
**Publication Date:** 2025  

## Abstract
Lunar base construction is a strategic focus involving in situ resource utilization (ISRU) and extrusion-based technologies. This study analyzes the wetting dynamics between lunar soil and binding agents (water or resin) using a custom-developed apparatus and numerical simulations. Results indicate that lunar particles are hydrophobic, a characteristic caused by unique surface morphologies resulting from space weathering. The findings provide empirical support for extraterrestrial construction.

## Experimental Methods
- **Sample:** Chang’E-5 lunar soil (CE5C0400).
- **Characterization:** Field emission scanning electron microscopy (FESEM) and energy dispersive spectroscopy (EDS) were used for surface morphology and composition analysis.
- **Measurement:** A custom system combining a liquid evaporator (LE) with an ultra-deep field microscope (UDFM) was developed to measure contact angles on micron-sized particles.
- **Simulation:** Two-phase flow phase-field method in COMSOL Multiphysics combined with MATLAB-generated surface models.

## Results and Discussion

### Surface Morphology
FESEM analysis revealed angular shapes, glassy condensates, porous structures, and mineral fragments. These features result from meteorite impacts and space weathering, contributing to high surface roughness.

### Numerical Simulation Results
- **Model:** The Cassie-Baxter model accurately described the wetting behavior.
- **Surface Tension:** The estimated surface tension of the lunar soil particle was 8.46 mN/m.
- **Resin Interaction:** The simulated apparent contact angle with epoxy resin was 102.40, with a Young contact angle of 96.18.

## Conclusion
The study confirms the hydrophobic nature of lunar soil particles and validates a novel framework for characterizing wettability at the micron scale. This provides critical data for optimizing binders in ISRU-based additive manufacturing for lunar habitats.
