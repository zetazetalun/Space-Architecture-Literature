# Energy Life Cycle in Extraterrestrial Construction Projects: Is Building Underground an Option?

**Authors:** Hashem Izadi Moud, Peyman Rahgozar, Sebastian T. Weber, Craig Capano, and Ian Flood
**Conference:** Earth and Space 2021
**Publisher:** ASCE

## ABSTRACT
Making life multi-planetary requires substantial knowledge of building structures in extraterrestrial bodies, where natural resources are scarce, human resources are expensive-to-utilize, and energy sources are vastly different from the Earth. Solar energy is expected to be the primary source for extraterrestrial construction projects. This paper presents energy cycle flowcharts for construction projects on Earth and beyond and argues about the expensive and unrealistic nature of building underground due to high energy consumption.

## INTRODUCTION
Future shelters must protect occupants from extreme conditions including lack of atmosphere, meteorite impacts, lack of magnetic fields, and radiation. Colonization is proposed as a three-phase program: (1) short-term missions, (2) slightly longer stays, and (3) permanent colonization. While modular structures can be shipped from Earth, the cost of transportation makes large-scale colonization dependent on ISRU and local energy sources.

## ENERGY IN CONSTRUCTION
### On Earth
Construction Management (CM) focuses on the "iron triangle": time, cost, and scope. Energy is rarely a priority due to the abundance of cheap fossil fuels. This access allows for massive infrastructure like underground subways and skyscrapers.

### In Space
Multiplanetary life requires a reliable energy source. Transportation of fossil fuels from Earth is cost-prohibitive ($15,000 - $45,000 per kg). Solar energy is the most common form available but faces challenges such as low intensity and long nights on Mars.

## CASE STUDY: BUILDING UNDERGROUND ON MARS
The study estimates the energy needed to build a small underground room requiring 600 cubic meters (m³) of space. Accounting for soil stability, ramps for machinery, and backfill, the total volume of soil to be removed is calculated at 1800 m³.

### Technical Calculations
- **Excavation Energy (Earth base):** 12.09 MJ/m³ for Silty Sand.
- **Total Energy for 1800 m³:** 21,762 MJ or 6,045 kWh.
- **Solar Radiance on Mars:** Yearly mean is ~200 W/m².
- **Solar Panel Efficiency:** Assumed at 30%.
- **Harvesting Capacity:** ~655.20 Wh/m²/day.

### Results
Using a 400 m² solar field (yielding 264 kWh per Martian day), it would take approximately **29 Martian Days (Sols)** just to harvest the energy required for excavation of a single 600 m³ room.

## DISCUSSION AND CONCLUSION
Underground construction using large-scale equipment like Tunnel Boring Machines (TBM) is energy-intensive. Without fossil fuels, developing cities beyond Earth requires a combination of:
1. Ultra-efficient and sustainable design of structures.
2. Smart and resource-efficient planning.
3. Energy-efficient construction techniques and machinery.

Building underground might not be a feasible option for initial large-scale colonization due to these extreme energy constraints.
