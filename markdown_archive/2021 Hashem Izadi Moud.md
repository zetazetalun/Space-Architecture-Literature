# Energy Life Cycle in Extraterrestrial Construction Projects: Is Building Underground an Option?

**Authors:** Hashem Izadi Moud, Peyman Rahgozar, Sebastian T. Weber, Craig Capano, and Ian Flood
**Published in:** Earth and Space 2021

## ABSTRACT
Making life multi-planetary requires substantial knowledge of building structures in extraterrestrial bodies, where natural resources are scarce, human resources are expensive-to-utilize, and energy sources are vastly different from the Earth. On Earth, fossil fuels have been the main source of energy for construction projects. In other extraterrestrial bodies, including Mars and Moon, easy-to-access fossil fuels are non-existent. Solar energy is, and probably will be, the primary source of energy accessible to extraterrestrial construction projects. This calls for ultra-efficient and sustainable design of structures and buildings, smart and resource-efficient planning of projects, and use of energy-efficient and smart construction techniques and machinery. This paper presents two energy cycle flowcharts for construction projects on Earth and beyond, which show the vastly different energy cycles in these environments. It further presents arguments about the expensive and unrealistic nature of building underground, as one of the key ideas for building beyond Earth.

## INTRODUCTION
Future shelters will have to be designed to adequately protect occupants from extreme environmental conditions; including no atmospheres, meteorite impacts, lack of protective magnetic fields, extreme temperature changes, and harmful radiation. Colonization could happen in a three-phase development program: (1) temporary short-term missions, (2) slightly longer stays, and (3) permanent colonization in structures built for long-term living.

## ENERGY IN CONSTRUCTION PROJECTS
### Energy on Earth
On Earth, energy is an integrated part of building processes but is often considered a low priority due to the widespread availability and cheap cost of fossil fuels and electricity. Decision-making between above-ground and underground structures is usually restrained by client needs rather than energy availability.

### Energy in Space
In space, transporting materials and energy from Earth is extremely expensive ($15,000 - $45,000 per kg to Mars). Nuclear batteries and reactors are options but research is immature. Solar energy is the most viable option despite drawbacks like low intensity and long nights. 

## CASE STUDY: BUILDING A SMALL STRUCTURE ON MARS
The study estimates energy needed for an underground room of 600 cubic meters (m³).
- **Total soil removed:** Room volume (600 m³) + Ramps volume (2 * 300 m³) + Backfill (2 * 300 m³) = 1800 m³.
- **Energy required for excavation:** 12.09 MJ/m³ (assumed based on Earth data for silty sand).
- **Total Energy:** 1800 m³ * 12.09 MJ/m³ = 21,762 MJ or 6,045 kWh.

### Solar Energy Availability on Mars
- **Mean Solar Radiance:** ~200 W/m².
- **Daylight duration:** ~12.13 hours.
- **Solar Panel Efficiency:** 30% (trackable panels with 90% useful daylight factor).
- **Energy Collection:** 655.20 Wh/m²/day.
- **Field Requirement:** A 400 m² solar field collects ~264 kWh per Martian day (Sol).
- **Result:** It takes approximately 29 Martian Days to harvest the energy needed strictly for soil excavation for this small structure.

## DISCUSSION AND CONCLUSION
Building underground on Mars is currently impractical due to the extravagant energy demands of excavation. Without fossil fuels, large-scale construction equipment like Tunnel Boring Machines (TBM) faces massive operational hurdles. The study concludes that extraterrestrial development must rely on: 
1. Ultra-efficient sustainable design.
2. Smart resource-efficient planning.
3. Energy-efficient construction techniques and machinery.
