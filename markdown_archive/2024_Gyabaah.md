# 3D printable regolith filled shape memory vitrimer composite for extraterrestrial application

**Authors:** Kingsley Yeboah Gyabaah, John Konlan, Obed Tetteh, Maryam Jahan, Enrique Jackson, Patrick Mensah, and Guoqiang Li  
**Journal:** Journal of Composite Materials  
**Date:** 2024, Vol. 58(24) 2639–2654  
**DOI:** 10.1177/00219983241274544

## Abstract
This study investigates a neoteric approach in manufacturing lunar regolith-filled shape memory vitrimer (SMV) composites for extraterrestrial applications. A SMV with robust mechanical properties was combined with locally available lunar regolith to form a composite material. Synthesis explored conventional methods as well as 3D printing (LCD-based vat photopolymerization). Compressive strength, flexural strength, and impact resistance were tested at room and elevated temperatures. Results suggest SMV-regolith composites are promising for lunar base applications due to simple manufacturing, mechanical properties, and low energy consumption.

## Introduction
Establishing extraterrestrial bases on the Moon requires protection from meteorite impacts, radiation, and extreme temperature fluctuations. The high cost of shipping materials from Earth necessitates In-Situ Resource Utilization (ISRU). Lunar regolith can be consolidated via sintering or by using polymer binders. This research introduces vitrimers—recyclable and self-healing polymers—as a matrix for regolith composites to improve sustainability and waste management in lunar construction.

## Experimental Methods
- **Materials:** Bisphenol A glycerolate dimethacrylate (BPAGMA) resin and lunar regolith simulant (LRS).
- **Formulations:** Regolith weight percentages of 10%, 20%, 30%, and 40%.
- **Manufacturing:** 
  - Conventional UV-curing and post-thermal curing.
  - 3D printing using an Anycubic Photon Mono (LCD-based 3D printer).
- **Testing:** Compressive testing (MTS Q150), Low-velocity impact (Instron Dynatup 8250 HV), 3-point bending (MTS Alliance RF/10), and self-healing evaluation at 150°C.

### 3D Printing Outcomes
3D printed samples achieved a compressive strength of 139.16 MPa and a tensile strength of 13.99 MPa at room temperature. The shape fixity ratio was 68.88% and the shape recovery ratio was 88.20%.

## Conclusion
The study demonstrates that SMV-regolith composites are viable for lunar construction. They offer recyclability, self-healing, and high mechanical performance. While LCD-based printing currently limits sample size, the material chemistry supports automated additive manufacturing using in-situ resources.
