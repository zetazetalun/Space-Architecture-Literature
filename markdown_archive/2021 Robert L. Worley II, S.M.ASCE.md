# Investigation into Measuring Low Levels of Cohesion of Regolith Simulants

**Authors:** Robert L. Worley II, Laura Obregon, Lane Feldeisen, Brandon Nimberger, Colin Palmer, and Mandar M. Dewoolkar

**Affiliation:** College of Engineering and Mathematical Sciences, Dept. of Civil and Environmental Engineering, Univ. of Vermont, Burlington, VT

## Abstract
Lunar and Martian regoliths are similar to terrestrial soils in that they appear granular but are expected to contain a small amount of cohesion. Cohesion in extraterrestrial regoliths poses challenges for future space operations such as in situ resource utilization (ISRU). This study evaluated two methods—vertical cut and simple direct shear testing—to assess their efficacy in measuring small amounts of cohesion in lunar simulants JSC-1A and GRC-3. Findings indicate that vertical cut testing cohesion estimates for JSC-1A at relative densities 0%–80% ranged between 0.115 and 0.971 kPa and for GRC-3 ranged between 0.190 and 1.872 kPa. Vertical cut testing provided monotonically increasing cohesion estimates with increased relative density, whereas simple direct shear testing did not establish a clear trend.

## Introduction
Understanding surface regolith properties is critical for landing, walking, and utilizing lunar resources for habitat development. Soil shear strength is expressed by the Mohr-Coulomb failure criterion:

$$\tau_f = \sigma \tan \phi + c$$ (Eq. 1)

Where $\tau_f$ is shear stress at failure, $\sigma$ is normal stress, $\phi$ is the internal friction angle, and $c$ is cohesion. Cohesion in lunar regolith is estimated at 0.1-1.0 kPa, likely due to mechanical interlocking of irregular particles.

### Vertical Cut Test
Cohesion is determined based on the 'critical height' ($H_c$) at which an unsupported vertical slope fails. The relationship is expressed as:

$$c = \frac{1}{2} \gamma H_c$$ (Eq. 2)

Where $\gamma$ is the unit weight of soil.

## Conclusions
Vertical cut testing provided cohesion estimates for JSC-1A that matched previous research and Apollo model estimates. The results support the hypothesis that regolith cohesion increases with relative density due to mechanical interlocking. Vertical cut testing is preferred over simple direct shear for estimating low levels of cohesion in regolith simulants due to higher resolution and established trends.
