# Determining the yield stress of a Biopolymer-bound Soil Composite for extrusion-based 3D printing applications\n\n## Abstract\nBiopolymer-bound Soil Composite (BSC) is a novel class of construction materials with potential use in extraterrestrial infrastructure development and more sustainable construction on Earth. Little is known about the rheological properties of this new material and its suitability for extrusion-based 3D printing (E3DP). This paper presents methods for testing BSC yield stress and proposes hydrodynamic and frictional yield stress models for predicting the static and dynamic yield stress of BSC mixtures. Using experimental results, a soil volume fraction range for BSC is identified that may be suitable for E3DP based on extrudability and shape stability requirements.\n\n## 1. Introduction\nExtrusion-based 3D printing (E3DP) has emerged as a viable alternative to form-based construction. BSC mixtures are being explored as an alternative to cementitious materials for sustainable construction and extraterrestrial applications via In Situ Resource Utilization (ISRU). BSC mixtures in this paper consist of lunar regolith simulant, bovine blood proteins as the binder, and deionized water.\n\n## 2. BSC-E3DP mixture design considerations\nBSC strength originates from biopolymer cross-links binding soil particles. Design must balance pumpability/extrudability in the wet state with compressive strength in the dry state. Research limits BSC soil volume fraction (phi_BSC) to <80% of the soil's maximum packing fraction (phi_mp) to ensure pumpability.\n\n## 3. Materials and methods\nSeven BSC mixtures using JSC-1A Lunar regolith simulant, bovine blood proteins (AP920), and deionized water were evaluated. Testing involved a vane geometry on an ARES-G2 Rheometer using Steady Stress Sweep (SS) and Constant Shear Rate (CSR) tests. Extrusion was tested using 9.5 mm and 25 mm nozzles.\n\n### Table 1: Biopolymer-Bound Soil Composite (BSC) Mixtures\n| Mix Name | Bio Sol Conc. Results and discussion\nSS method measured static yield stress; CSR measured dynamic yield stress. Static yield stress values were higher than dynamic ones below phi_BSC = 0.52. Above this value, values converged within 4%. Thixotropic behavior was linked to soil volume fraction and particle reconsolidation.\n\n## 5. Yield stress model\nThe Chateau-Ovarlez-Trung (COT) model successfully predicted dynamic yield stress in the hydrodynamic regime (phi_BSC < 0.52). De Larrard’s (DL) model accurately predicted static yield stress in the frictional regime.\n\n## 6. Design guide for BSC-E3DP mixtures\nPrintable range identified: 0.435 <= phi_BSC <= 0.548. The lower bound ensures a 5 mm print height (shape stability); the upper bound ensures extrudability through standard nozzles.\n\n## 7. Conclusion\nBSC using low-cohesion soil is suitable for E3DP. A transition point at phi_BSC = 0.52 separates hydrodynamic and frictional flow regimes. Models provided can aid designers in assessing printability and shape stability.
