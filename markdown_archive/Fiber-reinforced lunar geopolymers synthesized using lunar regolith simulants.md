# Fiber-reinforced lunar geopolymers synthesized using lunar regolith simulants

**Journal:** Acta Astronautica 214 (2024) 593–608
**Authors:** Solomon Debbarma, Xijun Shi, Anthony Torres, Mehrab Nodehi

## Abstract
In-situ resource utilization is extremely important to support additive construction on the Moon. This study investigates fiber-reinforced geopolymers using Lunar Mare Simulant (LMS-1) and Lunar Highlands Simulant (LHS-1). Key variables included simulant type, flowability, curing temperature (93 °C), fiber type (basalt and human hair), and flexural strength. Results show LHS-1 geopolymers exhibit higher compressive strength than LMS-1. Basalt and human hair fibers improved flexural strength by up to 32% and 15% respectively, though they reduced compressive strength.

## 1. Introduction
Conventional cement production is unrealistic for the Moon due to the lack of limestone and the high energy required (1450 °C). Geopolymers offer a cement-free alternative using Si and Al-rich regolith activated by alkali solutions. Lunar regolith typically has a Si/Al ratio between 2.5 and 3, making it suitable for geopolymerization.

## 2. Materials and Methods
### 2.1 Lunar Regolith Simulants
- **LMS-1 (Mare):** High in Pyroxene (32.8%) and Glass-rich basalt (32.0%).
- **LHS-1 (Highlands):** Dominated by Anorthosite (74.4%).

### 2.2 Reinforcements
- **Basalt Fiber (BF):** 12 mm length, 16 μm diameter.
- **Human Hair Fiber (HHF):** Length up to 15 mm.

### 2.3 Alkaline Activator
- Reagent sodium silicate (40% sodium silicate, 60% water).
- Liquid-to-binder (l/b) ratio: 0.45.

## 3. Results and Discussion
### 3.1 Compressive Strength
- **LHS-1 (Mix B):** 31.26 MPa (heat-cured at 93 °C).
- **LMS-1 (Mix A):** 8.24 MPa (heat-cured at 93 °C).
- **50:50 Mix (Mix D):** 29.75 MPa (heat-cured).

### 3.2 Flexural Strength
- **Control (Mix D):** 2.32 MPa.
- **1.2% BF (Mix G):** 3.07 MPa (32% increase).
- **1.5% HHF (Mix I):** 2.67 MPa (15% increase).

## 4. Conclusion
- Curing temperature is critical for strength development in LHS-1 geopolymers.
- Higher calcium content in LHS-1 compared to LMS-1 accelerates geopolymerization and strength gain.
- Fibers act as nucleation sites, enhancing flexural performance and creating a more ductile failure mode.
