# Starch-Regolith Aerogel Bricks as a Sustainable Building Material for In Situ Extraterrestrial Constructions

**Authors:** Christophe Bliard, Chadi Maalouf, Mohammed Lachi, Virginie Bogard, Sébastien Murer, Fabien Beaumont, Guillaume Polidori, and Fabien Bogard
**Journal:** Sustainability (MDPI)
**Year:** 2025
**DOI:** 10.3390/su17167260

## Abstract
Space exploration and the prospect of developing permanent lunar settlements in the near future will require the need for innovative building construction techniques using locally sourced materials... In this study, the OPRH2N’s Lunar Regolith Simulant (LRS) was used and agglomerated with Potato (Solanum tuberosum) Starch (PS) polymer aerogel. Starch was chosen because of its excellent binding properties at very low concentrations. The resulting low-starch-containing LRS/PS aerogel composite bricks (2 and 4 wt%) were tested for their mechanical and thermal insulation performance... This new lightweight and porous material shows promising mechanical and thermal performance, making it a potential candidate for the construction of larger structures, especially in low gravity.

## 1. Introduction
One of the key challenges remains using local resources from the Moon. In-situ resource utilization (ISRU) is the practice that promotes the use of local collection and indigenous material instead of bringing it from Earth... The Moon’s surface is covered by regolith... This regolith needs to be agglomerated to make cohesive composites, able to be used to create solid “Lunarcrete” blocks or bricks.

## 2. Materials and Methods
- **Simulant:** OPRH2N Lunar Highlands regolith (7:3 ratio of basaltic feedstock and Archean Anorthosite).
- **Binder:** Potato Starch (PS) at 2 wt% and 4 wt%.
- **Preparation:** Mixing starch with water to form a hydrogel, adding regolith to form a paste, molding (50 x 50 x 50 mm), freezing to -30 °C, and freeze-drying at 0.6 mbar.
- **Tests:** Compressive testing (Instron 34TR50), thermal conductivity (ISOMET 2114), thermal cycling (-40 to +170 °C), and beta radiation exposure (10 MeV electron beam, 210 kGy dose).

### Environmental Resistance
- **Thermal Cycling:** LRS-PS4 showed a 32% increase in σmax due to drying during the test setup, reaching 1.73 MPa.
- **Prolonged Heat (150 °C):** Both samples degraded significantly; σmax dropped by 67% (PS4) and 60% (PS2).
- **Radiation:** LRS-PS4 retained 0.56 MPa (53% decrease), while LRS-PS2 dropped to 0.30 MPa (34% decrease relative to its smaller base).
- **Recyclability:** Samples can be crushed, re-mixed with water, and re-freeze-dried to regain original properties.

## 4. Conclusions
The 4 wt% composite presents interesting compressive behavior and is resistant enough for lunar surface use. Starch can act as an efficient binder for ISRU-based lunar bricks, offering a lightweight and thermally insulating alternative to energy-intensive sintering methods.
