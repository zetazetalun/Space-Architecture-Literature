# Starch-Regolith Aerogel Bricks as a Sustainable Building Material for In Situ Extraterrestrial Constructions

## Abstract
Space exploration and the prospect of developing permanent lunar settlements will require innovative building techniques using locally sourced materials (ISRU). This study uses OPRH2N’s Lunar Regolith Simulant (LRS) agglomerated with Potato (Solanum tuberosum) Starch (PS) polymer aerogel. Low-starch-containing LRS/PS aerogel composite bricks (2 and 4 wt%) were tested for mechanical and thermal insulation performance. Results show promising mechanical and thermal performance, with resistance to beta-radiation and large thermal amplitudes, making it a candidate for lunar structures.

## 1. Introduction
One of the key challenges in returning to the Moon is using local resources. The Moon’s surface is covered by regolith, which needs to be agglomerated into cohesive composites like "Lunarcrete." Techniques include thermal processes (sintering) or chemical processes (binders/polymers). This study explores starch-regolith aerogels created via freeze-drying, which requires low energy and allows water recovery.

## 2. Materials and Methods

### 2.2. LRS Brick Preparation
Starch (2 and 4 wt% dry base) was mixed with water to form a paste, combined with LRS, molded (50x50x50 mm), frozen to -30°C, and freeze-dried at 0.6 mbar.

## 4. Conclusions
The LRS-PS4 composite (4 wt% starch) presents interesting compressive behavior and is resistant enough for lunar surface environments. It acts as a thermal buffer and maintains structural integrity after irradiation (210 kGy beta radiation) and thermal shock (-196 °C to +150 °C).
