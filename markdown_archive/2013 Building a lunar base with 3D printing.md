# Building a lunar base with 3D printing

**Date:** January 31, 2013  
**Source:** Phys.org / European Space Agency (ESA)

## Overview
Setting up a lunar base could be simplified by using 3D printing to construct habitats from local materials. The European Space Agency (ESA) joined with industrial partners, including Foster + Partners, to test the feasibility of using lunar soil for additive construction.

## Design and Structure
- **Architectural Design:** Foster + Partners devised a weight-bearing 'catenary' dome design.
- **Internal Habitat:** The design incorporates a pressurized inflatable to shelter astronauts.
- **Wall Structure:** A hollow closed-cell structure, reminiscent of bird bones, was used to provide a high strength-to-weight ratio.
- **Protection:** The cellular structured wall is designed to shield against micrometeoroids and space radiation.

## Construction Technology
- **Printer:** The UK’s Monolite supplied the D-Shape printer, featuring a mobile printing array of nozzles on a 6 m frame.
- **Printing Process:** The printer sprays a binding solution onto a sand-like building material. 
  - First, simulated lunar material is mixed with magnesium oxide to create the base material.
  - A binding salt (structural 'ink') is then applied, converting the material into a stone-like solid.
- **Performance:** The printer currently builds at a rate of 2 m per hour, with targets for 3.5 m per hour.
- **Vacuum Operations:** Testing by Alta SpA and Scuola Superiore Sant'Anna showed that inserting the nozzle beneath the regolith layer allows 2 mm-scale droplets to stay trapped by capillary forces, preventing the liquid from boiling away in a vacuum.

## Materials and Testing
- **Regolith Simulant:** The team identified basaltic rock from a volcano in central Italy that has a 99.8% resemblance to lunar soil.
- **Demonstration:** A 1.5-tonne building block was produced as a proof-of-concept demonstration.

## Challenges and Future Work
- **Dust and Thermal Factors:** Controlling lunar dust and managing extreme temperature variations (which affect 3D printing efficiency) require further study.
- **Location:** The lunar poles are suggested for settlement due to their more moderate temperature ranges.
