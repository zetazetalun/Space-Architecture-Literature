# Additive Manufacturing of Lunar Regolith: A Review

**Journal:** Additive Manufacturing Frontiers 4 (2025) 200225  
**Authors:** Wucheng Sun, Fen Dang, Yan Zhou, Shifeng Wen, Cheng Zhou, Yusheng Shi, Lieyun Ding  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.amf.2025.200225

## Abstract
Lunar in-situ construction using additive manufacturing (AM) technology has emerged as a critical pathway for sustainable extraterrestrial exploration. This review systematically evaluates two dominant AM paradigms for lunar regolith processing: low-temperature deposition forming (material extrusion and binder jetting), and high-energy beam additive manufacturing (powder bed fusion and directed energy deposition).

## 1. Introduction
Space exploration's focus has shifted towards permanent habitats and infrastructure. A central strategy is In-Situ Resource Utilization (ISRU), harnessing lunar regolith to minimize dependence on Earth-bound logistics.

## 2. Low-Temperature Deposition Forming

### 2.1. Material Extrusion (MEX)
- **Direct Ink Writing (DIW):** Involves biopolymer binding, matrix transformation, or alkali-activated geopolymers. Geopolymers (AAGs) achieved a compressive strength of 48.1 MPa.
- **Fused Deposition Modeling (FDM):** Uses thermoplastic-regolith composites. PEEK-regolith composites exhibited 67.05 MPa tensile strength at 30 wt% regolith loading.
- **Sulfur-Regolith Concrete (SRC):** Achieves peak compressive strength of 40.5–46.1 MPa with rapid curing.

### 2.2. Binder Jetting (BJT)
Utilizes liquid binders selectively deposited onto powder layers. Noted for high resolution and scalability for large-scale lunar construction (e.g., d-shape method).

## 3. High-Energy Beam Additive Manufacturing

### 3.1. Powder Bed Fusion (PBF)
- **Selective Laser Sintering/Melting (SLS/SLM):** Binder-free but faces challenges with porosity (30–49%) and brittle fracture. Compressive strengths achieved range from 4.2 to 31.4 MPa.
- **Selective Solar Light Sintering (SSLS):** Utilizes concentrated solar energy. RegoLight project achieved layered structures with compressive strength < 5 MPa.
- **Selective Microwave Sintering (SMWS):** Leverages microwave-absorbing properties of regolith to sinter to depths of 0.5 m.

### 3.2. Directed Energy Deposition (DED)
Involves simultaneous delivery of feedstock and high energy. Demonstrated dense, crack-free structures with an energy density of 2.12 J/mm².

## 5. Conclusion
Future research priorities include multi-scale characterization of regolith, in-situ material formation regulation, integrated structure-material-process co-design, and autonomous robotic construction systems.
