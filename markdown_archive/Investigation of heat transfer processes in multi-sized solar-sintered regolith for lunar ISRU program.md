# Investigation of heat transfer processes in multi-sized solar-sintered regolith for lunar ISRU program

**Journal:** International Journal of Heat and Mass Transfer  
**Volume:** 214 (2023) 124387  
**Authors:** Yuankun Zhang, Matthew Shaw, Geoffrey Brooks, et al.

## Abstract
Effective Thermal Conductivity (ETC) prediction of sintered lunar regolith plays a crucial role in developing extraterrestrial thermal processing and construction techniques for In-situ Resource Utilization (ISRU) projects on the Moon. Herein, a mechanistic model was presented to estimate the ETC of sintered lunar regolith using the Monte-Carlo method. The model was experimentally validated by the measured ETC and specific surface area of fabricated lunar simulant samples LMS-1. Transient behaviors during solar heating were simulated iteratively. The measured sintered depth was improved by 45% with an optimized particle size distribution (PSD) arrangement.

## 1. Introduction
As space exploration moves towards establishing habitats on extraterrestrial bodies, high-temperature ISRU processes driven by concentrated solar energy are recognized as potential pathways for construction. Advantages include sufficient energy supply, clean/renewable energy, and reduced transportation costs from Earth by using local lunar regolith.

## 2. Methodology
### 2.1 Sintered ETC Modeling
- **Monte-Carlo (MC) Method:** Used to simulate thermal neck resistance between multi-scaled particles.
- **Shape Factors:** Characterize irregular particle geometry.
- **Parameters:** Includes coordination number, solid particle conduction, initial contact area (JKR model), and neck formation (diffusion mechanisms).
- **Atmosphere:** Models include air, argon, and vacuum (10^-3 Pa).

### 2.2 Experimental Validation
- **Material:** LMS-1 lunar mare simulant.
- **Equipment:** High-flux solar simulator (HFSS) with metal-halide lamps (peak flux ~265 kW/m²).
- **Measurements:** ETC via HotDisk TPS 2500, specific surface area via BET, and microstructure via SEM/XRD.

## 3. Results
- **Thermal Conductivity Enhancement:** Sintering improves thermal conductivity by 5–20 times compared to 'green' (un-sintered) regolith samples.
- **Model Accuracy:** The simulated steady-state temperature profiles concur with measured data with an average RMSE of 5.5%.
- **Atmospheric Effects:** Samples sintered in air had about 12% higher ETC than those in vacuum at the same temperature.
- **PSD Optimization:** Arranging small/medium particles at the top and large particles at the bottom improved sintered depth significantly.

## 4. Conclusion
1. An analytical model for sintered LMS-1 thermal conductivity was developed and validated with a mean error of 20.9% for surface area and high accuracy for ETC.
2. The coupled FVM-MCRT model allows for the optimization of solar sintering layers.
3. Optimizing the layering of different particle sizes can increase the sintered depth by 45% compared to original distributions.
