# Designing sustainable built environments for Mars habitation: Integrating innovations in architecture, systems, and human well-being

**Authors:** Hongli Sun, Mengfan Duan, Yifan Wu, Yunyi Zeng, Hengxin Zhao, Shuangdui Wu, Borong Lin, Ronggui Yang, and Gang Tan
**Journal:** Nexus (Cell Press)
**Date:** September 17, 2024
**DOI:** [https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ynexs.2024.100030](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ynexs.2024.100030)

## Summary
Migration to Mars and building livable habitation gradually become prosperous and arduous roads for human progress. This review provides a new angle of providing a developmental path from "habitation design to operation," illustrating a systemic outlook of promising Mars habitation environment control technologies for the sustainability of both habitation resources and the occupant’s health.

## Introduction
Mars exploration is the next milestone after the moon. Mars’s environment is harsher for human life (colder, less solar energy, no oxygen, higher radiation), but it shows better comprehensive conditions (24 h rhythm, safer atmosphere, larger space) and in situ resources compared with the ISS and Moon.

## Mars Habitation Technologies

### Design of Mars Habitation Module
Habitation modules are categorized into:
1. **Above-ground:** Similar to prefab pressurized structures (often inflatable) with outer shells (e.g., Mars Ice House, Marsha).
2. **Underground/Semi-underground:** Utilizing Martian lava tubes to withstand harsh space risks.

### Construction Materials and ISRU
- **Basalt:** Abundant on Mars; provides radiation resistance and low permeability.
- **Martian Soil/Clay/Steel:** Explored for Martian concrete.
- **Sulfur-based Concrete:** Suitable due to Mars's high soil sulfur content (average 6 wt %, up to 37 wt %). Features fast solidification and recyclability.
- **Iron Minerals:** Magnetites (FeS, CuFe2S3, Fe2O3) can reach compressive strengths of 4.62 kgf/mm².

### Construction Methods
- **3D Printing:** Enables the use of ISRU materials for module construction.
- **Robotic Construction:** Systems like Team Zopherus (autonomous movement) and "Chinese Super Mason" (assembling planetary LEGO bricks).
- **High-temperature Sintering:** Used to produce bricks from regolith.

## Operation and Well-being
- **Thermal Environment:** Use of multi-layer insulation, PU polyfoam, and aerogels.
- **Air Quality:** Regenerative life support systems, electrolysis of water, and waste gas recovery.
- **Human Factors:** Dynamic lighting (circadian rhythms) and personalized environment control via AI and reinforcement learning.

## Challenges and Future Opportunities
The review identifies five critical issues:
1. Module appearance/form.
2. Building methodology.
3. Material selection.
4. Energy sources (Nuclear vs. Solar/Wind).
5. Health/Comfort of the indoor environment.
