# Lunar Regolith Improvement by Inducing Interparticle Adhesion with Capillary Forces

**Authors:** Karol Brzeziński, Joanna Julia Sokołowska, Bartłomiej Przybyszewski  
**Journal:** Materials 2025, 18, 2390  
**DOI:** [10.3390/ma18102390](https://doi.org/10.3390/ma18102390)

## Abstract
This paper concerns the assessment of the lunar regolith's ability to consolidate in the presence of liquid water and develop and sustain cohesion after drying. This type of cohesion originates from interparticle adhesion and can be potentially improved through grading modification. The research used the lunar regolith simulant (EAC-1A). Capillary-Consolidated Regolith (CCR) developed flexural strength exceeding 0.025 MPa. Mechanical grinding of the regolith prior to consolidation improved the flexural strength to 0.123 MPa.

## Introduction
Building a lunar habitat requires maximizing local resources (ISRU) due to high transport costs ($1.5M/kg to the South Pole). While previous research focused on sulfur, polymers, or hydraulic binders, this study explores capillary consolidation where water acts as a recoverable technological agent in a closed cycle.

## Materials and Methods
### Material
- **EAC-1A Simulant:** Basanite material from Siebengebirge, Germany.
- **PSD Modification:** Grinding was performed using a disc mill to increase specific surface area.

### Theoretical Foundations
Capillary bridges form during drying, drawing fine particles into contact. If forces are strong enough, they overcome electrostatic repulsion, resulting in stable van der Waals interactions (solid bridges) that persist after drying.

### Specimen Preparation
- **Dimensions:** 20 mm × 20 mm × 80 mm.
- **Mix:** 400 g dry LRS + 100 g distilled water (w/LRS = 0.25).
- **Curing:** Oven-dried at 127 °C for 22 h.

## Results and Discussion

### Particle Size Distribution (PSD) Analysis
| Parameter | Original EAC-1A | Ground EAC-1A |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| D10 [µm] | 5.87 | 1.73 |
| D50 (median) [µm] | 86.36 | 13.14 |
| SPA [cm²/cm³] | 4542.7 | 11,929.0 |

### Mechanical Strength
- **Original CCR:** Mean flexural strength of 0.029 MPa.
- **Ground CCR:** Mean flexural strength of 0.123 MPa (approx. 4x increase).

### Comparison with Other Techniques
| Improvement Technique | Flexural Strength (MPa) |
| :--- | :--- |
| Capillary Consolidation (Original) | 0.029 |
| Capillary Consolidation (Ground) | 0.123 |
| Sintering | 0.23–0.55 |
| Portland cement binder | 6.8 |
| Alkali-activation | 7.16–8.80 |

## Conclusions
1. Capillary forces can transform loose lunar dust into solid material.
2. Grinding increases strength by 400% due to higher specific surface area.
3. The method is sustainable as water can be recycled.
4. Potential applications include insulation barriers, pavements, and foundations in low-gravity environments.
