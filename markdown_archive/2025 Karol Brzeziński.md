# Lunar Regolith Improvement by Inducing Interparticle Adhesion with Capillary Forces

**Authors:** Karol Brzeziński, Joanna Julia Sokołowska, Bartłomiej Przybyszewski  
**Journal:** *Materials* 2025, 18, 2390  
**DOI:** 10.3390/ma18102390  
**Published:** 20 May 2025

## Abstract
This paper concerns the assessment of the lunar regolith ability to consolidate in the presence of liquid water and develop and sustain cohesion after drying. This type of cohesion originates from interparticle adhesion and can be potentially improved through grading modification. The research was conducted using the lunar regolith simulant (EAC-1A). It was shown that simple mechanical grinding enabled the improvement of interparticle adhesion with capillary forces, resulting in improved flexural strength of the consolidated material (0.123 MPa compared to 0.029 MPa for un-ground material).

## 1. Introduction
Large-scale lunar exploration requires the creation of a stationary base ("lunar habitat") for long-term stays. To minimize the high cost of transporting cargo from Earth, scientists are opting for solutions that maximize in situ resource utilization (ISRU). Current concepts include sulfuric binders, polymer binders, hydraulic binders (like OPC), and geopolymers. This study proposes using water as a temporary technological agent for capillary consolidation.

### 2.2. Specimens Preparation
- **Mix:** 400 g dry LRS with 100 g distilled water (ratio 0.25).
- **Molding:** Custom steel molds (20 mm x 20 mm x 80 mm) coated with PTFE (Teflon®).
- **Drying:** Air-drying for 2 h, followed by oven-drying at 127 °C for 22 h.

## 3. Results and Discussion
- **Consolidation Mechanism:** Capillary forces overcome electrostatic repulsion, drawing particles into contact where van der Waals interactions create stable bonds (solid bridges).
- **Flexural Strength:** 
    - Original PSD: Mean value of **0.029 MPa**.
    - Ground PSD: Mean value of **0.123 MPa**.

## 4. Conclusions
- Capillary forces can induce interparticle adhesion in loose regolith simulant without stabilizing agents.
- Grinding the simulant before consolidation increased strength by four times.
- Water used in the process can be recovered and reused in subsequent cycles.
- The material is suitable for non-structural or low-load applications such as insulation barriers, pavements, or foundations.
