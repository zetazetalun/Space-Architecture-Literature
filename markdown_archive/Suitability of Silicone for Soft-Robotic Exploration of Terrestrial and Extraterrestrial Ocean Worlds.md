# SUITABILITY OF SILICONE FOR SOFT-ROBOTIC EXPLORATION OF TERRESTRIAL AND EXTRATERRESTRIAL OCEAN WORLDS

**Authors:** A. NUNCIO ZUNIGA, W. FINK  
**Journal:** Space Science and Technology, 2023, Vol. 29, No. 3, pp. 47–56  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.15407/knit2023.03.047

## ABSTRACT
This work revisits relevant mechanical and chemical properties of silicone rubber — Ecoflex in this study — to assess its suitability and viability for use in soft-robotic explorer construction and subsequent deployment and as a sealant for communication beacons, sensor pods, and other electronics in extreme planetary liquid environments, such as the depths of Earth’s oceans and extraterrestrial ocean worlds. Strain at a range of temperatures, as an indicator for operational durability, was tested under various endpoint clamp forces for several compound ratios. The temperature range at which silicone rubber remains pliable was assessed to determine its deployability. The re-binding property of cured silicone rubber samples with newly curing samples was investigated for its potential for additive manufacturing in soft robotics. Finally, the dissolution resistance, non-polarity, and electrical non-conductivity of silicone rubber were studied to assess its suitability for sealing electronics to be submerged in the salt water of both ocean and saturated salinity, as well as in hydrocarbon liquids.

## 1. INTRODUCTION
Subsurface oceans on planetary bodies known as ocean worlds, such as Jupiter’s moon Europa and Saturn’s moons Titan and Enceladus, have been postulated as prime candidate locales for astrobiological exploration. Underwater autonomous vehicles are currently dominated by rigid frames, which limits their ability to bend through smaller apertures. This paper revisits the suitability of silicone rubber for constructing soft-robotic explorers and sealing communication beacons and sensor pods in extreme planetary liquid environments.

## 2. MATERIALS AND METHODS
### 2.1. Ecoflex Overview
Ecoflex™ 00-30 was chosen as a stand-in for other rubbers/polymers. It is a proprietary silicone rubber made by mixing 2 compounds, A and B. It cures in approximately 4 hours at room temperature.
### 2.2. Strain Testing
Ecoflex prisms (1.25 x 1.00 x 7.50 cm) were cast at three ratios: 20%:80%, 50%:50%, and 80%:20%. Testing was conducted at -5°C to 0°C, room temperature (~22.4°C), and 39°C to 40°C.
### 2.4. Very Low Temperature Testing
Ecoflex was tested for freezing behavior at -7.5°C, -20°C, -80°C, and -196°C (liquid nitrogen).
### 2.5. Re-bonding Ability
Cured samples were placed in molds and new Ecoflex was added to test the interface strength between old and new material.

## 4. RESULTS
### 4.1. Strain Testing Across Temperatures
Increased amounts of compound A led to stiffer Ecoflex. Strain results appeared to be largely temperature independent within the measured range.
### 4.2. Room Temperature Stretching to Failure
Five I-beam prisms (50%:50% mix) showed an elongation percent at breaking point of 553.3 ± 20.5%.
### 4.3. Very Low Temperature Testing
Samples did not freeze at 0°C or -20°C. All ratios froze at -80°C, but the process was fully reversible. Submergence in liquid nitrogen (-196°C) caused shattering, but the material returned to pre-freezing pliability once thawed.
### 4.4. Re-bonding Ability
New Ecoflex curing adjacent to cured material resulted in a near-indistinguishable bond. The material behaves as a single piece, supporting additive manufacturing applications.
### 4.5. Dissolution Resistance and Conductivity
Cured Ecoflex showed no signs of degradation in saltwater (3.5% and saturated) for at least 21 days, or in mineral oil (hydrocarbon stand-in) for two months. It is non-polar and electrically non-conductive, protecting encased Raspberry Pi electronics.

## 5. DISCUSSION & CONCLUSIONS
Ecoflex 00-30 is an excellent encapsulation option. Pliability is preserved down to at least -20°C. While it freezes at -80°C (beyond the -53°C manufacturer limit), it remains functional for terrestrial and some extraterrestrial aqueous missions. For Titan's hydrocarbon lakes (~94 K), different polymers remaining flexible at such low temperatures would be required.
