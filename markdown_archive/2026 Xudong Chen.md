# Tailoring the microstructure evolution in lunar soil simulant via thermal treatment

## Abstract
Establishing lunar bases, an essential step for future space exploration, necessitates high-performance and durable construction materials. In-situ resource utilization (ISRU) of lunar soil to fabricate glass-ceramics offers a sustainable route to reduce dependence on costly Earth-based supply chains, contingent on precise crystallization control, a capability that critically depends on resolving key microscale dynamic features during thermal treatment. Here, we investigate the microscale crystallization behavior of a lunar soil simulant under three thermal schemes using the single hot thermocouple technique (SHTT). Results show a transition from equiaxed to columnar and planar crystal growth as the isothermal temperature increases from 1000 °C to 1200 °C. Kinetic analysis indicates volume nucleation with an activation energy of 461.55 kJ·mol⁻¹. These findings provide guidance for the sustainable production of high-performance materials from extraterrestrial resources.

## 1. Introduction
Space exploration and the establishment of lunar bases require substantial amounts of construction materials. To reduce reliance on Earth-based supply chains, ISRU of lunar soil is a promising strategy. Materials such as sintered bricks, geopolymers, ceramics, and glass-ceramics are being explored. Glass-ceramics, comprising both glassy and crystalline phases, provide tunable properties suitable for components like walls, roads, and windows.

## 2. Methodology
### 2.1. Experimental system and procedure
The Single Hot Thermocouple Technique (SHTT) was used for real-time observation of crystallization. The system consists of an embodiment, temperature controller, and PC. Samples were melted at 1500 °C, quenched to parent glass, and then subjected to three thermal schemes to mimic glass-ceramic processing conditions.

### 2.2. Simulated lunar soil
A representative lunar soil simulant was formulated targeting key oxides (CaO, SiO2, Al2O3, MgO, FeO) identified in mare-influenced lunar soil profiles.

### 2.3. Data processing
Crystallization parameters including incubation time (τi), overall crystallization time (τg), crystal phase content (α), and crystal growth rates (vα, vτ) were analyzed using ImageJ software.

## 3. Results and discussion
### 3.1. Phenomenological crystallization behavior
*   **Scheme I & II:** At 1000 °C, equiaxed growth was observed. At 1100 °C, columnar growth occurred. At 1200 °C, growth transitioned to planar mode. At 1300 °C, no crystallization occurred under direct isothermal holding.
*   **Scheme III:** Temperature transitions (e.g., 1100 °C to 950 °C) were used to control nucleation and growth. Nuclei pre-formed at lower temperatures could immediately grow upon reheating.

### 3.2. Quantitative assessment
*   **Crystallization Metrics:** Peak growth rates (vτ) increased with temperature. Schemes with temperature jumps produced multi-peak growth rate curves.
*   **Kinetics:** Data fitted the Johnson-Mehl-Avrami (JMA) model. The Avrami exponent (n > 1) indicated volume nucleation dominated.
*   **Activation Energy:** Estimated at 461.55 kJ·mol⁻¹.

## 4. Conclusion
Crystallization behavior of lunar soil simulants exhibits strong dependence on thermal conditions. Controlling supercooling during thermal processing is key to tailoring microstructures. High supercooling favors nucleation (I) over growth (U), leading to finer, more uniform grain distributions suitable for extraterrestrial construction applications.
