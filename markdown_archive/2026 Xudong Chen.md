# Tailoring the microstructure evolution in lunar soil simulant via thermal treatment

## Metadata
- **Journal:** International Journal of Heat and Mass Transfer 267 (2026) 128955
- **Authors:** Xudong Chen, Qiqi Han, Yucheng Qiao, Yu Tan, Zhang Bai, Bin Ding
- **Keywords:** Lunar construction, Glass-ceramics, Heat treatment scheme, Crystallization phenomena, Growth mode

## Abstract
Establishing lunar bases necessitates high-performance and durable construction materials. In-situ resource utilization (ISRU) of lunar soil to fabricate glass-ceramics offers a sustainable route to reduce dependence on costly Earth-based supply chains. This research investigates the microscale crystallization behavior of a lunar soil simulant under three thermal schemes using the single hot thermocouple technique (SHTT). Results show a transition from equiaxed to columnar and planar crystal growth as isothermal temperature increases from 1000 °C to 1200 °C. Lower temperatures favor nucleation, while higher temperatures enhance growth via atomic mobility, resulting in up to a 28-fold variation in crystallization time.

## 1. Introduction
Space exploration considers lunar bases essential for long-term habitation. Given the harsh Moon environment, materials must meet strict mechanical, thermal, and optical standards. Lunar soil-based materials include sintered bricks, geopolymers, ceramics, and glass-ceramics. Glass-ceramics are particularly suitable for construction components like walls, roads, and windows due to their hybrid structure.

## 2. Methodology
### 2.1. Experimental System
A single hot thermocouple technique (SHTT) was used for real-time observation of crystallization. A type-B thermocouple held the sample, acting as both a heating element and sensor.
### 2.2. Thermal Schemes
- **Scheme I:** Direct reduction to target isothermal temperature (1000, 1100, 1200, 1300 °C).
- **Scheme II:** Temperature-jump process with a 4-second hold at 1200 °C before shifting to targets.
- **Scheme III:** Temperature transitions (1100 °C to 950 °C and vice versa) with varied soaking times.

## 3. Results and Discussion
- **Nucleation and Growth:** At 1000 °C, numerous fine crystals aggregated (equiaxed growth). At 1100 °C, dendritic crystals developed (columnar growth). At 1200 °C, strip-like crystals formed (planar growth).
- **Crystallization Time:** Lower temperatures resulted in faster nucleation but slower growth due to viscosity. High temperatures provided the thermal energy for rapid post-nucleation growth.
- **Kinetics:** Kinetic analysis using the Johnson-Mehl-Avrami (JMA) model indicated volume nucleation with an activation energy of 461.55 kJ/mol.

## 4. Conclusion
Controlling supercooling during thermal treatment is critical for tailoring microstructures. Strong supercooling (large temperature gradients) promotes the formation of numerous small and uniformly distributed crystals, enhancing the structural integrity and durability of lunar soil-derived glass-ceramics for extraterrestrial construction.
