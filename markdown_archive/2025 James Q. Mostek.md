# Atlas of Lunar Historical Sites and Proposed Bases and Infrastructure

**Authors:** James Q. Mostek, Travis M. Grile, Robert A. Bettinger, Alexander M. Petrocelli, Nathan R. Boone  
**Journal:** Aerotecnica Missili & Spazio (2025) 104:355–370  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1007/s42496-024-00239-z  
**Published online:** 24 November 2024

## Abstract
The 21st century has witnessed an expansion of space operations throughout the Earth–Moon system. Site selection for the construction of basing/infrastructure is a critical function to enhance mission assurance. In this paper, an atlas of human activity on the lunar surface is presented featuring historical impactor/soft-landing sites and proposed future basing locations. The atlas seeks to gain insight into where future lunar bases may be located and establish historical patterns of proposed basing.

## 1 Introduction
Site selection for basing and infrastructure represents a critical first step to any lunar construction initiative. Future lunar missions will require specialized terrain surveying to update cartographical and geologic data. This paper provides a collection of maps and tables corresponding to locations of interest.

## 3 Proposed Lunar Basing and Infrastructure
Two main project types were collected: proposed bases (sustaining human life) and infrastructure (supporting base functions, e.g., antennas, mines).

### Site Selection Factors
- **Polar Regions:** Benefits include 90% illumination for solar power, access to water ice (oxygen/hydrogen), and constant views of the celestial field. Negatives include communication difficulties and extreme contrast.
- **Maria/Equatorial Regions:** Benefits include flatness (easier construction/transport) and direct line-of-sight communication with Earth. Negatives include 14-day lunar nights.
- **Lava Tubes:** Offer protection from radiation, micrometeoroids, and constant temperature, though existence is currently unconfirmed in many regions (e.g., Marius Hills).

## 5 Lunar Infrastructure and Space Debris Considerations
Simulations of debris impact locations from spacecraft breakup events in Low Lunar Orbit (LLO) suggest risks to infrastructure. A low-risk zone was identified between 100° W and 0° longitude in the lunar midlands, whereas high-risk impact zones appear between 40° E to 80° E.

## 6 Conclusion
Proposed base and infrastructure sites generally fall into two categories: lunar poles (resource and illumination advantages) or near-side maria (communication and terrain advantages). This atlas serves as a living document to inform site selection for future lunar exploration.
