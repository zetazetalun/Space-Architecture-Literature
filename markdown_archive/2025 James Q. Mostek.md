**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1007/s42496-024-00239-z  
**Authors:** James Q. Mostek, Travis M. Grile, Robert A. Bettinger, Alexander M. Petrocelli, Nathan R. Boone  
**Published:** 24 November 2024

## Abstract
The 21st century has witnessed an expansion of space operations throughout the Earth–Moon system. Many countries are endeavoring to move outside geosynchronous orbit to pursue missions on the lunar surface. Site selection for the construction of basing/infrastructure will become a critical function. In this paper, an atlas of human activity on the lunar surface is presented featuring a concise mapping of locations associated with historical lunar impactor and soft-landing sites and proposed future basing and infrastructure reports.

## 1 Introduction
Site selection for basing and infrastructure represents a critical first step to any lunar construction initiative. Future lunar missions will require specialized terrain surveying to update existing cartographical and geologic data. This paper provides a collection of maps and associated tables related to locations of interest on the lunar surface.

## 4 Analysis and Risk Assessment
- **Lunar Maria:** Desirable for flatness, easing transport and construction.
- **Lava Tubes:** Identified in the Marius Hills region; offer protection from radiation, meteorites, and constant temperature.
- **South Pole:** Favored for future bases due to solar illumination and resource access (water ice).
- **Space Debris Hazards:** Monte Carlo simulations identify high-risk zones for debris impacts in Low Lunar Orbit (LLO). Regions between 40° E to 80° E and 20° N to 85° S contain a majority of debris impact locations and should be avoided for critical infrastructure unless specialized shielding is used.

## 6 Conclusion
This atlas documents 92 annotated sites. It serves as a risk assessment tool for site selection, informing future lunar exploration and settlement projects.
