# Multidimensional ergonomic latent modeling of prefabricated adaptive decoration by robotic 3D printing

**Authors:** Gangwei Cai, Binyan Xu, Zehuan Hu, Zhenwei Guo, Sheng He, Feidong Lu, Yiping Xia, Ziming Ren, Bart Julien Dewancker & Weijun Gao
**Journal:** Humanities & Social Sciences Communications
**Year:** 2026
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1057/s41599-026-06688-0

## Abstract
Chain hotels constitute 40.09% of all hotels, with indoor room decorations typically undergoing replacement cycles every 2 to 5 years. Robotic 3D printing-enabled prefabrication offers transformative potential for sustainable construction, combining precision engineering with environmental consciousness. This study explores its application in the hospitality sector, addressing both sustainability and ergonomic data. Using AI-driven multidimensional ergonomic latent modeling, key environmental factors such as sound insulation, lighting adaptability, and air quality are evaluated for their impact on service flexibility, safety, and user experience. Moreover, this work extends the exploration of robotic 3D printing prefabricated solutions to the context of extreme environments and even extraterrestrial buildings, such as building habitats on Mars or the Moon.

## Introduction
The research aims to catalyze a paradigm shift in sustainable construction, highlighting prefabrication technologies—particularly robotic 3D printing and prefabricated decoration—as tools for reducing environmental footprints. Notably, the study extends the application of robotic 3D printing prefabrication to the exploration of extreme environments and even extraterrestrial architecture, such as Martian habitats or lunar bases, by contributing valuable insights from the perspective of human perception.

## Methodology
This study adopts a structured methodology (CFA-SEM) to investigate the relationship between green prefabricated hotel room features and tourist satisfaction. The framework is grounded in international and national low-carbon and prefabrication standards.

## Results
The Structural Equation Modeling (SEM) results show that all 11 hypotheses were statistically supported at the 0.05 level. Direct determinants of satisfaction are led by Sound Environment (SE) (β = 0.70), followed by Lighting/Illumination/View (LIV) (β = 0.40), Health, Safety, and Security (HSS) (β = 0.19), and Green Service (GS) (β = 0.14).

## Discussion and Conclusion
The findings underscore the importance of combining advanced manufacturing technologies with human factors engineering to redefine sustainable hospitality and expand its frontiers beyond conventional contexts into extreme environments like high-humidity islands, polar regions, and extraterrestrial habitats.
