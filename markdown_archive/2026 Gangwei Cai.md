# Multidimensional ergonomic latent modeling of prefabricated adaptive decoration by robotic 3D printing

## Abstract
Robotic 3D printing-enabled prefabrication offers transformative potential for sustainable construction. This study evaluates AI-driven multidimensional ergonomic latent modeling for environmental factors such as sound insulation, lighting, and air quality. The research extends the exploration of robotic 3D printing to extraterrestrial buildings, such as habitats on Mars or the Moon, contributing valuable human factors engineering insights.

## Introduction
Robotic 3D printing-enabled prefabrication represents an AI-driven integration of precision engineering and sustainability. This technology enables efficient modular component production, reducing construction waste and supporting adaptive designs. Notably, the study extends the application to the exploration of extreme environments, such as Martian habitats or lunar bases, contributing valuable insights from the perspective of human perception.

## Literature Review
The integration of robotic 3D printing into prefabrication processes allows for precise customization, material efficiency, and reduced waste. Studies on thermal comfort and air quality (TCAQ) suggest that robotic 3D printing modules offer superior performance in regulating indoor environments.

## Methods
This study adopts a structured methodology investigating the relationship between green prefabricated hotel room features and tourist satisfaction. It utilizes Confirmatory Factor Analysis (CFA) and Structural Equation Modeling (SEM).

## Results
SEM results show that all 11 hypotheses were statistically supported. The sound environment (SE) positively influences flexible serviceability (FS) and health, safety, and security (HSS). Direct determinants of tourist satisfaction (TS) are led by SE (β = 0.70; 49%), followed by LIV (β = 0.40; 28%), HSS (β = 0.19; 13%), and GS (β = 0.14; 10%).

## Discussion and Conclusion
The study underscores the potential of green prefabrication technologies, particularly those leveraging robotic 3D printing, in advancing sustainable construction. The findings provide a foundation for further exploration into extraterrestrial habitats where modularity, rapid deployment, and ergonomic considerations are critical.
