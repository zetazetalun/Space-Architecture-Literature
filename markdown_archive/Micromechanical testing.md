# Micromechanical testing and property upscaling of planetary rocks: A critical review

**Authors:** Yiwei Liu, Guoping Zhang, Jiangmei Qiao, Xuhai Tang  
**Journal:** International Journal of Mining Science and Technology 34 (2024) 1217–1241  
**DOI:** 10.1016/j.ijmst.2024.08.002

## Abstract
Knowledge of the mechanical behavior of planetary rocks is indispensable for space explorations. The scarcity of pristine samples and the irregular shapes of planetary meteorites make it difficult to obtain representative samples for conventional macroscale rock mechanics experiments (macro-RMEs). This critical review discusses recent advances in microscale RMEs (micro-RMEs) techniques and the upscaling methods for extracting mechanical parameters. First, we summarize several mainstream methods for obtaining the mineralogy and microstructure of planetary rocks. Then, nondestructive micromechanical testing methods, nanoindentation and atomic force microscopy (AFM), are detailed reviewed. Subsequently, several feasible upscaling methods that bridge the micro-measurements of meteorite pieces to the strength of the intact body are introduced. Finally, the potential applications of planetary rock mechanics research to guiding the design and execution of space missions are envisioned, ranging from sample return missions and planetary defense to extraterrestrial construction.

## 1. Introduction
Understanding the mechanical properties of planetary rocks is crucial for tasks such as planetary sampling, establishing space research stations, planetary defense, and future space mining endeavors. These properties influence material selection, processing methods, and overall in-situ resource utilization (ISRU) strategy. Traditional macroscale testing requires standard shapes difficult to prepare from scarce planetary rocks; thus, micro-RMEs (nanoindentation and AFM) have become vital tools.

## 2. Methods of Mineralogical and Microstructural Analyses
*   **Microscopy:** SEM (secondary electron and backscattered electron imaging) and TEM for atomic-level resolution.
*   **Energy-dispersive spectroscopy (EDS):** Used to identify and quantify elemental composition.
*   **X-ray Microscopy (XCT):** Non-destructive 3D visualization of internal structures, useful for investigating internal porosity and density.
*   **X-ray Diffraction (XRD):** Determines crystalline structure and mineral phases.
*   **Electron probe X-ray microanalysis (EPMA):** High-efficiency chemical composition analysis.

## 3. Micromechanical Experiments
### 3.1 Nanoindentation
Used to measure hardness and elastic modulus at the microscale. It offers minimal sample damage, allowing precious samples to be reused.

### 3.2 Atomic Force Microscopy (AFM)
Achieves atomic or near-atomic resolution for surface topography and nanomechanical mapping. PeakForce QNM is highlighted for quantitative mapping of surfaces.

## 4. Upscaling of Microscale Mechanical Properties
To relate micro-measurements to the parent body strength, several homogenization schemes are used:
*   **Mori-Tanaka (M-T) Scheme:** Considers interaction among inclusions and the matrix.
*   **Self-Consistent Scheme (SCS):** Accounts for stress and strain inhomogeneity.
*   **Voigt-Reuss-Hill Average:** Provides upper and lower bounds for elastic modulus.
*   **Accurate Grain-Based Modeling (AGBM):** A numerical simulation technique incorporating detailed microstructural data from micro-RMEs into 2D finite element models.

## 5. Prospective Applications
*   **ISRU and Extraterrestrial Construction:** Assessing regolith-based constructions is essential for reliability in extreme environments. Methods to consolidate regolith include cold sintering, laser sintering, geopolymerization, and 3D printing.
*   **Asteroid Mining:** Identifying mechanical properties for drilling and sample collection.

## 6. Conclusions
1. Non-destructive micro-RMEs offer profound insights into planetary rock properties.
2. Upscaling methods like AGBM provide more accurate predictions than traditional homogenization schemes.
3. Future work should focus on establishing a comprehensive database to inform planetary defense and exploration strategies.
