# An assumption of in situ resource utilization for “bio-bricks” in space exploration

**Authors:** Hongyan Zuo, Shuisong Ni, Meiying Xu
**Published:** 06 April 2023
**DOI:** 10.3389/fmats.2023.1155643

## Abstract
Microbially induced carbonate precipitation (MICP) mimics the natural cementation process that occurs in various geological settings by using the bicarbonate minerals resulting from various bacterial metabolic pathways as cementing agents. This bio-technique can be used to manufacture so-called “bio-bricks,” which rival regular bricks in strength and durability. Recently, this process has been shown to have great potential as an energy-saving and cost-effective means of in situ resource utilization (ISRU) to produce construction materials for extraterrestrial human settlements. We review natural cementation, anaerobic and aerobic bacterial metabolic activities, properties of lunar regolith, and the production of bio-bricks.

## 1 Introduction
Space exploration faces significant economic hurdles due to the high cost of transporting materials from Earth ($10,000 USD per pound). ISRU is essential for sustainable settlements. Traditional processing methods like sintering and melting are energy-intensive. Bio-cementation offers a 'green' alternative, using microorganisms to induce mineral precipitation.

## 2 Microbial carbonate precipitation by different metabolic activities
Metabolic activities are classified into aerobic and anaerobic groups:
- **Aerobic:** Photosynthesis, ureolysis, and ammonification.
- **Anaerobic:** Sulfate reduction, denitrification, and methane oxidation (essential for oxygen-poor environments like the Moon).

## 3 Properties of sand-sized material on the moon
Lunar regolith consists of fragmental and unconsolidated rock material. 
- **Thickness:** 2–15 m.
- **Specific Gravity:** 2.9–3.5 g/cm³.
- **Particle Size:** Average mean particle size $D_{50} = 72 \mu\text{m}$.
- **Mineralogy:** Plagioclase, pyroxene, ilmenite, and olivine.

## 5 Research needs and future directions
- **Low-gravity testing:** Survival and efficiency of microbes in microgravity.
- **Extreme environment survival:** Strategies like cyst/spore formation.
- **Nutrient sources:** Using human urine as a source of urea to reduce shipping mass.
- **Cell-free systems:** Utilizing isolated enzymes (urease) to bypass biological survival challenges.
