# Suitability of Silicone for Soft-Robotic Exploration of Terrestrial and Extraterrestrial Ocean Worlds

**Journal:** Space Science and Technology (Космічна наука і технологія)
**Volume/Issue:** 2023, Vol. 29, No. 3, pp. 47–56
**DOI:** 10.15407/knit2023.03.047

## Abstract
This work revisits mechanical and chemical properties of silicone rubber — Ecoflex — to assess its suitability for use in soft-robotic explorer construction and deployment as a sealant for communication beacons and electronics in extreme planetary liquid environments (e.g., Europa, Titan, Enceladus). The study tests strain across temperatures, re-bonding properties for additive manufacturing, and resistance to saltwater and hydrocarbon corrosion.

## 1. Introduction
Subsurface oceans on planetary bodies like Europa, Titan, and Enceladus are prime locales for astrobiological exploration. These environments require flexible explorers (soft robotics) to navigate small apertures. This paper examines silicone rubber for constructing these explorers and sealing associated electronics against extreme salinity, hydrocarbon liquids, and low temperatures.

## 2. Materials and Methods
- **Material:** Ecoflex™ 00-30 (Smooth-On, Inc.), mixed at various ratios (20:80, 50:50, 80:20).
- **Testing:**
  - Strain testing at varying temperatures (-5°C to 40°C).
  - Stretching to failure at room temperature (~23°C).
  - Very low temperature testing (submersion in liquid nitrogen at -196°C).
  - Re-bonding ability: Testing the interface strength of newly cured silicone to older samples (up to 6 days old).
  - Chemical Resistance: Submersion in 3.5% and saturated salt water, and mineral oil.
  - Electrical Non-Conductivity: Encapsulation of a Raspberry Pi-based beacon.

## 3. Results

### 3.1. Mechanical Performance
- **Strain:** Stiffer ratios (80% Compound A) showed lower strain. Strain results were largely temperature-independent within the measured -5°C to 40°C range.
- **Elongation at Failure:** The 50:50 mix reached a breaking point at an elongation of 553.3 ± 20.5%.

### 3.2. Temperature Resilience
- **Pliability:** Samples remained pliable at -20°C.
- **Freezing:** All ratios froze at -80°C but returned to pre-freezing pliability upon thawing.
- **Liquid Nitrogen:** Submersion at -196°C caused samples to freeze and shatter; however, they regained pliability once returned to room temperature.

### 3.3. Re-bonding and Additive Manufacturing
- New Ecoflex cured adjacent to samples up to 6 days old formed a near-indistinguishable bond that did not separate under stress, enabling additive manufacturing techniques for encapsulation.

### 3.4. Chemical and Electrical Properties
- **Dissolution Resistance:** No signs of degradation after 21 days in saturated salt water or 2 months in mineral oil.
- **Non-Conductivity:** Encapsulated electronics (Raspberry Pi) functioned perfectly for 6 hours underwater without short-circuiting.
- **Hermeticity:** Wireless communication was maintained through the silicone barrier for 21 days in salt water.

## 4. Discussion & Conclusions
Ecoflex 00-30 is an excellent encapsulation and construction material for soft robotics in extreme environments down to -20°C (and likely -50°C). While it freezes at the extremely low temperatures of Titan's lakes (-179°C), it serves as a stand-in for future low-temperature polymers. The re-bonding property allows for complex additive construction and repair of robotic structures and electronic housing in-situ.
