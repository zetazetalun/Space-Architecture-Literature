# Designing sustainable built environments for Mars habitation: Integrating innovations in architecture, systems, and human well-being

**Authors:** Hongli Sun, Mengfan Duan, Yifan Wu, Yunyi Zeng, Hengxin Zhao, Shuangdui Wu, Borong Lin, Ronggui Yang, Gang Tan
**Publication:** Nexus 1, 100030, September 17, 2024
**DOI:** 10.1016/j.ynexs.2024.100030

## Summary
Migration to Mars and building livable habitation are arduous roads for human progress. This review provides a developmental path from "habitation design to operation," illustrating a systemic outlook of promising Mars habitation environment control technologies for the sustainability of both habitation resources and the occupant's health.

## Mars Habitation Technologies: From Design to Operation

### Design of Mars Habitation Module
Mars habitation involves multiple aspects: module form, material, and construction. Ideas include:
- **Above-ground:** Prefab pressurized (often inflatable) cores with outer shells to resist radiation/storms (e.g., Mars Ice House, Marsha).
- **Underground/Semi-underground:** Utilizing lava tubes to withstand space risks.

### Construction Materials & ISRU
In Situ Resource Utilization (ISRU) is essential due to transport costs. Potential materials include:
- **Martian Basalt:** High radiation resistance, specific heat, and low permeability.
- **Sulfur-based Concrete:** Soil on Mars contains average 6 wt% sulfur. Sulfur concrete is suitable due to ease of operation and fast solidification in dry/cold environments.
- **Magnetite-Sulfur Mix:** Compressive strength of 4.62 kgf/mm² on Mars.
- **Basalt Fiber:** Low thermal conductivity (0.031–0.038 W/(m·K)).

### Automated Construction
- **3D Printing:** Offers possibilities for utilizing in situ materials.
- **Robotics:** Systems like "Team Zopherus" (internal printing chamber) and "Chinese Super Mason" (assembling planetary bricks).

### Operation and Environmental Control
- **Thermal Control:** High-insulation materials (aerogels), heat pipes, and phase change materials.
- **Life Support:** Electrolyzed water for oxygen, mechanical ventilation for CO2 removal, and air purification.
- **Lighting:** Rhythmic artificial lighting to simulate Earth-like cycles and support human circadian rhythms.

| Gravity | 1 g | None | 1/6 g | 1/3 g |
| Atmosphere | 78%N2, 21%O2 | Artificial | None | Rarefied CO2 (95.3%) |
| In situ resources | Wide varieties | None | SiO2, Al2O3, CaO | Martian soil, Basalt, Volcanic ash |
| Energy | Solar, Wind, Tidal | Solar | Solar, He-3 | Solar, Radioactive isotope |

## Key Findings
- **Performance-Centered Design:** Use of AI and genetic algorithms for multi-objective optimization of building forms can reduce energy consumption (e.g., by 15.8% in terrestrial analogs).
- **ISRU Viability:** Sulfur-based concrete and basalt-derived materials are primary candidates for Class III habitats.
- **Human-Centricity:** Environmental control must transition from static stability to dynamic/bionic regulation to prevent "sick-building syndrome" in isolated space habitats.
