# The first biological experiment on lunar surface for Humankind: Device and results

## Authors
GengXin Xie, JingHang Ding, YuanXun Zhang, MaoZhi Ren, Dan Qiu, Xi Wang

## Abstract
The Bioregenerative Life Support System (BLSS) is indispensable for extraterrestrial bases to provide food, air, and waste recycling. The Chinese Chang’e 4 lunar lander carried the Biological Experiment Payload (BEP), a micro-ecosystem, to the back of the Moon. The payload (2.608 kg) contained cotton, potato, Arabidopsis, and rape seeds, fly eggs, and yeast. Results showed that cotton seeds germinated faster on the Moon (22 h) than in ground controls (53 h) and survived a lunar night at -52 °C in a dormant but green state. This experiment demonstrated the feasibility of plant growth using natural lunar sunlight and provides data on biological responses to 1/6 g gravity and radiation.

## 1. Introduction
Lunar bases must be self-sufficient due to transportation costs. BLSS, an artificial ecosystem of producers, consumers, and decomposers, is a critical technology for sustainability. Previous experiments like BIOS-3 and Biosphere-2 established the viability of such systems on Earth, but the effects of the lunar environment (low gravity, radiation) remained untested until the Chang’e 4 mission.

## 2. The Biological Experiment Payload (BEP)
### 2.1 Device Specifications
- **Weight:** 2.608 kg
- **Dimensions:** 173 mm (diameter) × 198 mm (height)
- **Volume:** Total 0.82L; Bio-activity space 0.42L
- **Sealing:** Air leakage rate < 1.9 × 10⁻⁵ Pa·cm²/s
- **Components:** Biological seedbed (vermiculite substrate), water storage (18 ml), lighting system (light guide tubes), and thermal control (multilayer insulation and thermoelectric coolers).

### 2.2 Biological Composition
- **Producers:** Cotton, Potato, Arabidopsis, Rape seeds
- **Consumer:** Fly eggs (Drosophila melanogaster)
- **Decomposer:** Yeast (Saccharomyces fermentati)

## 3. Results
### 3.1 Germination and Growth
- **Cotton:** First plant to grow on the Moon. Germination captured 22 h after watering.
- **Morphology:** Lunar cotton showed curved stems, fat streamlined nodes close to the substrate, and curled leaves that did not unfold.
- **Ground Sync:** Cotton in ground controls germinated at 53 h but failed to survive the simulated lunar night, whereas lunar seedlings remained green.

### 3.2 Environmental Data
- **Internal Pressure:** Ranged from 98 to 104 kPa during the first lunar day.
- **Internal Temperature:** Maintained between 16.7 °C and 36.5 °C during active periods; dropped to -52 °C during the lunar night.

## 4. Discussion and Conclusion
The experiment proved that plants can survive the initial growth stages on the lunar surface. Use of light guide fibers successfully channeled natural sunlight for photosynthesis, reducing power requirements. Future research must address soil improvement for cultivation and the utilization of lunar rocks/lava tubes for habitat construction.
