# The first biological experiment on lunar surface for Humankind: Device and results\n\n**Authors:** GengXin Xie, JingHang Ding, YuanXun Zhang, MaoZhi Ren, Dan Qiu, Xi Wang\n**Published in:** Acta Astronautica 214 (2024) 216–223\n**DOI:** 10.1016/j.actaastro.2023.10.023\n\n## Abstract\nThe Bioregenerative Life Support System (BLSS) is an indispensable part of extraterrestrial bases as it can provide food, air, and waste recycling. The Chinese Chang’e 4 lunar lander carried the Biological Experiment Payload (BEP) to the back of the Moon to assess the effects of low gravity, radiation, and light. The 2.608 kg payload contained cotton, potato, Arabidopsis, and rape seeds, plus fly eggs and yeast. Results showed cotton seeds germinated faster on the Moon (22 h) than on Earth (53 h) and demonstrated unexpected resilience to extreme cold (-52 °C) during the lunar night.\n\n## 1. Introduction\nBuilding self-sufficient lunar bases requires BLSS, an artificial ecosystem following Earth's biosphere principles. Key historical facilities include BIOS-3 and Biosphere-2. This study aims to fill the gap in knowledge regarding organism responses to real lunar 1/6 gravity and extreme environments.\n\n## 2. The Biological Experiment Payload (BEP)\n- **Structure:** Cylindrical aluminum shell, 173 mm diameter × 198 mm height.\n- **Internal Volume:** 0.82 L total, 0.42 L bio-activity space.\n- **Weight:** 2.608 kg.\n- **Substrate:** Vermiculite.\n- **Lighting:** Natural sunlight via light-guiding fibers (10 mm diameter hole).\n- **Temperature Control:** Multilayer insulation and thermoelectric coolers (TEC).\n- **Sealing:** Leakage rate < 1.9 × 10⁻⁵ Pa·cm²/s.\n\n## 3. Results of Biological Experiments on the Lunar Surface\n- **Landing:** Jan 3, 2019, Von Kármán crater.\n- **Germination:** Cotton seed germinated 22 h after watering. Leaves observed at 82 h.\n- **Morphology:** Curved stems, long parts close to substrate, fat streamlined nodes, and curled leaves.\n- **Lunar Night Survival:** After the first lunar night (-52 °C), cotton seedlings remained upright and green, while terrestrial controls died.\n- **Total Operation:** 1300 hours over 5 lunar days.\n\n## 4. Discussion and ISRU Implications\nReasonable use of natural light reduces BLSS energy usage. Future research must address ISRU, including: \n1. Improving lunar soil for cultivation.\n2. Utilizing lunar rocks as construction materials.\n3. Using lunar lava tube caves for building bases.\n4. Producing oxygen and water from lunar minerals.\n\n## 5. Conclusion\nThe BEP proved that cultivation on the Moon is possible. Despite limitations in weight and energy, the experiment provides a foundation for establishing sustainable lunar bases.
