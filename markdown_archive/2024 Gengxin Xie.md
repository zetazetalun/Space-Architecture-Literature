# The first biological experiment on lunar surface for Humankind: Device and results ## Abstract The Bioregenerative Life Support System (BLSS) is an indispensable part of extraterrestrial bases as it can provide food, fresh air, and waste recycling. The Chinese Chang’e 4 lunar lander carried a Biological Experiment Payload (BEP) to the back of the Moon to assess the effects of low gravity, radiation, and light on living organisms. The 2.608 kg payload contained cotton seeds, potato seeds, Arabidopsis seeds, rape seeds, fly eggs, and yeast. Results showed cotton seed germination within 22 hours of watering, faster than the 53 hours in ground synchronization experiments. The seedlings adapted to temperatures as low as -52 C during the lunar night. This experiment proved plants can grow on the lunar surface using sunlight for photosynthesis. ## 1. Introduction Building a lunar base requires self-sufficiency due to high transportation costs. BLSS technology, mimicking Earth's biosphere principles, is critical for supplying food, oxygen, and water. Prior experiments like BIOS-3 and Lunar Palace 1 have demonstrated high system closure on Earth, but lunar surface responses to 1/6 gravity and extreme radiation were previously untested. ## 2. The Biological Experiment Payload (BEP) BEP was an independent cylindrical aluminum unit (173 x 198 mm) with thermal control and a leakage rate below 1.9 x 10^-5 Pa-cm2/s. It featured a 0.42L bio-activity space, a water storage bag (18 ml), light-guiding fibers for natural sunlight, and CCD cameras for monitoring. The payload used vermiculite as a substrate and utilized a water-soluble fiber to secure materials during launch. ## 3. Results of biological experiments on the lunar surface BEP was switched on Jan 3, 2019. Water was injected 30 minutes later. Cotton seeds germinated at 22h, with first leaves at 82h. Growth arrested after 190h. Seedlings exhibited curved stems and wrapped, curled leaves, differing from terrestrial controls. Despite surviving a lunar night at -52 C, the seedlings shriveled by the third lunar day. The payload operated for 1300 hours over five lunar days. ## 4. Discussion and Conclusion The experiment proved the feasibility of lunar cultivation using natural sunlight. Key findings include the specialized phenotypes developed in microgravity and the survival of extreme cold. Future research must address soil improvement, ISRU, and utilization of lunar lava tubes for sustainable bases.
