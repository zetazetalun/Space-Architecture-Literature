## ABSTRACT
The authors propose a framework to utilize an ordinary portland cement (OPC) alternative class of materials for both extraterrestrial habitats and terrestrial 3D construction applications. Magnesia-based binders (MBBs) show promise for additive construction (AC) due to superior properties (high compressive strength, rapid setting) and the possibility of harvesting them from lunar and Martian regolith. The primary objective is to prove the concept of utilizing magnesia phosphate cement (MPC) composites with additives like boric acid, graphene nanoplatelets (GnP), and regolith simulants. Experimental results show that boric acid improves the initial setting time and alters binding products. Artificial neural networks (ANN) are used to optimize formulations for real-time adjusting of 3D printing processes.

## INTRODUCTION
Planetary autonomous construction faces challenges such as the scarcity of printable materials and material science issues specific to planetary surfaces. Utilizing in-situ resources (ISRU) for habitat construction on the Moon and Mars reduces payload costs. Magnesium phosphate cement (MPC), formed by the reaction between magnesium oxide and a soluble phosphate compound, is a candidate binder as both Moon and Mars surfaces are rich in magnesium-bearing minerals. However, its extremely prompt setting time (seconds) usually excludes it from AC technology without retarders.

## METHODOLOGY
The study examines the effect of:
1. **M/P Ratio:** Magnesia to phosphate molar ratio.
2. **W/P Ratio:** Water to phosphate molar ratio.
3. **B/M Ratio:** Boric acid to magnesia mass ratio.

**Characterization Scheme:**
- **Physical:** Initial setting time (Vicat apparatus).
- **Mechanical:** Compressive strength (ASTM C109, 7-day curing).
- **Modeling:** ANN models to predict fresh and hardened properties.

## RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
- **Influence of Boric Acid:** Initial setting time increases with B/M ratio up to a maximum at 5% boric acid. For certain mixes, setting time increased from 3.5 to 15 minutes. Beyond 5%, setting time may reduce due to fluidity loss.
- **Compressive Strength:** High M/P ratios (6–8) generally yielded better results depending on the W/P ratio. Adding boric acid reduced compressive strength significantly in low M/P ratio specimens (21-64% reduction) but had marginal effects on high M/P ratio specimens.
- **ANN Simulation:** A 5-5-3 feed-forward backpropagation model successfully predicted MPC properties (flow, setting time, strength) with high confidence (R² training = 0.93).

## CONCLUSIONS
MPC fresh and hardened properties can be tailored for 3D printing by manipulating M/P, W/P, and B/M ratios. Using a molar ratio representation provides better understanding than weight ratios. Up to 5% boric acid improves initial setting time (workability window), though it affects the compressive strength of low M/P ratio pastes.
