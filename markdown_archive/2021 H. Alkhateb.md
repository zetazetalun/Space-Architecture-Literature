# Optimizing Magnesium Phosphate Binders with Boric Acid for Additive Construction Applications

**Authors:** H. Alkhateb; H. Almashaqbeh; J. Edmunson; M. Fiske; Y. Najjar; and Damian Stoddard  
**Publication:** Earth and Space 2021, ASCE

## Abstract
The authors propose a framework to utilize magnesia-based binders (MBBs) as an ordinary portland cement (OPC) alternative for extraterrestrial habitats. MBBs show promise for additive construction (AC) due to high compressive strength, rapid setting, and potential for harvesting from lunar and Martian regolith. The research focuses on magnesia phosphate cement (MPC) with boric acid additives to optimize setting time for 3D printing. Artificial neural networks (ANN) are used to investigate and optimize formulations.

## Introduction
Additive Manufacturing (AM) in construction reduces costs and time, but planetary autonomous construction faces challenges regarding printable materials in extreme environments. Utilizing in-situ resources (ISRU) for habitat structures on the Moon and Mars reduces Earth-launched payloads. MPC, formed by reacting magnesium oxide with phosphate compounds, is a candidate binder as Moon/Mars surfaces are rich in magnesium-bearing minerals. However, MPC's extremely rapid setting time (seconds) must be controlled for AC.

## Magnesium Phosphate Cements
MPC belongs to the chemically bonded ceramics (CBCs) group. The main reaction produces K-struvite (MgKPO4·6H2O). The study explores factors tailoring MPC properties: M/P (magnesia to phosphate) molar ratio, W/S (water to solid) ratio, and retarders like boric acid (H3BO3).

## Methodology
The experimental program studied the effect of boric acid on MPC performance using M/P, W/P, and B/M (boric acid to magnesia) ratios. ANN models were developed to predict performance.

### Materials
- **MgO:** Dead-burned magnesium oxide (98% purity).
- **Phosphate:** Monopotassium phosphate (KDP).
- **Retarder:** Boric acid (99.9% purity).

## Results and Discussion
- **Influence of Boric Acid:** Increasing B/M ratio up to 5% increases initial setting time. Beyond 5%, setting time may decrease due to fluidity loss. Boric acid forms a temporary lunebergite layer that hinders cation dissolution.
- **Mechanical Properties:** For low M/P ratios, boric acid significantly reduces compressive strength (up to 64% reduction at 10% B/M). For high M/P ratios, the effect is marginal.
- **ANN Simulation:** A 5-5-3 feed-forward backpropagation model successfully predicted MPC properties (flow, setting time, strength) with an R2 of 0.89.

## Conclusions
MPC fresh and hardened properties can be tailored for 3D printing by manipulating M/P, W/P, and B/M ratios. Using up to 5% boric acid improves setting time for construction, but excessive amounts impact strength. Molar ratio representation provides better understanding than weight ratios for MPC behavior.
