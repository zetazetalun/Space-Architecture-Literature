### Abstract
The dust vacuum chamber is an essential infrastructure for the verification of space exploration equipment, especially extraterrestrial missions for in situ resource utilization (ISRU). This study presents a new approach in soil mechanics that is particularly applicable to the dust vacuum chamber. One of the main issues in operating the dust vacuum chamber is soil disturbance problems during the evacuation. Thus, the appropriate vacuum rate was suggested to minimize the soil disturbance in the dust vacuum chamber using theoretical and numerical approaches. Firstly, the simple analytical solutions were used to provide approximate maximum discharge velocity and evacuation time to finish air flow through a soil specimen at a given vacuum pressure. Then, a two dimensional finite element model was developed which simulates the hydro-mechanical behavior of the soil specimen in a vacuum chamber with respect to different vacuum rates. From the numerical analysis results, optimum guidelines have been proposed to ensure that soil disturbance is minimized.

### Introduction
Developing and verifying exploration equipment for lunar surface missions requires performance tests in a dirty vacuum chamber (DVC). A primary challenge is soil disturbance caused by depressurization, which alters the initial condition of soil bins. This study uses poroelasticity theory and finite element analysis to establish vacuum operation guidelines that minimize these disturbances.

### Poroelasticity Theory
The study applies Biot’s (1941) poroelasticity theory. The constitutive equation relates stress, strain, and pore air pressure:

$$\sigma = C\epsilon - \alpha_B u_a I$$

Where:
- $\sigma$ is total stress
- $\epsilon$ is strain tensor
- $u_a$ is air pressure in the pore
- $\alpha_B$ is the Biot-Willis coefficient
- $C$ is the elastic matrix

Seepage stress $j$ leads to disturbance when it exceeds the dead load of the soil specimen. The maximum air velocity limit ($q_{max}$) is defined as the point where seepage force exceeds the soil's weight.

### Results and Discussion
As absolute pressure approaches vacuum, air velocity increases rapidly due to exponential pressure gradients, inducing high drag forces. For the tested properties, the calculated $q_{max}$ (causing soil disturbance) was approximately 0.000235 m/s. At a vacuum rate of -0.33 kPa/sec, disturbance occurred at -94 kPa. By reducing the rate to -0.11 kPa/sec before reaching the critical velocity, the air velocity dropped, preventing disruption. No disruptions are typically observed below 2.5 Torr regardless of pump rate.

### Conclusion
1. Poroelasticity theory provides insights into soil hydro-mechanical behavior during depressurization.
2. Finite element models can determine appropriate vacuum rates to prevent soil disturbance.
3. Vacuum rates must be controlled adequately until the pressure reaches a stable threshold (e.g., 2.5 Torr) to maintain specimen integrity.
