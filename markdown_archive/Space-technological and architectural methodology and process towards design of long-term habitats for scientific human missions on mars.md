# Space-technological and architectural methodology and process towards design of long-term habitats for scientific human missions on Mars

## Abstract
The study presents Martian Habitat Units (MHUs) designed for clusters of 10 units, each supporting 9 crew members. The design moves beyond mere survival shelters to provide fulfilling environments for multi-year missions. Core concerns include redundant systems, geometric modularity, and reduced construction costs for deep space.

## Method Details
**Subject area:** Space Technology and Architecture
**Method name:** Iterative Design
**Keywords:** MHUs, Modular design, Hierarchical modulation, Closed-loop systems, 3D printing.

## Design Requirements
The project utilizes NASA-STD-3001 and COSPAR policies. Key considerations include environmental hazards (low gravity, radiation, dust storms), habitat comfort (pressurization, ventilation, thermal control), and spatial requirements (personal suites, laboratories, medical areas).

## Martian Habitat Units (MHUs)
MHUs are designed as two-decked habitats within a rhombus-shaped cluster. The design features:
- **Modular Construction:** 4x4x4m modules.
- **Hierarchy:** Four-layered hexagonal construct with a growth rate of sqrt(3).
- **Interior:** Includes gardens, working areas, and private suites with virtual reality interfaces.
- **Protection:** Anti-Dust Settlement Membrane (ADSM) on top to protect photovoltaic arrays.

## Manufacturing and Conclusion
Habitats are built via programmed robotic agents using in-situ Martian materials (regolith) and AI-assisted 3D printing. The study concludes that modular design and high levels of redundancy in energy and life support are vital for the feasibility of long-term Martian settlement.
