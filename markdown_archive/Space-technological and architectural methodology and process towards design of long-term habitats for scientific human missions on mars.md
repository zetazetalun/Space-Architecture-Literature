# Space-Technological and Architectural Methodology for Martian Habitats

## Overview
The paper details an iterative design process for Martian Habitat Units (MHUs), intended for long-duration scientific missions. It shifts focus from minimal survival shelters to fulfilling environments that support crew well-being over several years.

## Design Methodology
- **Iterative Process**: Uses a multi-step flowchart involving mission specifications, site selection, spatial requirements, and output design.
- **Modularity**: Implements a four-layered hierarchical hexagonal module system to reduce construction and maintenance costs.
- **Redundancy**: Adopts an 'Active/Active' operational mode with three-fold redundancies for critical life support and power systems.

## Construction and Technology
- **ISRU & 3D Printing**: Proposes AI-assisted 3D printing using granulated Martian regolith as the primary structural material.
- **Enclosure**: Features a double-layered pressure vessel and an adjustable Anti-Dust Settlement Membrane (ADSM) to protect solar arrays from dust accumulation.
- **Power Architecture**: Integrates nuclear fission (Kilopower) as primary, multi-junction PV as secondary, and wind turbines as tertiary power sources.

## Spatial Configuration
- Each unit accommodates up to 9 crew members.
- Interior includes gardens for psychological health and food production.
- The design utilizes virtual reality interfaces to compensate for the lack of traditional windows.
