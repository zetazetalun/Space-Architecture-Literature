# Fabrication of Flexible Thermoelectric Energy Harvesting System

## Abstract
Thermoelectric energy harvesting is promising for sustainable power in extraterrestrial environments. This paper presents an electrically parallel thermoelectric energy harvester design on flexible substrates. Theoretical analysis and experimental validation using thick film printing indicate that this parallel architecture achieves higher energy efficiency compared to conventional series-connected modules.

## Introduction
Thermoelectric generators (TEGs) are critical for autonomous space missions where solar energy is intermittent or weak. Existing TEGs often suffer from low efficiency due to material property mismatch in series architectures. This research proposes an electrically parallel structure to improve efficiency and simplify manufacturing for large-area flexible applications.

## Key Sections
- **Electrically Series TEM Efficiency:** Analysis of how material mismatches (p-type vs n-type) degrade overall module performance.
- **Electrically Parallel TEM Efficiency:** Derivation showing that parallel connections mitigate performance losses caused by property mismatch.
- **Fabrication Process:** Detailed steps using thick film printing with Bi2Te3-epoxy composite inks on flexible polyimide substrates.
- **Characterization:** Testing using a trans-impedance amplifier circuit and a custom temperature-controlled experimental setup.

## Conclusion
The electrically parallel TEM structure is a viable, high-efficiency solution for extraterrestrial energy harvesting. While initial prototypes used planar printing, future work focuses on multilayered, sandwiched structures to scale production.
