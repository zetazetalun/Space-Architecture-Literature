## Abstract
A critical aspect of human space exploration and eventual settlement is the ability to construct habitats while minimizing payload mass launched from Earth. We propose fungal bio-composites for growing extra-terrestrial structures directly at the destination. These composites are fire-resistant, insulating, do not consist of VOCs, and can be used independently or in conjunction with regolith. This research builds on the "Myco-architecture Off Planet" NASA NIAC project.

## 1 Introduction
Building on the Moon and Mars involves trade-offs between transport mass ($2,720–$10,000 per kg to LEO) and the need for structural materials, radiation protection, and infrastructure. Mycelium-based bio-composites are proposed as Engineered Living Materials (ELMs) to provide regenerative and adaptive habitats.

## 6 Off-Earth Myco-architecture
Two main strategies are proposed:
1. **Standalone Approach (Class II):** Using sealed deployable bags with lightweight scaffolding for mycelium to grow independently.
2. **Regolith Biocomposite (Class III):** Mycelium acts as a binder for Martian/Lunar regolith, reducing the need for imported biomass.

## 10 Conclusion
Myco-architecture offers a low-mass, energy-efficient alternative to traditional ISRU methods. It enables self-healing structures and the potential for integrated life-support systems through bio-engineering.
