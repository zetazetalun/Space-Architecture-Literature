# Biological growth as an alternative approach to on and off-Earth construction

**Authors:** Monika Brandić Lipińska, Chris Maurer, Dave Cadogan, James Head, Martyn Dade-Robertson, Ivan Glaucio Paulino-Lima, Chen Liu, Ruth Morrow, Debbie G. Senesky, Magdalini Theodoridou, Maikel C. Rheinstädter, Meng Zhang, and Lynn J. Rothschild.

**Published:** 19 September 2022  
**Journal:** Frontiers in Built Environment  
**DOI:** 10.3389/fbuil.2022.965145

## Abstract
A critical aspect of human space exploration is the ability to construct habitats while minimizing payload mass launched from Earth. This paper proposes the use of fungal bio-composites for growing extra-terrestrial structures directly at the destination. These mycelium-based composites are fire-resistant, insulating, and can be used independently or in conjunction with regolith to create structures resilient to extra-terrestrial hazards.

## 1. Introduction
The cost of transporting 1 kg of mass to LEO is between $2,720 and $10,000. Using local resources (ISRU) is crucial. This research builds on the "Myco-architecture off planet" NASA NIAC project, exploring mycelium-based biocomposites as an alternative to traditional rigid or inflatable modules.

## 4. Mycelium as a medium
Fungal mycelium consists of branching hyphae. When grown as a composite, it creates a light fibrous structure with excellent mechanical and radiation protection properties without outgassing. Properties can be adjusted during growth.

## 6. Off-Earth Myco-architecture
Two main strategies are proposed:
1. **Standalone approach:** Mycelium grows in a sealed, deployable inflatable bag with lightweight scaffolding.
2. **Regolith biocomposite:** Mycelium acts as a binder for Martian/Lunar regolith, reinforcing the loose soil to create stable structures with minimal added biomass.

### 6.3 Environmental Testing
Samples are tested in a "Planet Simulator" capable of controlling temperature (-30°C to 130°C), humidity, radiation (UVC/UVB), and pressure (0.8–760 Torr).

## 10. Conclusion
Mycotecture offers a low-upmass, sustainable solution for space architecture. Future developments in Engineered Living Materials (ELMs) will provide functionally graded materials capable of self-healing and sensing environmental stimuli.
