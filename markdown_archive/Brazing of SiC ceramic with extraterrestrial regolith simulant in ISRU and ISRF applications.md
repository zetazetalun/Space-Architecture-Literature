# Brazing of SiC ceramic with extraterrestrial regolith simulant in ISRU and ISRF applications

**Authors:** Wei Zheng, Guofu Qiao  
**Publication:** Advances in Space Research 70 (2022) 752–761  
**DOI:** 10.1016/j.asr.2022.05.015

## Abstract
In-situ resources play an important role in future extraterrestrial human activities for facility repair and habitat construction, especially in sustainable space exploration of the Moon and Mars. This paper verifies the feasibility of lunar and Martian regolith simulant for ceramic joining in which SiC ceramic was made into specific size samples and then brazed with the regolith under argon atmosphere, respectively. The average ultimate shear strength of joints of SiC brazed with lunar regolith simulant (LRS-SiC), and SiC brazed with Martian regolith simulant (MRS-SiC) was 21.5 ± 6.5 MPa and 27.3 ± 13.4 MPa, respectively.

## 1. Introduction
Establishing a permanent lunar base requires in-situ resource utilization (ISRU) and in-situ fabrication and repair (ISFR) technologies to reduce mass, cost, and risk. High-temperature bonding of regolith is a simple process requiring only heat. This study explores brazing—a common method for joining ceramics—using molten regolith as a filler material to bond SiC components.

## 3. Results and Discussion
- **Wetting Behavior:** Both simulants fully melted before 1400 °C. LRS and MRS exhibited good wettability on SiC substrates at high temperatures.
- **Shear Strength:** The LRS-SiC joints reached 21.5 ± 6.5 MPa, while MRS-SiC joints reached 27.3 ± 13.4 MPa.
- **Bonding Mechanism:** Interfacial reactions produced TiC and Fe3Si. Molten glass from the regolith infiltrated the surface pores of the SiC, creating a mechanical and chemical bond.
- **Thermal Compatibility:** The Coefficient of Thermal Expansion (CTE) mismatch between the regolith glass and SiC was managed by the reaction layers, preventing joint failure during cooling.

## 4. Conclusions
This study proves the feasibility of using regolith as a brazing filler for ceramic joining. This method can support equipment maintenance and infrastructure fabrication in future Moon and Mars missions.
