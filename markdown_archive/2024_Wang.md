# To boldly go where no one has gone before: Sulfur concrete, a promising construction material fulfilling the demands for a sustainable future on celestial objects: A review

**Authors:** Q. Wang, D. Snoeck
**Publisher:** Elsevier, Materials Today, Volume 72, January/February 2024

## Abstract
The need to build a long-term or even permanent base is now a significant concern with the development of the exploration of extraterrestrial celestial bodies. Sulfur concrete, first proposed as a new building material in the 20th century, has attracted interest as sulfur is one of the most accessible resources on the Moon and Mars via In-Situ Resource Utilization (ISRU). This review paper summarizes the development of sulfur concrete, its unique advantages as a waterless material, and its potential for recyclability and circularity in extraterrestrial environments.

## Introduction
Human space exploration goals by NASA (Artemis), ESA, and China emphasize a sustainable long-term presence on the Moon and Mars. Local resources (ISRU) are critical to avoid the prohibitive costs of transporting materials from Earth (est. $130,000/kg). Sulfur concrete is a high-strength, chemical-resistant material that solidifies when cooling below 140°C, requiring no water for mixing.

## Characteristics of Sulfur Concrete
Sulfur concrete forms a binder through the solidification of molten sulfur. Key advantages include:
1. **Waterless:** No water needed for fabrication.
2. **Fast Curing:** High early strength achieved upon cooling.
3. **Recyclability:** Can be remelted and recast without loss of performance.
4. **Sustainability:** Utilizes waste sulfur or locally extracted sulfur (ISRU).

## Building on the Moon and Mars (ISRU)
- **Moon:** Sulfur is found as troilite (FeS). Extraction with 90% yield is possible by heating to 1000°C using solar energy.
- **Mars:** Mars is sulfur-rich (SO3 up to 37 wt%).
- **Automated Construction:** Techniques such as Contour Crafting (extrusion), Selective Laser Melting (SLM), and Fused Deposition Modeling (FDM) are proposed for printing domes and structures using sulfur/regolith mixes.

## Challenges and Solutions
- **Sublimation:** In a vacuum, sulfur can sublimate. A 30 cm layer of loose regolith can act as insulation to prevent this.
- **Phase Transformation:** Shrinkage (up to 7%) occurs during cooling (monoclinic to orthorhombic phase). Modified sulfur (e.g., using dicyclopentadiene - DCP) prevents macro-crystal formation and increases durability.
- **Thermal Fluctuations:** Use of Phase-Change Materials (PCMs) or protective coatings can mitigate internal stresses caused by high thermal gaps (-233°C to 123°C on the Moon).

## Conclusion
Sulfur concrete is a promising extraterrestrial construction material due to its mechanical properties, waterless nature, and ISRU potential. Future research must focus on long-term durability in vacuum/thermal cycling and the development of large-scale automated construction systems.
