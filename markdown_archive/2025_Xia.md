# Lunar base infrastructure construction: Challenges and future directions\n\n## Abstract\nAs deep space exploration advances, agencies such as NASA and ESA, along with other nations, have developed mid-to-long-term plans for lunar habitation. This article reviews existing lunar construction concepts, analyzing their importance for sustained human presence. It summarizes research progress in transportation, navigation, communication, energy, and habitation infrastructure, addressing construction processes, site selection, building materials, and structural design.\n\n## 1. Introduction\nGlobal space initiatives recognize the Moon's strategic advantages: helium-3 resources, low-gravity environment, and its role as a stepping stone for Mars. Key programs include the US Artemis Program, China's International Lunar Research Station (ILRS), and ESA's Terrae Novae strategy. Constructing in extreme environments requires overcoming bottlenecks in materials science, manufacturing, and intelligent equipment.\n\n### Technical Clusters:\n- Multi-scale characterization of lunar regolith.\n- Autonomous construction processes (concrete-like materials, high-energy sintering).\n- Intelligent construction equipment (RASSOR rover, mobile 3D printing, robotic masonry).\n\n## 2. Transportation Infrastructure\n- **Landing and Launching Pads:** Essential for dust mitigation and stability. Methods include robotic teleoperation and in-situ manufacturing (e.g., vacuum sintering of interlocking bricks).\n- **Roadbed and Pavement:** Utilizing ISRU (microwave heating or solar sintering) to stabilize the surface for heavy machinery.\n- **Elevators:** A long-term conceptual strategy (Galactic Harbor) for cost-effective cislunar transport using carbon nanotubes or graphene.\n\n## 3. Navigation and Communication\n- **Navigation:** NASA's PNT architecture relies on ground stations, lunar orbit satellites (LunaNet), and surface assets.\n- **Communication:** Utilizing 3GPP/5G cellular technology and RTG-powered fixed/mobile stations to ensure high-bandwidth data transmission.\n\n## 4. Energy Infrastructure\n- **Solar Power:** Proposed 100 kW solar towers and folding panel designs (56% weight reduction).\n- **Nuclear Power:** Fission surface power concepts providing a stable 40 kW supply.\n- **Energy Storage:** Utilizing processed regolith for thermal storage (retaining 95% energy during lunar night) and regenerative fuel cells.\n\n## 5. Habitation Infrastructure\n- **Launched Habitation:** Class I (Rigid) and Class II (Inflatable/Expandable, e.g., NASA TransHab).\n- **In-situ Habitation (Class III):** 3D printing (e.g., 'Lunar Ampullae') and masonry assembly (e.g., 'Xuan Wu' plan using sintered bricks).\n- **Lava Tubes:** Natural underground hollows providing radiation shielding and thermal insulation.\n\n## 6. Challenges and Future Directions\n1. **Material Scaling:** Difficulty in producing large structural components from regolith.\n2. **Transportation:** Lack of regular crew/cargo ferry systems.\n3. **Energy:** Current designs (40 kW) are insufficient for industrial-scale operations.\n4. **Communication:** Interoperability and signal stability in complex topography.\n5. **Life Support:** Long-term durability of ecological recycling systems.\n\n## References\nSummarized from 134 highly relevant documents (2020-2024).
