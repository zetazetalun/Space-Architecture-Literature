# Eco-friendly additive construction: Analysis of the printability of earthen-based matrices stabilized with potato starch gel and sisal fibers

## Abstract
3D printing for construction is a disruptive technology with potential for rapid applications in social housing, disaster relief, and extraterrestrial habitats. This study focuses on earthen-based matrices using raw soil stabilized with potato starch gel and reinforced with sisal fibers to improve fresh and hardened properties for 3D printing.

## 1. Introduction
3D printing (additive manufacturing) in construction offers environmental, economic, and design benefits. It reduces waste, improves safety, and allows for complex bio-inspired geometries. For remote or extreme environments, cement-based materials are problematic due to availability and carbon footprint. Earthen construction using local soil is a viable alternative.

## 2. Additive construction using earthen-based matrices
### 2.1. Summary of recent advances
The literature review indicates that 46% of publications on soil-based 3D printing focus on regolith-based printing for extraterrestrial construction (e.g., NASA Centennial Challenge). Technologies include sintering (microwave, solar, laser) and extrusion of regolith-binder mixtures.

### 2.2. Colibri System
A gantry-type 3D printing system scaled prototype (1 x 1 x 1 m) was developed at PUCP. It uses a mortar pump (MAI 2PUMP-PICTOR) and a 25mm diameter nozzle, controlled via G-code and Arduino Mega 2560.

## 3. Analysis of Printability
### 3.1. Materials
- **Base Soil:** Low plasticity sandy clay (CL) from Carabayllo, Peru.
- **Stabilizer:** Food-grade potato starch (amylose content 18.2%).
- **Reinforcement:** Sisal fibers (10 mm length, 137 μm diameter).

### 3.2. Experimental Program
The study tested various liquid percentages, starch concentrations (2.5%, 5%), and fiber content (1%). Tests included extrudability, pumpability, shear vane (yield strength), cylinder stability (settlement), stacking, shrinkage cracking, and compressive strength.

## 4. Results and Discussion
- **Fresh State:** Yield strength and cylinder settlement are critical. Starch increases viscosity, requiring higher liquid percentages. Fibers help lower mixture viscosity and allow extrusion with less liquid.
- **Shrinkage:** Unreinforced mixtures failed with cracks up to 8.49 mm. 1% sisal fibers reduced cracking to <1 mm (0.60 mm for 5% starch).
- **Mechanical Strength:** Compressive strength increased significantly with starch gel. The 5% starch mixture (EFS5.0) achieved 2.2 MPa at 28 days (157% increase over control).
- **Durability:** Starch gel significantly reduced water capillarity absorption coefficients.

## 5. Conclusions
The Colibri system successfully printed earthen-based specimens. The combination of 5% potato starch gel and 1% sisal fibers provided the best results for additive construction, offering improved strength, reduced cracking, and lower water absorption.
