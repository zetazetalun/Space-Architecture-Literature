# Eco-friendly additive construction: Analysis of the printability of earthen-based matrices stabilized with potato starch gel and sisal fibers

## Abstract
3D printing for construction purposes is a disruptive technology with potential for extraterrestrial habitats. This study evaluates earthen-based matrices using potato starch as a natural stabilizer and sisal fibers to control shrinkage cracking. Results indicate that matrices with 1% sisal fibers and up to 5% starch gel show improved workability, reduced cracking, and adequate mechanical properties for additive manufacturing.

## 1. Introduction
Additive construction (3D printing) reduces material waste and provides design flexibility. Raw earth construction requires significantly less energy than conventional concrete and is suitable for remote locations where cement is unavailable.

## 2. Additive construction using earthen-based matrices
### 2.1. Summary of recent advances
A literature review indicates that 46% of publications on local soil 3D printing focus on regolith-based applications for extraterrestrial construction (e.g., NASA Centennial Challenge). Technologies include solar, microwave, and laser sintering of regolith simulants.

### 2.2. Colibri: 3D Printing System
The 'Colibri' prototype is a gantry-type system with a 1m x 1m x 1m volume, utilizing a progressive cavity pump for continuous extrusion of high-viscosity earthen mixtures.

## 3. Materials and Methods
* **Base Soil**: Low plasticity sandy clay (CL).
* **Stabilizer**: Aqueous potato starch gel (gelatinized at 90°C).
* **Reinforcement**: 10mm sisal fibers (1% by weight).
* **Testing**: Extrudability, shear vane (yield strength), cylinder stability (settlement), stacking (buildability), and uniaxial compression.

## 4. Results and Discussion
* **Extrudability**: Lowering liquid percentage increases buildability but raises pump pressure. Optimized mixtures operated below the 15-bar pump limit.
* **Shrinkage Cracking**: Control mixtures failed with 8.49mm cracks. Fibered mixtures (EFS series) passed with cracks < 1.0mm (0.60-0.80mm).
* **Mechanical Strength**: Starch and fiber addition increased 28-day compressive strength to 2.2 MPa (157% increase over control).
* **Buildability**: Control soil supported 16 layers, while 5% starch gel mixtures supported 12 layers due to thixotropic effects.

## 5. Conclusions
The integration of potato starch gel and sisal fibers produces a printable earthen composite with low environmental impact and improved mechanical resistance, suitable for extreme environment construction.
