# Utilisation of Sulphur By-Products in Diverse Civil Engineering Applications: A Systematic Review

## Abstract
Sulphur, a major by-product of the oil and gas industry, has emerged as a promising construction material in both sulphur concrete (SC) and sulphur-extended asphalt (SEA) applications. The review examines mixed formulations, mechanical properties, durability metrics, and innovative applications ranging from acid-resistant industrial structures to sustainable pavement materials and even extraterrestrial construction. Key advantages include rapid strength gain (~50 MPa within 1–2 days), chemical durability, and full recyclability. Future research directions include improving ductility, advancing 3D printing techniques, and field validation.

## 1. Introduction
Sulphur concrete (SC) is a cement-free composite produced by mixing molten sulphur (130–140 °C) with dry aggregates. It gains strength upon cooling rather than hydration, eliminating curing requirements and enabling rapid development. It reduces CO2 emissions by at least 40% compared to Portland cement.

## 2. Methodology
This systematic review followed PRISMA guidelines. The search across Scopus and Web of Science yielded 912 records, narrowed down to 13 primary studies for deep review, supplemented by broader bibliometric analysis from the 1970s to 2025.

## 3. Materials and Processing
Typical SC contains 15–30% sulphur binder and 70–85% aggregate. Additives like dicyclopentadiene (DCPD) at 5–10% reduce crystallinity and shrinkage. Aggregates must be completely dry and preheated to ~150 °C to avoid moisture-related issues during mixing.

## 4. Extraterrestrial Construction (Section 3.4 Summary)
Sulphur concrete is considered a leading candidate for lunar and Martian bases due to:
- **ISRU:** Sulphur is available in lunar/Martian regolith or extracted from minerals.
- **Waterless:** No water required for production, a critical advantage in space.
- **Performance:** Good vacuum and low-temperature performance; strength is retained in cold environments.
- **3D Printing:** Recent experiments (Gruber et al., Boldini et al.) have successfully printed regolith-sulphur components robotically.

## 5. Conclusions
Sulphur-based materials represent a paradigm shift, transforming industrial waste into high-performance resources. They are particularly attractive for waterless/remote/extraterrestrial construction. Key gaps include long-term behavior under fire/high heat and the need for standardized design codes.
