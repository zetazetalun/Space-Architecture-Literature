# To boldly go where no one has gone before

**Authors:** Q. Wang, D. Snoeck
**Journal:** Materials Today, Volume 72, January/February 2024
**Publisher:** Elsevier
**DOI:** 10.1016/j.mattod.2023.12.005

## Abstract
The need to build a long-term or even permanent base is now a significant concern with the development of the exploration of extraterrestrial celestial bodies. Sulfur concrete was first proposed as a new building material in the 20th century. Recently, sulfur concrete has attracted much interest, as sulfur is considered one of the most accessible resources on the Moon and Mars, thanks to the in-situ resource utilization methodology. This review paper summarizes the history of sulfur concrete development and different modified sulfur concretes. Previous research on extraterrestrial building materials is also reviewed. The unique advantage of sulfur concrete as an extraterrestrial material is justified, as no water is used during mixing. Lunar and Martian soil simulants are also examined as possible aggregate types.

## Key Advantages of Sulfur Concrete in Space
1. **Waterless:** Mixing does not require water, a scarce resource.
2. **ISRU Potential:** Sulfur is abundant on the Moon (as troilite) and Mars (sulfates/sulfur-rich soil).
3. **Recyclability:** The material is thermoplastic and can be remelted and reused.
4. **Rapid Strength Gain:** Reaches full strength in hours upon cooling.
5. **Low Thermal Conductivity:** Provides good insulation (0.128 W/m·K).

## Challenges and Solutions
- **Sublimation:** In a vacuum, sulfur can sublimate at high temperatures (e.g., 120°C on the lunar surface). Protection with regolith layers is proposed.
- **Thermal Expansion:** Mismatch between sulfur and aggregate expansion coefficients can cause internal stress. Modification with polymers (e.g., DCPD) is suggested.
- **Brittleness:** Internal stresses from phase transformation during cooling can be mitigated via fiber reinforcement and polymer modification.

## Additive Manufacturing Techniques Mentioned
- **Contour Crafting:** Extrusion of sulfur-regolith mixtures.
- **Microwave Sintering:** Using microwaves to melt sulfur/aggregate mixtures.
- **Selective Laser Melting:** Precision sintering of powder beds.
