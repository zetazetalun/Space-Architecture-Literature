# Preparation and characterization of elevated and cryogenic temperature-resistant regolith-based epoxy resin composites ## Abstract The construction of lunar bases requires the integration of multiple manufacturing techniques. In-situ utilization of lunar regolith for fabrication has been proven feasible, among which polymer forming possesses promising prospects due to simple manufacturing, excellent mechanical properties, and low energy consumption. This study investigates regolith-based polymer composites prepared via the Taguchi method. Results revealed that curing temperatures and mass ratio of curing agents to the hybrid epoxy matrix significantly affect strength. Maximum 24 h compressive and flexural strength was 156.22 MPa and 103.40 MPa, respectively, remaining stable after ten freeze–thaw cycles. ## 1. Introduction ISRU technology provides a promising solution for lunar base construction. Establishing permanent habitats on the Moon requires shielding against radiation, impacts, and temperature fluctuations. Polymerization refers to preparing mortar-type structures with silicate-based material and appropriate additives like epoxy resin. ## 2. Materials and Methods Two lunar regolith simulants (LR150 and SC80) were used. Epoxy resins (AG-80 and E-42) and hybrid curing agents (D400 and MXDA) were mixed with simulants. Specimens were prepared using the Taguchi method L16(4^5) to optimize factors: curing agent ratio, LRS/resin ratio, and curing temperature. ## 3. Results and Discussion Compressive strength increased with curing temperature up to 60°C. SC80 samples (finer particles) showed higher strength due to better bonding. Maximum strengths reached 156.22 MPa (compressive) and 103.40 MPa (flexural). Freeze-thaw tests (180°C to -196°C) showed the material maintains structural integrity and strength over 160 MPa. ## 4. Conclusion 1. LRS (LR150 and SC80) can be used as feedstock for polymer concrete. 2. Optimal formulation: curing agent/resin/LRS = 0.4:1:4 at 60°C. 3. Increased curing agent accelerates curing and enhances strength through flexible polyether chains.
