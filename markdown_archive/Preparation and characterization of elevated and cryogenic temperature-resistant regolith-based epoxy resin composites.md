<h1>Preparation and characterization of elevated and cryogenic temperature-resistant regolith-based epoxy resin composites</h1> <p><strong>Authors:</strong> Chengwei Bao, Yanen Wang, Ray Tahir Mushtaq, Xiaohu Chen, Zhisheng Liu, Xinpei Li, Minyan Liu</p> <p><strong>Journal:</strong> Construction and Building Materials 387 (2023) 131560</p> <h2>Abstract</h2> <p>The construction of lunar bases, with their crucial role in future deep space exploration and extraterrestrial resource harvesting, requires the integration of multiple manufacturing techniques. Selecting appropriate solutions to meet the feature requirements is of particular importance. Extreme environments on lunar surfaces pose uphill challenges for construction, and transportation between Earth-to-Moon involves costs and risks. In-situ utilization of lunar regolith for fabrication has been proven feasible, among which polymer forming possesses promising prospects due to its sample manufacturing process, excellent mechanical properties, and low energy consumption. To investigate the feasibility of regolith-based polymer composites, specimens with varying proportions were prepared based on the Taguchi method. The lunar regolith simulants (LRS) and resulting polymer were characterized by laser particle analyzer and X-ray diffraction (XRD), Fourier Transform Infrared Spectroscopy (FTIR), and scanning electron microscopy (SEM). Their elevated (180 C) and cryogenic (-196 C) temperature resistance properties were investigated by compressive and flexural strength tests after shifting freeze-thaw cycle tests. The results revealed that curing temperatures and mass ratio of curing agents to the hybrid epoxy matrix significantly affect the compressive strength and flexural strength, respectively. The maximum 24 h compressive and flexural strength of regolith-based polymer composites was obtained as 156.22 MPa and 103.40 MPa, respectively. And remained stable over 160 MPa and 70 MPa after ten freeze-thaw cycles. Microstructural results imply variations in the curing agent's and aggregate's ratio, which tentatively explain the reaction mechanism of regolith-based polymer composites. This study demonstrates the feasibility of preparing polymer utilizing LRS that accommodates immense temperature fluctuation on the Moon, with undemanding fabrication, excellent mechanical properties, and low energy consumption, providing potential applications for future lunar construction, especially for rapid repair and reinforcement of launch pads and runways.</p> <h2>1. Introduction</h2> <p>Humans never stop dreaming of exploring other planets. The Moon, as the closest celestial body to Earth, is rich in various mineral and energy resources... NASA, ESA, CNSA, and other major space agencies have all exhibited the long-term goal of exploring inhabited space and building lunar bases. The prohibitive cost of transporting source materials from Earth has significantly restricted lunar construction. Therefore, in-situ resource utilization (ISRU) technology provides a promising solution for lunar base construction. Sintering, concrete, and polymerization are primary schemes for consolidating and hardening lunar soil to develop construction materials. Polymerization refers to preparing mortar-type, three-dimensional network structures with appropriate additives, such as alkaline solution, epoxy resin, and polylactide.</p> <h2>2. Material and methods</h2> <p>Two types of lunar regolith simulant powder, LR150 and SC80, were used. Epoxy resins (E42, AG-80), polyetheramines (D400), m-xylylenediamine (MXDA), and silane coupling agent (KH-560) were used as received. LRS was ground in a ball mill. The optimal formula of each component was determined by the Taguchi method (L16 orthogonal array), varying curing agent mass, simulant mass, and curing temperature.</p> <h2>3. Results and discussion</h2> <p>The Taguchi analysis revealed the maximum compressive and flexural strength of SC80 samples was 156.22 MPa and 103.40 MPa respectively. The optimal test combination was A2B4C3 (curing agent ratio 40%, LRS ratio 400%, curing temperature 60 C). The stress-strain curves after 1, 5, and 10 freeze-thaw cycles (-196 C to 180 C) showed that compressive strength increased marginally and maintained around 165 MPa, while flexural strength stabilized above 70 MPa. The system exhibited excellent low-temperature resistance and could accommodate the extreme temperature of the Moon.</p> <h2>4. Conclusion</h2> <p>(1) Utilized lunar regolith simulants LR150 and SC80 can be used as feedstock. The optimal formulation was curing agent: epoxy matrix: simulant = 0.4:1:4 at 60 C. (2) Increased curing agent accelerated the curing process, and the introduction of flexible polyether chains and multi-functional epoxy groups provided excellent mechanical properties. (3) The material is well-adapted for the extreme environment of the Moon and could be used for rapid repair of damaged structures and building severely temperature-resistant surfaces.</p>
