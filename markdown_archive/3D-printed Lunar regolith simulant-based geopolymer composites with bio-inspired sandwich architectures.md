# 3D-printed Lunar regolith simulant-based geopolymer composites with bio-inspired sandwich architectures

**Journal:** Journal of Advanced Ceramics
**Year:** 2023, 12(3): 510–525
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.26599/JAC.2023.9220700

## Abstract
Over time, natural materials have evolved to be lightweight, high-strength, tough, and damage-tolerant due to their unique biological structures. This study reports an ink-based 3D printing strategy for structural design of a Lunar regolith simulant-based geopolymer (HIT-LRS-1 GP). High-precision carbon fiber/quartz sand-reinforced biomimetic patterns inspired by the cellular sandwich structure of plant stems were fabricated. The study demonstrates how cellular sandwich structures balance the structure–property relationship and achieve unprecedented damage tolerance for geopolymer composites. Components exhibited stable non-catastrophic fracture characteristics and mechanical anisotropy.

## 1 Introduction
Human exploration of the Moon requires in situ resource utilization (ISRU) to minimize transportation costs. Geopolymers are potential alternatives to cement for Moon construction because raw materials can be obtained from Lunar regolith. While geopolymers have high strength, their inherent brittleness remains an obstacle. This study explores bio-inspired structural design (honeycomb, triangular, wave, and rectangular) to enhance toughness and damage tolerance.

## 2 Materials and Methods
### 2.1 Preparation of Lunar regolith simulant
- **Simulant:** HIT-LRS-1 (designed based on Apollo 12, 16, and 17 samples and JSC-1A).
- **Components:** Basalt (40 wt%), plagioclase (20 wt%), forsterite (10 wt%), and others.
- **Synthesis:** Ball milling and sieving to match the particle size distribution of Lunar regolith.

### 2.2 3D printing of geopolymer inks
- **Inks:** Short carbon fiber (average length 600 µm) and quartz sand reinforced geopolymer.
- **Process:** Direct Ink Writing (DIW) using a 0.82 mm needle.
- **Curing:** 7 days at room temperature followed by 2 days at 60 °C.

### 2.3 Design of biomimetic structures
Four regular cellular sandwich architectures inspired by the plant *Elytrigia repens*:
1. Honeycomb
2. Triangular
3. Wave
4. Rectangular

## 3 Results and Discussion
### 3.1 Rheological properties
The addition of quartz sand and carbon fibers increased initial viscosity and yield stress, facilitating smooth extrusion and rapid solidification. The Herschel–Bulkley model was used to characterize the pseudoplastic behavior.

### 3.2 Mechanical properties
- **Fiber Alignment:** Fibers showed excellent directional arrangement parallel to the extrusion direction.
- **Flexural Strength:** Reached a maximum of 41.6 MPa at 1.0 vol% carbon fiber content (a 50.7% increase over unreinforced geopolymer).
- **Compressive Strength (Y-axis vs Z-axis):**
  - **Honeycomb:** 15.6 MPa (Y) / 46.7 MPa (Z)
  - **Triangular:** 17.9 MPa (Y) / 26.5 MPa (Z)
  - **Wave:** 11.3 MPa (Y) / 23.8 MPa (Z)
  - **Rectangular:** 20.1 MPa (Y) / 34.4 MPa (Z)

### 3.3 Fracture Behavior
All bio-inspired patterns exhibited a pseudo-plastic (non-catastrophic) fracture behavior, characterized by a linear elastic stage, plateau stage, and compression stage. This is a significant improvement over the typical brittle failure of geopolymers.

## 4 Conclusions
The study demonstrated successful 3D printing of Lunar regolith simulant-based geopolymers with high spatial resolution. Bio-inspired architectures provide anisotropic mechanical properties and high crashworthiness. Compressive strengths in the Z-axis (up to 46.7 MPa) outperform most state-of-the-art 3D-printed cement/geopolymer composites, making them robust candidates for Lunar base construction.
