# 2023 3D-printed Lunar regolith simulant-based

**Journal:** Journal of Advanced Ceramics  
**Year:** 2023, 12(3): 510–525  
**DOI:** 10.26599/JAC.2023.9220700  

## Abstract
Over time, natural materials have evolved to be lightweight, high-strength, tough, and damage-tolerant due to their unique biological structures. This study reports an ink-based 3D printing strategy for the structural design of a Lunar regolith simulant-based geopolymer (HIT-LRS-1 GP). High-precision carbon fiber/quartz sand-reinforced biomimetic patterns inspired by the cellular sandwich structure of plant stems were fabricated. The results showed stable non-catastrophic fracture characteristics and significant anisotropy in mechanical properties. Compressive strengths reached up to 46.7 MPa in the Z-axis, indicating robust potential for Lunar base construction.

## 1 Introduction
Human exploration of the Moon necessitates the use of In Situ Resource Utilization (ISRU) to minimize transportation costs. Geopolymers are identified as promising building materials for Moon bases as they can be synthesized from Lunar regolith with minimal Earth-delivered additives. However, their inherent brittleness remains a challenge. This study uses biological inspiration to design sandwich architectures (honeycomb, triangular, wave, and rectangular) to enhance toughness and damage tolerance.

## 2 Materials and Methods
### 2.1 Preparation of Lunar regolith simulant (HIT-LRS-1)
The simulant was designed based on JSC-1A, simulating Maria geological terrain. Raw materials included basalt, plagioclase, forsterite, albite, ilmenite, hematite, silica, alumina, and cristobalite powders.

### 2.2 3D printing of geopolymer inks
Inks consisted of HIT-LRS-1 GP reinforced with chopped carbon fiber (average length 600 μm) and quartz sand. Direct Ink Writing (DIW) was used with a 0.82 mm diameter needle, 40 mm/s speed, and 70–80 psi pressure.

### 2.3 Design of biomimetic structures
Four regular cellular sandwich architectures were constructed: (1) honeycomb, (2) triangular, (3) wave, and (4) rectangular, inspired by the rhizome of *Elytrigia repens* and bamboo.

## 3 Results and Discussion
### 3.1 Rheological properties
The addition of quartz sand and carbon fibers significantly improved the printability of the geopolymer inks by increasing the yield stress (τ0) and apparent viscosity. The 1Csf/20QS@HIT-LRS-1 GP ink exhibited ideal shear-thinning behavior.

### 3.2 Mechanical properties and fracture behavior
- **Anisotropy:** Compressive strengths in the Z-axis (deposition direction) were significantly higher than in the Y-axis.
- **Damage Tolerance:** All biomimetic structures showed non-catastrophic fracture behavior, characterized by a pseudo-plastic plateau stage.

## 4 Conclusions
Successful DIW of Lunar regolith-based geopolymer composites was achieved. Biomimetic sandwich designs provided excellent crashworthiness and strength, outperforming bulk materials and many Earth-based 3D-printed alternatives. These architectures are robust candidates for future Lunar base construction.
