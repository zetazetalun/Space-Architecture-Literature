# Silicate-lunar regolith composite: A vacuum self-hardened and comprehensively durable extraterrestrial construction material

**Journal:** Construction and Building Materials 449 (2024) 138517  
**Authors:** Zifan Geng, Lizhi Zhang, Zhiwen Wu, Jiale Huang, Xiangyu Wang, Ming Jen Tan, Wei She, Hao Zhou, Guoqing Geng

## Abstract
Extraterrestrial exploration, represented by Lunar base construction, has been promoted worldwide as a multi-disciplinary, cutting-edge and strategic issue. Herein we proposed a novel extraterrestrial construction material composed of Lunar/Martian regolith simulants and only 4 wt% of silicate adhesive. This silicate-regolith composite can be directly self-hardened in vacuum within 4.5 h without any curing to achieve 29 MPa of hardening strength. Meanwhile, it shows comprehensive durability with stable microstructures and >70 % of residual strength against long-term vacuum exposure, huge temperature difference and intense γ-ray and proton radiations. Its in-situ solidification and durability originate from vacuum dehydration, where silica clusters in solution spontaneously cohere into a solid network with strong connections of silica tetrahedra and bound water. A moderate silicate modulus around 2.5 and appropriate modifications can disperse finer silica clusters to increase oligomer silica units in solution and enhance hardening strength. Compared with lunar geopolymer and other existing extraterrestrial construction materials, this vacuum self-hardening composite allows a cryogenic preparation even below -40 °C using diverse regolith with a wide range of compositions and sizes, proposing a facile, durable and versatile solution towards in-situ extraterrestrial constructions.

## 1. Introduction
The infrastructure on the Moon will face rigorous environments including huge temperature differences (-200 °C to 120 °C), high vacuum (10^-10 - 10^-9 Pa), intense radiation, and potential meteoroid impacts. In-situ resource utilization (ISRU) is critical due to high transport costs. Current methods like sintering require high energy (>1000 °C), while polymers or sulfur binders face durability risks. Lunar geopolymers require high-temperature curing (>60 °C) for long periods.

## 2. Materials and Methods
### 2.1 Materials
- **Regolith Simulants:** LRS-1 (Apollo-17 like), LRS-2 (Apollo-14 like), LRS-3 (Apollo-12 like), and MRS (Martian Regolith Simulant).
- **Binder:** Sodium Silicate (SS) solution (30 wt% solution). Modulus varied (2.0, 2.5, 3.0). Modifiers included sodium pyrophosphate (P), sodium dodecyl sulfate (SDS), and hydrothermal treatment (HT).

### 2.2 Sample Preparation
Regolith and silicate solution (0.14:1 ratio) were mixed in a hermetic environment. Powders were cold-pressed into 40x10x10 mm³ green bodies under 9.5 MPa and immediately placed in a vacuum environment (<10 Pa).

## 3. Results and Discussion
- **Hardening:** Achieved ~29 MPa compressive strength within 4.5 hours in vacuum without heating.
- **Cryogenic Preparation:** Successfully hardened at -40 °C, achieving 19.54 MPa compressive strength after 15 hours.
- **Durability:** Reserved 71-81% of flexural strength and 88-98% of compressive strength after 4 months of simulated lunar diurnal cycles.
- **Microstructure:** SEM and EDS showed stable bridging of regolith particles by silicate membranes. Porosity increased slightly within the first lunar day but stabilized thereafter.

## 4. Conclusion
The Silicate-Lunar Regolith Composite (SLRC) offers a low-energy, ISRU-based solution for lunar construction. Its unique vacuum self-hardening mechanism allows for construction in shaded regions and cryogenic polar locations, using minimal Earth-shipped material.
