# Mechanical properties of meteoritic Fe–Ni alloys for in-situ extraterrestrial structures\n\n## Abstract\nA critical requirement for outposts and settlements on the Moon and Mars is the use of in-situ resources to create pressurized, human-rated habitats with low mass, high reliability, and easy processability. This study assesses the capability of binary Fe–Ni alloys - with Ni content of 5–11 wt%, typical of M-type asteroids and iron meteorites. Tensile properties increase linearly with Ni content: yield stress from 260 to 440 MPa and UTS from 340 to 560 MPa. A modeled 400 m³ habitat with 1 bar atmosphere requires Fe-7Ni plates of 10 mm thickness to prevent plastic deformation, confirming the feasibility of using processed meteorites for habitat shells.\n\n## 1. Introduction\nExtraterrestrial settlements require pressurized habitats made from materials with high tensile strength and toughness. ISRU is favored due to high Earth-to-orbit transport costs. Sintered regolith is weak in tension; Fe-based alloys are better candidates for pressurized thin shells. Fe–Ni alloys are abundant as meteorites on Moon/Mars surfaces or in asteroids. NASA rovers have already identified multiple metallic meteorites on Mars.\n\n## 2. Experimental Study\nBinary Fe-xNi alloys (x = 5, 7, 9, 11, 13 wt%) were arc-melted and studied in as-cast conditions. Tensile testing at 21 °C and compression testing down to -89 °C were conducted.\n\n### Table 1: Experimental tensile data measured at 21 °C\n| Alloy | Ni % | Grain size (μm) | Yield stress (MPa) | UTS (MPa) | Strain at Failure (%) |\n|---|---|---|---|---|---|\n| Fe–5Ni | 5.0 | 90 | 260 | 342 | 20 |\n| Fe–7Ni | 7.1 | 86 | 369 | 427 | 19 |\n| Fe–9Ni | 9.2 | 87 | 383 | 444 | 7 |\n| Fe–11Ni| 10.9| 91 | 439 | 561 | 3 |\n\n## 3. Finite Element Modeling\nFEA was performed using COMSOL to simulate a half-cylindrical 'Quonset hut' habitat (5m radius, 10m length). For a 101 kPa internal pressure under regolith shielding, a shell thickness of 10 mm using Fe–7Ni is sufficient to remain safely below the yield stress despite stress concentrations at welds.\n\n## 5. Conclusions\nFe–Ni alloys from meteorites are suitable for load-bearing structures. Strength increases with Ni content and decreasing temperature. 12 metric tons of alloy are required for a 20-person habitat, which is achievable with 20–30 harvested meteorites.
