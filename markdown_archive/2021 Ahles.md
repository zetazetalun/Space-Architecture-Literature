# Mechanical properties of meteoritic Fe–Ni alloys for in-situ extraterrestrial structures

## Abstract
A critical requirement for outposts and settlements on the Moon and Mars is the use of in-situ resources to create pressurized, human-rated habitats. This study assesses binary Fe–Ni alloys with Ni content of 5–11 wt%, typical of M-type asteroids and iron meteorites. Hardness and tensile properties were measured at 21 ◦C in the as-cast state. As Ni concentration increases from 5 to 11 wt%, yield stress increases from 260 to 440 MPa and UTS increases from 340 to 560 MPa, while ductility decreases from 20 to 3%. A pressurized habitat (Quonset hut) was modeled via FEA, determining that 10 mm thick Fe–7Ni plates are sufficient to prevent plastic deformation under 1 bar pressure on the Moon or Mars.

## 1. Introduction
Extraterrestrial habitats require materials with high tensile strength and toughness. ISRU is favored due to transport costs. Fe-Ni alloys from meteorites are pre-alloyed and reduced, requiring less energy than regolith-based iron extraction. NASA rovers have identified multiple metallic meteorites on Mars, providing a ready source of material.

## 2. Experimental Study
Binary Fe-xNi alloys (x = 5, 7, 9, 11, 13 wt%) were arc-melted and studied in as-cast condition. Tensile testing and compression testing (at temperatures from -89 to 21 ◦C) were performed.

## 3. FEA Modeling
A half-cylindrical pressurized habitat (5 m radius, 10 m length) was simulated with flat Fe-Ni plates. The model included regolith shielding and a 1 bar internal atmosphere. Results indicate 10 mm thick Fe-7Ni plates are sufficient, with maximum Mises stresses remaining below the yield stress except at weld joints, which undergo minor plastic hinging.

## 5. Conclusions
Fe–Ni meteorites can credibly be used after melting and casting to create metal sheets for pressurized habitats. A ~400 m³ habitat requires 12–31 MT of alloy, equivalent to 24–60 medium-sized meteorites.
