# Bio-Manufacturing of Engineered Living Materials for Martian Construction: Design of the Synthetic Community

**Authors:** Nisha Rokaya, Erin C. Carr, Richard A. Wilson, Congrui Jin  
**Publication:** ASME Journal of Manufacturing Science and Engineering, August 2025, Vol. 147  
**DOI:** 10.1115/1.4068792

## Abstract
As the next step in extraterrestrial exploration, many engineers and scientists revealed their intense interest in enabling multiplanetary human life, including colonizing Mars. This study demonstrates that architecture on Mars can be realized by designing a synthetic community, including diazotrophic cyanobacteria and filamentous fungi, which produce large amounts of biomaterials to bond Martian regolith particles into a consolidated body. Through 3D printing, a wide range of structures can be fabricated, such as buildings, houses, tables, and chairs. Within the synthetic community, diazotrophic cyanobacteria will (1) fix carbon dioxide and dinitrogen from the atmosphere and convert them into oxygen and organic nutrients to help the survival and growth of filamentous fungi and (2) increase the concentration of carbonate ions by photosynthetic activities. Filamentous fungi will (1) bind metal ions onto fungal cell walls and serve as nucleation sites for bio mineral production and (2) enhance the growth of cyanobacteria by providing them with water, minerals, and carbon dioxide. In this study, such coculture systems have been created and displayed robust growth solely based on Martian regolith simulants, air, light, and an inorganic liquid medium without any additional carbon or nitrogen sources.

## 1. Introduction
The goal of this project is to bring in game changers—radical changes to the state-of-the-art construction technologies such that the construction process can adjust to the restricted resources and adverse environments on new planets. The project aims to engineer microorganisms to create biomaterials that glue Martian regolith particles into structures.

## 2. Background and Motivation
To realize in situ utilization of Martian regolith, various methodologies for bonding regolith particles have been proposed, including magnesium-based cement, sulfur concrete, and geopolymer concrete. However, these require significant human assistance. Microbe-mediated self-growing technology (like Myco-Architecture) offers an autonomous alternative but currently lacks independence from external nutrient supplies. This project aims for a completely autonomous system using a synthetic lichen-like community.

## 3. Design Considerations
*   **Bonding Agent:** Heterotrophic filamentous fungi are selected over bacteria due to superior survivability and ability to promote larger amounts of biomineral precipitates.
*   **Self-Sustaining System:** Pairing fungi with photoautotrophic diazotrophic cyanobacteria allows for a system where the phototroph provides organic nutrients while the heterotroph enhances phototroph growth.

## 4. Materials and Methods
*   **Strains:** Alkaliphilic strains were sourced from extreme environments (e.g., Nottingham Serpentine Barrens) to match the high pH (9.5) of Martian regolith simulant MGS-1.
*   **Testing:** Four-step investigation: survivability testing, mutualism testing, quantitative characterization (resazurin assay, phycocyanin assay), and material characterization (SEM/EDS).
*   **Sugar Detection:** Benedict’s test was used to confirm that cyanobacteria produce reducing sugars to support fungal growth.

## 5. Results
### 5.1 Survivability and Mutualism
Six stable symbioses were identified:
1. *Anabaena sp.* + *Penicillium sipitatus*
2. *Anabaena sp.* + *Aspergillus niger*
3. *Anabaena sp.* + *Trichoderma reesei*
4. *Anabaena sp.* + *Trichoderma viride* (HF3TV)
5. *Anabaena sp.* + *Trichoderma viride* (HF3MP)
6. *Anabaena sp.* + *Aspergillus flavus*

### 5.2 Biomaterial Characterization
*   *T. reesei* precipitates prism-like crystals.
*   *T. viride* (HF3MP) precipitates plate-like crystals.
*   *P. sipitatus* precipitates crystals with gel-like structures.
*   EDS analysis confirmed the crystals are mainly calcium carbonate.

## 6. Implementation for 3D Printing
The project is developing a regolith ink for 3D printing using Direct Ink Writing (DIW). The ink consists of:
*   Cyanobacterial cells
*   Fungal cells
*   Martian regolith
*   Alginate (for initial physical crosslinking via calcium ions in regolith).

Biomineralization provides the permanent bonding of the structure within one month.
