# Optimizing cementitious mortars for lunar construction: a multi-criteria decision-making approach

**Authors:** Mohammad Mehdi Karbala, Sheida Goudarzi, Hamid Reza Nejati & Masoud Abedini
**Journal:** Journal of Sustainable Cement-Based Materials, 2026, Vol. 15, No. 1, 73–94
**DOI:** 10.1080/21650373.2025.2558809

## Abstract
This study investigates eight cementitious materials for lunar construction to support sustainable habitats in the Moon’s extreme environment. The materials evaluated include geopolymer, sintered material, polymer-bound regolith, regolith-based magnesium oxychloride, Ordinary Portland cement (OPC), sulfur concrete, microbial-induced calcite precipitation (MICP), and geo-thermite products. Evaluations were conducted using fifteen criteria through the Entropy Weight Method (EWM) and Multi-criteria decision-making (MCDM) techniques (TOPSIS, VIKOR, and WASPAS). Geopolymer and sintered material emerged as top performers.

## 1. Introduction
Building habitats on the Moon requires leveraging In Situ Resource Utilization (ISRU) to avoid the high costs of transporting materials from Earth. Moon conditions include 1/6th gravity, vacuum (3.03 Pa), radiation, and temperature swings (-173 °C to 127 °C). Binders discussed for space construction include OPC, AAC, Geopolymer, Sulfur, MSBB, and novel materials like sintered bricks and MICP.

## 2. Methodology
The study evaluates eight materials against 15 criteria: availability, shipping feasibility, water requirements, technical working conditions, curing time, operating temperature, total energy consumption, strength, durability, cosmic radiation shielding, required additives, sustainability, safety, and recyclability.

### 2.1 Construction Materials
1. **OPC:** Proven but requires significant water and Earth-sourced precursors.
2. **Sulfur Concrete:** Abundant on Moon, no water needed, but brittle and temperature-sensitive.
3. **Geopolymer Concrete:** High strength, uses regolith, low energy, but requires alkali activators.
4. **Sintered Materials:** Fuses regolith with heat (solar/microwave), no binders needed, energy-intensive.
5. **Polymer-bound Regolith:** Flexible, lightweight, but relies on imported polymers.
6. **Geo-Thermite Reactions:** Strong, metallic products, but complex process and energy-heavy.
7. **Magnesium Oxychloride:** Fast-setting, strong, but needs chloride sources and moisture-sensitive.
8. **MICP:** Eco-friendly, self-healing, but requires controlled biological environments.

### Table 2. Advantage and Disadvantage of Methods

| :--- | :--- | :--- |

| Polymer-bound | Flexible, lightweight, radiation shield | Requires imported polymers, degrades over time |

## 4. Conclusion
Geopolymer and sintered materials emerged as the top construction materials for lunar habitats based on the average ratings across MCDM methods. Temperature was identified as the most critical weighting factor in the decision-making process. The framework provides a systematic approach for material selection in extreme environment construction.
