# Optimizing cementitious mortars for lunar construction: a multi-criteria decision-making approach

**Authors:** Mohammad Mehdi Karbala, Sheida Goudarzi, Hamid Reza Nejati & Masoud Abedini  
**Journal:** Journal of Sustainable Cement-Based Materials (2026), Vol. 15, No. 1, 73–94  
**DOI:** 10.1080/21650373.2025.2558809

## Abstract
This study investigates eight cementitious materials for lunar construction to support sustainable habitats in the Moon's extreme environment. The materials—geopolymer, sintered material, polymer-bound regolith, regolith-based magnesium oxychloride, Ordinary Portland cement (OPC), sulfur concrete, microbial-induced calcite precipitation, and geo-thermite products—were evaluated using fifteen criteria, including availability, water use, curing time, temperature resilience, strength, durability, and radiation shielding. The Entropy Weight Method (EWM) identified temperature as the most critical factor. Multi-criteria decision-making (MCDM) techniques (TOPSIS, VIKOR, and WASPAS) ranked geopolymer and sintered material as the top performers.

## 1. Introduction
In situ resource utilization (ISRU) is critical for long-term planetary missions. The Moon's environment (low gravity, vacuum, meteorite impacts, radiation, and temperature extremes from −173°C to 127°C) requires specialized construction materials. This study aims to provide a comprehensive comparison of proposed binders for lunar habitats using an MCDM framework.

## 2. Methodology
### 2.1 Construction Materials Evaluated
*   **Ordinary Portland Cement (OPC):** High durability but requires significant water and lacks local lunar limestone.
*   **Sulfur Concrete:** Uses abundant lunar sulfur, waterless, but brittle and temperature-sensitive.
*   **Geopolymer Concrete:** High strength/durability using lunar regolith; requires alkali activators and some water.
*   **Sintered Materials:** Uses solar/microwave energy to fuse regolith; no binders needed but energy-intensive.
*   **Polymer-bound Regolith:** Flexible and lightweight but requires importing polymers from Earth.
*   **Geo-Thermite Reaction Products:** High heat resistance using lunar elements; complex process.
*   **Regolith-based Magnesium Oxychloride:** Fast-setting and strong but needs scarce chlorides.
*   **Microbial Induced Calcite Precipitation (MICP):** Eco-friendly and self-healing; requires a controlled biological environment.

### 2.2 Criteria for Selection
15 criteria were used: Availability, Shipping, Water requirements, Technical working conditions, Curing time, Operating Temperature, Total energy consumption, Strength, Durability, Cosmic radiation shielding (Density and Hydrogen content), Required additives, Sustainability, Safety, and Recyclability.

## 3. Results and Discussion
Using the Entropy Weight Method (EWM), the weights for the criteria were determined. Temperature (0.1657) and Shipping (0.1078) were identified as the most significant factors, while Density (0.0109) and Safety (0.0147) were the least significant.

## 4. Conclusion
Geopolymer and sintered materials emerged as the top candidates for lunar construction based on average ratings across three MCDM models. Polymer-bound regolith and magnesium oxychloride were ranked second, while OPC, sulfur concrete, and MICP were placed lower due to logistics or environmental sensitivities.
