# In-depth analysis of sintering, exposure time, and layer height (um) in LRS 3D printed devices with DLP ## Abstract This study presents a quantitative assessment of the mechanical properties of moon dust (Lunar Regolith Simulants) printed via vat polymerization (DLP). It investigates the effects of layer height (10-60 um), exposure time (3000-11,000 ms), and sintering temperature (1075-1090 C). Results show that 30 um layer height with 11,000 ms exposure time exhibits maximum compressive and flexural strength of 330 MPa and 100 MPa respectively at 1085 C. ## 1. Introduction Space agencies are exploring In-situ Resource Utilization (ISRU) to create building supplies from planetary surfaces. Additive Manufacturing (AM) using regolith can reduce launch costs (currently ~$75,000/kg). This study focuses on vat photopolymerization for high precision and mechanical robustness. ## 2. Experimental - Material: CLRS-2 basaltic simulant (low-titanium). - Slurry: 55% volume LRS in acrylic resin with TPO photoinitiator. - Equipment: Ceramatrix DLP printer (405 nm UV). - Sintering: Two-stage process (debinding up to 600 C, sintering at 1085 C). ## 3. Results - Rheology: 55% solid content exhibited shear-thinning behavior suitable for printing. - Particle Size: Ball milling reduced d50 from 88.2 um to 7.62 um. - Mechanical Properties: Sintering at 1085 C was optimal; higher temperatures caused over-sintering and strength degradation. - Sintering Rate: Fast sintering (3 C/min) produced better densification and higher strength (330 MPa compressive) than slow sintering. ## 4. Conclusion The study confirms that DLP printing of LRS with optimized parameters provides a viable method for creating high-strength structural components for lunar infrastructure.
