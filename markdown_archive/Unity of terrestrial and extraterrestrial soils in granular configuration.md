# Unity of terrestrial and extraterrestrial soils in granular configuration\n\n**Authors:** Jun Zhang, Yong Li, Yifei Cui, et al.\n**Journal:** Earth and Planetary Science Letters 654 (2025) 119239\n**DOI:** [10.1016/j.epsl.2025.119239](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.epsl.2025.119239)\n\n## Abstract\nGrain size distribution (GSD) is crucial for understanding soil properties and surface processes. We find that both terrestrial soils and lunar soils are subjected to a unified GSD function, $P(D)= g(\mu)D^{-\mu}\exp(-D/D_c)$, reducing the textural fractions and grade modes to a parameter pair $(\mu, D_c)$, which unifies terrestrial and extraterrestrial soils in granular configuration, beyond the environments and mechanisms of soil genesis. Random simulation reproduces multiple grade modes and derives the unified GSD function. This GSD-paradigm promotes the generation of lunar regolith data in favor of in-situ resource utilization (ISRU) and base construction on the moon.\n\n## 1. Introduction\nBoth Earth and Moon are covered by soil skins dominating surface processes. While mineral constituents differ, the paradigm of terrestrial soil (TS) studies applies to lunar soil (LS) and martian soil (MS). Challenges exist in data acquisition and generalization due to scarcity of site samples. Simulants are used but often lack mineralogical or geomechanical accuracy. This paper proposes that a unified GSD function can bridge the gap between terrestrial and extraterrestrial materials to facilitate data harmonization and base construction.\n\n## 2. Materials and Methods\n### 2.1 Variety in Textures and Grade Modes\nThousands of samples were analyzed, including Apollo, Luna, and Chang'E mission samples. Textures cover the USDA triangle, presenting unimodal, bimodal, trimodal, and multimodal distributions. LS and MS are predominantly bimodal.\n\n### 2.2 Unity in GSD\nAll soils satisfy the unified GSD function: $P(D) = g(\mu)D^{-\mu}\exp(-D/D_c)$. Rescaled curves collapse upon a unique exponential curve, confirming the function's robustness. This configuration governs major physical/mechanical properties like porosity, density, and bearing capacity.\n\n## 3. Unified Model of Soil Genesis\nSoil genesis is conceptualized as a stochastic process involving: \n1. Fractal fragmentation of parent rocks.\n2. Random contributions from other processes (impacts, transport).\nRandom simulations using Pareto and Normal distributions successfully reproduce the grade modes and the unified GSD function observed in nature.\n\n## 4. Discussions: Application and Implication\n### 4.1 Unified Indicators\nThe $(\mu, D_c)$ pair maps texture fractions to low-dimensional codes. $\mu$ acts as a maturity index for LS. Apollo and Chang'E-5 samples cluster together but differ from Chang'E-6 (farside samples), which align closer to MS groups.\n\n### 4.2 GSD-based Numerical Simulants\nThe GSD-function allows the creation of \"numerical simulants,\" replication of GSD for returned LS samples, and derivation of geotechnical properties as functions of $(\mu, D_c)$.\n\n### 4.3 Prospect of GSD in ISRU\nGSD-fields enable 3D maps of regolith and identification of composites (ilmenite, glass, olivine), which are essential for identifying potential resources and preparing feedstocks for oxygen production.\n\n## 5. Conclusion\nSoils across the solar system are unified in granular configuration. The GSD-paradigm provides a tool for classification, simulation, and 3D regolith mapping, significantly impacting ISRU and space base construction planning.
