# Mechanical Properties and Additive Manufacturing of Alkali-Activated Lunar Regolith in Artificial Lunar Environments

**Zifan Geng, Lizhi Zhang, Zhiwen Wu, Yi Wei Daniel Tay, Sean Gip Lim, and Ming Jen Tan**

## Abstract
Lunar base construction has become a cutting-edge and multi-disciplinary research issue worldwide. The development of sustainable lunar infrastructure is essential for future exploration and a major challenge for constructing on the Moon is the availability of suitable building materials. In this regard, the research on alkali-activated lunar regolith simulants (AALRS) is a significant breakthrough. In this paper, the mechanical properties and additive manufacturing of AALRS are investigated with reference to the lunar environments. The study has shown that the type of alkali, curing temperature and vacuum exposure have a prominent effect on the solidification behaviour of AALRS. Furthermore, the acceptable long-term properties of silicate-based AALRS were also revealed after undergoing several lunar diel temperature cycles. The scanning electron microscopy (SEM) and energy dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS) analysis have indicated the presence of two solidification mechanisms, geopolymerization and dehydration, within AALRS, which have contributed to its microstructure stability. The development of an innovative 3D printer for AALRS is a crucial step towards the future construction of a lunar base. Several conceptualized printers that can extrude, compress, and deposit treated lunar regolith will be presented.

## 1 Introduction
The lunar base requires sustainable construction techniques using in-situ resources (ISRU) due to launch costs and severe lunar environments (temperature differences, high vacuum, radiation, low gravity). Recently proposed schemes include sintered regolith, sulfur concrete, polymer concrete, and lunar geopolymer. Lunar geopolymer (alkali-activated material) shows promise in terms of strength and durability.

## 2 Materials and Experiments
- **Raw Material:** Lunar regolith simulant (LRS) made from volcanic ash (similar to Apollo-17 samples).
- **Activators:** Sodium hydroxide (SH) and sodium silicate (SSx).
- **Testing Conditions:** Vacuum (~10 Pa), thermal cycles (-196 °C to 85 °C).

## 3 Solidification Properties
- Alkali type, curing temperature, and vacuum exposure significantly influence AALRS solidification.
- SH-based AALRS performs poorly in vacuum.
- SS-based AALRS (specifically SS2.0) exhibits superior solidification in vacuum, reaching 4.61 MPa bending strength and 15.5 MPa compressive strength within 5 hours at 34 °C.

## 4 Long-Term Properties
- SS-based AALRS maintains good durability under artificial lunar environments.
- Compressive strength showed little variation after 4 lunar diel cycles (~108 days), while bending strength decreased by ~20% initially but remained stable thereafter (within 5% decrease).

## 5 Microstructure Characterization
- Two mechanisms: **Geopolymerization** (forming N-A-S-H hydrates) and **Dehydration** (forming a Si-rich film/membranate product).
- Dehydration is the primary mechanism for early strength in vacuum conditions.

## 6 Future Prospects on Additive Manufacturing
- A conceptual 3D printer for AALRS is proposed. 
- Unlike cement-based 3D printing, it uses "damp powdery ink."
- The design includes a robotic arm, transport pipe, and a printhead with roller systems to extrude and compress the damp regolith onto surfaces.

## 7 Conclusions
Sodium silicate (SS) is the preferred activator for lunar applications. AALRS demonstrates durability against lunar thermal cycles. A conceptual extrusion/compression-based 3D printer design provides a pathway for sustainable lunar construction.
