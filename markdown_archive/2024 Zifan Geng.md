# Silicate-Lunar Regolith Composite: A Vacuum Self-Hardened and Durably Extraterrestrial Construction Material

## Metadata
- **Journal:** Construction and Building Materials
- **Volume/ID:** 449 (2024) 138517
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.conbuildmat.2024.138517
- **Authors:** Zifan Geng, Lizhi Zhang, Zhiwen Wu, Jiale Huang, Xiangyu Wang, Ming Jen Tan, Wei She, Hao Zhou, Guoqing Geng

## Abstract
Extraterrestrial exploration, represented by Lunar base construction, has been promoted worldwide as a multi-disciplinary, cutting-edge and strategic issue. Herein we proposed a novel extraterrestrial construction material composed of Lunar/Martian regolith simulants and only 4 wt% of silicate adhesive. This silicate-regolith composite can be directly self-hardened in vacuum within 4.5 h without any curing to achieve 29 MPa of hardening strength. Meanwhile, it shows comprehensive durability with stable microstructures and >70 % of residual strength against long-term vacuum exposure, huge temperature difference and intense γ-ray and proton radiations.

## 1. Introduction
Lunar construction faces extreme challenges: 
- Temperature differences (-200 °C to 120 °C)
- High vacuum (10⁻¹⁰ to 10⁻⁹ Pa)
- Intense radiation and micrometeoroids
- High transport costs from Earth

## 2. Materials and Methods
### 2.1 Materials
- **Regolith Simulants:** LRS-1 (Apollo-17), LRS-2 (Apollo-14), LRS-3 (Apollo-12), and MRS (Martian simulant).
- **Binder:** Sodium silicate (SS) solution with varying moduli (2.0, 2.5, 3.0).

### 2.2 Preparation Process
1. Mixing regolith with 4 wt% silicate binder (mass ratio 0.14:1 solution to regolith).
2. Cold-pressing under 9.5 MPa to form a green body.
3. Exposure to vacuum (< 10 Pa) for self-hardening through dehydration.

## 3. Results and Discussion
- **Hardening Performance:** The material achieves ~29 MPa compressive strength in vacuum within 4.5 hours.
- **Environmental Stability:** Maintained >70% flexural strength and >88% compressive strength after 108 days of simulated lunar diurnal cycles.
- **Cryogenic Capability:** Successful hardening at -40 °C (freeze-dried SLRC) achieving ~19.5 MPa compressive strength.
- **Radiation Resistance:** Minimal strength loss after γ-ray and proton radiation exposure.

## 4. Conclusion
The SLRC provides a facile, low-energy, and durable solution for in-situ construction. The unique vacuum self-hardening mechanism allows for automated construction even in shaded/cryogenic regions (like lunar poles) without artificial heat input.
