# Future-Ready Workforce Development: Integrating Computational Thinking into Construction Education Using Virtual Reality

**Hameedreza Gucci et al.**  
*Computing in Civil Engineering 2025, ASCE, pp. 1019-1028*

## ABSTRACT
The construction industry is rapidly evolving as robotics and automation advance. However, traditional construction education often falls short in equipping students with the computational thinking (CT) skills required to address the challenges of technology-driven workplaces. This study aims to bridge this gap by developing a virtual reality (VR) training environment that centers on CT. Through immersive human–robot interaction (HRI) scenarios in both terrestrial and extraterrestrial construction contexts, the VR environment offers hands-on learning experiences to enhance problem-solving and adaptability through iterative learning with progressively complex tasks. Participants engage in structured pick-and-place tasks targeting CT components: decomposition, abstraction, pattern recognition, and algorithmic thinking. A pilot human–subject experiment with five construction management students demonstrated the VR environment’s effectiveness, with results indicating significant improvements in computational thinking and adaptability to novel scenarios.

## INTRODUCTION
The construction industry is undergoing a rapid technological transformation driven by advancements in robotics. This evolution is further underscored by the ambitious goals of NASA and ESA to establish permanent human habitats on the Moon and Mars by 2040. Extraterrestrial construction—building structures on planetary bodies outside Earth's atmosphere—represents a significant frontier. Due to extreme conditions, extraterrestrial construction will be technology-intensive, fundamentally changing the role of construction workers.

## RESEARCH METHODOLOGY

### Scenario Design and Iterative Learning
We developed a series of HRI scenarios to incrementally build CT competency focusing on pick-and-place tasks using a robotic arm mounted on a Mecanum wheel platform. The levels include:
- **Training Task 1:** Simple pick-and-place with static obstacles.
- **Training Task 2:** Incorporates dynamic, moving obstacles.
- **Training Task 3:** Static obstacles with inclines and sloped surfaces.
- **Training Task 4:** Combines dynamic obstacles with inclined surfaces.
- **Testing Task 1:** Realistic terrestrial construction site.
- **Testing Task 2:** Futuristic extraterrestrial construction site featuring low-gravity conditions and unique spatial constraints.

### VR Training Environment Development
The designed scenarios were integrated into a VR training environment using Unreal Engine, replicating real-world and space construction conditions.

## RESULTS AND DISCUSSIONS
A pilot experiment involved five participants from LSU’s Construction Management program. 

### Skill Metrics
- **Accuracy:** All five participants completed tasks with 100% accuracy.
- **Speed & Safety:** Speed improved steadily during training. While Testing Task 1 (terrestrial site) showed challenges, performance improved in Testing Task 2 (extraterrestrial), demonstrating adaptability. Collision rates decreased significantly by the final test.

### Knowledge Metrics
Confidence in decomposition, pattern recognition, abstraction, and algorithmic thinking showed positive trends. Confidence in decomposition remained stable during testing, reflecting effective skill transfer to extraterrestrial tasks.

### Attitude Metrics
Confidence in dealing with complexity improved steadily. Persistence and tolerance for ambiguity demonstrated steady growth, with testing phases showing enhanced resilience.

### Correlations
Negative correlations with speed suggest participants with higher CT competencies prioritize precision and safety over speed. Strong negative correlations between abstraction and safety highlights that advanced cognitive competencies lead to fewer collisions.

## CONCLUSION
The study highlights the effectiveness of VR environments in enhancing cognitive and behavioral competencies for HRI tasks. Enhanced CT competencies contribute to precise and efficient task execution, which is critical for high-stakes extraterrestrial construction scenarios.
