# Future-Ready Workforce Development: Integrating Computational Thinking into Construction Education Using Virtual Reality

## Abstract
The construction industry is rapidly evolving as robotics and automation advance. However, traditional construction education often falls short in equipping students with the computational thinking (CT) skills required to address the challenges of technology-driven workplaces. This study aims to bridge this gap by developing a virtual reality (VR) training environment that centers on CT. Through immersive human-robot interaction (HRI) scenarios in both terrestrial and extraterrestrial construction contexts, the VR environment offers hands-on learning experiences to enhance problem-solving and adaptability through iterative learning with progressively complex tasks. A pilot human-subject experiment with five construction management students demonstrated the VR environment's effectiveness, with results indicating significant improvements in computational thinking and adaptability to novel scenarios.

## Introduction
The evolution in training is further underscored by the ambitious goals of NASA and ESA to establish permanent human habitats on the Moon and Mars by 2040. Extraterrestrial construction—building structures on planetary bodies outside Earth's atmosphere—represents a significant frontier for the construction industry. Due to the extreme and hazardous conditions of outer space, extraterrestrial construction will be technology-intensive, fundamentally changing the role of construction workers.

## Research Methodology
### Scenario Design and Iterative Learning
The study developed six levels of difficulty:
- **Training Tasks 1-4:** Progressive robotic pick-and-place tasks involving static/dynamic obstacles and inclined surfaces.
- **Testing Task 1:** Realistic terrestrial construction site.
- **Testing Task 2:** Futuristic extraterrestrial construction site featuring low-gravity conditions and unique spatial constraints.

### VR Training Environment Development
Scenarios were integrated into a VR training environment using Unreal Engine, replicating real-world and space construction conditions (low gravity, unique terrain).

## Results and Discussions
- **Skill Metrics:** All participants completed tasks with 100% accuracy. Speed and safety improved significantly by the time they reached Testing Task 2 (Extraterrestrial environment).
- **Knowledge Metrics:** Confidence in CT components (decomposition, pattern recognition, abstraction, algorithmic thinking) showed positive trends and stability during testing.
- **Attitude Metrics:** Improvements were noted in handling complexity, persistence, and willingness to learn/adapt.

## Conclusion
The findings highlight the environment's effectiveness at enhancing cognitive and behavioral competencies critical for systematic problem-solving in robotic construction. Strong correlations between CT knowledge/attitudes and performance metrics (safety and energy efficiency) emphasize that enhanced CT competencies contribute to precise task execution in extreme environments.
