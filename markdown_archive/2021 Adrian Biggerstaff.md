# Determining the yield stress of a Biopolymer-bound Soil Composite for extrusion-based 3D printing applications

**Authors:** Adrian Biggerstaff, Gerald Fuller, Michael Lepech, David Loftus
**Journal:** Construction and Building Materials 305 (2021) 124730
**DOI:** 10.1016/j.conbuildmat.2021.124730

## Abstract
Biopolymer-bound Soil Composite (BSC) is a novel class of construction materials with potential use in extraterrestrial infrastructure development and more sustainable construction on Earth. Little is known about the rheological properties of this new material and its suitability for extrusion-based 3D printing (E3DP). This paper presents methods for testing BSC yield stress and proposes hydrodynamic and frictional yield stress models for predicting the static and dynamic yield stress of BSC mixtures. Using experimental results, a soil volume fraction range for BSC is identified that may be suitable for E3DP based on extrudability and shape stability requirements.

## 1. Introduction
Extrusion-based 3D printing (E3DP) has emerged as a viable alternative to form-based construction. Material rheological properties are critical to proper design and control of the printing process. E3DP materials must have a low enough yield stress and viscosity to be pumped and extruded, but high enough static yield stress to demonstrate shape stability. BSC mixtures consist of lunar regolith simulant, bovine blood proteins as the binder, and deionized water. This paper explores the rheological properties of BSC with low-cohesion soil to assess the feasibility of using it for E3DP.

## 2. BSC-E3DP Mixture Design Considerations
For BSC mixtures using low-cohesion soil, strength originates from the biopolymer forming rigid cross-links. Designing pumpable and extrudable mixtures may result in lower final strength. The research limits the BSC soil volume fraction (ϕBSC) to <80% of the soil’s maximum packing fraction (ϕmp) to ensure pumpability.

## 3. Materials and Methods
Seven BSC mixtures were evaluated using JSC-1A Lunar regolith simulant, bovine blood proteins (tradename: AP920), and deionized water. Rheological measurements were performed using an ARES-G2 Rheometer with a vane geometry. Static yield stress was measured via steady stress sweep (SS), and dynamic yield stress via constant shear rate (CSR) tests.

## 4. Results and Discussion
Static yield stress values were higher than dynamic values for ϕBSC < 0.52. Above this value, the two converged within 4%. This suggests thixotropic behavior is related to soil volume fraction and particle reconsolidation. The transition point between the hydrodynamic regime and the frictional regime was identified at ϕBSC = 0.52.

## 7. Conclusion
BSC using low-cohesion soil is suitable for E3DP within a soil volume fraction range of 0.435 ≤ ϕBSC ≤ 0.548. Hydrodynamic (COT) and frictional (DL) models successfully predicted yield stresses within this range. These models assist in assessing printability and shape stability for extraterrestrial habitat construction.
