# Space-native construction materials for earth-independent and sustainable infrastructure\n\n## Abstract\nA successful space exploration requires establishing permanent and earth-independent infrastructure that are not only resilient to the extreme environment of space but also preferably made of sustainable and indigenous \"space-native\" construction materials. This review covers feasibility of exploiting in-situ lunar and Martian resources as well as harvesting of elements and compounds, from near Earth objects (NEOs), to produce extraterrestrial materials suitable for construction of space-based infrastructure. This review also details material features and characteristics required to withstand the unique, and harsh, effects of space environment.\n\n## 1. Introduction\nSpace exploration migration is highly dependent upon development of resilient, economical, and sustainable infrastructure. Transportation costs range from $5,000 to $20,000 per kg to the Moon. Utilizing indigenous resources (ISRU) promotes Earth-independent and sustainable development.\n\n### Table 1: Selected differences between Earth, Moon, and Mars\n| Parameter | Earth | Moon | Mars |\n| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |\n| Total mass compared to Earth (%) | 1.0 | 1.2 | 10.7 |\n| Day duration (hrs) | 23.9 | 655.7 | 24.7 |\n| Average surface temperature (ºC) | 13 | -30 | -57 |\n| Atmospheric pressure (kPa) | 101.3 | negligible | 0.7 |\n\n## 2. Space environment, and resources\n### 2.2.1. In-situ resource utilization (ISRU)\nRegolith is a surficial layer covering the entire lunar and Martian surface. It consists of fine particles formed by meteorite impact. Chemical analysis (Table 2) shows high concentrations of SiO2 (37-45%), Al2O3, and FeO.\n\n### Table 3: Properties of lunar in-situ materials\n| Property | Basalt rock | Cast regolith | Lunar glass |\n| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |\n| Compressive strength (MPa) | 140–290 | 538 | – |\n| Tensile strength (MPa) | – | 34.5 | – |\n| Density (kg/m3) | 2600 | 3000 | – |\n| Modulus of elasticity (GPa) | – | 100 | 450 |\n\n## 3. Properties of space-native construction materials\n### 3.1. Concrete-like hybrids\nTraditional concrete behavior under vacuum leads to water loss, but compressive strength does not necessarily drop. Alternatives include:\n- **Polymer concrete**: Uses epoxy/resin binders (17–60 MPa).\n- **Geopolymers**: Formed by aluminosilicate interaction with alkaline solutions.\n- **Sulfur concrete**: Water-free, rapid curing, made by heating sulfur (120–150 °C). Achieves 39–75.7 MPa (lunar) and 20–63 MPa (Martian).\n\n### 3.2. Metals and alloys\nAbundant elements include Al, Mg, Fe, and Ti. Aluminum and magnesium are suitable for interplanetary construction due to low density and easy processing.\n\n## 4. Design considerations\nStructural design in low gravity (17% Moon, 38% Mars) allows for larger spans and thinner members. Mass can be reduced by up to 90% compared to Earth designs. Radiation shielding requires covering structures with 1-6m of regolith.\n\n## 6. Summary and conclusions\n- Indigenous materials can reduce human settlement costs from $100B to $40B.\n- Sulfur concrete and geopolymers are prime candidates for water-independent construction.\n- Low gravity enables significant savings in material mass and erection time.
