# Mechanical Properties and Additive Manufacturing of Alkali-Activated Lunar Regolith in Artificial Lunar Environments

**Authors:** Zifan Geng, Lizhi Zhang, Zhiwen Wu, Yi Wei Daniel Tay, Sean Gip Lim, and Ming Jen Tan

## Abstract
Lunar base construction has become a cutting-edge and multi-disciplinary research issue worldwide. The development of sustainable lunar infrastructure is essential for future exploration and a major challenge for constructing on the Moon is the availability of suitable building materials. In this regard, the research on alkali-activated lunar regolith simulants (AALRS) is a significant breakthrough. In this paper, the mechanical properties and additive manufacturing of AALRS are investigated with reference to the lunar environments. The study has shown that the type of alkali, curing temperature and vacuum exposure have a prominent effect on the solidification behaviour of AALRS. Furthermore, the acceptable long-term properties of silicate-based AALRS were also revealed after undergoing several lunar diel temperature cycles. The scanning electron microscopy (SEM) and energy dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS) analysis have indicated the presence of two solidification mechanisms, geopolymerization and dehydration, within AALRS, which have contributed to its microstructure stability. The development of an innovative 3D printer for AALRS is a crucial step towards the future construction of a lunar base. Several conceptualized printers that can extrude, compress, and deposit treated lunar regolith will be presented. Additive manufacturing of lunar regolith has the potential to reduce the cost and increase the efficiency of building on the Moon.

## 1 Introduction
Earth is the cradle of humanity, but one cannot remain in the cradle forever. The lunar base has been proposed as a multi-disciplinary, cutting-edge and strategic research issue. Sustainable construction techniques using in-situ resources must be developed due to huge launching costs and severe environments (temperature differences, high vacuum, radiation, and low gravity). Alkali-activated materials (lunar geopolymer) show promising results in terms of strength, durability, and moderate processing conditions.

## 2 Materials and Experiments
### Raw Materials
- **Lunar Regolith Simulant (LRS):** Made from volcanic ash in Huinan Town, Jilin Province. Mean particle diameter: ~40 μm.
- **Activators:** Sodium hydroxide (SH) and sodium silicate (SSx).

### Experimental Setup
Mixtures (13 wt% alkaline solution, 87 wt% LRS) were compressed into blocks under 9.5 MPa. Specimens were tested under vacuum (~10 Pa) and thermal cycles (maximum 85 °C to cryogenic -196 °C via liquid N2).

## 3 Solidification Properties
- SH-based AALRS requires high curing temperatures (82 °C) and performs poorly in vacuum.
- SS-based AALRS exhibits better solidification in vacuum. SS2.0 contributes to higher early strength (4.61 MPa bending, 15.5 MPa compressive within 5 hours).
- Sodium Silicate (SS) with modulus ≥ 2 is the preferred activator.

## 4 Long-term Properties
Under lunar diel temperature cycles (~27 terrestrial days per cycle), SS-based AALRS maintained stability. After 4 cycles (108 days), groups sustained strength with less than 5% decrease. Bending performance showed an initial ~20% reduction with increased brittleness after the first cycle but stabilized thereafter.

## 5 Microstructure Characterization
Two mechanisms identified:
1. **Geopolymerization:** Formation of sodium aluminosilicate hydrates (N-A-S-H).
2. **Dehydration:** Formation of smooth membranate Si-rich films (specifically in SS-based samples) which connects particles and promotes strength in vacuum.

## 6 Future Prospects on Additive Manufacturing
A conceptual 3D printer is proposed involving a robotic arm with a water-impermeable pipe system and a printhead using roller systems to extrude and compress damp powdery "ink."

## 7 Conclusions
AALRS is a viable potential lunar construction material. Sodium silicate is an optimized activator for lunar vacuum conditions. Dual hardening patterns (geopolymerization and dehydration) provide durability under thermal cycling. A conceptual extrusion-compression 3D printer is proposed for future sustainable lunar construction.
