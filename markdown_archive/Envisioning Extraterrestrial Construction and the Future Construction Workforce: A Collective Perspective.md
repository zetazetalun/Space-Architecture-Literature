# Envisioning Extraterrestrial Construction and the Future Construction Workforce: A Collective Perspective

**Authors:** Amirhosein Jafari, Yimin Zhu, Suniti Karunatillake, Jennifer Qian, Shinhee Jeong, Ali Kazemian, and Andrew Webb
**Venue:** 41st International Symposium on Automation and Robotics in Construction (ISARC 2024)

## Abstract
Humanity’s evolution toward an interplanetary species poses a new frontier for the construction industry: extraterrestrial construction. With fast technological advancements in manufacturing and robotics, it is a matter of when, not if, before humans make perpetual habitats on nearby planetary bodies. This paper summarizes the outcomes of a national interdisciplinary workshop structured around three core themes: future work (extraterrestrial construction), future workers (extraterrestrial workforce), and future technology (emerging technologies for workforce training).

## 1 Introduction
NASA and ESA reveal plans to establish permanent human habitats on the Moon and Mars by 2040. Extraterrestrial construction is defined as building anthropogenic environments in outer space to enable temporary or perpetual human presence. Conventional labor-intensive processes are unsuited for space; instead, automated construction processes optimized for the environment and requiring in-situ resource utilization (ISRU) are envisioned.

## 2 Methodology
The study utilized a two-day virtual interdisciplinary workshop titled “Roadmap to Future-ready Extraterrestrial Construction Workforce Development.” A cohort of 26 experts from NASA, R1 research institutions, and industry participated.

## 3 Summary of Outcomes

### Theme 1: Extraterrestrial Construction
- **Site Conditions:** Differences in radiation, gravity, vacuum, and temperature must be determined. Harsh conditions like abrasive regolith and electrostatic dust are critical factors.
- **Protocols:** Conventional tasks change; e.g., low gravity on Mars/Moon means small forces can dislodge massive objects.
- **ISRU:** Necessity due to high delivery costs from Earth. Material resiliency and longevity are vital research areas.
- **Human-Robot Collaboration (HRC):** Construction will be robot-intensive but requires human oversight for troubleshooting, repair, and creative problem-solving.
- **Teleoperation:** Necessary for safety but limited by latency (up to 20 minutes for Mars).

### Theme 2: Extraterrestrial Construction Workforce
- **Situational Awareness:** Crucial for monitoring site hazards despite immediate tasks.
- **Cross-disciplinary Skills:** Workers need knowledge in geology, mechatronics, electrical engineering, and programming.
- **Trust in Technology:** Balancing reliance on AI and autonomous systems with human oversight.
- **Psychological Factors:** Managing fatigue, boredom, and isolation in extreme environments.

### Theme 3: Emerging Technologies for Training
- **AI-XR Training:** Immersive, interactive, and intelligent environments (VR/AR) are essential for simulating low-gravity and high-stress scenarios.
- **Digital Twins:** Effective for simulating physical reactions and physiological impacts.
- **NASA Task Load Index (TLX):** Recommended for measuring subjective mental workload during training.

## 4 Conclusions and Takeaways
Extraterrestrial construction requires a paradigm shift from traditional methods to a technology-intensive, robot-led approach. A future-ready workforce must be trained through immersive simulations that account for the unique constraints of space environments, including sensory deprivation and communication delays.
