# Envisioning Extraterrestrial Construction and the Future Construction Workforce: A Collective Perspective

**Authors:** Amirhosein Jafari, Yimin Zhu, Suniti Karunatillake, Jennifer Qian, Shinhee Jeong, Ali Kazemian, and Andrew Webb  
**Affiliations:** Louisiana State University and Texas State University, USA  
**Publication:** 41st International Symposium on Automation and Robotics in Construction (ISARC 2024)

## Abstract
Humanity’s evolution toward an interplanetary species poses a new frontier for the construction industry: extraterrestrial construction. With fast technological advancements in manufacturing and robotics, it is a matter of when, not if, before humans make perpetual habitats on nearby planetary bodies. This paper summarizes the outcomes of an interdisciplinary workshop structured around three core themes: future work (extraterrestrial construction), future workers (extraterrestrial workforce), and future technology (emerging technologies for workforce training).

## 1. Introduction
NASA and ESA reveal plans to establish permanent human habitats on the Moon and Mars by 2040. Extraterrestrial construction is defined as the theory and practice of building anthropogenic environments in outer space. Conventional construction processes requiring massive human labor are ill-suited for the near-vacuum, radiation, and extreme environments of space. Instead, habitats are envisioned to require automated processes and in-situ construction (ISRU).

## 2. Summary of Outcomes

### 4.1 Theme 1: Extraterrestrial Construction
1. **Site Conditions:** Differences in atmospheric disturbances, quakes, radiation, gravity, and abrasive regolith must be determined.
2. **Protocols:** Conventional tasks (e.g., excavation) change dramatically in low gravity; high momentum objects are dislodged by small forces.
3. **ISRU:** In-situ Resource Utilization is a necessity due to the prohibitive cost of Earth-delivery.
4. **Robotics:** Construction will be robot-intensive. However, human-in-the-loop (HITL) is required for repairs, error intervention, and creative oversight.
5. **Teleoperation:** Latency (e.g., 20 mins for Mars) is a significant limitation for real-time control.

### 4.2 Theme 2: Extraterrestrial Construction Workforce
Required competencies include:
- **Situational Awareness:** Monitoring for unrecognized site hazards.
- **Geological Knowledge:** Understanding planetary rotation, thermal gradients, and fluid pressures.
- **Teleoperation Proficiency:** Transitioning from direct to remote equipment operation.
- **Trust in Technology:** Balancing explainability with performance.
- **Cross-disciplinary skills:** Mechatronics, programming, and safety procedures across domains.

### 4.3 Theme 3: Emerging Technologies for Workforce Training
AI-XR (Extended Reality) is identified as the optimal training tool to simulate:
- Low gravity manipulation.
- Sensory deprivation in space suits.
- Unforeseen hazardous scenarios.
- Interactive collaboration between remote experts.

## 5. Conclusions and Takeaways
Extraterrestrial construction requires a shift from traditional manual labor to managing cyber-physical systems. Training must evolve from behaviorist methods to immersive, learner-centered constructivist paradigms using AI and XR to replicate harsh planetary dynamics.
