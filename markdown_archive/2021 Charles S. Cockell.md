# Microbially-Enhanced Vanadium Mining and Bioremediation Under Micro- and Mars Gravity on the International Space Station

**Published:** 01 April 2021  
**DOI:** 10.3389/fmicb.2021.641387

## Abstract
As humans explore and settle in space, they will need to mine elements to support industries such as manufacturing and construction. We conducted the ESA BioRock experiment on the International Space Station to investigate whether biological mining could be accomplished under extraterrestrial gravity conditions. We tested the hypothesis that the gravity (g) level influenced the efficacy with which biomining could be achieved from basalt by three different microorganisms under microgravity, simulated Mars, and Earth gravitational conditions. The results showed that *Sphingomonas desiccabilis* and *Bacillus subtilis* enhanced the leaching of vanadium under the three gravity conditions compared to sterile controls by 184.92 to 283.22%, respectively. Gravity did not have a significant effect on mean leaching, showing the potential for biomining on Solar System objects with diverse gravitational conditions.

## Introduction
To move permanently into space, we must be able to implement industrial processes that can support settlement, such as mining natural resources. Microorganisms can be used for biomining and bioremediation. Vanadium (V) is a metal of strong industrial interest, used in ferrous and non-ferrous alloys to enhance hardness, tensile strength, and fatigue resistance for structural materials, pressurized buildings, and rovers.

## Methods
### BioRock Experiment
The experiment was performed on the ISS in 2019 using the KUBIK incubator. It compared bioleaching at three gravity levels: microgravity, simulated Mars gravity, and simulated terrestrial gravity.

### Microorganisms
1. *Sphingomonas desiccabilis* CP1D
2. *Bacillus subtilis* NCIB 3610
3. *Cupriavidus metallidurans* CH34

### Substrate
Basalt slides (1.5 cm x 1.6 cm x 3 mm) were used as the substrate. The basalt was an olivine basalt collected near Reykjavik, Iceland, chosen for its similarity to lunar and Martian basalts.

## Results
### Vanadium Mining Efficacy
Across all gravity conditions, *S. desiccabilis* and *B. subtilis* showed significantly higher leaching of vanadium compared to sterile controls. Mean values for *S. desiccabilis* and *B. subtilis* ranged from 19.84 ± 2.29 to 30.38 ± 3.90 ng, while sterile samples ranged from 10.30 ± 2.65 to 10.84 ± 3.19 ng.

## Discussion and Conclusion
Gravity conditions (microgravity, Mars, and Earth) did not significantly influence the final quantity of vanadium leached. This suggests that biological ISRU processes are robust and can be applied across different planetary environments. The BioRock reactor demonstrates the principle of a miniature space biomining reactor that could be upscaled for industrial-scale extraction of elements for extraterrestrial construction.
