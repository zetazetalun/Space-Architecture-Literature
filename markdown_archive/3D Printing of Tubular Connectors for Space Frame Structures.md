## Abstract
In the construction industry, wire-based steel 3D printing (WBS-3DP) technology for structural parts has yet to be explored. As 3D printing becomes highly productive and cost-effective with the development of qualification standards, it opens a wide range of opportunities for the steel-based additive construction segment too. The major advantage of large-scale 3D printing techniques like wire-arc additive manufacturing (WAAM) is the ability to develop components that are more compatible through topology optimization and with the mechanical requirements of the structural standards. This research aims to discuss the application of WAAM in the fabrication of space frame structures for T-K-Y joints with structural integrity. In this study, a space frame structure was designed and developed with multi-branch tube connectors using WBS-3DP technology. These tube connectors are used to accommodate any number of steel tubes at arbitrary angles, such that the resulting T-K-Y joints are smooth and lightweight. It was concluded that WBS-3DP has the potential to revolutionize the construction industry by producing innovative parts such as tube connectors with ease of fabrication.

## 1 Introduction
Additive Manufacturing (AM), or 3D printing, is a layer-based fabrication process. Manufacturing industries such as aerospace, automotive, and energy have a strong interest in realizing metal 3D printing’s full potential. Steel tubes are frequently used in construction for trusses and space frames. The conventional way of T-K-Y connection fabrication requires profile cutting, edge preparation, and complex manual welding, often leading to stress concentration and brittle failure.

## 2 Materials and Methods

### 2.1 Prototype for Tubular Joints Design
A prototype spaceframe structure model was designed to accommodate all possible types of T-K and Y-type tube connections. A topology optimization tool from Altair Inspire Print3D was used for the nodes/tube connectors to reduce material usage and print time.

### 2.2 Tubular Joining Using Additive Manufacturing
The printing was conducted at the Centre for Advanced Welding and Joining (CAWJ), Tata Steel. The facility uses a robotic wire arc additive manufacturing (WAAM) system consisting of Metal Inert Gas (MIG) welding connected to a 6-axis robotic arm.

### 2.3 Printing Process
*   **Feedstock:** Carbon steel wire (0.8 mm diameter)
*   **Shielding Gas:** 82% Ar / 18% CO2
*   **Bead Dimensions:** 2 mm height, 6 mm width
*   **Interpass Temperature:** Kept below 120 °C

### Mechanical Properties
Tensile tests showed no significant differences with respect to the printing direction (isotropic nature). 
*   **Yield Strength:** 395–405 MPa
*   **Tensile Strength:** 497–507 MPa
*   **Elongation:** 32–40%

Microstructure analysis revealed an equiaxed ferrite matrix with 7–10% pearlite. Repeatability of the WAAM procedure was approximately 99%.

## 4 Conclusions
This research successfully demonstrated the feasibility of joining steel tubes using 3D-printed connectors. The technique reduces welding difficulty levels (from 6GR to 5F) and solves skilled personnel shortages. WAAM-produced connectors achieve the desired tensile strength matching conventional tubular steels.
