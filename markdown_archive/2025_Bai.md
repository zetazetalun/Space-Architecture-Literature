# Effect of Low-Temperature Fluctuations on Martian Steel Structures: Numerical and Analytical Studies

**Authors:** Yongtao Bai, Jing Li, and Xuhong Zhou
**Publication:** Journal of Cold Regions Engineering, 2025, 39(2): 04025002
**DOI:** 10.1061/JCRGEI.CRENG-875

## Abstract
To harness a variety of unique resources, humanity is progressively venturing deeper into space. This endeavor necessitates a comprehensive investigation into the formidable challenges presented by hostile environments, particularly the impact of extreme low-temperature fluctuations on extraterrestrial engineering and construction. This paper employs numerical simulation methodologies to scrutinize the displacement variations and stress distribution within a full-scale circular arch steel structure subjected to diverse thermal cycles, thereby assessing the influence of severe cryogenic temperature fluctuations on Martian steel infrastructure. The findings of this research contribute to understanding the resilience of such structures within fluctuating low-temperature conditions, thereby offering an empirical foundation for the architectural design and material selection pertinent to the establishment of a Martian base.

## Introduction
Mars is considered the most similar planet to Earth, making it a primary choice for human migration. Selecting appropriate materials for Martian construction is critical, with metals playing an indispensable role due to their mechanical properties, thermal performance, and recyclability. Steel structures are particularly noted for their low cost, easy processing, and excellent low-temperature resistance compared to titanium or aluminum. Crucially, the availability of iron and nickel in reduced, elemental form on the Martian surface supports the use of in-situ resources (ISRU) for steel production.

## Overview of the Martian Environment
### Low-Temperature Alternating Environment
Mars' diurnal cycle (sol) lasts 24 h, 39 min, and 35 s. Due to the thin atmosphere, temperatures fluctuate significantly, causing thermal stress. This study uses a temperature cycle ranging from +20°C to -110°C over a 24-hour cycle, adopting a triangular wave simulation where cooling lasts 16 hours and warming lasts 8 hours.

### Structure Form and Loading
The research focuses on a semicircular arch steel member with a flat floor. 
- **Structural Radius:** 5 m
- **Thickness:** 50 mm
- **Longitudinal Span:** 10 m
- **Internal Pressure:** 101.325 kPa (1 atm)
- **External Pressure (Regolith):** 18.687 kPa (assuming 3m of regolith shielding)
- **Gravity:** 3.73 m/s²

## Key Findings and Results
- **Structural Integrity:** The structure survived simulated conditions for up to 10 Martian years (6,870 cycles) without cracking or failure.
- **Stress Concentration:** In a simple stress field (no thermal cycles), stress is localized on the arch. After thermal cycling, stress concentration shifts to the floor member and the juncture between the arch and the floor.
- **Displacement Patterns:** Unlike the linear displacement observed in static loading, temperature cycling causes cyclical displacement fluctuations. The maximum displacement at the vault apex recorded was 13.1124 mm after 1,374 cycles, later stabilizing around 12.6 mm in subsequent cycles.
- **Design Recommendation:** Martian habitat designs should incorporate thickened floor members and optimized joints between the arch and floor to mitigate stress concentration and fatigue damage.

## Conclusion
The study confirms that a 50-mm-thick steel arch structure is capable of meeting Martian environmental requirements for a 5-10 year lifespan. While cyclical thermal loading introduces fatigue and alters stress distribution, the material largely remains within the elastic deformation range under the specified conditions.
