# Space-technological and architectural methodology and process towards design of long-term habitats for scientific human missions on mars

**Journal:** MethodsX 11 (2023) 102270  
**Authors:** Kasra Amini, Mojgan Moradi, Bahareh Vossoughi, Ehsan Dehghani Janabadi

## Abstract
Centered on the core idea of long duration habitat design for research crew on Mars, the Martian Habitat Units (MHUs) are designed as a cluster of 10 units each with the maximum capacity of 9 crew members. This manuscript provides detailed insight on the lessons learned regarding comprehensive design criteria, redundancy requirements, and geometric modularity for deep space missions.

## Method Details: Iterative Design
The methodology utilizes an iterative design process based on mission-specific requirements, NASA standards (NASA-STD-3001), and COSPAR policies. The design focuses on settlement-type inhabitation rather than simple survival shelters.

### Key Design Features
1.  **Extreme Redundancies:** Three-fold redundancy in an "Active/Active" operational mode for life support and electrical systems.
2.  **Anti-Dust Settlement Membrane (ADSM):** An inflatable elastic membrane on top of units that deviates wind paths to prevent dust settlement on PV cells.
3.  **Double-Layered Pressure Vessel:** An interspace where pressure is maintained at 80% of 1 atmosphere, creating a two-step pressure increase from outside to inside.
4.  **Additive Manufacturing:** AI-based 3D printing using in-situ materials (Martian regolith) is identified as the ultimate solution for construction to minimize costs and human risk.
5.  **Modular Design:** A 4-layered hexagonal pattern of modules based on a 4x4x4m module size.

## Construction Process
The habitats are designed to be built prior to crew arrival via programmed robotic agents. The process involves robotic arms providing granulated Martian regolith as raw material for 3D printers. The building blocks are hierarchical and interconnected hexagonal modules with a growth rate of √3 among the four layers of the construct.

## Conclusion
The MHU project addresses the transition from survival-only missions to long-term settlement. By utilizing AI-assisted 3D printing and in-situ resource utilization, the payload requirement for construction materials is significantly reduced, making the mission feasible under current launcher advancements.
