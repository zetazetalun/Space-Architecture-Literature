# Extraterrestrial Construction in Lunar and Martian Environments

**Authors:** M. Z. Naser and Qiushi Chen  
**Publication:** Earth and Space 2021, ASCE

## ABSTRACT
Space exploration and terraforming nearby planets have been fascinating concepts for the longest time. Nowadays, technological advancements in manufacturing, robotics, and propellants are thriving, it is only a matter of time before humans can start colonizing nearby moons and planets. In recognition of the 50th anniversary of the first manned lunar landing, NASA, together with the European Space Agency (ESA), revealed plans to establish a permanent human presence (habitats) on the Moon and Mars by 2040. In order to facilitate feasible and sustainable space exploration, such habitats are envisioned to be primarily built from lunar and Martian in-situ resources. This paper explores the suitability of construction materials derived from lunar and Martian regolith, along with terrestrial derivatives, for interplanetary construction. This paper also identifies key processing techniques suitable to produce extraterrestrial construction materials under alien environments (i.e., vacuum, low gravity, etc.) and showcases prominent design concepts for “space-resilient” habitats and colonies.

## EXTRATERRESTRIAL CONSTRUCTION MATERIALS
- **Concrete-like derivatives:** Sulfur concrete, geopolymer concrete, polymer concrete, and waterless concrete.
- **Metals:** Extraction of Aluminum (Al), Magnesium (Mg), Iron (Fe), and Titanium (Ti) from regolith.
- **Advanced materials:** Composites, shape memory materials, and self-healing/sensing materials.

## PROCESSING TECHNIQUES
- **Sintering:** Heating powder below its melting point using solar, microwave (2.45 GHz), or laser energy (reported at 2.12 J/mm²).
- **Cold-pressing:** Leveraging adhesive forces and montmorillonite minerals in regolith to transform material into a solid under pressure.
- **Biocementation (MICP):** Using microorganisms (e.g., *Haematococcus pluvialis*) to precipitate CaCO₃ as a binding agent, potentially Mars-compatible using atmospheric CO₂.

## CONCEPTS FOR HABITATS
1. **Rigid Habitats:** Designed for load actions without secondary systems; high mass and transportation volume.
2. **Inflatable Habitats:** High packing coefficient for shipping; unfolded and inflated via mechanical/automated processes.
3. **Underground Habitats:** Utilization of natural lava tubes to provide shielding and stable environments.
4. **Mobile Habitats:** Concepts involving groups of independent rovers to improve exploration range.
