# Sulfur-based materials for martian construction: Pioneering sustainable extraterrestrial building solutions

**Authors:** Wei Sun, Jiabao Zhao, Handong Yan, Zijin Xiao, Tiejiong Lou  
**Journal:** Alexandria Engineering Journal (2025) 117, 27–44  
**Date:** Available online 11 January 2025  

## Abstract
The overarching purpose of this study is to devise sustainable construction materials and techniques for Martian infrastructure. Challenges include water scarcity and extreme temperatures. The study utilizes Martian soil (MS) simulant and sulfur as a binder. Key findings reveal that MS simulant enhances brick compressive strength (20% content gives 25.2 MPa), 3 mm is the optimal bond thickness (1.42 MPa bond strength), and sulfur-based bricks are recyclable. The study explores pyramid-shaped structures for load distribution and in-situ resource utilization (ISRU).

## 1. Introduction
Constructing habitable structures on Mars is essential for long-term exploration. The Martian environment lacks liquid water and features low atmospheric pressure (636 Pa), extreme temperatures, and high radiation. Conventional construction (Earth-based) is water-dependent. This study proposes water-free elemental sulfur (ES) as a binder, which can be extracted from Martian sulfides.

## 2. Experiments
### 2.1. Materials selection and preparation
- **Binder:** Sulfur powder heated to 140°C (molten state).
- **Aggregates:** Martian Soil (MS) simulant (JSC-1m), SiO2, Al2O3, and Fe2O3.
- **Process:** Mixing molten sulfur with aggregates, secondary heating, pouring into 25.4 mm cube molds, and curing for 24h at 20°C.

### 2.2. Test Procedures
- **Compressive Tests:** Conducted on cube specimens using a displacement-controlled system (0.18 mm/min).
- **Bond Strength Tests:** Four-point bending tests on bonded bricks with varying thickness (2mm to 6mm).

## 3. Results and Discussion
### 3.1. Components of MS Simulant
- Bricks with 20% MS simulant achieved an average strength of 25.2 MPa.
- Strength increases with MS content (0% MS = 16.8 MPa).
- **Empirical Equation:** $f_{u,c} = 242.86c^2 - 4.37c + 16.69$ (where $c$ is the MS fraction up to 20%).

### 3.2. Thickness of Bond Layer
- Optimal bond thickness identified at 3 mm (1.42 MPa strength).
- Increasing thickness beyond 3 mm results in a decrease in strength (0.91 MPa at 6 mm).

### 3.3. Various Aggregates
- **Fe2O3:** Highest compressive strength (39.2 MPa).
- **SiO2:** Comparable strength (33.4 MPa) and superior bond strength (1.32–1.35 MPa).
- **Al2O3:** 27.0 MPa.

### 3.4. Number of Recycles
- First recycling cycle: Strength drop from 33.4 MPa to 26.3 MPa.
- Subsequent cycles: Strength recovered/stabilized to ~29.9 MPa.

## 4. Design of Martian Pyramid-Shaped Structures
Pyramid designs offer stable bases for internal pressure (negative pressure) and deflect Martian dust/wind. The study proposes a modular assembly using ice blocks as temporary supports, which are then evaporated.

## 5. Conclusions
1. MS simulant increases sulfur brick strength by ~50% (up to 25.2 MPa).
2. 3 mm is the optimal bond thickness for masonry assembly.
3. Fe2O3 aggregates provide the highest material strength.
4. Sulfur-based bricks are recyclable with minimal long-term loss.
5. Pyramid geometry is highly suitable for Martian load conditions.
