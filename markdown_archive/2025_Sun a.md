# Sulfur-based materials for martian construction: Pioneering sustainable extraterrestrial building solutions

**Authors:** Wei Sun, Jiabao Zhao, Handong Yan, Zijin Xiao, Tiejiong Lou  
**Journal:** Alexandria Engineering Journal 117 (2025) 27–44  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.aej.2025.01.008

## Abstract
This study devises sustainable construction materials for Martian infrastructure using sulfur as a bonding agent and Martian soil (MS) simulant. Experiments tested brick compressive strength and bond strength across variables including MS percentage, aggregate types (SiO2, Al2O3, Fe2O3), bond thickness, and recycling iterations. Key findings reveal that 20% MS content yields 25.2 MPa compressive strength, 3 mm is the optimal bond thickness (1.42 MPa), and Fe2O3 aggregates provide the highest compressive strength (39.2 MPa). The research also explores pyramid-shaped structures for load distribution and seismic resilience.

## 1. Introduction
Constructing habitats on Mars requires overcoming water scarcity, low atmospheric pressure (636 Pa), and extreme temperatures (-143°C to 20°C). Water-free construction methods, such as elemental sulfur (ES) concrete, are ideal as sulfur can be extracted from Martian sulfides. This research addresses the gap in utilizing molten sulfur for bonding structural components in masonry construction.

## 2. Experimental Investigation

### 2.1 Materials
- **Martian Soil Simulant:** Analogous to JSC-1m.
- **Aggregates:** SiO2, Al2O3, Fe2O3.
- **Binder:** Elemental Sulfur (ES) heated to 140°C.

### 2.3 Key Experimental Results (Selected from Table 2)
- **Pure Sulfur (100%):** 16.8 MPa (Compressive Strength).
- **20% MS Simulant:** 25.2 MPa (Compressive Strength).
- **20% Fe2O3 Aggregate:** 39.2 MPa (Compressive Strength).
- **20% SiO2 Aggregate:** 33.4 MPa (Compressive Strength).
- **Bond Strength (3mm thickness):** 1.42 MPa.
- **Recycling (1 cycle):** Strength reduced from 33.4 to 26.3 MPa.

## 3. Structural Design: Pyramidal Geometry
Pyramid-shaped structures are proposed due to:
1. **Load Distribution:** Efficiently channels internal pressure toward the base.
2. **Aerodynamics:** Deflects wind and dust storms.
3. **Seismic Resistance:** High stability against Marsquakes.
4. **Construction:** Allows modular assembly using Martian ice as temporary support/formwork.

## 5. Conclusions
1. MS simulant increases sulfur-brick strength from 16.8 MPa to 25.2 MPa (at 20% content).
2. 3 mm is the optimal joint thickness for bond strength and workability.
3. Fe2O3 provides the highest compressive strength due to small particle size and uniform distribution.
4. Sulfur-based bricks are recyclable, with strength partially recovering in subsequent cycles after an initial drop.
