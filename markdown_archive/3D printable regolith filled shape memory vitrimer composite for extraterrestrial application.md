# 3D printable regolith filled shape memory vitrimer composite for extraterrestrial application

**Journal:** Journal of Composite Materials  
**Volume:** 58(24) 2639–2654  
**Date:** 2024  
**Authors:** Kingsley Yeboah Gyabaah, John Konlan, Obed Tetteh, Maryam Jahan, Enrique Jackson, Patrick Mensah and Guoqiang Li  
**DOI:** 10.1177/00219983241274544

## Abstract
This study investigates a neoteric approach in manufacturing lunar regolith-filled shape memory vitrimer (SMV) composites for extraterrestrial applications. A SMV with robust mechanical properties was combined with locally available lunar regolith to form a composite material. The research explored conventional synthesis and 3D printing methods (vat-photopolymerization). The 3D printed SMV-regolith composite exhibited a compressive strength of 139.16 MPa and good shape memory effect. The material is promising for lunar base applications due to its simple manufacturing process, excellent mechanical properties, and recyclability.

## Introduction
Developing independent extraterrestrial settlements requires protection from meteorite impacts, radiation, and temperature fluctuations. In-situ resource utilization (ISRU) is essential to minimize logistics costs. Lunar regolith is a primary candidate for building materials. While sintering and polymer concretes have been explored, this study introduces vitrimers—recyclable and self-healing polymers—as a matrix for regolith composites.

## Experimental Methods
### Materials
- **Matrix:** Bisphenol A glycerolate dimethacrylate (BPAGMA) with photoinitiator.
- **Filler:** Lunar Regolith Simulant (LRS) from The Exolith Lab.
- **Reinforcement:** Bidirectional plain woven glass fabric (for specific laminate tests).

### Fabrication Processes
1. **Conventional Procedure:** Mixing regolith into resin, UV curing, and post-thermal curing (180°C for 3h).
2. **3D Printing:** Using an Anycubic Photon Mono LCD-based printer (Vat photopolymerization). 20 wt% regolith was used for the printing mix.

## Results and Discussion
### Mechanical Properties

### Self-Healing
Healing was performed at 150°C under 16 MPa pressure for 2 hours. 
- **Control:** ~99% healing efficiency (crack initiation energy).
- **20 wt% Regolith:** ~97.4% healing efficiency in the first cycle.
- **40 wt% Regolith:** ~92.29% healing efficiency in the first cycle.

### Shape Memory Effect
- **Shape Fixity Ratio (F):** 68.88 ± 1.35%
- **Shape Recovery Ratio (R):** 88.20 ± 1.28%

## Conclusion
SMV-regolith composites are viable for lunar construction. They offer high compressive strength, especially when 3D printed, and provide sustainable advantages through self-healing and recyclability. Future work will address the limitations of vat photopolymerization regarding sample size and harsh lunar environmental factors.
