# Energy Life Cycle in Extraterrestrial Construction Projects: Is Building Underground an Option?

**Authors:** Hashem Izadi Moud, Peyman Rahgozar, Sebastian T. Weber, Craig Capano, and Ian Flood
**Affiliations:** Florida Gulf Coast University; University of Florida
**Source:** Earth and Space 2021, ASCE

## Abstract
Making life multi-planetary requires substantial knowledge of building structures in extraterrestrial bodies, where natural resources are scarce and energy sources differ from Earth. This paper presents two energy cycle flowcharts for construction projects on Earth and beyond. It argues against the feasibility of building underground due to the excessive energy required for soil removal, using a Martian case study.

## Introduction
Future shelters must protect occupants from extreme conditions: vacuum or low pressure, meteorite impacts, radiation, and temperature swings. Construction models may evolve through three phases:
1. Short-term missions (Class I/II).
2. Slightly longer stays (Class II/III).
3. Permanent colonization (Class III).

Energy is a critical constraint. On Earth, fossil fuels provide cheap, abundant energy. In space, solar energy is likely the primary source, requiring ultra-efficient design and planning.

## Energy in Construction Processes
### Earth vs. Space
*   **Earth:** Energy is rarely a primary concern in Construction Management (CM) due to low cost and high availability. Figure 1 illustrates the integrated but often overlooked role of energy in Earth-based CM.
*   **Space:** Energy is scarce and expensive. Shipping fuel from Earth is cost-prohibitive (e.g., $15k-$45k/kg delivery cost). Solar energy is the most viable option, despite drawbacks like low intensity on Mars.

## Case Study: Underground Structure on Mars
To evaluate the feasibility of underground construction, the study estimates the energy needed to excavate a small room.

### Excavation Parameters
*   **Room Volume:** 600 m³
*   **Total Soil Removed:** 1800 m³ (includes room volume plus two ramps and backfill).
*   **Energy Intensity (Earth baseline for silty sand):** 12.09 MJ/m³ (3.36 kWh/m³).
*   **Total Energy Required:** 21,762 MJ or 6,045 kWh.

### Solar Energy Supply on Mars
*   **Yearly Mean Radiance:** ~200 W/m².
*   **Efficiency:** 30% panel efficiency, 90% useful daylight factor.
*   **Daily Yield:** ~655.20 Wh/m²/sol.
*   **Scenario:** A 400 m² solar field yields 264 kWh per Martian day (sol).

## Results and Discussion
To produce the 6,045 kWh needed for excavation with a 400 m² solar field, it would take approximately **29 Martian Days**. Alternatively, to complete the excavation in one day, a solar field of 11,424 m² (approx. 2.8 acres) would be required.

### Conclusion
The study concludes that large-scale excavation and underground construction are currently energy-impractical on Mars without fossil fuels. Development must focus on:
1. Ultra-efficient sustainable design.
2. Resource-efficient project planning.
3. Smart, energy-efficient construction machinery.
