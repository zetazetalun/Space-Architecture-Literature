# Biomaterials for organically generated habitats beyond Earth

**Authors:** Robin Wordsworth, Rafid Quayum, Elida Kocharian, Ann Pearson, Xavier Portillo, Madeleine Yang, Charles S. Cockell, Shannon Nangle, George Church
**Journal:** Science Advances
**Volume:** 11, eadp4985 (2025)
**Published:** 2 July 2025

## Abstract
Sustaining life beyond Earth requires the creation of habitats, which is typically assumed to require costly transport of high-mass components from Earth. Here, we investigate an alternative approach based on in situ fabrication using biologically generated materials. We show that several common biomaterials are capable of blocking UV radiation, transmitting visible light, and maintaining pressure differences sufficient to permanently stabilize liquid H2O in a vacuum or low-pressure environment. As a proof of concept, we then demonstrate growth of eukaryotic green alga in a 3D printed PLA bioplastic habitat under Mars-relevant conditions of a 600 Pa CO2 background atmosphere. Our results demonstrate that products of biology itself can be used to create habitats in extraterrestrial environments. This approach is scalable, sustainable, and plausibly could be extended to construction of human habitats in the future.

## Introduction
Long-term human presence in space will require high levels of in situ resource utilization (ISRU). Biotechnology has the potential to provide food, structural materials, and fuel while recycling waste efficiently. Current space habitat concepts rely on industrially manufactured modules; this study proposes transparent surface habitats constructed from biologically generated materials to allow self-sustaining biomass generation with minimal power requirements.

Key Findings:
- All samples fully blocked UV-C radiation.
- PLA showed high yield strength (27.9 MPa) and good transparency.
- 1-mm wall thickness is sufficient to maintain a pressure difference of 10 kPa in a habitat with a 10 cm radius.

### Algal growth experiments
A proof-of-concept habitat was 3D printed using translucent PLA with a 1-mm wall thickness. It was sealed with an organic wax/resin mixture. Experiments in a Mars-analog environment (600 Pa CO2) showed robust growth of the alga *Dunaliella tertiolecta*. Internal pressures were maintained at 3–4 kPa. Growth rates (μ) peaked at ~0.9 day⁻¹, comparable to Earth-based control experiments.

### CO2 Permeability and pH
Habitat walls were found to be permeable to CO2 (effective permeability ~230 barrer), which helped maintain nutrients for growth but also affected medium pH. In seawater medium, pH was maintained in the 7.0–8.1 range.

## Discussion
Biologically produced materials can maintain habitable conditions in extraterrestrial environments. This 'biology-driven' approach allows for exponential habitat growth; excess bioplastic production can expand habitat surface area over time. Future research should focus on synthetic biology to improve feedstock production and investigate automated aerogel deposition for temperature regulation.
