# Biomaterials for organically generated habitats beyond Earth

**Authors:** Robin Wordsworth, Rafid Quayum, Elida Kocharian, Ann Pearson, Xavier Portillo, Madeleine Yang, Charles S. Cockell, Shannon Nangle, George Church
**Journal:** Science Advances | Research Article (2025)
**DOI:** 10.1126/sciadv.adp4985

## Abstract
Sustaining life beyond Earth requires the creation of habitats, typically assumed to require costly transport of high-mass components from Earth. This study investigates an alternative approach based on in situ fabrication using biologically generated materials. Several common biomaterials are shown to be capable of blocking UV radiation, transmitting visible light, and maintaining pressure differences sufficient to permanently stabilize liquid H2O in vacuum or low-pressure environments. As a proof of concept, eukaryotic green alga was grown in a 3D printed PLA bioplastic habitat under Mars-relevant conditions (600 Pa CO2).

## Introduction
Sustainable long-term human presence in space requires high levels of in situ resource utilization (ISRU). Biotechnology has the potential to provide critical resources such as food, structural materials, and chemical fuels. If transparent surface habitats could be constructed from biologically generated materials, it would allow self-sustaining generation of biomass for life support with minimal power requirements.

### Material Requirements
*   Transmit visible radiation (photosynthetically active radiation).
*   Block harmful ultraviolet (UV) radiation.
*   Maintain pressure and temperature for liquid water.
*   Withstand pressure gradients of several kilopascals (saturation vapor pressure of H2O is 1.2 to 4.2 kPa at 10° to 30°C).

## Results

### Bioplastic Material Properties
| Biomaterial | $\kappa_{vis}$ (mm⁻¹) | $\kappa_{UV-AB}$ (mm⁻¹) | $\sigma$ (MPa) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Agarose | 0.54 ± 0.28 | 1.93 ± 1.02 | 9.84 ± 5.71 |
| Translucent PLA | 0.65 ± 0.06 | 0.94 ± 0.10 | 27.9 ± 5.74 |
| PHA | >2.2 | >3.4 | 9.16 ± 1.89 |
*Note: All samples fully blocked UV-C radiation. $\sigma$ is the average ultimate stress.*

### Algal Growth Experiments
*   **Organism:** *Dunaliella tertiolecta* (eukaryotic green alga).
*   **Habitat:** 3D printed (Dremel 3D45) translucent PLA, 1 mm wall thickness, sealed with wax/resin mixture.
*   **Conditions:** Mars analog 600 Pa CO2 atmosphere, 23 ± 1°C.
*   **Outcome:** Robust growth close to control experiments (1 atm) was observed. Growth rates peaked at 0.9 day⁻¹.

## Discussion
Habitable conditions can be maintained using only biologically produced materials. PLA can be produced via synthesis of sugars from CO2 and H2O, followed by fermentation to lactic acid and polymerization. PHA is produced as intracellular granules in bacteria, allowing direct extraction. These materials enable exponential growth of habitat area through biomass-to-bioplastic conversion cycles.

## Materials and Methods
*   **3D Printing:** Dremel 3D45 for PLA; PRUSA MK4 for PHA.
*   **Structural Testing:** Instron single-column strength testing machine.
*   **Chamber:** 12-inch modular vacuum cube with CO2 mass flow control and RGA monitoring.
