# SPACE RESEARCH AND EXTRATERRESTRIAL CONSTRUCTION INDUSTRY

**Authors:** Yusuf Cengiz TOKLU (Okan University), Ali Erdem ÇERÇEVIK (Bilecik Seyh Edebali University)  
**Published:** 2017 (IEEE)

## Abstract
Advance of civilization has been realized through the ages by exploration of unknown parts of the Earth and by exploiting new resources thus acquired. As a natural continuation of this process, humans will now explore and exploit new areas beyond Earth. Habitation on these new space bodies will necessitate construction of outposts. This article presents an overview of space research together with innovations in space construction and the feedback of these studies to the general construction industry.

## I. Introduction
The expansion of human environments is modeled as a loop: Richer resources lead to higher levels of economy and technology, which in turn enable the conquest of new areas. This process, which led to globalization on Earth, is now leading toward 'spatialization'.

## II. Spatialization - World 2050
By 2050, human presence is expected to extend to the Moon and Mars. Manned flight records show a jump from low Earth orbit to potential distances of over 200 million km. The future human 'world' will include Earth, Moon, Mars, and the space between them.

## III. Pioneers of Going to Moon and Mars
Currently, 70 countries have space programs, but only 10 can launch satellites and only 3 (US, Russia, China) can send humans to space. Manned missions to Mars are anticipated around 2050, likely involving permanent settlements rather than short trips due to travel time and launch windows.

## IV. Habitation / Construction on Space Bodies
Hostile environments necessitate special measures for survival. Launch costs (e.g., $20,000/kg to the Moon) dictate the maximum usage of in-situ materials.

Initial ISRU applications include:
- Coverage of structures by regolith for radiation protection.
- Construction of walls using reinforced regolith.
- Forming structures by gabions filled with in-situ dust.

### B. Structural Considerations
Space structures must handle internal pressure (cracks not allowed) and hostile environments. Proposed formats include:
- Constructions in lava tubes.
- Habitats embedded in regolith.
- Tensegric structures.
- Inflatable structures.
- Structures with no stress concentrations.

## V. Advanced Systems
- **Automated Construction:** Fully automated mining, earthwork, and construction due to the danger of manual labor.
- **Special Construction Machines:** Electrically operated, telecommanded, dust-resistant, and optimized for low-gravity environments.
- **Building Information Systems (BIS):** Central management systems using sensors (pressure, temperature, CO2, strain) to create 'smart' mechatronic habitats.
- **Waste Treatment:** Maximum recycling of liquid, solid, and gaseous wastes.

## Conclusions
Civil engineering is transitioning from globalization to spatialization. Future success depends on fully elaborated plans, ISRU utilization, and the integration of smart mechatronic systems into habitat design.
