# The Research Facility on the Moon

**Author:** Mikolaj S. Trus  
**Affiliation:** Graduate of Architecture Studies, Cracow University of Technology, Kraków, Poland.  
**Publication:** Earth and Space 2021, ASCE, pp. 1342-1350.

## Abstract
The research facility on the Moon is an experimental, structural design for space exploration. Developed as a Student Master Thesis at Cracow University of Technology, the concept emphasizes modularity, multifunctionality, and simplicity. The facility consists of seven modules formed from repetitive, easy-to-transport components, designed to host twelve astronauts with future expansion capabilities.

## Introduction
### Concept
The project addresses the hostile lunar environment (low gravity, lack of atmosphere, radiation, meteors, and high temperature amplitudes). Transport constraints defined strict requirements for mass and dimensions. The design explores innovative structural solutions that prioritize safety, ease of transport, and flexibility.

## Methods
1.  **Hand Drawing:** Used for rapid visualization of experimental ideas, identifying spherical shapes as the most efficient.
2.  **Virtual Model:** Developed in Blender 3D to control geometry, scale, and modularity. Platonic solids, specifically truncated icosahedrons, were selected as the modular base.
3.  **3D Printing:** Used to verify virtual design results by printing scale models (1:200).
4.  **Technical Drawings:** CAD-based sections and floor plans to determine functional interior arrangements.

## Results
### Construction
The study compared inflatable structures vs. repetitive rigid assembly elements. Rigid structures were chosen for their predictability in altered gravity, despite being heavier. 
*   **Geometry:** Based on a truncated icosahedron (12 pentagons, 20 hexagons) for optimal pressure distribution and volume-to-surface ratio.
*   **System:** A light rod construction (exoskeleton) with internal support bars. 
*   **Modularity:** The spherical construction originates from an icosahedron and is divided into 20 repetitive elements.
*   **Dimensions:** 
    *   Edge of truncated icosahedron: 2.10 m.
    *   Diameter of spherical module: 10.02 m.
    *   Accessible volume: 520 m³.
    *   Corridor components: Pentagonal (4m long, 30.35 m³) and Hexagonal (2.5m long, 28.64 m³).

### Protection
A multilayer shell was designed to mitigate radiation and temperature swings:
*   **Internal Layer:** 20 cm thick thermal insulation.
*   **Vacuum Gap:** 5 cm gap acting as a thermal insulator.
*   **External Layer:** 5 cm thin layer for radiation/temperature absorption.
*   **Regolith:** The final stage involves covering the structure with lunar regolith for radiation and meteor shower protection.

### Function and Logistics
*   **Capacity:** 7 modules (6 in a circle, 1 central node) for 12 astronauts.
*   **Zoning:** Modules are divided into living/sleeping areas, dining/kitchen/storage, laboratory sectors, and gym/physical activity areas.
*   **Power:** Solar energy is the primary source.

## Discussion and Conclusion
The design utilizes regular polyhedrons to solve transport and expansion issues. Compared to Foster + Partners' inflatable concepts, this rigid modular system offers a different approach to additive growth and structural predictability. The study concludes that the concept meets the physical and structural requirements of the lunar environment and provides a flexible, adjustable proposal for extraterrestrial architecture.
