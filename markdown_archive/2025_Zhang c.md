# Wettability characteristics of lunar soil particles from the Chang’E-5 mission: A single-particle surface analysis

**Authors:** Haotian Zhang, Jun Liu, Zhiping Yuan, Zhenxing Li, Xiaoyu Yao, Qianghui Xu, Tongcai Wang, Yanjie Zheng, Gong Wang, Hu Zhang, Jun Shen
**Journal:** Applied Surface Science 702 (2025) 163331
**DOI:** 10.1016/j.apsusc.2025.163331

## Abstract
Lunar base construction has emerged as a strategic focus for global space agencies, with extrusion-based in situ resource utilization (ISRU) technologies recognized as a promising approach. However, a critical aspect lies in understanding the wettability characteristics between lunar soil and binding agents. This study quantitatively analyzes wetting dynamics through a custom experimental apparatus. Results show an average contact angle with water of 108.73°, indicating hydrophobicity attributed to surface morphology. Numerical simulations confirm the Cassie-Baxter model describes these phenomena. This research provides crucial empirical support for extrusion-based extraterrestrial construction.

## 1. Introduction
ISRU of lunar soil combined with additive manufacturing (AM) is a viable solution for lunar base construction. Key technologies include Stereolithography (SLA), Selective Laser Sintering (SLS), and Extrusion Molding (ED). Extrusion molding is preferred for large-scale structures but requires precise understanding of the interaction between lunar soil and resin binders.

## 2. Experimental
- **Sample:** Chang’E-5 lunar soil (CE5C0400), collected from Oceanus Procellarum.
- **Pretreatment:** Selection of particles (~100s μm) using microscopy.
- **Measurement:** A custom contact angle measurement system using a Liquid Evaporator (LE) and Ultra-Deep Field Microscope (UDFM) was developed to observe micron-sized particles.

## 3. Results and Discussion

### 3.1 Surface Morphology
SEM analysis reveals:
- Angular shapes with uneven undulations.
- Adherents (glassy condensates) and porous structures.
- Mineral fragments (pyroxene, olivine, plagioclase).
- High surface roughness due to space weathering.

### 3.3 Numerical Simulation Results
- **Model:** Cassie-Baxter wetting model was validated.
- **Surface Tension:** Calculated as 8.46 mN/m for the lunar soil particle.
- **Resin Interaction:** For epoxy resin, the simulated apparent contact angle was 102.40° with a theoretical Young contact angle of 96.18°.

## 4. Conclusions
The study establishes a framework for characterizing contact angles on rough micron-scale particles. Findings confirm that lunar soil particles are hydrophobic, a significant departure from terrestrial minerals like pyroxene. This hydrophobicity must be accounted for when designing resin-soil slurries for lunar 3D printing.
