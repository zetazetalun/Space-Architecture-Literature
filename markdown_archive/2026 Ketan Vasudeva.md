# Lunar construction: A state-of-the-art survey\n\n**Authors:** Ketan Vasudeva, M. Reza Emami, Cameron Dickinson\n**Journal:** Progress in Aerospace Sciences 161 (2026) 101159\n\n## Abstract\nThe survey presents a comprehensive review of lunar construction, including environmental characterization, infrastructure development, construction methods, and robotic systems. It highlights challenges like extreme temperature variations, radiation, and lunar dust. It identifies habitats, power stations, landing pads, and blast berms as critical infrastructure and analyzes methods for building them using local resources.\n\n## 1. Introduction\nRenewed focus on the Moon is driven by the Artemis program (NASA), Moon Village (ESA), ILRS (CNSA/Roscosmos), and Vision 2047 (ISRO). A major challenge is the scale of work required for lunar infrastructure, necessitating extensive ISRU.\n\n## 2. Lunar Site Surveying\n* **Environment:** Gravity (1/6g), vacuum (3 x 10^-15 atm), radiation (GCRs and SPEs), and extreme temperatures (-171°C to 111°C). Regolith shielding (2-3m) is necessary for habitat safety.\n* **Regolith:** Fine-grained layer formed by meteorite impacts. Properties include high abrasiveness and electrostatic charging. Bulk density: 1.3 to 1.83 g/cm^3 depending on depth.\n* **Dust Mitigation:** Classified into passive (coatings, seals), active (electrodynamic dust shields - EDS, brushing, vibration), and implicit (compliant mechanisms).\n\n## 3. Lunar Infrastructure\n* **Lunar Habitats:**\n    * **Class I (Landed):** Self-contained, e.g., Mankins Habot.\n    * **Class II (Deployable/Inflatable):** Hybrid structures like TransHab or ESA's Moon Village concept.\n    * **Class III (ISRU-based):** 3D printed habitats using regolith, e.g., Project Olympus (ICON/NASA) and Lunar Lantern.\n* **Other Systems:** Power stations (solar/nuclear), landing pads (inner zone sintering, outer zone gravel), and blast berms (regolith stones/sandbags).\n\n## 4. Lunar Construction Methods and Materials\n### 4.1 Regolith Binding\n* **Sulfur Concrete:** Waterless, 70-80% strength in 24h, but melts at 128°C.\n* **Geopolymer Cement:** Minimizes water usage, high compressive strength (up to 19 MPa in simulated lunar conditions).\n* **Polymer Cement:** Epoxy-based, quick and viable but requires Earth-transported binders.\n### 4.2 Additive Manufacturing (AM)\n* **Extrusion-based:** Contour crafting using regolith inks.\n* **Sintering:** Includes laser sintering (high accuracy), microwave sintering (37 MPa strength), and solar sintering (low energy input required).\n\n## 5. Lunar Construction Systems\n* **Excavation:** RASSOR (dual-bucket drum), CASPER (Archimedes screw), and IPEx.\n* **Robotics:** Cranes (LSMS variations), mobile manipulators (ATHLETE, Helelani), and 3D printers (MIRA3D, Project Meeka).
