# Solar-Powered Additive Manufacturing in Extraterrestrial Environments

**Authors:** Steven D. Anderson, Jekan Thangavelautham
**Affiliation:** Space and Terrestrial Robotic Exploration Laboratory, Univ. of Arizona
**Conference:** Earth and Space 2021

## Abstract
Kickstarting a space economy will require building communication relays, refueling depots, repair depots, habitats, and mining bases from in-situ resources. This paper proposes the development of solar additive manufacturing printers that replace lasers with large Fresnel lenses to focus sunlight for sintering material. This system relies on renewable solar energy, reducing the power consumption and supply chain requirements for extraterrestrial construction.

## Introduction
Sustainable construction methods are needed for long-duration missions. Additive manufacturing (AM) has risen as a key technology for habitat construction. However, importing materials is costly, making In-Situ Resource Utilization (ISRU) paramount.

## Challenges to Extreme Environment Additive Manufacturing
1. **Material Availability:** Importing materials is too expensive; ISRU is required.
2. **Power Systems:** Energy intensive operations like heating material require robust power sources.
3. **Environmental Conditions:** Lack of atmosphere, pressure, and temperature control.

## Design Method
While Fused Deposition Modeling (FDM) is common, Selective Laser Sintering (SLS) is promising due to minimal material pre-processing. By replacing the CO2 laser with a Fresnel lens, the energy requirement from onboard systems is nearly eliminated.

## Employment of Technology
Solar sinter technology is best suited for robotic fleets performing autonomous construction. Mobile rovers equipped with a Fresnel lens could sinter foundations, road networks, and structural pieces directly onto the surface.

## Experimental Objective and Design
To prove feasibility, a 3D solar sinter machine is being developed to reach 1100°C for quartzite sand sintering. Early feasibility tests using a small 8.5” x 11” Fresnel lens successfully reached sintering temperatures and created rough 3D structures.

## Conclusion
Solar sintering offers a practical, low-energy option for constructing infrastructure where power conservation is a priority. By adjusting parameters like feed rates and lens aperture, the system can be tailored to various extraterrestrial environments.
