## Abstract
In the construction industry, wire-based steel 3D printing (WBS-3DP) technology for structural parts has yet to be explored. As 3D printing becomes highly productive and cost-effective, it opens opportunities for steel-based additive construction. The major advantage of large-scale 3D printing techniques like wire-arc additive manufacturing (WAAM) is the ability to develop components compatible through topology optimization. This research discusses the application of WAAM in the fabrication of space frame structures for T-K-Y joints with structural integrity. It was concluded that WBS-3DP has the potential to revolutionize the construction industry by producing innovative parts such as tube connectors with ease of fabrication.

## 1 Introduction
Additive Manufacturing (AM) involves depositing thin layers of material based on a 3D computer model. While aerospace and automotive industries have embraced metal 3D printing, the steel construction industry has moved at a slower pace due to part size and load capacity requirements. Steel tubes are frequently used in trusses and space frames, but conventional fabrication of T-K-Y connections requires complex profile cutting, edge preparation, and manual welding, which often leads to stress concentrations and high residual stress.

## 2 Materials and Methods

### 2.1 Prototype for Tubular Joints Design
A prototype spaceframe structure was designed to accommodate all possible types of T-K and Y-type tube connections (six types: A, B, C, D, E & F). Topology optimization using Altair Inspire Print3D was applied to reduce material usage and print time while ensuring structural integrity.

### 2.2 Tubular Joining Using Additive Manufacturing
Printing was conducted at the Centre for Advanced Welding and Joining (CAWJ), Tata Steel. The system used an integrated WAAM setup consisting of a Metal Inert Gas (MIG) welding power source connected to a 6-axis robotic arm.

### 2.3 Printing Process
- **Feedstock:** Carbon steel wire (0.8 mm diameter).
- **Shielding Gas:** 82% Ar / 18% CO2 (15 l/min).
- **Parameters:** Bead height 2 mm, width 6 mm, bead overlap 1.5 mm.
- **Temperature:** Interpass temperature kept below 120 °C.

### 3.2 Mechanical Properties
Tensile tests showed no significant difference regarding the print direction (isotropic nature):
- **Yield Strength:** 395–405 MPa
- **Tensile Strength:** 497–507 MPa
- **Elongation:** 32–40%

### 3.3 Repeatability
The weight of three F-connectors printed using the same procedure was 6.95 kg, 6.97 kg, and 6.93 kg, demonstrating a repeatability of approximately 99%.

## 4 Conclusions
This research successfully demonstrated the use of an integrated WAAM system for producing multi-branch tube connectors. The printed connectors can replace traditional T-K-Y connections with better tolerances and matching mechanical properties. The method reduces the effort required for 6GR-qualified welds by converting them into automated processes, potentially solving skilled personnel shortages.
