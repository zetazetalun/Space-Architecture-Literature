## Abstract
Microbially induced carbonate precipitation (MICP) mimics natural cementation to create "bio-bricks" that rival regular bricks in strength. This energy-saving and cost-effective in situ resource utilization (ISRU) technique is evaluated for lunar exploration settlements. This review covers bacterial metabolic activities (aerobic and anaerobic), properties of lunar regolith, bio-brick production, and future research needs.

## 1 Introduction
NASA estimates it costs approximately $10,000 USD to launch one pound of material into Earth orbit. ISRU is an economic imperative for sustainable space exploration. While methods like sintering/melting are energy-intensive, bio-cementation is a "green" solution using microorganisms to induce carbonate mineral precipitation for bonding construction materials.

## 2 Microbial carbonate precipitation by different metabolic activities
MICP pathways include:
- **Aerobic:** Photosynthesis, Ureolysis, and Ammonification.
- **Anaerobic:** Sulfate reduction, Denitrification, and Methane oxidation.

Ureolysis (enzymatic degradation of urea) is the most studied due to its efficiency and high calcite precipitation. *Sporosarcina pasteurii* (formerly *Bacillus pasteurii*) is the standard model organism for this process.

## 3 Properties of sand-sized material on the moon
- **Regolith Thickness:** 2–15 m.
- **Specific Gravity:** 2.9–3.5 g/cm³.
- **Composition:** Plagioclase, pyroxene, ilmenite, olivine, and impact glass.
- **Particle Size:** Broadly distributed (0.002 to 4 mm); average mean particle size D₅₀ = 72 μm.

## 4 Characteristics of artificial bio-bricks
Bio-bricks produced using sands (0.36–0.73 mm) have shown unconfined compressive strengths (UCS) between 1 MPa and 23 MPa.

## 5 Research needs and future directions
- **Micro-gravity effects:** Survival and metabolic activity of microbes in space conditions.
- **By-products:** Avoidance of undesired chemical outputs.
- **Logistics:** Utilizing human urine (urea source) to reduce launch costs.
- **Scaling:** Moving from laboratory 3D printing to large-scale habitat construction.
