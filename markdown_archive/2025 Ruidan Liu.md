# An innovative structural energy storage solution using fly ash-cement composites for net-zero energy buildings

**Journal:** Cement and Concrete Composites 157 (2025) 105960

**Authors:** Ruidan Liu, Pan Feng, Zhaolong Liu, Long Yuan, Guanghui Tao, Zhenqi Yu, Xiangyu Meng, Jian Chen

## Abstract
The rapid advancement of renewable energy highlights the urgent need for safe, cost-effective, and scalable energy storage solutions, particularly for net-zero energy buildings. In this study, we introduce an innovative energy storage solution utilizing fly ash-cement composites (FCS) as multifunctional components. The FCS, incorporating fly ash as a mineral admixture, achieves a refined pore structure and homogeneous air void distribution during early hydration, leveraging the ball-bearing characteristics and pozzolanic effect of fly ash. The optimized FCS, containing 50 wt% fly ash, exhibited an impressive ionic conductivity of 25.6 mS cm−1 and a compressive strength of 5.5 MPa after just one day of curing. When integrated into structural energy storage systems, it delivers a high specific capacitance of 102.4 mAh g−1 at 0.1 A g−1, an energy density of 73 Wh kg−1, and a power density of 76.3 W kg−1, maintaining 92.9 % capacitance retention over 2000 cycles. These results underscore the scalability, cost efficiency, and structural benefits of FCS, offering a promising pathway to integrate energy storage directly into buildings and infrastructure. Moreover, this strategy provides a sustainable, high-value application for large volumes of industrial solid waste while addressing energy challenges in extreme environments such as deep-sea, deep-earth, and extraterrestrial constructions.

## 1. Introduction
Structural energy storage devices (SESDs) that not only store electrical energy efficiently but also bear mechanical loads are proposed to possess significant potential to reduce the carbon footprint of the construction industry. Among various types of SESDs, structural supercapacitors (SSCs) are particularly promising due to their outstanding energy storage capacity, high power density, and robust load-bearing capability. Aqueous zinc-ion hybrid supercapacitors have emerged as a promising technology, offering inherent safety and abundant raw materials.

## 2. Materials and Methodology

## 3. Results

### 3.1. Ionic conductivity and compressive strength
- **Ionic Conductivity:** The addition of fly ash significantly increased ionic conductivity at early curing ages (1d and 3d), with FZ1 achieving 25.6 mS cm−1.
- **Compressive Strength:** Samples soaked in ZnSO4 solution exhibited significantly higher strength than those in deionized water. ZnSO4 reacts with hydration products to form ZSH (zinc sulfate hydroxide hydrate) and gypsum, which fill pores and refine the structure.

### 3.2. Electrochemical Performance
- **Specific Capacitance:** 102.4 mAh g−1 at 0.1 A g−1.
- **Energy Density:** Reached 73 Wh kg−1.
- **Cycling Stability:** 92.9% capacitance retention after 2000 cycles for FZ1.
- **Scaling:** A 100 cm² bulk FZ1 device successfully powered a blue LED board at 1.8 V.

## 5. Conclusion
The study developed a novel zinc-ion hybrid structural supercapacitor using porous fly ash-cement composites. The 50 wt% fly ash FCS provides sufficient compressive strength and high ionic conductivity while preventing internal short circuits as a separator. This provides a breakthrough for using industrial waste in high-value structural energy storage, potentially facilitating net-zero energy consumption in worldwide buildings and extraterrestrial habitats.
