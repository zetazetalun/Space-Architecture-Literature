# An innovative structural energy storage solution using fly ash-cement composites for net-zero energy buildings

## Metadata
- **Journal:** Cement and Concrete Composites 157 (2025) 105960
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cemconcomp.2025.105960
- **Authors:** Ruidan Liu, Pan Feng, Zhaolong Liu, Long Yuan, Guanghui Tao, Zhenqi Yu, Xiangyu Meng, Jian Chen
- **Keywords:** Cement, Fly ash, Zinc-ion hybrid supercapacitor, Pore structure, Scalable energy storage

## Abstract
The rapid advancement of renewable energy highlights the urgent need for safe, cost-effective, and scalable energy storage solutions, particularly for net-zero energy buildings. In this study, we introduce an innovative energy storage solution utilizing fly ash-cement composites (FCS) as multifunctional components. The FCS, incorporating fly ash as a mineral admixture, achieves a refined pore structure and homogeneous air void distribution during early hydration, leveraging the ball-bearing characteristics and pozzolanic effect of fly ash. The optimized FCS, containing 50 wt% fly ash, exhibited an impressive ionic conductivity of 25.6 mS cm⁻¹ and a compressive strength of 5.5 MPa after just one day of curing. When integrated into structural energy storage systems, it delivers a high specific capacitance of 102.4 mAh g⁻¹ at 0.1 A g⁻¹, an energy density of 73 Wh kg⁻¹, and a power density of 76.3 W kg⁻¹, maintaining 92.9 % capacitance retention over 2000 cycles. These results underscore the scalability, cost efficiency, and structural benefits of FCS, offering a promising pathway to integrate energy storage directly into buildings and infrastructure. Moreover, this strategy provides a sustainable, high-value application for large volumes of industrial solid waste while addressing energy challenges in extreme environments such as deep-sea, deep-earth, and extraterrestrial constructions.

## 1. Introduction
Structural energy storage devices (SESDs) that store electrical energy while bearing mechanical loads possess significant potential to reduce the carbon footprint of the construction industry. Structural supercapacitors (SSCs) are promising due to their high power density, long cycle life, and load-bearing capability. Construction materials possess inherent structural strength and ion transport potential, making them ideal candidates for load-bearing and ion-conductive separators in SSCs.

## 2. Materials and Methodology
### 2.1. Materials
- **Binder:** Type 42.5R Portland cement.
- **Admixture:** Class C fly ash from Nanjing coal power plant.
- **Foaming Agent:** Al powder (0.1 wt%).
- **Electrolyte:** 2M ZnSO₄ solution.
- **Electrodes:** Activated carbon (AC) cathode and zinc foil anode.

### 2.2. Preparation of FCS
Water-to-cement ratio: 0.5. Specimens were classified into Group C (100% cement) and Group F (50% fly ash, 50% cement). Samples were cured for 1, 3, 7, and 28 days before testing.

## 3. Results and Discussion
### 3.1. Ionic Conductivity and Compressive Strength
- **Ionic Conductivity:** FCS with fly ash (FZ1) reached a peak of 25.6 mS cm⁻¹ at 1 day. Conductivity decreases with curing age as the microstructure densifies.
- **Compressive Strength:** ZnSO₄ soaking significantly improves strength compared to deionized water. FZ1 reached 5.5 MPa at 1 day. The reaction of ZnSO₄ with hydration products (forming gypsum and zinc sulfate hydroxide hydrate/ZSH) fills pores and refines the structure.

### 3.2. Electrochemical Performance
- **Specific Capacity:** 102.4 mAh g⁻¹ at 0.1 A g⁻¹ (for FZ1).
- **Energy Density:** 73 Wh kg⁻¹ at a power density of 76.3 W kg⁻¹.
- **Cycling Stability:** 92.9% capacitance retention after 2000 cycles at 0.2 A g⁻¹.

## 4. Conclusion
The study successfully developed a zinc-ion hybrid structural supercapacitor based on porous fly ash-cement composites. The addition of fly ash improves pore connectivity and distribution, enhancing ion transport without compromising structural integrity. This multifunctional material provides a breakthrough for low-carbon construction and energy management in extreme environments, including extraterrestrial scenarios.
