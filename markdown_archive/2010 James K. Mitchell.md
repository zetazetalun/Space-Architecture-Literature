# Geotechnical Engineering Considerations for Extraterrestrial Infrastructure Construction

**Authors:** James K. Mitchell, W. David Carrier III
**Publisher:** American Society of Civil Engineers (ASCE)
**Publication:** The Art of Foundation Engineering Practice Congress 2010

## Abstract
It is only a matter of time until space exploration enters a new phase that will include additional manned and unmanned landings and explorations on the Moon, Mars, and perhaps on asteroids. Detailed knowledge of the soils and rocks will be needed for the design of foundations for structures, for excavation and underground space utilization, for design of surface vehicles and construction of transportation systems, for evaluation of their suitability as construction materials and development of construction methods. In-situ resource utilization (ISRU) can be expected to play a major role in these activities owing to the high cost and difficulty in transporting needed materials from Earth.

## Lunar Soil Properties
Lunar soil, or regolith, covers the surface to depths of 2m to 20m. It is a broadly-graded silty fine sand to sandy silt. Engineering properties depend primarily on relative density, which increases rapidly with depth:
- **0-5 cm:** 0 to 30% (loose)
- **10 cm:** >80% (dense)
- **Below 30 cm:** >90% (very dense)

## Martian Soil Properties
Martian regolith is more complex due to hydrological, volcanic, and aeolian processes. It is generally described as similar to moderately dense soils on Earth, such as clayey silt with embedded sands, granules, and pebbles.

## Key Considerations for Infrastructure
- **Foundations:** Lunar regolith at shallow depths can support high foundation loads. Mars requires more site-specific assessment.
- **Excavation:** Core tubes and drill holes on the Moon remain open; vertical walls can remain stable up to 2m deep.
- **Dust:** Creates health risks and damage to equipment/spacesuits via electrostatic adhesion.
- **ISRU:** NASA's long-range goal is to extract metals for parts fabrication and utilize lunar soils as construction materials to save on the ~$100,000/kg transport cost.
