# Mechanistic insights on the synergistic roles of glass content and curing temperature in reaction kinetics and microstructural evolution of alkali-activated lunar regolith simulants

**Journal:** Cement and Concrete Composites 170 (2026) 106586  
**Authors:** Zhengzheng Liu, Yukun Qin, Chuanlin Hu, Huajun Sun, Xueping Peng, Fazhou Wang

## Abstract
The highly reactive glass phase in lunar regolith is a critical yet insufficiently understood component. This study revealed the synergistic mechanism of glass content and curing temperature on the reaction pathways, microstructural evolution, and mechanical properties of alkali-activated lunar regolith simulants. Curing at 60 °C promoted continuous structural development and steady strength gain through the coexistence of N-A-S-H gel with well-ordered crystals. At 90 °C, samples with low glass content suffered from strength regression due to disordered petal-shaped zeolites causing microcracks. Conversely, high glass content enabled continuous densification and strength improvement (up to 49.4 MPa) by forming a stable gel network.

## 1. Introduction
Constructing a permanent lunar base requires ISRU technology to mitigate the high cost of Earth-supplied materials. Lunar regolith solidification methods include adhesive bonding, high-temperature sintering, and hydration-based methods (cementitious binders and geopolymers). Geopolymers offer advantages such as high mechanical strength and stability under extreme thermal fluctuations.

## 2. Materials and Methods
### 2.1 Sample Preparation
- **Precursor:** Basalt powder and synthesized basalt glass (quenched from 1400 °C).
- **Activator:** 8 mol/L NaOH solution.
- **Liquid-to-solid ratio:** 0.28.
- **Curing:** Sealed curing at 60 °C and 90 °C for 1, 3, 7, and 28 days.

## 3. Results
### 3.1 Compressive Strength
- **60 °C Curing:** Incorporation of glass phase significantly enhanced early-age strength. SH-4-60 (60% glass) achieved 1-day strength >10x the glass-free control. 28-day strength increased from 11.9 MPa (0% glass) to 42.6 MPa (30% glass).
- **90 °C Curing:** Reaction kinetics accelerated. SH-4-90 reached the highest 28-day strength of 49.4 MPa. Low-glass samples showed strength regression between 7 and 28 days.

### 3.2 Microstructural Analysis
- **XRD:** At 60 °C, zeolites (Na-P1, analcime) were metastable and partially dissolved to contribute to the N-A-S-H gel network. At 90 °C, zeolites were thermodynamically stable.
- **MIP (Porosity):** High glass content (60%) effectively reduced total porosity and refined pore structures (gel and mesopores dominated) at both temperatures.
- **XPS:** Confirmed an increase in bridging oxygen (BO) and Si/Al binding energy in high-glass samples, signifying a more polymerized, stable network.

## 4. Conclusion
1. At 60 °C, increased glass content promotes continuous strength development as zeolites gradually contribute to the gel phase.
2. At 90 °C, high glass content is mandatory to prevent strength regression caused by disordered zeolite crystallization that induces microcracks.
3. The study establishes a materials design principle for extraterrestrial construction: optimizing glass-to-crystalline ratios based on ambient curing conditions to ensure durability.
