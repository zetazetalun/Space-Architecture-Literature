# Mechanistic insights on the synergistic roles of glass content and curing temperature in reaction kinetics and microstructural evolution of alkali-activated lunar regolith simulants  **Journal:** Cement and Concrete Composites  **DOI:** 10.1016/j.cemconcomp.2026.106586  **Authors:** Zhengzheng Liu, Yukun Qin, Chuanlin Hu, Huajun Sun, Xueping Peng, Fazhou Wang  ## Abstract  The highly reactive glass phase in lunar regolith is a critical component for alkali-activated materials. This study reveals the synergistic mechanism of glass content and curing temperature on reaction pathways and mechanical properties. Curing at 60 °C promoted continuous structural development, while 90 °C caused strength regression in low-glass samples due to disordered zeolite crystallization. High glass content (60%) stabilized the gel network, ensuring densification and strength.  ## 1. Introduction  ISRU technology is essential for sustainable lunar construction due to high transport costs. Solidification methods include adhesive bonding, sintering/melting, and hydration-based systems (geopolymers). Lunar regolith contains substantial glassy phases from meteoritic impacts and volcanic activity, which are highly reactive precursors for geopolymerization.  ## 2. Methods and Materials  ### 2.1. Preparation of basalt glass  Basalt powder was melted at 1400 °C and quenched in water to form amorphous glass, then ground to <75 μm.  ### 2.2. Geopolymer preparation  Mixes used basalt powder and basalt glass with an 8 mol/L NaOH activator (Liquid/Solid ratio 0.28). Samples were cured at 60 °C and 90 °C for up to 28 days.  **Table 1: Mix proportions**  | Sample | NaOH (g) | Water (g) | Basalt (g) | Glass (g) | Temperature (°C) | | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | | SH-1-60 | 8.15 | 25.45 | 120 | 0 | 60 | | SH-2-60 | 8.15 | 25.45 | 102 | 18 | 60 | | SH-3-60 | 8.15 | 25.45 | 84 | 36 | 60 | | SH-4-60 | 8.15 | 25.45 | 48 | 72 | 60 | | SH-1-90 | 8.15 | 25.45 | 120 | 0 | 90 | | SH-2-90 | 8.15 | 25.45 | 102 | 18 | 90 | | SH-3-90 | 8.15 | 25.45 | 84 | 36 | 90 | | SH-4-90 | 8.15 | 25.45 | 48 | 72 | 90 |  ## 3. Results  - **Compressive Strength:** Under 60 °C, strength increased with glass content. SH-4-60 (60% glass) reached ~42.6 MPa. At 90 °C, SH-4-90 reached 49.4 MPa, but lower-glass samples (SH-1-90) showed strength regression after 7 days.  - **Microstructure:** SEM and XRD revealed that high temperature (90 °C) promotes zeolite (ZK-14, Na-P1, Analcime) stability. In low-glass samples, excessive zeolite growth causes microcracking. In high-glass samples, a stable N-A-S-H gel network envelopes these crystals.  ## 5. Conclusion  1. Increased glass content promotes continuous strength development.  2. Curing temperature dictates zeolite stability (metastable at 60 °C, stable at 90 °C).  3. High glass content (60%) is decisive for maintaining strength at high curing temperatures by forming a cross-linked gel network that suppresses microcracking.
