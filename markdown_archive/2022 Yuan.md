# Robotic 3D printed lunar bionic architecture based on lunar regolith selective laser sintering technology

## Abstract
This paper presents an integrated fabrication process for a bionic lunar base prototype using 3D printing and lunar regolith. It covers the printing mechanism, real-time control of powder laying, and the development of a tool end system. The study addresses in situ fabrication questions through optimization design, material gradation (lunar soil-PAEK hybrid), and dual-light field laser printing.

## 1 Introduction
Space exploration agencies aim to establish lunar bases. Construction involves stages from preparation to post-operation. Environmental challenges include vacuum, fine dust, micrometeorites, radiation, and extreme temperatures. Three development phases for lunar bases are discussed: pre-integrated Earth modules, inflatable devices, and in situ resource utilization (ISRU) based construction.

## 2 State-of-the-art
### 2.1 Lunar base design based on in situ methods
Comparison of projects like NASA's Sinterhub, ESA's Lunar Habitation, and HZUST's Chinese Super Mason. Key differences involve sintering methods (microwave vs. autoclave) and energy requirements.

### 2.2 Lunar soil in situ 3D printing technology
Five primary types of additive manufacturing (AM) for regolith: 
- Material extrusion/jetting
- Powder bed fusion (PBF)
- Laser Engineered Net Shaping (LENS)
- Selective Microwave Sintering (SMWS)
- Stereolithography (SLA)

## 3 Architectural conceptual design for lunar habitats
Bionic structures inspired by mollusk shells are proposed for their structural efficiency and protection capabilities. Design steps include environmental analysis, geometric prototyping using microgravity state analysis, and robotic simulation.

## 4 Lunar base 3D laser-printing construction
Focuses on the interaction between lunar soil-PAEK hybrid powder and laser beams. PAEK is selected as a binder to lower sintering temperature and improve efficiency. A dual-light field coupling strategy is used to optimize temperature distribution during printing.

## 5 Experiments
Using JSC-1A simulant, the study identifies optimal temperature ranges (360-450°C) for sintering. Compressive strength was tested against resin content and processing pressure. Large-scale horizontal and vertical samples were successfully printed to verify the method.

## 6 Conclusion
In situ construction is identified as the most cost-effective path. 3D printing offers high flexibility for lunar base construction. The study establishes a reliable baseline for bionic, ISRU-based habitat fabrication.
