# A review towards the design of extraterrestrial structures: From regolith to human outposts

## Abstract
The design of a permanent human habitat on a planetary body other than the Earth is an idea introduced many decades ago... The scope of this paper is to present a meticulous selection of the most significant publications within the scientific areas related to: (a) geotechnical engineering aspects... (b) ground motions generated by different types of Moonquakes and meteoroid impacts; (c) the different concepts and types of extraterrestrial (ET) structures (generic, inflatable, deployable, 3D-printed), as well as overall views of proposed ET habitats.

## 1. Introduction
The 'space age' began in 1957. Civil engineering must now expand toward the construction of Lunar and Martian structures. Key environmental challenges include low gravity, temperature fluctuations, radiation, lack of atmosphere, and meteoroid impacts.

### 2.2. Martian Regolith
Martian regolith is a mix of weathered and windblown material. The upper 5m layer is mainly cohesionless basaltic sand and rocks. Estimates suggest a minimum cohesion of 1–2 kPa based on slope stability back-analyses from InSight pits.

## 2.4. Anchoring in Regolith
Anchoring is essential for modular structures in microgravity to prevent potential uplifting. Methods reviewed include helical anchoring, suction drilling, circular wedge anchoring, and claw anchoring.

## 2.5. Regolith-based Landing Pads
Landing pads mitigate dust and plume effects. Materials reviewed include Lunar concrete (KOHLS-1), basalt pavers, and sintered regolith tiles using Contour Crafting or interlocking systems.

## 4. Extraterrestrial Structures
### 4.1. Generic Structural Concepts
Includes trusses supporting regolith shields (Benaroya), cable systems (Ettouney), and tensegrity structures.

### 4.2. Inflatable Structures
Inflatables are favored for their high volume-to-mass ratio and internal pressure resistance. Examples include TransHab and the Astrophytum concept.

### 4.3. Deployable Structures
Utilizing integral folding hinges (IFH) or origami-inspired designs for compact stowage during transport.

### 4.4. 3D-printed ET Structures
Additive Manufacturing (AM) technologies include:
* **Selective Laser/Solar/Microwave Sintering:** Fusing regolith particles using heat.
* **Contour Crafting:** Extrusion-based layering of regolith mixed with binders (e.g., sulfur concrete).
* **D-shape:** Binder jetting methodology using regolith simulants.

## 6. Discussion
The review highlights that while regolith cohesion is low (<6 kPa), the internal friction angle is significant (>40°), facilitating foundation load transfer. Design evolution has moved from rigid Earth-made modules to hybrid inflatable-ISRU structures and finally to fully 3D-printed habitats utilizing microwave sintering.
