# A review towards the design of extraterrestrial structures: From regolith to human outposts

**Authors:** Nicos Kalapodis, Georgios Kampas, Olga-Joan Ktenidou
**Journal:** Acta Astronautica 175 (2020) 540–569
**Publisher:** Elsevier

## Abstract
The design of a permanent human habitat on a planetary body other than the Earth is an idea introduced many decades ago... The scope of this paper is to present a meticulous selection of the most significant publications within the scientific areas related to: (a) geotechnical engineering aspects, including mechanical properties and chemical composition of Lunar and Martian regolith; (b) ground motions generated by Moonquakes and meteoroid impacts; (c) concepts and types of extraterrestrial (ET) structures (generic, inflatable, deployable, 3D-printed).

## 1. Introduction
Civil engineering must expand towards the design and construction of Lunar and Martian structures. Key environmental challenges include low gravity, high temperature fluctuations, hazardous radiation, lack of atmosphere leading to meteoroid impacts, and dangerous Lunar dust.

## 2. Aspects of Geotechnical Engineering
### 2.1 Lunar Regolith
- **Physical Properties:** Density, porosity, granulometric composition, adhesion, cohesion ($c$), and internal friction angle ($\phi$).
- **Bulk Density ($\rho$):** Increases with depth from $1.50 \pm 0.05$ g/cm³ (0–15 cm) to $1.74 \pm 0.05$ g/cm³ (30–60 cm).
- **Relative Density ($D_R$):** Increases abruptly between 10 and 20 cm depth.

### 2.2 Martian Regolith
Tested in-situ via Viking landers, Phoenix, and rovers (Spirit, Opportunity, Curiosity). Composition is primarily basaltic sand with weathered materials. Oxides like $SiO_2$ and $Al_2O_3$ are most abundant.

### 2.4 Anchoring and Landing Pads
Anchoring is essential for stability in microgravity. Landing pads mitigate dust problems and plume effects. Materials like Lunar concrete (KOHLS-1) and basalt-derived tiles are proposed for ISRU-based construction.

## 3. Extraterrestrial Ground Motions
Four types of Moonquakes: deep, shallow, thermal, and meteoroid impacts. Shallow moonquakes are the most energetic ($m_b$ ~ 4.8). Marsquakes recorded by InSight are smaller but show higher attenuation than Lunar recordings.

## 4. Extraterrestrial Structures
Categorized into:
1. **Generic:** Trusses, cabled systems, tensegrity (Class I/Pre-fabricated).
2. **Inflatable:** Prevalent due to low volume during transport and high internal pressure capacity (Class II).
3. **3D-Printed:** Utilizing ISRU/regolith for radiation shielding and structure (Class III).
