# In Situ Utilization of Martian Resources: Advances and Prospects in Plasma Technology

**Authors:** Haiwei Zhu, Shihao Tan, Cuntao Lan, Dawei Liu, and Xinpei Lu  
**Journal:** ACS Sustainable Chemistry & Engineering  
**Year:** 2025, 13, 8406−8423  
**DOI:** 10.1021/acssuschemeng.5c02413

## Abstract
In situ resource utilization (ISRU) is essential for sustainable Mars exploration. This review examines recent advances in nonthermal plasma systems for key ISRU tasks, including CO2 decomposition for oxygen and fuel production, water extraction from hydrated minerals, and regolith sintering for habitat construction. Special attention is given to microwave and dielectric barrier discharge plasmas and spark plasma sintering for producing high-strength ceramics.

## 1. Introduction
Mars exploration has evolved from flybys to rovers (Pathfinder, Curiosity, Perseverance, Tianwen-1). Human colonization is attractive due to the 24.6 h diurnal cycle and CO2 atmosphere. Key challenges include the 6-9 month travel time and high transportation costs ($10,000s/kg).

## 2. Martian Environment and Exploitable Resources
*   **Atmosphere:** ~95.3% CO2, 2.7% N2, 1.6% Ar; Pressure: 6-7 mbar.
*   **Soil:** Rich in iron oxides (hematite, magnetite) and hydrated minerals (gypsum, montmorillonite).
*   **Water:** Extensive ice reserves beneath the surface in polar and midlatitude regions.

## 4. Application of Plasma Technology in Mars ISRU
### 4.1. Mars Air Purification
Plasma-based electrostatic precipitators (ESP) can remove Martian dust effectively. Studies show optimized Z-shaped corrugated plates increase capture efficiency by 20%.

### 4.2. CO2 Decomposition for Oxygen
Microwave plasma reactors achieved O2 production rates of 75.7 ± 0.9 g/h at 25 mbar, which is nearly 15 times the output of NASA's MOXIE system. Energy conversion efficiency was ~0.013 kWh/g O2.

### 4.3. Preparation of Construction Materials
Spark Plasma Sintering (SPS) uses pulsed electrical currents and pressure to densify regolith. Sintered volcanic rock (lunar/martian simulant) reached compressive strengths up to 566 MPa and Vickers hardness of 650 HV at 1000 °C.

### 4.4. Water Production and Purification
Microwave heating (250 W, 2.45 GHz) successfully extracted up to 67% of water content from simulants within 25 minutes. Plasma jets (generating H2O2, radicals) provide high-efficiency sterilization for water purification.

## 5. Technical Challenges
Challenges include extreme temperature swings (-125 °C to 20 °C), pervasive dust infiltrating electronics, and intense radiation. Solutions involve specialized ceramics, radiation-hardened electronics, and self-cleaning surfaces.

## 6. Conclusion
Plasma technology offers a transformative approach to Mars ISRU due to its high efficiency and adaptability. Continued research in miniaturization and integration with renewable energy is vital for future habitation.
