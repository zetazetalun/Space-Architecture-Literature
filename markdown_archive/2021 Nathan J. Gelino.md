# Off Earth Landing and Launch Pad Construction—A Critical Technology for Establishing a Long-Term Presence on Extraterrestrial Surfaces

**Authors:** Nathan J. Gelino, Robert P. Mueller, Robert W. Moses, James G. Mantovani, Philip T. Metzger, Brad C. Buckles, and Laurent Sibille.

**Affiliations:** NASA Kennedy Space Center (Swamp Works), NASA Langley Research Center, University of Central Florida, The Bionetics Corp., Southeastern Universities Research Association.

## ABSTRACT
A long-term presence on the Moon will require numerous lunar landings and launches. Rocket engine plume interactions with unprepared lunar regolith cause high-velocity ejecta (>2,000 m/s), risking damage to vehicles and surface assets. This paper baselines the state of the art for Off-Earth Launch & Landing Pad (LLP) construction, establishes metrics for comparison (trade studies), and identifies key technology gaps.

## INTRODUCTION
A Launch & Landing Complex (LLC) includes structural supports, exhaust management, and Foreign Object Debris (FOD) mitigation. Repeated operations at a single site require durable landing pads. The paper focuses on the technologies and methods needed to construct the Launch & Landing pad (LLP) and associated blast barriers.

## PRELIMINARY ENGINEERING ASSESSMENT
*   **CLPS Landers:** Payload <300 kg, thrust <4000 N.
*   **HLS Landers:** Payload >12 t, thrust ~100 kN.
*   **Erosion Scales:** Apollo (16 kN thrust) displaced 2.6 metric tons of regolith. Scaling models suggest HLS landers could displace 16 metric tons, with ejecta exceeding lunar escape velocity.

## STATE OF THE ART AND PAST WORK
The current overall TRL for LLP construction is approximately **TRL-2**. Key previous works include:
*   **PISCES (2016):** Robotic construction of a pad from sintered basalt pavers in Hawaii. Testing showed lifting of pavers due to seam weakness.
*   **Van Susante & Metzger (2016):** Soil stabilization tests; rock beds were more effective than loose or compacted regolith.
*   **Korean Lunar Simulants (2012):** Concrete pad made with polymer binders and glass fiber.

## TRADE STUDY CRITERIA
The paper proposes 15 criteria across three phases:
1.  **Preparation and Staging:** Up-mass, Difficulty of ISRU processing, Site prep time, Reliance on other assets/crew, Robotics/Autonomy, Robustness, TRL, Required Power.
2.  **Construction Phase:** Constructability, Versatility, Construction Time, Ability to Verify Performance.
3.  **Operations and Maintenance:** Performance as a surface, Expected Life, Ease of Repairability, Lifecycle cost.

## TECHNOLOGY GAPS
Significant gaps exist in automated emplacement systems, material processing strategies, and long-term durability under lunar conditions (vacuum, thermal cycling, radiation). Prototypes must be tested in simulated environments to raise TRL.
