# Reimagining Lunar Dust: A Novel Reinforcement for Fiber-Reinforced Polymer Matrix Composite Materials

**Authors:** Denizhan Yavas, Ashraf F. Bastawros  
**Journal:** Advanced Engineering Materials  
**Publication Date:** 2026  
**DOI:** 10.1002/adem.202502670

## Abstract
The prohibitive cost of transporting construction materials to space underscores the need for in-situ resource utilization strategies (ISRU). Lunar regolith dust represents an abundant and sustainable reinforcement for advanced composites. Here, we investigate the use of Black Point-1 (BP-1) lunar soil simulant as a novel filler in fiber-reinforced polymer composites. Composites containing 1–10 wt% BP-1 were systematically evaluated through tensile testing, interlaminar shear strength (ILSS) measurements, fracture toughness characterization, and low-velocity impact experiments. At optimal loadings, BP-1 increases ILSS by 30%–40% and impact resistance by 15% while preserving the intrinsic properties of the polymer matrix. Micro-toughening mechanisms, including fiber bridging and crack deflection promoted by particle–matrix interactions, effectively suppress interlaminar damage initiation and propagation. Compared to conventional fillers, BP-1 delivers superior reinforcement efficiency on a weight-normalized basis, attributed to its angular morphology and high surface area. These results demonstrate that lunar dust can serve as an effective reinforcement for fiber-reinforced composites, offering a resource-efficient pathway toward durable materials for extraterrestrial and terrestrial infrastructure.

## 1 | Introduction
Lunar exploration drive has created an urgent need for materials capable of enduring extreme thermal, mechanical, and environmental conditions. Lunar regolith is an abundant resource on the Moon, characterized as a fine, angular particulate rich in oxides and silicates. While historically investigated for sintered blocks and concrete, its potential as a reinforcement in fiber-reinforced polymer matrix composites (PMCs) remains underexplored.

## 2 | Results and Discussion

### 2.1 | Mechanical and Fracture Properties of Lunar Dust-Reinforced Epoxy
- **Stiffening effect:** Tensile testing revealed a clear stiffening effect; elastic stiffness increased by approximately 15%.
- **Strength:** Low filler loadings led to a modest increase in ultimate tensile strength due to improved load transfer at the particle-matrix interface.
- **Fracture Toughness:** Moderate filler content enhances resistance to crack growth via crack deflection and local energy dissipation.

### 2.2 | Interlaminar Shear Strength (ILSS)
Short-beam bending (SBB) tests for composites with 1–10 wt% BP-1 showed:
- **Load Capacity:** Additions of 1–5 wt% BP-1 increased load-bearing capacity by up to 65% at 3% filler content.
- **ILSS Enhancement:** ILSS increased by 30%–40% relative to neat epoxy, peaking at 3–7.5 wt%.
- **Morphology Advantages:** The sharp edges and high surface roughness of BP-1 particles promote mechanical interlocking with the epoxy matrix.

### 2.3 | Low-Speed Impact Characteristics
- **Energy Absorption:** BP-1 reinforcement increases peak normalized force and prolongs impact duration.
- **Delamination Suppression:** BP-1 particles suppressed widespread delamination, confining damage to shallow surface layers.
- **Efficiency:** On a weight-normalized basis (specific gravity vs performance), BP-1 outperformed many conventional fillers like silica and alumina.

## 3 | Conclusions
BP-1 lunar dust is an effective multifunctional additive for high-performance composites. Modest loadings (3 wt%) strengthen interlaminar regions by impeding crack initiation and propagation without significantly altering matrix ductility. These findings support the viability of lunar regolith as an ISRU resource for fabricating durable, lightweight structural components directly on the Moon.

## 4 | Experimental Section
- **Materials:** Saertex Biaxial Glass Fabric (444 g/m²), West Systems Resin 105, Hardener 206.
- **Filler:** Black Point-1 (BP-1) lunar soil simulant (average particle size 6–7 μm).
- **Fabrication:** Hand layup followed by vacuum bagging and curing at room temperature for 24 hours.
