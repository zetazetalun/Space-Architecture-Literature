# Reimagining Lunar Dust: A Novel Reinforcement for Fiber-Reinforced Polymer Matrix Composite Materials

**Authors:** Denizhan Yavas, Ashraf F. Bastawros
**Journal:** Advanced Engineering Materials
**Year:** 2026
**DOI:** 10.1002/adem.202502670

## Abstract
The prohibitive cost of transporting construction materials to space underscores the need for in-situ resource utilization (ISRU). Lunar regolith dust represents an abundant and sustainable reinforcement for advanced composites. This paper investigates Black Point-1 (BP-1) lunar soil simulant as a novel filler in fiber-reinforced polymer composites. Composites containing 1–10 wt% BP-1 were systematically evaluated through tensile testing, interlaminar shear strength (ILSS) measurements, fracture toughness characterization, and low-velocity impact experiments. At optimal loadings, BP-1 increases ILSS by 30%–40% and impact resistance by 15%.

## 1 | Introduction
The establishment of a sustained human presence on the Moon requires advanced materials capable of enduring extreme conditions. Lunar regolith, rich in oxides and silicates, is an attractive candidate for ISRU. Historically investigated for sintered blocks and concrete, its potential as a reinforcement in fiber-reinforced polymer matrix composites (PMCs) remains underexplored.

## 2 | Results and Discussion

### 2.1 | Mechanical and Fracture Properties of Lunar Dust-Reinforced Epoxy
- **Stiffening Effect:** Addition of BP-1 particles (1-5 wt%) leads to a modest increase in elastic stiffness (~15%) and tensile strength.
- **Fracture Toughness:** Moderate filler content enhances resistance to crack growth via crack deflection and local energy dissipation. Higher concentrations (>5 wt%) lead to agglomeration and reduced performance.

### 2.2 | Interlaminar Shear Strength (ILSS)
- **Performance Increase:** Additions of 1–5 wt% BP-1 increase load-bearing capacity. Peak performance is observed at ~3 wt% filler content, showing an ILSS enhancement of 30-40% compared to neat epoxy.
- **Mechanism:** BP-1 particles promote mechanical interlocking and act as local bridges between fibers and the matrix, suppressing delamination.

### 2.3 | Low-Speed Impact Characteristics
- **Energy Absorption:** At 3 wt% BP-1, composites exhibit a marked increase in absorbed energy density.
- **Damage Suppression:** Post-impact thermography indicates that delamination damage is significantly reduced in BP-1 reinforced samples, with damage confined primarily to shallow surface layers.

## 3 | Conclusions
BP-1 lunar dust is a promising reinforcement for aerospace composites. Modest loadings (3 wt%) significantly strengthen interlaminar regions by impeding crack initiation without altering intrinsic matrix properties. This approach is particularly relevant for early-stage lunar missions utilizing ISRU.

## 4 | Experimental Section
- **Materials:** Saertex Biaxial Glass Fabric (444 g/m²), West Systems Resin 105, Hardener 206, BP-1 simulant (6–7 μm average size).
- **Fabrication:** Hand layup followed by vacuum-bagging and curing at room temperature for 24 h.
