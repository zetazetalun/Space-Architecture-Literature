# Mechanical property and micro-mechanism of Nano-SiO2 enhanced simulated lunar soil-based geopolymer

**Journal:** Journal of Building Engineering  
**Volume/Article:** 116 (2025) 114730  
**Authors:** Zhenyu Wang, Liqing Li, Faping Li, Banglu Xi  
**DOI:** 10.1016/j.jobe.2025.114730

## Abstract
The lunar research station serves as critical infrastructure for establishing deep space exploration bases. To improve the mechanical properties and microstructure of simulated lunar soil-based geopolymer (SLSG), this study utilized TJ-1 lunar soil simulant as the precursor and systematically investigated the influence of Nano-SiO2 (NS) on the water recovery rate, flowability, mechanical properties, and mass-strength efficiency. Results indicate that the co-addition of NS and alkali activator with a water-binder ratio of 0.27 represents the optimal preparation method. The 28-day compressive and flexural strengths reached 40.41 MPa and 10.85 MPa, respectively, with improvements of 40.80% and 57.02% over the reference group. 

## 1. Introduction
Lunar surface construction faces challenges due to extreme environmental conditions and the high cost of Earth-Moon transportation. In situ resource utilization (ISRU) is expected to address these issues. Geopolymers are inorganic cementitious materials synthesized via alkali activation of silica-aluminum-rich precursors like lunar soil. They offer high mechanical strength, resilience to temperature cycling, and compatibility with 3D printing methodologies.

## 2. Materials and Methods

### 2.1. Raw Materials
- **Precursor:** TJ-1 simulated lunar soil (similar to Apollo 14 and Chang’e-5 samples).
- **Activators:** Sodium hydroxide (SH), sodium silicate (SS), and calcium hydroxide (CH).
- **Additive:** Nano-SiO2 (NS) dispersion (particle size 30 nm).

### 2.2. Experimental Design
- **Water-binder ratios:** 0.24, 0.27, 0.30.
- **NS dosages:** 0, 0.50, 0.75, 1.00, 1.25 wt%.
- **Mixing sequences:** NA (NS first), AN (Activator first), CA (Co-addition).
- **Curing:** 100 °C for 24 hours.

## 3. Results and Discussion

### 3.1. Mechanical Properties
- **Optimal Dosage:** 0.75 wt% NS achieved the highest strength.
- **Compressive Strength:** 40.41 MPa (+40.80% vs baseline).
- **Flexural Strength:** 10.85 MPa (+57.02% vs baseline).
- **Mechanism:** NS promotes geopolymerization via "nucleation" and "chemical bonding" effects, while the "pore-filling effect" reduces total porosity by 4.52%.

### 3.2. Water Recovery Rate
- **Efficiency:** SLSG reached water recovery rates >98.5% after 7 days.
- **Early Stage:** NS reduces the 1-day water recovery (to ~40%), extending the geopolymerization period, which is beneficial for vacuum applications where rapid water loss can hinder chemical setting.

### 3.3. Thermal Stability
- TG-DSC-DTG analyses demonstrated that NS improves high-temperature thermal stability and reduces high-temperature mass loss (by 8.6%).

## 4. Conclusions
1. The optimal preparation involves co-addition of NS and activator at a water-binder ratio of 0.27.
2. 0.75 wt% NS significantly enhances mechanical strength and mass-strength efficiency.
3. NS improves structural density, inhibits coarse pore formation, and enhances reaction homogeneity.
4. High water recovery rates confirm the suitability of SLSG for sustainable lunar construction.
