# Mechanical property and micro-mechanism of Nano-SiO2 enhanced simulated lunar soil-based geopolymer

**Journal of Building Engineering** 116 (2025) 114730

**Authors:** Zhenyu Wang, Liqing Li, Faping Li, Banglu Xi

## Abstract
The lunar research station serves as critical infrastructure for establishing deep space exploration bases and achieving long-term extraterrestrial presence. To improve the mechanical properties and microstructure of simulated lunar soil-based geopolymer (SLSG), this study utilized TJ-1 lunar soil simulant as the precursor and systematically investigated the influence of Nano-SiO2 (NS) on the water recovery rate, flowability, mechanical properties, and mass-strength efficiency of SLSG. Results indicate that the co-addition of NS and alkali activator with a water-binder ratio of 0.27 represents the optimal preparation method. The 28-day compressive and flexural strengths reached 40.41 MPa and 10.85 MPa, respectively, corresponding to improvements of 40.80 % and 57.02 % over the reference group. The material allows a water recovery rate exceeding 98.5 % after 7 days, providing theoretical insights for enhanced lunar construction materials.

## 1. Introduction
Lunar surface construction faces challenges due to extreme environments and the high cost of Earth-Moon transportation. In situ resource utilization (ISRU) is essential. Geopolymers are inorganic cementitious materials synthesized via alkali activation of silica-aluminum-rich precursors like lunar soil. They offer high strength, rapid development, and compatibility with 3D printing methodologies, enabling automated construction.

## 2. Materials and Methods
- **Precursor:** TJ-1 simulated lunar soil (similar to Apollo 14 and Chang'e-5 samples).
- **Activators:** Sodium hydroxide (SH), sodium silicate (SS), and calcium hydroxide (CH).
- **Enhancer:** Nano-SiO2 (NS) aqueous dispersion (30 nm particle size).
- **Variables:** Mixing sequence (NA, AN, CA), water-binder ratio (0.24, 0.27, 0.30), and NS dosage (0.50–1.25 wt%).

## 3. Results and Discussion
- **Mixing Order:** Co-addition (CA) of NS and activator demonstrated superior performance.
- **Water Recovery:** SLSG achieves nearly complete water recovery (>98.5%) after 7 days, making it suitable for water-scarce lunar environments.
- **Mechanical Strength:** The optimal NS dosage is 0.75 wt%. Compressive strength improved by 40.80% and flexural strength by 57.02%.
- **Microstructure:** NS reduces total porosity by 4.52% through a pore-filling effect and promotes geopolymerization via a nucleation effect.

## 4. Conclusions
1. Optimal preparation: Co-addition of NS/activator with a water-binder ratio of 0.27.
2. Optimal dosage: 0.75 wt% NS leads to 40.41 MPa compressive and 10.85 MPa flexural strength.
3. High water efficiency: >96% water recovery at 3 days and >98.5% at 7 days.
4. Micro-mechanism: NS densifies the matrix by filling pores, sealing cracks, and acting as nucleation sites for geopolymer gel formation.
