# Karst Cave as Terrestrial Simulation Platform to Test and Design Human Base in Lunar Lava Tube

**Authors:** Jinghang Ding, Gengxin Xie, Linli Guo, Xin Xiong, Ya Han, and Xi Wang  
**Journal:** Space: Science & Technology (AAAS Science Partner Journal)  
**Volume:** 2022, Article ID 9875780  
**Published:** 4 November 2022  
**DOI:** 10.34133/2022/9875780

## Abstract
Developing efficient approaches to building a suitable environment for humans on the moon plays a key role in future long-term sustainable lunar exploration activities. Lunar lava tubes offer stable structures, suitable temperatures, low radiation doses, and low meteorite impact rates. This paper proposes using Earth’s karst caves (specifically in Chongqing) as simulation sites to address technical bottlenecks such as cave sealing technology, efficient daylight systems, and artificial ecosystem internal circulation.

## 1. Introduction
Lunar lava tubes are geological structures formed by volcanic activity. Their hard basalt roofs provide natural protection against extreme temperatures, high vacuum, cosmic radiation, and meteorite impacts, making them ideal candidates for human habitats (Class III).

### 2.2 Safety Assessment
*   **Radiation:** Lava tubes buried >6m deep provide near-zero radiation exposure from cosmic rays and solar particles.
*   **Meteorites:** Burial depth protects habitats; the safety condition is defined by the ratio of target thickness to crater diameter (<0.87).
*   **Moon Dust:** The interior of lava tubes is a permanent shadow zone where charged moon dust is hardly produced.
*   **Stability:** Lunar lava tubes have survived moonquakes for millions of years, indicating structural stability.

## 3. Karst Cave as Simulation Platform
Karst caves in Chongqing, China (e.g., Wulong, Youyang) are proposed as analogs. While formation mechanisms differ (dissolution vs. volcanic), they share similarities in size, semicircular geometry, and thermal stability (internal temperatures 14-21°C).

### 3.1 Research Directions
1.  **Cave Sealing Technology:** Developing low-cost sealing and maintenance solutions using in situ resources.
2.  **Daylight Systems:** Using light pipes to introduce sunlight for ecological laboratories.
3.  **Automated Construction:** Utilizing robots for measurement and construction within the cave environment.

## 4. Challenges for Future Development
1. Lack of internal measurement data for lava pipelines.
2. Incomplete large-scale artificial closed ecosystems.
3. Requirement for improved in situ resource utilization (ISRU) and low-gravity adaptation technologies.

## 5. Conclusion
Lunar lava tubes provide a superior alternative to surface bases due to reduced energy requirements for life support and natural shielding. Terrestrial karst caves offer a valuable simulation platform to test the necessary technologies for long-term lunar habitation.
