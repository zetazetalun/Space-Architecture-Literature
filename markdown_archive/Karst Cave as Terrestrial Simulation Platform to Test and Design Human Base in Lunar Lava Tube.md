# Karst Cave as Terrestrial Simulation Platform to Test and Design Human Base in Lunar Lava Tube

**Authors:** Jinghang Ding, Gengxin Xie, Linli Guo, Xin Xiong, Ya Han, and Xi Wang
**Publication:** Space: Science & Technology (A Science Partner Journal), Volume 2022
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.34133/2022/9875780

## Abstract
Developing efficient approaches to building a suitable environment for humans on the moon plays a key role in future long-term sustainable lunar exploration activities. The lava tubes discovered by the Kaguya mission offer huge potential sites to host such bases. Through computation and analysis, the authors show that lunar lava tubes offer stable structures, suitable temperatures, low radiation doses, and low meteorite impact rates. The paper proposes using Earth’s karst caves in Chongqing as simulation sites to address cave sealing technology, efficient daylight systems, and artificial ecosystems.

## 1. Introduction
Extraterrestrial environments are hostile due to vacuum, low gravity, cosmic radiation, and micrometeorite impacts. Lunar lava tubes, formed by volcanic activity, offer natural basalt roofs that provide a stable thermal environment and protection. The width of these tubes is expected to reach 1-2 km and lengths of tens to hundreds of kilometers.

### 2.2 Safety Assessment
- **Radiation:** Lava tubes buried >6m below the surface are protected from cosmic rays and solar wind.
- **Meteorite Impact:** Tubes are safer if the burial depth is sufficient (ratio of target thickness to crater diameter > 0.87).
- **Dust:** Minimal dust inside lava tubes compared to the lunar surface.
- **Moon Quake:** While quakes occur, lava tubes have survived for millions of years, suggesting structural stability.

## 3. Using Karst Caves for Simulation
Karst caves in Chongqing (Wulong, Youyang) are proposed as analogs due to similar semicircular structures and mild internal environments (14–21°C). 

### 3.3 Proposed Simulation Platform
The platform involves:
1. **Sealing Technology:** Using in situ resources to seal cave sections.
2. **Automated Construction:** Research on robotic systems for underground habitat setup.
3. **Artificial Ecosystems:** Testing interaction between animals, plants, and natural limestone/soil.

## 4. Conclusion
Lunar lava tubes are superior candidates for lunar bases due to their natural protection. The energy required to maintain a stable environment in a lava tube is approximately five times lower than on the lunar surface. Karst caves on Earth provide a viable platform for testing necessary survival and construction technologies.
