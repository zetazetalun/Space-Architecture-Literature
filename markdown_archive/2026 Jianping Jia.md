# Fabrication of lunar basalt bulk using the DC FHPs method

**Authors:** JIANPING JIA, ZIRUN YANG, HUIHUI ZHANG, YANG ZHANG and KUNYOU ZHANG  
**Journal:** Bull. Mater. Sci. (2026) 49:113  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1007/s12034-026-03623-6

## Abstract
With the advancement of lunar exploration programs, lunar basalt materials have demonstrated significant potential for aerospace, construction, and other applications due to their high strength, thermal stability and other exceptional properties. This study successfully achieved the rapid preparation of bulk lunar basalt materials using the direct current (DC) fast hot-pressed sintering (FHPs) method. The phase composition, microstructure and mechanical properties were systematically analyzed. Results indicated optimal material performance at 1000°C, 40 MPa, and 10-min holding time, yielding a density of 3.12 g/cm³, Vickers hardness of 853.7 HV, fracture toughness of 2.2 MPa m^1/2 and bending strength of 85 MPa.

## 1. Introduction
Lunar base construction faces a key bottleneck in the efficient utilization of in-situ resources. Basalt, accounting for more than 60% of material in lunar mare regions, is a core raw material for preparing structural materials. Current technologies like laser or microwave sintering face high energy consumption and low efficiency. DC Fast Hot-Pressed Sintering (FHPs), relying on DC Joule Heating, achieves fast heating rates (1000°C/min) and high energy conversion efficiency (85%), making it suitable for lunar surface applications.

## 2. Experimental
Lunar basalt powder derived from simulated materials was loaded into graphite molds. 
- **Small samples:** 20 mm diameter, sintered at 900–1000°C, 40 MPa, 10 min.
- **Large-scale samples:** 80 × 55 × 10 mm and 210 × 110 × 10 mm, sintered at 960–1000°C, 30–40 MPa, 10–20 min.
Analytical techniques included XRD, SEM, Archimedes density measurement, Vickers hardness, and three-point bending tests.

## 3. Results and Discussion
- **Microstructure:** SEM analysis shows a clear sintering evolution from loosely packed particles at 900°C to continuous molten layered structures with nearly eliminated pores at 1000°C.
- **Chemical Composition:** Simulation of lunar basalt is verified as effective, containing primary minerals like plagioclase, pyroxene, and olivine.
- **Mechanical Performance:** Density increased from 2.75 to 3.12 g/cm³ as temperature rose from 900°C to 1000°C. Bending strength reached 85 MPa.
- **Energy Efficiency:** The energy consumption was quantified at 2.8 ± 0.3 kWh/kg, significantly lower than laser sintering (15–20 kWh/kg).

## 4. Conclusions
1. Optimal mechanical properties achieved at 1000°C, 40 MPa, 10 min: Density 3.12 g/cm³, 853.7 HV hardness, 2.2 MPa m^1/2 fracture toughness, 85 MPa bending strength.
2. Rapid densification (10–20 min) is significantly faster than conventional hot-pressed methods (approx. 1 h).
3. The work provides key parameters for the in-situ fabrication of construction materials for lunar bases.
