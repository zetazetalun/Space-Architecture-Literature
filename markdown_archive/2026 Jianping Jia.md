# Fabrication of lunar basalt bulk using the DC FHPs method

**Authors:** JIANPING JIA, ZIRUN YANG, HUIHUI ZHANG, YANG ZHANG, and KUNYOU ZHANG  
**Journal:** Bull. Mater. Sci. (2026) 49:113  
**DOI:** 10.1007/s12034-026-03623-6  

## Abstract
With the advancement of lunar exploration programs, lunar basalt materials have demonstrated significant potential for aerospace, construction, and other applications due to their high strength and thermal stability. This study successfully achieved the rapid preparation of bulk lunar basalt materials using the direct current (DC) fast hot-pressed sintering (FHPs) method. Optimal material performance was achieved under sintering conditions of 1000°C, 40 MPa, and a 10-min holding time. The resulting samples exhibited a density of 3.12 g/cm³, a Vickers hardness of 853.7 HV, a fracture toughness of 2.2 MPa·m¹/2, and bending strength of 85 MPa.

## 1. Introduction
Lunar base construction faces a key bottleneck in the efficient utilization of in-situ resources. The cost of Earth–Moon transportation ($50,000–$90,000 per kg) makes in-situ conversion of lunar soil inevitable. Basalt is a core raw material for preparing structural materials, comprising over 60% of lunar mare regions. Existing technologies like laser and microwave sintering face high energy consumption and low efficiency. The DC FHPs method, relying on Joule Heating, achieves fast heating rates (1000°C/min) and high energy conversion efficiency (85%), making it suitable for extraterrestrial ISRU.

## 2. Experimental
Simulated lunar basalt powder was loaded into graphite molds.  
**Sintering Conditions:**  
- Heating rate: 100C/min  
- Vacuum: < 10 Pa  
- Temperature: 900–1000°C  
- Pressure: 30–40 MPa  
- Holding time: 10–20 min  
- Sample Sizes: 20 mm diameter (small); 80 x 55 x 10 mm and 210 x 110 x 10 mm (large).

## 3. Results and Discussion
### Phase and Microstructure
XRD analysis revealed primary mineral composition: plagioclase, pyroxene, and olivine. SEM analysis showed that at 900°C, particles remain loosely packed. At 1000°C, a continuous molten layered structure forms with nearly eliminated pores and strong interfacial diffusion.

### Mechanical Properties
- **Density:** 3.12 g/cm³ (95% of theoretical density)
- **Vickers Hardness:** 853.7 HV
- **Bending Strength:** 85 MPa
- **Fracture Toughness (KIC):** 2.2 MPa·m¹/2
- **Energy Consumption:** 2.8 ± 0.3 kWh/kg (significantly lower than laser sintering at 15–20 kWh/kg).

## 4. Conclusions
The DC FHPs method is an efficient and scalable path for lunar basalt ISRU. Rapid densification within 10–20 minutes at 960–1000°C provides a technological framework for lunar infrastructure construction.
