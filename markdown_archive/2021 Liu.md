# Review of research into bioregenerative life support system(s) which can support humans living in space

**Journal:** Life Sciences in Space Research 31 (2021) 113–120
**Authors:** Hong Liu, Zhikai Yao, Yuming Fu, Jiajie Feng

## Abstract
To travel beyond the Earth and realize long-term survival in deep space, humans need to construct Bioregenerative Life Support Systems (BLSS), which reduce the requirement for supplies from Earth by in situ regenerating oxygen, water, and food, and preventing pollution by recycling waste. Since the 1960s, the USSR/Russia, the United States, Europe, Japan, and China have carried out numerous studies in BLSS systematic theories, unit technologies, design/construction, and long-term operation. China's "Lunar Palace 365" experiment realized Earth-based closed human survival for a year with a material closure of >98%. Future BLSS research will focus on lunar probe payloads to study the impact of space environmental conditions on the ecosystem, providing theoretical and technological support for crewed deep space exploration.

## 1. Introduction
BLSS is an artificial closed ecosystem composed of humans, plants, animals, and microorganisms based on ecological principles. It combines biotechnology and engineering control to recycle and regenerate oxygen, water, food, and other essential substances. It is a key technology for crewed deep-space exploration, supporting long-term autonomous existence.

## 2. BLSS's theoretical and technological system framework
The framework is multidisciplinary, including biology, environmental engineering, ecology, nutrition, and materials science. The development path involves studying individual units, followed by artificial ecosystem technologies, design/construction methods, and finally experimental research with human participation.

## 3. Research progress of earth-based BLSS

### 3.1 Earth-based BLSS research: the Soviet Union/Russia
*   **BIOS-3:** A 315 m³ system with four compartments. It carried out crewed experiments for 4–6 months, achieving >90% closure in gas and water and meeting 40–45% of food needs for three people. In some experiments, 95% closure was reached.

### 3.2 Earth-based BLSS research: the United States
*   **NASA CELSS/ALS:** Developed for lunar and Martian bases. Experiments concluded that 43 m² of higher plant cultivation can provide 90% of a person's nutritional requirements.
*   **LMLSTP:** Integrated biological and physical/chemical technologies; the third stage achieved four-person closed survival for 91 days.
*   **Biosphere 2:** The largest closed ecological system (1.3 hectares). While the first experiment faced carbon-dioxide absorption issues into unsealed concrete, the second achieved 100% food production.

### 3.3 Earth-based BLSS research: Japan
*   **CEEF:** Introduced the first animal unit (goats). It used physical and chemical equipment for rapid circulation, though it largely ignored the role of microorganisms.

### 3.4 Earth-based BLSS research: Europe
*   **MELiSSA:** Managed by ESA, focusing on a five-compartment cycle (waste decomposition, nitrification, photosynthetic food production, and crew).
*   **DLR Lunar Greenhouse:** Studied optimal structural design using rigid-inflatable hybrid structures (semi-cylindrical, 20 m length, 3 m radius).

### 3.5 Earth-based BLSS research: China
*   **Lunar Palace 1:** A 500 m³ facility. The "Lunar Palace 365" experiment (2017-2018) involved four crew members for 370 days. It achieved 100% oxygen/water regeneration, 83% food regeneration, and an overall material closure of 98.2%.

## 4. Prospect of spatial BLSS experimental research
Experiments must move from Earth to extraterrestrial environments (Moon/Mars) to account for gravity, magnetic fields, and radiation. The Moon is the most suitable initial body, specifically the polar regions due to continuous illumination and potential water ice. In-situ resources (lunar soil) should be studied for utilization in large-scale closed ecosystems.

## 5. Development path and evaluation indices
**Three-stage Strategy:**
1.  **First Stage:** Hydroponics and processing in situ resources (lunar/Martian soil) with waste to prepare soil-like substrates.
2.  **Second Stage:** Expansion to large-scale soil-like substrate cultivation, including insects (mealworms) for animal protein.
3.  **Third Stage:** Complete cycling of inorganic salts and organic waste, utilizing artificial oceans for fish cultivation.

**Three Key Conditions for Gas Balance (Corrected):**
1.  If all food comes from internal biosynthesis, oxygen production is sufficient for breathing.
2.  Organic matter input from outside must be balanced by organic matter output of the same respiration quotient synthesized inside.
3.  Gas balance requires human intervention and cannot be achieved by biological self-organization alone.

## 6. Overall prospect
Future development will integrate lightweight, automated, real-time, and modular designs, utilizing nanotechnology, plant probiotics, and in situ resources to minimize transportation costs and environmental disturbance.
