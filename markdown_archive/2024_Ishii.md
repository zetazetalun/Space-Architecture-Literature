# Low-velocity impact response of lunar and Martian regolith simulants: Implications for lunar and Martian surface explorations

**Authors:** Takuma Ishii, Arata Kioka, Jyh-Jaan Steven Huang, Yoshiki Tabuchi, Yasuhiro Yamada
**Journal:** *Physics of Fluids* 36, 113318 (2024)
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1063/5.0233884

## Abstract
The granular mechanics of lunar and Martian regolith remain inadequately understood, impeding progress in successful exploration, landing, drilling, sampling, and construction activities on extraterrestrial surfaces. This study investigates the granular behavior of lunar (LHS-1, LMS-1) and Martian (MGS-1) regolith simulants compared to terrestrial silica sand under low-velocity impact conditions. Findings show that crater depths in lunar highlands and Martian surfaces are significantly greater than in mare regolith or terrestrial sand, providing insights for future drilling and resource utilization.

## Results and Discussion
- **Impact Scaling:** Crater depth increases linearly with impact velocity (0.44–0.70 m/s). 
- **Material Resistance:** Lunar highland (LHS-1) and Martian (MGS-1) regolith simulants show approximately two times the crater depth of terrestrial silica sand at similar impact velocities. 
- **Geomechanical Factors:** The differences in crater depth are attributed to lower cohesion and higher porosity in LHS-1 and MGS-1 compared to the more resistant lunar mare (LMS-1).
- **Scaling Laws:** Power-law scaling indicates that extraterrestrial surfaces offer less resistance to impact than terrestrial sand, which is critical for landing and sampling tool design.

## Conclusions
The unique granular mechanics of different regolith surfaces must be considered when assessing landing, sampling, and infrastructure construction. The higher porosity of highlands and Martian surfaces makes them more compressible, while the significant cohesion of regolith overall poses challenges for effective drilling operations compared to Earth-based soils.
