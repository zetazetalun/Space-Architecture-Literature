# Dual-Functional Metallized Basalt Fiber with Exceptional Electromagnetic Interference Shielding and Joule Heating Capabilities under Simulated Lunar Temperature Conditions

**Authors:** Che Zhou, Lin Zhao, Lei Hui, Zeyu Yang, Tao Fu, Jian Huang, and Dongzhen Chen
**Publication:** ACS Appl. Mater. Interfaces 2026, 18, 39093−39105
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1021/acsami.6c07813

## Abstract
The lunar mare region is rich in basaltic minerals, and in situ resource utilization (ISRU) is a fundamental strategy for sustainable extraterrestrial construction. However, combining electroless plating with basalt fibers for such construction leads to a significant mismatch of coefficient of thermal expansion (CTE) between the fiber substrate and metal coating. Under extreme temperature alternations, this mismatch induces interfacial thermal stress concentration, causing coating peeling and performance failure. To address this issue, this study, using commercial terrestrial basalt fiber as an analogue for lunar basaltic materials, proposes an ISRU-inspired metallized fiber composite suitable for wide-temperature-range applications. By sequential electroless nickel plating and copper electroplating on basalt fibers, a nickel–copper-coated basalt fiber fabric (BF@Ni@Cu) was successfully fabricated, exhibiting high electrical conductivity, excellent electromagnetic interference shielding effectiveness (62.59 dB), and significant joule heating performance. The Ni interlayer forms a CTE gradient transition between the basalt substrate and the outer Cu layer, mitigating interfacial thermal stress. After annealing and PDMS encapsulation, the surface reflection characteristics are effectively regulated. To verify reliability under lunar diurnal temperature variations, cold–thermal shock cycle tests simulating the lunar range (from −196 to 130 °C) are conducted. After 30 cycles, the material maintained structural integrity without cracking or peeling, successfully overcoming interfacial thermal stress concentration. Consequently, the EMI shielding and joule heating performance showed only slight degradation, demonstrating excellent temperature shock resistance. This study not only provides a fiber metallization strategy that retains high performance under extreme temperature alternations but also offers a potential technical pathway inspired by ISRU for multifunctional protection and thermal management materials in future lunar base construction, through the design concept of thermal stress alleviation and failure-mode control via a gradient interlayer.

## 1. Introduction
Global lunar exploration focuses on long-term habitation. Materials face challenges including intense cosmic radiation and drastic temperature fluctuations (-180 to 130 °C). Developing materials integrating structural load-bearing, EMI shielding, and active thermal management is critical. Basalt Fiber (BF) is a key ISRU candidate due to the abundance of basaltic minerals in lunar mare regions.

## 2. Experimental Section
### 2.1 Materials
- Basalt fiber (BF) plain weave fabric.
- Glass fiber (GF) as control.
- Chemicals: AgNO3, SnCl2, NiSO4, Cu2P2O7, etc.

### 2.2 Preparation of Basalt Fiber@Ni
Surface sizing was removed via acetone. Acid treatment with H2SO4/HNO3 created surface grooves. Sensitization with SnCl2 followed by activation with AgNO3 provided catalytic sites. Electroless nickel plating was performed at 80 °C.

### 2.3 Preparation of Basalt Fiber@Ni@Cu
Electroplating of copper was performed using the Ni-coated fiber as the cathode.

### 2.5 Annealing and PDMS Encapsulation
Samples were annealed in a muffle furnace and then encapsulated with PDMS (10:1 ratio) to regulate reflection characteristics and provide environmental protection.

## 3. Results and Discussion
### 3.1 Preparation and Properties
Acid treatment decreased tensile strength from 1.34 to 1.09 GPa due to surface defects, but Ni/Cu deposition increased it to 1.19 GPa by repairing surface flaws and enhancing load transfer.

### 3.2 EMI Shielding Performance
BF@Ni@Cu achieved a shielding effectiveness (SET) of 62.59 dB. Annealing and PDMS encapsulation shifted the shielding mechanism toward absorption-dominated performance (SE value of 44.74 dB with increased absorption coefficient).

### 3.3 Joule Heating Performance
Under a voltage of 1-2 V, the fabric rapidly heated (within 10s). At 2V, it reached 240 °C. The heating range allows for routine thermal management or high-power emergency heating.

### 3.4 Simulated Lunar Temperature Performance
Cold-thermal shock tests involved 30 cycles between 130 °C and -196 °C. BF@Ni@Cu showed only 0.4% weight loss. Resistance increased slightly (0.061 to 0.122 Ω), and EMI shielding dropped from 62 to 50.5 dB, still maintaining high effectiveness. The Ni interlayer successfully prevented the delamination observed in BF@Cu controls.

## 4. Conclusions
The study successfully demonstrated a robust Ni-Cu composite metal layer on basalt fiber. The gradient buffering structure formed by the Ni layer effectively alleviates thermal stress, ensuring structural integrity and functional stability for lunar base applications.
