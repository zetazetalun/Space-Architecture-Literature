# Dual-Functional Metallized Basalt Fiber with Exceptional Electromagnetic Interference Shielding and Joule Heating Capabilities under Simulated Lunar Temperature Conditions

**Authors:** Che Zhou, Lin Zhao, Lei Hui, Zeyu Yang, Tao Fu, Jian Huang, and Dongzhen Chen
**Published in:** ACS Applied Materials & Interfaces 2026, 18, 39093−39105

## Abstract
The lunar mare region is rich in basaltic minerals, and in situ resource utilization (ISRU) is a fundamental strategy for sustainable extraterrestrial construction. However, combining electroless plating with basalt fibers for such construction leads to a significant mismatch of coefficient of thermal expansion (CTE) between the fiber substrate and metal coating. Under extreme temperature alternations, this mismatch induces interfacial thermal stress concentration, causing coating peeling and performance failure. To address this issue, this study, using commercial terrestrial basalt fiber as an analogue for lunar basaltic materials, proposes an ISRU-inspired metallized fiber composite suitable for wide-temperature-range applications. By sequential electroless nickel plating and copper electroplating on basalt fibers, a nickel–copper-coated basalt fiber fabric (BF@Ni@Cu) was successfully fabricated, exhibiting high electrical conductivity, excellent electromagnetic interference shielding effectiveness (62.59 dB), and significant joule heating performance. The Ni interlayer forms a CTE gradient transition between the basalt substrate and the outer Cu layer, mitigating interfacial thermal stress. After annealing and PDMS encapsulation, the surface reflection characteristics are effectively regulated. To verify reliability under lunar diurnal temperature variations, cold–thermal shock cycle tests simulating the lunar range (from −196 to 130 °C) are conducted. After 30 cycles, the material maintained structural integrity without cracking or peeling, successfully overcoming interfacial thermal stress concentration. 

## 1. Introduction
Building materials for lunar bases face challenges from the celestial environment: intense cosmic radiation, solar particle events, and drastic lunar surface temperature fluctuations between −180 and 130 °C. Developing novel composite materials that integrate structural load-bearing, electromagnetic protection, and built-in heating functions is key to overcoming technological bottlenecks in lunar base construction.

## 2. Experimental Section
### 2.1. Materials
*   Basalt fiber (BF) plain weave fabric.
*   Glass fiber (GF) plain weave fabric (as parallel control).
*   Chemicals: Silver nitrate, Stannous chloride, nickel sulfate, etc.

### 2.2. Preparation of Basalt Fiber@Ni
1.  Acetone soak (30 min) for sizing removal.
2.  Acid treatment (H2SO4 and HNO3) with ultrasonic oscillation.
3.  Sensitization and Activation.
4.  Electroless nickel plating (80 °C, 30 min).

### 2.3. Preparation of Basalt Fiber@Ni@Cu
Electroplating of copper using the BF@Ni as the cathode and a Cu rod as the anode.

## 3. Results and Discussion
*   **Interfacial Bonding:** Acid treatment created grooves on the BF surface, enhancing mechanical interlocking. The Ni interlayer (CTE ~12.9 × 10⁻⁶/K) provided a buffer between the BF (CTE ~6 × 10⁻⁶/K) and Cu (CTE ~17.4 × 10⁻⁶/K).
*   **EMI Shielding:** BF@Ni@Cu achieved 62.59 dB shielding effectiveness in the X-band (attenuation of over 99.9999%).
*   **Joule Heating:** Stable surface temperatures up to 240 °C were achieved at 2 V. The relationship between temperature and the square of the voltage was linear.
*   **Lunar Simulation:** After 30 cycles of cold-thermal shock (-196 to 130 °C), the material showed no peeling or cracking. Resistance increased only slightly (from 0.061 to 0.122 Ω).

## 4. Conclusions
This study successfully constructed a robust nickel–copper composite metal layer on basalt fabric. The gradient buffering structure effectively alleviated interfacial thermal stress. The material maintains structural integrity and high performance under simulated lunar temperature conditions, offering a potential pathway for multifunctional protection materials in future lunar bases.
