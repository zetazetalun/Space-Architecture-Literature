# Brazing of SiC ceramic with extraterrestrial regolith simulant in ISRU and ISRF applications

**Wei Zheng**, **Guofu Qiao**
*Advances in Space Research 70 (2022) 752–761*

## Abstract
In-situ resources play an important role in future extraterrestrial human activities for facility repair and habitat construction, especially in sustainable space exploration of the Moon and Mars. This paper verifies the feasibility of lunar and Martian regolith simulant for ceramic joining in which SiC ceramic was made into specific size samples and then brazed with the regolith under argon atmosphere, respectively. The results show that the average ultimate shear strength of joints of SiC brazed with lunar regolith simulant (LRS-SiC) and Martian regolith simulant (MRS-SiC) was 21.5 ± 6.5 MPa and 27.3 ± 13.4 MPa, respectively.

## 1. Introduction
Establishing a permanent lunar base requires raw materials for equipment maintenance and infrastructure construction. In-situ resource utilization (ISRU) and in-situ fabrication and repair (ISFR) technologies can radically reduce mass and cost. High-temperature bonding of regolith is a simple process requiring only heat and the material itself.

### 2.2. Determination of joining temperature
The fully melting temperature of both simulants occurred before 1400 °C, which was chosen as the joining temperature for the brazing process with a holding time of 1 hour.

## 3. Results and Discussion

### 3.1. Shear strength of brazing samples
- **SiC-LRS Joints:** 21.5 ± 6.5 MPa.
- **SiC-MRS Joints:** 27.3 ± 13.4 MPa.
- These strengths are comparable to existing aerospace joining systems and sufficient for emergency repair/construction applications.

### 3.2. Microstructures and Reaction Mechanisms
Microstructural analysis (SEM/BSE) showed that the regolith filler infiltrated into the pores of the SiC substrate. XRD analysis confirmed the formation of new phases at the interface: **TiC** and **Fe3Si**. The bonding mechanism is attributed to intergranular diffusion and direct atomic bonding between the molten regolith glass and the SiC phase.

## 4. Conclusions
This study verifies the feasibility of brazing SiC ceramics using extraterrestrial regolith. The resulting joints demonstrate sufficient mechanical properties for in-situ equipment repair and habitat assembly on the Moon and Mars.
