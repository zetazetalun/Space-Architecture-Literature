# Exploring the Foundations of Interplanetary Logistics: Spaceport Infrastructure for the Moon and Mars

**Author:** Wanjiku Chebet Kanjumba
**Publication:** New Space (Mary Ann Liebert, Inc.)
**DOI:** 10.1177/21680256261469319

## Abstract
Sustainable human presence on the Moon and Mars requires surface infrastructure capable of supporting landing, refueling, cargo handling, and ISRU. This paper presents a systems architecture for extraterrestrial spaceports, informed by terrestrial logistics analogs and space mission data. Key components include regolith-mitigating landing pads, autonomous construction using robotic excavators, ISRU-to-propulsion chains, and modular habitats. The analysis identifies key gaps: limited validation of ISRU-derived materials, absence of standardized interface protocols, and insufficient power solutions for extreme environments. A phased implementation strategy is proposed, from robotic site surveys (2030s) to crew-tended hubs (2040s).

## Infrastructure Requirements
Spaceports must function as multifunctional nodes. Design must account for extreme thermal cycles (-173°C to +127°C on Moon; -80°C to +20°C on Mars) and radiation.

## Phased Development Approach
1. **Phase 1: Lunar Prototyping (2030-2040):** Robotic site validation and ISRU demonstration.
2. **Phase 2: Martian Precursor (2040-2050):** Autonomous construction of foundational infrastructure using local materials.
3. **Phase 3: Full-Scale Operations (2050+):** Hubs for scientific research and commercial interplanetary transit.

## Conclusion
This paper outlines a technically grounded architecture for spaceports integrating ISRU, autonomy, and modular design. Future work should prioritize Earth-analog demonstrations and open-architecture interface protocols.
