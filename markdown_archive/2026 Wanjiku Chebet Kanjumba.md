# Exploring the Foundations of Interplanetary Logistics: Spaceport Infrastructure for the Moon and Mars

**Author:** Wanjiku Chebet Kanjumba<sup>1,2</sup>
<sup>1</sup> Vicillion, Newark, Delaware, USA.
<sup>2</sup> University of Florida, Gainesville, Florida, USA.

## Abstract
Sustainable human presence on the Moon and Mars requires surface infrastructure capable of supporting landing, refueling, cargo handling, and ISRU. This paper presents a systems architecture for extraterrestrial spaceports, informed by terrestrial logistics analogs and space mission data (Artemis, Perseverance, VIPER). Key components include regolith-mitigating landing pads, autonomous construction using robotic excavators (e.g., RASSOR), ISRU-to-propulsion chains, and modular habitats. The analysis identifies key gaps: limited validation of ISRU-derived materials under lunar/Martian stressors, absence of standardized interface protocols, and power solutions for extreme cycles. A phased implementation strategy is proposed (2030s–2040s).

## Spaceport Infrastructure

### Current State and Identified Gaps
- **ISRU:** Lack of operational ISRU-based propellant production systems.
- **Construction:** No standardized landing pad designs resilient to regolith plume effects.
- **Governance:** Lack of binding international frameworks for spaceport safety and environmental protection.

## Phased Development Roadmap
- **Phase 1 (2030–2040):** Lunar Prototyping. Focus on site validation, ISRU demonstration, and 2 sintered regolith pads (50m diameter).
- **Phase 2 (2040–2050):** Martian Precursor Missions. Deployment of 3 pads with blast shield berms and a 4-person temporary outpost.
- **Phase 3 (2050+):** Full-Scale Operations. Sustained crewed operations with 12-person permanent crew and full closed-loop propellant supply.
