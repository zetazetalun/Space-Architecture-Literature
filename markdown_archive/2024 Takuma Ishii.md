# Low-velocity impact response of lunar and Martian regolith simulants: Implications for lunar and Martian surface explorations

**Cite as:** Phys. Fluids 36, 113318 (2024); **doi:** 10.1063/5.0233884
**Submitted:** 18 August 2024 . **Accepted:** 3 October 2024 . **Published Online:** 5 November 2024

**Authors:** Takuma Ishii, Arata Kioka, Jyh-Jaan Steven Huang, Yoshiki Tabuchi, and Yasuhiro Yamada

## ABSTRACT
The granular mechanics of lunar and Martian regolith remain inadequately understood, impeding progress in successful exploration, landing, drilling, sampling, and construction activities on extraterrestrial surfaces. This study aims to bridge this knowledge gap by investigating the granular behavior of the lunar and Martian regolith under impact conditions. Impact cratering experiments were conducted for the lunar highlands, lunar mare, Martian regolith simulants (LHS-1, LMS-1, and MGS-1, respectively), and terrestrial silica sand with similar particle sizes as target granular materials, with a sphere projectile dropping at low velocities. 

## I. INTRODUCTION
The lunar surface is covered by a layer of loose debris known as lunar regolith, with its average thickness varying from approximately 5 meters on the mare surfaces to around 10 meters on the highland surfaces. Understanding the geomechanical characteristics of regolith, including its strength, stiffness, and cohesion, is indispensable for designing successful landing and efficient drilling and coring tools on the lunar and Martian surfaces, optimizing the coring process, and enhancing sample collection methods.

### B. Preparation of spherical projectiles
A spherical alumina projectile HD-10 (diameter of 10 mm) was used. The vertical falls were made by varying heights: 10.0, 15.0, 20.0, and 25.0 mm. A total of 112 impact cratering experiments were conducted.

## III. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

### A. Crater depths
The depths of generated craters for all studied granular materials increased with the height of the fall. Notably, the crater depths in LHS-1 and MGS-1 were nearly identical and exhibited the greatest depth, whereas T-8 had the smallest depth. 

**Key Crater Depth Results (Fall heights 10 mm and 20 mm):**
- LHS-1: 5.0 ± 0.7 mm and 6.9 ± 0.8 mm
- LMS-1: 3.0 ± 0.2 mm and 3.8 ± 0.3 mm
- MGS-1: 4.9 ± 0.9 mm and 6.6 ± 0.8 mm
- T-8: 2.0 ± 0.3 mm and 3.3 ± 0.7 mm

### B. Scaling laws
The crater depth *d* is scaled by the sphere radius *R* according to the power-law scaling equation:

`d / 2R = C1 * [(ρi / (ρs * μ^2)) * (β * H / 2R)]^α`

Our data indicate that the crater depth is scaled with the exponent *α* ranging from 0.50 to 0.72. The exponent *α* of the LMS-1 layer was the lowest. When the exponent *α* was fixed (α = 0.6165), the C1 values for regolith simulants were around 0.06, about two times higher than that for T-8 (C1 = 0.034).

## IV. CONCLUSIONS
Our results indicate variations in the response of different regolith and terrestrial sand surfaces to impact events. The lunar highlands (LHS-1) and Martian surface (MGS-1) exhibit reduced resistance to impact due to higher porosity. This highlights the importance of considering unique granular mechanics when assessing landing and sampling missions and potential challenges for drilling operations.
