# Low-velocity impact response of lunar and Martian regolith simulants: Implications for lunar and Martian surface explorations

**Journal:** Physics of Fluids 36, 113318 (2024)  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1063/5.0233884  
**Authors:** Takuma Ishii, Arata Kioka, Jyh-Jaan Steven Huang, Yoshiki Tabuchi, Yasuhiro Yamada  

## ABSTRACT
The granular mechanics of lunar and Martian regolith remain inadequately understood, impeding progress in successful exploration, landing, drilling, sampling, and construction activities on extraterrestrial surfaces. This study investigates the granular behavior of lunar and Martian regolith under impact conditions. Impact cratering experiments were conducted for lunar highlands (LHS-1), lunar mare (LMS-1), Martian regolith (MGS-1) simulants, and terrestrial silica sand. Findings demonstrate that crater depths in lunar highlands and Martian surfaces are greater than those in lunar mare and terrestrial sand. At impact velocities of 40–70 cm/s, highland regolith crater depth is twice that of terrestrial sand. The study highlights significant differences in granular behavior, providing insights for future exploration, coring, and resource utilization.

### Experimental Setup
Spherical alumina projectiles (10 mm diameter) were dropped from heights of 10.0, 15.0, 20.0, and 25.0 mm. 112 experiments were conducted. Crater depth was measured using 3D topographical scanning (0.05 mm accuracy) and X-ray computed tomography (X-CT).

## RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

### Crater Depths
Crater depths increased with fall height across all materials. 
- **LHS-1 & MGS-1:** Exhibited the greatest depths and nearly identical behavior.
- **T-8 (Terrestrial Sand):** Exhibited the smallest depths, indicating higher resistance to penetration.
- **LMS-1:** Showed intermediate resistance, greater than highlands but less than terrestrial sand.

### Power-Law Scaling
The relationship between kinetic energy (Ek) and crater depth (d) followed power laws:
- LHS-1: d ~ Ek^0.553
- LMS-1: d ~ Ek^0.417
- MGS-1: d ~ Ek^0.473
- T-8: d ~ Ek^0.672

## CONCLUSIONS
The study reveals that the lunar highlands and Martian surfaces may exhibit reduced resistance to impact compared to the lunar mare due to higher porosity and lower cohesion in the uppermost layers. However, substantial cohesion in regolith compared to terrestrial sand presents unique challenges for drilling. These insights are crucial for designing landing pads, foundations, and optimizing resource extraction (ISRU) on the Moon and Mars.
