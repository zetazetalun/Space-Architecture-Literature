# A Survey on Extraterrestrial Habitation Structures with a Focus on Energy-Saving 3D Printing Techniques

## Abstract
In the past two decades, various space agencies have shown great enthusiasm for constructing habitable structures on lunar and Martian surfaces. The objective is to evaluate different habitat construction techniques from technical, economic, and energy-consumption perspectives. The primary focus is on the 3D printing technique. It was found that the combination of 3D-printed components along with an internal breathable inflatable module is the most promising technique for habitat development on the Moon and Mars. Moreover, the microwave sintering method was identified as the most energy-saving and reliable approach for melting on-site regolith for use in the 3D printing process.

## 1. Introduction
Future space habitation is envisioned for sustainability in the solar system. Key missions like NASA’s Mars Exploration Program and the Artemis program are establishing permanent habitats on the Moon and Mars. Safety requires resilient structures sheltering inhabitants from harsh atmospheres and radiation.

## 2. Materials and Methods
### 2.1. Physics-Based Challenges
Primary challenges identified include microgravity, cryogenic temperatures (-230°C to -140°C), airless atmospheres, lack of magnetic fields, surface radiation (solar winds/cosmic rays), and micrometeoroid impacts.
### 2.2. Aggregate Ability of the Regolith
Regolith can be used in raw form (e.g., inside bags for shielding) or synthesized form (e.g., combined with sulfur or epoxy binders). Sulfur concrete and sintered regolith are noted for high compressive strength (up to 55 MPa).
### 2.3. Outpost Construction Techniques
Techniques reviewed: 
- **Flat Truss**: Lightweight aluminum/composite trusses with regolith covers.
- **Inflatable Structures**: Compactable, lightweight, and pressurized; often made of Kevlar or ETFE.
- **Tensegrity**: Interlocking cables and bars.
- **Deployable Structures**: Size-variable components like umbrellas/antennas.
- **Arch Structures**: Fabricated aluminum arches protected by regolith.
- **3D Printing**: Automated layer-by-layer manufacturing using local regolith.

## 3. Results and Discussion
- **Inflatable Modules**: Most appropriate for providing a breathable environment due to high strength in tension and low transportation costs.
- **3D Printing**: Ideal for external protective shells. Microwave sintering is highlighted as requiring 33–44 times less energy than laser sintering and offering better heat penetration.
- **Geotechnical Factors**: Regolith bulk density and slope stability differ between Moon and Mars, impacting the energy required for robotic excavation.

## 4. Conclusions
A safe, reliable outpost is most constructible if 3D-printed components are used in conjunction with an internal breathable inflatable module. Future work should focus on investigating binders that can be produced on-site to reduce interplanetary transportation costs by over 60%.
