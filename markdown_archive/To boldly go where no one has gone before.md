# To boldly go where no one has gone before: Sulfur concrete, a promising construction material fulfilling the demands for a sustainable future on celestial objects: A review

**Authors:** Q. Wang, D. Snoeck
**Journal:** Materials Today, Volume 72, January/February 2024
**DOI:** 10.1016/j.mattod.2023.12.005

## Abstract
The need to build a long-term or even permanent base is now a significant concern with the development of the exploration of extraterrestrial celestial bodies. Sulfur concrete was first proposed as a new building material in the 20th century. Recently, sulfur concrete has attracted much interest, as sulfur is considered one of the most accessible resources on the Moon and Mars, thanks to the in-situ resource utilization methodology. This review paper summarizes the history of sulfur concrete development and different modified sulfur concretes. Previous research on extraterrestrial building materials is also reviewed.

## Introduction
NASA's Artemis program and ESA's space resource strategy aim for a sustainable human presence on the Moon and Mars. Local resource utilization is critical due to the high cost of transport from Earth (estimated at $130,000/kg). Sulfur concrete is a high-strength, waterless material that solidifies below 140°C.

## Characteristics of Sulfur Concrete
- **Binder:** Sulfur acts as a thermoplastic binder, melting at ~120°C and solidifying into a high-strength matrix.
- **ISRU Potential:** Sulfur is available on the Moon (troilite) and Mars (sulfates/ores). 
- **Sustainability:** It is 100% recyclable by melting.
- **Challenges:** 
  1. **Sublimation:** In a vacuum, sulfur can evaporate (1cm thickness in 2 hours at 120°C).
  2. **Thermal Expansion:** Mismatch between sulfur and silica aggregates can cause debonding.
  3. **Phase Transition:** Transformation from monoclinic to orthorhombic sulfur causes a 7% volume shrinkage, leading to internal stress.

## Additive Manufacturing and ISRU
- **Contour Crafting:** Recent studies demonstrate the use of sulfur-regolith mixtures in extrusion-based 3D printing.
- **Pre-heating:** Essential for regolith simulants to ensure uniform melting and prevent cracking during extrusion.
- **Sintering:** Solar or microwave sintering can be used to extract elemental sulfur from lunar/Martian soil.

## Solutions for Improvement
- **Modifiers:** Adding dicyclopentadiene (DCP) or olefinic polysulfide prevents macro-sulfur crystal formation and reduces shrinkage.
- **Phase-Change Materials (PCMs):** Using paraffins to mitigate thermal fluctuations.
- **Self-Healing:** Inspired by asphalt, sulfur concrete's thermoplastic nature allows for potential thermal self-repair of micro-cracks.

## Conclusions
Sulfur concrete is a highly promising ISRU material due to its lack of water requirement and fast strength development. Future research must focus on its durability under extreme vacuum/thermal cycling and the development of specialized automated construction techniques.
