# Space-technological and architectural methodology and process for long-term Mars habitats\n\n## Abstract\nThis study details the design of Martian Habitat Units (MHUs) intended for long-duration research missions. The approach shifts from minimal survival shelters to fulfilling settlement environments for crew members living on Mars for several years.\n\n## Methodology\nThe research utilizes an iterative design process that integrates mission specifications, environmental constraints, and architectural requirements. Key features include hierarchical modularity, extreme system redundancy, and site-specific criteria like dust settlement control.\n\n## Technical Design Specifications\n- **Structure:** Cluster of 10 units, each accommodating 9 crew members. The building envelope is a four-layered hexagonal construct with a growth rate of √3 between layers.\n- **Safety:** Three-fold redundancy in life support and power systems. A double-layered pressure vessel with an interspace at 80% atmospheric pressure facilitates a safer pressure gradient.\n- **Environmental Protection:** An Anti-Dust Settlement Membrane (ADSM) serves as a paraboloid dome to protect solar arrays from Martian dust storms.\n- **Human Factors:** Incorporation of interior gardens for psychological well-being, virtual reality interfaces to replace traditional windows, and private suites for crew autonomy.\n\n## Construction and ISRU\nThe MHUs are designed for automated construction via AI-assisted 3D printing using granulated Martian regolith. Robotic arms are tasked with pre-fabricating the main structures before crew arrival to minimize risk and logistics costs.\n\n## Power Systems\nThe power grid combines three sources:\n1. **Nuclear Fission:** 18-27 Kilopower reactors (10 kWe each) as primary power.\n2. **Solar Power:** Multi-junction PV cells carpeted in the cluster center.\n3. **Wind Power:** Five horizontal axis wind turbines (HAWT) as tertiary support during dust seasons when solar flux is low.\n\n## Conclusion\nThe paper concludes that modular, ISRU-based habitats are a viable solution for long-term Martian colonization, provided there is a high degree of robotic automation and system redundancy.
