# A new deployable pantographic lunar habitat\n\n## Abstract\nDeployable lunar structures have been and are being developed as an appropriate solution to the volumetric limitations of cargo mission flights. A deployable lunar habitat consisting of linear and planar elements connected through simple movable joints is introduced. This new structure is made of some linear units consisting of two types of scissor-like elements (SLE): 1) a modified polar scissor-like element and 2) a closed-loop of angulated scissor-like elements. After conducting theoretical research on lunar habitats, the evaluation criteria of transformable lunar structures are derived and then many new concepts are evaluated. The design considerations of such structures are discussed, and a proper form for these habitat types is selected. An experimental study is run to assess the best deployment strategy for the whole structure. By introducing the dimensional properties of the lunar habitats through physical and digital modeling process per crewmember, the derived alternatives from the previous stage are analyzed and the best transformation strategy that meets the aforementioned considerations is developed. Providing relatively high degrees of protection against environmental hazards through the combination of rigid plates with membrane, this proposed structure reduces the required extravehicular activity (EVA) time for assembly because of its quick and simple deployment mechanism. The folded state of the structure provides a low volume for the integration of subsystems.\n\n## 1. Introduction\nConnolly et al. describe the Lunar Surface Habitation System as the combined set of habitation equipment on the lunar surface which is essential for a safe and productive crewed lunar mission. Provision of living and working pressurized units for supporting self-sufficiency in space missions where the research and exploration efforts are run by human beings is considered the main task of the Exploration Habitats (XHabs). As to the planetary surface habitat design, there exist many approaches ranging from hard-shell modules to deployable units and even structures made of in-situ resources. The taxonomy applied to classify possible structural concepts is as follows: class-I: Pre-integrated, hard-shell module (rigid); class-II: prefabricated, destination assembled (deployable); and class-III: In-Situ Resource Utilization (ISRU) derived structure.\n\n## 2. Design considerations\nThe space environment can pose different problems for the crew residing and working in extraterrestrial missions. Habitation design is directly influenced by many of these problems. The micrometeoroids, radiation, and vacuum phenomena are among the main inevitable environmental hazards which affect the design process and make space architects propose practical solutions.\n\n## 3. Scissor-hinge systems\nA deployable structure, a member of the mechanisms category is an assembly of rigid elements attached through mobile connections. The arrangement of the elements provides the capability of transformation from a compact configuration to a deployed configuration.\n\n## 5. Conclusions\nA new deployable lunar habitat where the SLE structures are applied is proposed on a conceptual level, and its geometric and kinetic properties are assessed through a parametric modeling process in digital and small-scale physical prototypes. Providing a nominal internal volume of approximately 170 m3, this proposed structure will allow four astronauts to settle on the lunar surface for a 180-day mission. This proposed system has the potential to combine quick deployment mechanism extracted from inflatable structures with proper protection characteristics of rigid structures to reduce the costs associated with lunar settlement programs.
